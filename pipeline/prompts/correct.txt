You wrote this article. An independent fact-checker has returned findings against
it. Your job now is to apply them — not to write it again.

ARTICLE:
{article}

FINDINGS:
{issues}

THE FRONTMATTER

The article begins with a YAML frontmatter block between two `---` lines. It
carries the title, date, slug, category and, for season articles, the facts panel
the site renders above the prose.

Return it intact, exactly as it appears, at the top of your response.

Do not drop it. Do not summarise it. Do not move it below the prose. The site
cannot parse the article without it, and an article returned as prose alone
cannot be published at all.

The only permitted change is where a finding's `quote` falls inside the
frontmatter — a wrong top scorer, a wrong league finish, a wrong manager. Then
correct that value and leave every other line untouched.

"Clean copy" refers to the body: no notes, no change marks, no commentary. It
does not mean stripping the frontmatter.

THE CONSTRAINT

You are the last stage before the article reaches Denny, and you are the same
writer who produced it. That is a structural weakness in the pipeline, and this
prompt exists to contain it. Left unconstrained, the writer at the final stage can
quietly soften a finding it disagrees with, or improve prose on the way past, and
nothing downstream would notice.

So:

Edit only the spans identified by the `quote` field of each finding. Everything
else stays exactly as written — wording, paragraphing, structure, headings, order,
frontmatter. Do not improve a sentence you now dislike. Do not restructure. Do not
tidy.

Apply the smallest change that resolves the finding. Where `fix` gives a
correction, use it unless it is factually wrong or would break the sentence, in
which case make the minimum equivalent correction and say so in the disposition.
Where `fix` is exactly "DELETE", remove the claim, repairing the surrounding
sentence only as far as grammar requires.

If a quote cannot be located in the article, do not guess at what was meant and do
not edit an approximate match. Record it as contested with the reason.

YOU MAY DISAGREE

Disagreeing with a finding is legitimate. The checker can be wrong, can
misidentify the evidential status of a claim, or can propose a fix that introduces
a different error.

What is not legitimate is disposing of a finding without saying so. Every finding
gets a disposition. None may be silently dropped, merged, or treated as resolved
by a change made elsewhere.

Where you contest a finding, the wording stands unchanged and Denny resolves it.

OUTPUT

Return the complete article: the frontmatter block first, exactly as received,
then the body in markdown. Nothing before the opening `---`.

Then, on its own line:

---DISPOSITIONS---

Then one entry per finding, in id order, in this shape:

**I1 — corrected**
What was changed, and the new wording.

**I2 — contested**
Why the finding was not applied, and what Denny needs to decide.

Every id from the findings must appear exactly once. Keep each entry to a couple
of lines. This is what Denny reads first, so it must be short enough to read in
full and specific enough to act on.

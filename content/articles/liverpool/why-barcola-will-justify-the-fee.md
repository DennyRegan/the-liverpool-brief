The argument against Bradley Barcola is that he did not start every week for Paris Saint-Germain. It gets repeated as though it settles something.

Look at who he was behind. Khvicha Kvaratskhelia, who for my money is the best left winger in Europe. And Desire Doue on the other side, with only Lamine Yamal and Michael Olise ahead of him among right-sided players in the game right now. That is not a player who could not get in a team. That is a player in a queue behind two of the best wide forwards in world football, at a club winning back-to-back Champions Leagues.

Being third in that particular queue is not the criticism people think it is.

## Liverpool have not had one of these for a while

Since Sadio Mane and Mohamed Salah, Liverpool have not had a genuinely world-class wide player. Some will point to Luis Diaz. I would say Diaz's game went to another level after he joined Bayern, not before, and Liverpool sold the version that came before the leap.

That is the gap Barcola is being paid to fill. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/football/2026/aug/31/liverpool-confirm-bradley-barcola-signing-psg) report the deal as £106m up front with a further £17m in add-ons tied to individual and team achievements. At that price you are not buying a good winger. You are buying one of the best in the world at what he does.

## What the numbers actually say

The interesting thing about Barcola is that the stats do not describe the player most people think they are getting.

[Opta's analysis of his PSG career](https://theanalyst.com/articles/bradley-barcola-liverpool-psg-transfer-stats) has him as the club's leading open-play creator last season, with 72 chances created across all competitions. More than anyone at a team that won the Champions League. For a player filed as a runner rather than a creator, that is the number that should make Liverpool supporters sit up.

The carrying numbers say the same thing in a different way. Among PSG players with over a thousand minutes last season, nobody produced more chance-creating carries or more shot-ending carries per ninety minutes than Barcola. He is not just quick. He is quick with an end product attached, which is a considerably rarer thing.

He presses too. Thirty possessions won in the final third across the season, second at PSG only to Vitinha on thirty-six.

None of this is new. Playing for Lyon against Montpellier in May 2023, aged 20, he produced three assists in a single Ligue 1 match, all of them for Alexandre Lacazette in a 5-4 win. [Ligue 1's own records](https://ligue1.com/en/articles/l1_article_2586-history-and-records-of-the-ligue-1-mc-donald-s-assist-leader-rankings) list him as the youngest player to register an assist hat-trick since that ranking began.

The moment most people know him for came in September 2024, thirteen seconds into France's Nations League game against Italy. [UEFA](https://www.uefa.com/uefanationsleague/news/0293-1c4eec602eac-82476e51f46e-1000--fastest-nations-league-goals-bradley-barcola-thorgan-haza/) have it as the fastest goal in the competition's history. It was reported as the fastest in French national team history too, though [Opta noted](https://www.goal.com/en-us/lists/move-over-kylian-mbappe-bradley-barcola-scores-france-fastest-goal-italy-nations-league/blt3b26b8ced8963c0b) France scored three first-minute goals before 1993 with no reliable timings, so treat that part as unofficial.

And for a player supposedly kept out of the side, he did not leave empty-handed. [PSG's own farewell](https://www.psg.fr/en/content/bradley-barcola-signs-for-liverpool-fc) puts his record at 152 appearances, 39 goals and 37 assists across three seasons, with thirteen trophies in that time. He had not scored a senior professional goal until January 2023.

## He is an Iraola player

Opta's own conclusion, before the deal was done, was that Barcola could be perfect for what Iraola wants. The reasoning is straightforward. Iraola's teams press high and attack space at pace, and Barcola's entire game is running onto the ball rather than asking for it to feet.

[Sky](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/11095/13565590/bradley-barcola-to-liverpool-or-arsenal-psg-and-france-forward-is-quick-strong-versatile-intelligent-and-in-demand) made the related point that this is exactly what Liverpool have been missing. Without the movement of Diaz, Diogo Jota and Darwin Nunez, the side looked very different last season, and Florian Wirtz is at his best sliding passes through the lines for someone willing to run.

Barcola has said much the same himself, [telling the club](https://www.liverpoolfc.com/news/interview-every-word-bradley-barcola-why-liverpool-his-new-home) that his style and the manager's are closely aligned, and that Iraola told him directly he was counted on. Iraola, for his part, [said Barcola arrived with the right mentality](https://www.liverpoolfc.com/news/im-very-happy-andoni-iraola-bradley-barcolas-arrival-and-availability-ipswich). Neither of those things wins football matches, but a player who wants to be here and a manager who knows what he wants from him is a better starting point than most big signings get.

## The awkward part

He plays on the left. Opta have him at 77% of his PSG minutes there against 13% on the right, so this is not a preference, it is where he lives. Iraola has said he wants wingers who can do both, and Barcola is right-footed, so the door is not completely shut. But you do not spend £106m to solve a problem on the opposite flank and hope.

Which means Cody Gakpo moves right or sits on the bench.

I have written elsewhere that Liverpool have spent money in an area that did not need it, and I stand by that. It is a fair thing to hold against the club. It is not a thing to hold against the player, and the two get collapsed into one far too often. Whether the squad is balanced and whether Barcola is worth the fee are separate questions, and the second can be yes even when the first is no.

## What I expect

Twenty goal involvements as a minimum in his first season. Below that and £106m looks heavy whatever the performances have looked like.

But the number is not really the test. The test is whether he looks like a constant threat every week, the way the very best wide players do, where the opposition full-back is thinking about him from the first minute to the last. That is what Liverpool have been missing since Salah's best years, and it is not something you can buy cheaply.

I think he is going to be the best wide player in the league. Given what has been paid, he should be.

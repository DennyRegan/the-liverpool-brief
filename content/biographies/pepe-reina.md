Pepe Reina made 394 competitive appearances for Liverpool and kept 177 clean sheets, according to LFChistory. He joined from Villarreal in July 2005, weeks after Liverpool had won the European Cup in Istanbul, and went straight into the team. He won the Premier League Golden Glove in each of his first three seasons. He saved three penalties in the shoot-out that won the 2006 FA Cup final and two more against Chelsea in the 2007 Champions League semi-final. He was Liverpool's goalkeeper for eight seasons.

## From Barcelona to Anfield

Reina was born in Madrid on 31 August 1982. He played first-team football for Barcelona between 2000 and 2002, making 30 La Liga appearances, before moving to Villarreal. Liverpool signed him on 4 July 2005 for £6 million. He was 22.

Rafael Benítez picked him at once. Jerzy Dudek had saved Liverpool in the Istanbul final only weeks before, but he was not even on the bench when Reina made his debut against Total Network Solutions at Anfield on 13 July 2005. It was a Champions League qualifier, and Steven Gerrard scored a hat-trick in a 3–0 win.

His first trophy came on 26 August in Monaco. CSKA Moscow led the UEFA Super Cup through Carvalho in the 28th minute. Djibril Cissé came off the bench to equalise in the 82nd minute, scored again in extra time and then crossed for Luis García to make it 3–1.

## The clean sheets

Reina wore the number 25 shirt. John Achterberg, who coached Liverpool's goalkeepers, later picked out what set him apart. "Reina was unbelievable in his distribution and his game-reading as well," he told the club.

On 15 December 2005 Liverpool beat Deportivo Saprissa 3–0 in Yokohama at the Club World Championship. It was their 11th clean sheet in a row, a club record.

That season he won the Golden Glove with 20 Premier League clean sheets. He won it again in 2006–07 with 19 and in 2007–08 with 18.

## Cardiff, 2006

The FA Cup final against West Ham United was played at the Millennium Stadium on 13 May 2006. Reina had a difficult afternoon. Jamie Carragher turned Scaloni's cross into his own net in the 20th minute. Seven minutes later Reina spilled a shot from Etherington and Dean Ashton scored. Cissé and Gerrard brought Liverpool level, but Paul Konchesky's cross went into the net in the second half to make it 3–2 to West Ham.

Gerrard's last-minute strike made it 3–3. Extra time brought no more goals, and the final went to penalties. Hamann, Gerrard and Riise scored for Liverpool, and Hyypiä missed. Reina saved from Bobby Zamora, Konchesky and Anton Ferdinand. Liverpool won the shoot-out 3–1.

## Chelsea and Athens

Chelsea came to Anfield for the second leg of the Champions League semi-final on 1 May 2007 with a 1–0 lead. Daniel Agger levelled the tie in the 22nd minute. Reina blocked a shot from Didier Drogba after the striker had beaten the offside trap. There were no more goals, and the tie went to penalties.

Zenden, Alonso, Gerrard and Dirk Kuyt scored for Liverpool. Reina saved from Arjen Robben and Geremi. Liverpool won 4–1 on penalties and reached the final.

Milan beat them 2–1 in Athens on 23 May. Filippo Inzaghi deflected Andrea Pirlo's free-kick in just before half-time, then went round Reina from Kaká's pass in the 82nd minute. Kuyt headed one back in the 89th minute.

Reina's father, Miguel, had also kept goal in a European Cup final. He was in goal for Atlético Madrid against Bayern Munich in 1974. Bayern equalised in the last minute to make it 1–1 and won the replay 4–0.

## Close to the title

In 2008–09 Reina kept 20 Premier League clean sheets again, equalling the club record. Liverpool won 4–1 at Old Trafford on 14 March 2009, but Manchester United held them off for the title.

On 25 October 2009 Liverpool beat United 2–0 at Anfield. Torres scored first, and David Ngog added the second late on at the Kop end. Reina ran the length of the pitch to join the celebration. "It was a surprise for me to see Pepe when I turned back to celebrate!" Ngog told the club years later. Reina called it his best moment at Liverpool.

For Spain, Iker Casillas was first choice. Reina was in the squads that won the European Championship in 2008 and 2012 and the World Cup in 2010, and won 36 caps between 2005 and 2017. He started the 2–1 win over Greece at Euro 2008 in Salzburg on 18 June, with Álvaro Arbeloa at right-back and Xabi Alonso wearing the armband.

## Through three managers

On 9 April 2010 Reina signed a new six-year contract. Liverpool were in the Europa League semi-finals that month and lost the first leg 1–0 at Atlético Madrid on 22 April. Supporters voted him their Player of the Season for 2009–10, with 75 per cent of the vote.

Benítez left that summer. In Roy Hodgson's first league match, at home to Arsenal on 15 August 2010, Joe Cole was sent off before half-time and Ngog put ten-man Liverpool ahead just after the break. With seconds of normal time left, Chamakh's header hit the post and rebounded to Reina, who put the ball into his own net. It finished 1–1.

Under Kenny Dalglish, Liverpool reached the League Cup final against Cardiff City on 26 February 2012. It finished 2–2 after extra time. Reina did not need to make a save in the shoot-out. Miller and Gestede hit a post each and Anthony Gerrard shot wide, and Liverpool won 3–2.

On 1 April 2012 Reina was sent off in a 2–0 defeat at Newcastle after an altercation with James Perch. Liverpool had used all their substitutes, so José Enrique went in goal. The three-match ban ruled Reina out of the FA Cup semi-final against Everton, and Brad Jones played instead.

## Leaving

Reina stayed first choice in Brendan Rodgers' first season. His last game for Liverpool was on 19 May 2013. That summer Liverpool signed Simon Mignolet, and on 29 July 2013 the club confirmed Reina's season-long loan to Napoli, where he joined Benítez again.

In a farewell letter to supporters he wrote that Liverpool "agreed to loan me to Napoli without telling me first". He said he had asked the club to consider Barcelona if an offer came, but had also talked about extending his contract if it did not. He wrote that his children consider Liverpool their home.

He did not return. Liverpool confirmed his permanent transfer to Bayern Munich on 8 August 2014, on a three-year deal. His 394 Liverpool appearances included 285 in the Premier League and 56 in the Champions League.

After retiring as a player in 2025, he began coaching at Villarreal's youth level.

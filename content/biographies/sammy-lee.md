Sammy Lee started every match of Liverpool's 1983–84 season. There were 67 of them, from the Charity Shield to the European Cup final, and Joe Fagan picked him for all 67. Liverpool won the league, the League Cup and the European Cup. In all, Lee made 295 appearances for the club and scored 19 goals, and he started two winning European Cup finals. Later he came back to Anfield as a coach and then as assistant manager.

## A goal on debut

Lee was born in Liverpool on 7 February 1959 and signed professional terms in April 1976. His senior debut came at home to Leicester City on 8 April 1978. He replaced the injured David Johnson after six minutes. Liverpool fell behind twice, and Lee scored the second equaliser before Tommy Smith got the winner in a 3–2 victory.

The goal did not earn him a place. He made two appearances that season and two more in 1978–79. His chance came late in the following campaign, when an injury to Terry McDermott opened a place in midfield. In 1980–81 he established himself, taking over from Jimmy Case on the right.

## Breitner and Paris

That season Bob Paisley gave him a specific job in the European Cup semi-final. In the second leg in Munich, Lee was told to man-mark Bayern's playmaker, Paul Breitner. Phil Neal, who played behind him, later remembered Paisley telling Lee to mark Breitner. Neal called him one of the side's "hardworking bees". The match finished 1–1, and Liverpool went through on away goals.

Lee started the final against Real Madrid in Paris, which Liverpool won 1–0. Earlier that spring he had helped Liverpool beat West Ham United in a replay to win the League Cup for the first time. Three league championships in a row followed, in 1982, 1983 and 1984, along with three more League Cups.

## England

Bobby Robson gave him his England debut on 17 November 1982, in a European Championship qualifier away to Greece. Lee scored the third goal in a 3–0 win, so he had now scored on his debut for club and country. He won 14 caps and scored twice. All of them came while he was a Liverpool player.

## Sixty-seven out of sixty-seven

Fagan took over as manager in 1983–84. Lee started all 42 league matches, every cup tie and the Charity Shield. Liverpool won three trophies that season, and he was not left out once.

He also scored one of the season's important goals. On 11 April 1984, in the first leg of the European Cup semi-final against Dinamo Bucharest at Anfield, he headed in Alan Kennedy's cross for a 1–0 win. Thirty years later he told the club's website: "I didn't score many goals and certainly didn't score many with my head." He added: "To score it at the Kop end is something I'll never forget and I never want to forget it."

Liverpool won the return leg 2–1. In the final against Roma, Lee played all 120 minutes of a 1–1 draw, and Liverpool won the penalty shoot-out 4–2. It was his second European Cup final, three years after the first, and his second winner's medal.

## Leaving Anfield

After that season his place was less secure. He made 25 appearances in 1984–85 and 26 in 1985–86, when Liverpool won the league and FA Cup double. In August 1986 he joined Queens Park Rangers. He left with 197 league appearances and 13 league goals for Liverpool.

After a season at QPR he moved to Osasuna in Spain. A short spell at Southampton and a move to Bolton Wanderers followed before his playing career ended.

## Back on the staff

In the early 1990s he returned to Liverpool under Graeme Souness, first working with the reserves. He stayed through Roy Evans's time as manager and became a first-team coach under Gérard Houllier. He was on the staff when Liverpool won the League Cup, FA Cup and UEFA Cup in 2001.

Players coming through the club remembered him. Tom Culshaw recalled Lee, his reserve-team coach, making him captain. Steven Gerrard later named Lee with Houllier and Phil Thompson as the first-team staff he was passed on to from the youth coaches.

Lee combined his Liverpool work with a part-time England coaching role, then joined the national set-up full-time in 2004. He went on to Bolton as Sam Allardyce's assistant and succeeded him as manager in April 2007. He was in charge until October of that year.

## Assistant manager

Rafael Benítez brought him back to Liverpool as assistant manager in May 2008. Benítez described him as "a very experienced and very well respected coach in his own right" and as someone who knew the club well.

When Benítez was absent in December 2008, Lee took charge of the side for a 1–1 draw with Arsenal on 21 December. He said the game plan was already in place and it was business as usual.

He stayed through two more changes of manager. When Roy Hodgson's first pre-season began in the summer of 2010, Liverpool had no goalkeeping coach. John Achterberg, then working with the younger teams, remembered the call: "Sammy Lee rang us and said, 'You need to start the pre-season with us because we have no goalie coach.'" Under Kenny Dalglish the following year, the reserve-team coach Rodolfo Borrell said Lee and Steve Clarke were "always coming to the Academy and watching games and training sessions."

Lee left Liverpool by mutual consent in the summer of 2011, having worked under Benítez, Hodgson and Dalglish in that final spell. He went on to coach at several other clubs and with England.

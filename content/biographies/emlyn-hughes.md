Emlyn Hughes made 665 appearances for Liverpool and scored 49 goals. Bill Shankly signed him as a 19-year-old in 1967, and he became captain in 1973. He lifted the European Cup in Rome in 1977 and again at Wembley in 1978, the club's first two. He won four league titles and two UEFA Cups, and in 1977 the Football Writers' Association named him Footballer of the Year. He also won 62 England caps and captained his country 23 times.

## Barrow and Blackpool

Hughes was born in Barrow-in-Furness on 28 August 1947. His father, Fred Hughes, was a rugby league international. He played schoolboy football in Barrow, where he was judged too small, before Blackpool offered him an opportunity.

That did not come at the first attempt either. In an early interview with Jimmy Armfield, which LFChistory preserves, Hughes recalled: "First they say I was too young and too small and advised me to come back again in another year." He came back. He described returning to the ground in the afternoons to work on his speed over the first ten yards and his quickness on the turn, and studying positional play in the big matches. Armfield praised his willingness to learn.

He developed into a first-team player under Ron Suart and made his senior debut in the 1965–66 season.

## Shankly's signing

Shankly paid Blackpool £65,000 for him in February 1967. Hughes made his debut against Stoke City at Anfield on 4 March, a 2–1 win, and played ten league games before the end of the season. He could play at full-back or in midfield, which gave Shankly options as he began to replace the side that had won the league in 1964 and 1966.

His nickname was "Crazy Horse".

The trophies did not come straight away. Liverpool lost the 1971 FA Cup final to Arsenal and finished third in 1971–72, one point behind Derby County.

## Champions in 1973

The following season Hughes played 65 competitive matches. On 3 March 1973 Liverpool went to Goodison Park, and the derby was goalless with ten minutes left. Hughes rounded the Everton goalkeeper, David Lawson, to score, then added a second eight minutes later. The 2–0 win kept Liverpool top of the table.

They won the league, and with it the club's first European trophy. Liverpool beat Borussia Mönchengladbach 3–0 at Anfield in the first leg of the UEFA Cup final, then lost 2–0 in Germany and took the cup 3–2 on aggregate. Tommy Smith was still captain.

## Captain

Shankly gave Hughes the captaincy in the summer of 1973, and he moved permanently to centre-back. The change soured his relationship with Smith, who had been at the club far longer and made his resentment plain in later years. Bob Paisley later wrote: "Smithy and Hughes never spoke to each other."

Hughes's new partner in the middle of defence was Phil Thompson. "We were basically two midfield players playing at centre-back and Shanks was changing the way Liverpool play," Thompson later wrote on the club's website.

In the 1974 FA Cup final they kept Malcolm Macdonald and Newcastle United out. Kevin Keegan scored twice and Steve Heighway once in a 3–0 win, and Hughes collected his first major trophy as captain. Shankly's last season had ended with a trophy, and the player he had signed as a teenager was leading the side.

## Paisley's Liverpool

Under Paisley, Liverpool won the league and the UEFA Cup again in 1975–76. In the final against Club Brugge they were 2–0 down at Anfield before winning 3–2. In Belgium Brugge scored first, Keegan equalised, and the match finished 1–1 for a 4–3 aggregate win.

In 1976–77 Hughes played every competitive match, 62 in all. Liverpool retained the league title. Manchester United beat them in the FA Cup final on 21 May. Four days later they played Mönchengladbach in Rome in the European Cup final.

Hughes played in central defence. Terry McDermott put Liverpool ahead, Allan Simonsen equalised, and Smith headed Liverpool back in front before Phil Neal's penalty made it 3–1. Hughes lifted the cup. Liverpool added the UEFA Super Cup that year.

In 1978 they reached the final again, against Club Brugge at Wembley. The first half was goalless. Kenny Dalglish scored the only goal from Graeme Souness's pass, and Hughes lifted the European Cup for the second time.

## The last season

Injury and the emergence of Alan Hansen cut Hughes's part in 1978–79 to 16 league appearances, 28 in all competitions. Liverpool won the title, conceding 16 league goals, and Hughes had his fourth championship medal.

He joined Wolverhampton Wanderers in August 1979.

## England

Hughes made his England debut against the Netherlands in November 1969. He won 62 caps and captained the side 23 times. Fifty-nine of the caps came while he was at Liverpool and the last three at Wolves. He was in the squad for the 1970 World Cup but did not play.

## After Liverpool

At Wolves he won a trophy Liverpool had not given him. On 15 March 1980 he captained them to a 1–0 win over Nottingham Forest in the League Cup final, partnering George Berry in defence, with Andy Gray scoring the winner.

He became player-manager of Rotherham United in 1981. That job ended in 1983, and short spells as a player followed, including at Hull City and Swansea City. He was also a team captain on A Question of Sport.

Hughes died of a brain tumour on 9 November 2004, aged 57.

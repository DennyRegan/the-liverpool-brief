Ian Callaghan played 857 competitive games for Liverpool, more than anyone else in the club's history, and scored 68 goals. He made his debut in the Second Division in April 1960 and started the European Cup final in Rome in May 1977. In between he won five league titles, two FA Cups and two UEFA Cups. He began as a right winger in Bill Shankly's first great side, moved into central midfield after a cartilage operation, and was still a regular under Bob Paisley at 35.

## Toxteth to Anfield

Callaghan was born in Toxteth on 10 April 1942. He joined Liverpool as a local boy in 1957 and turned professional on 28 March 1960. His debut came on 16 April 1960, six days after his 18th birthday, in a 4–0 home win over Bristol Rovers. LFChistory records that he came in for Billy Liddell, his boyhood idol.

A regular place took longer. Shankly signed Kevin Lewis for the 1960–61 season, and Lewis scored 22 goals in 36 games, so Callaghan had to wait. He became a regular from November 1961 and made 23 league appearances as Liverpool won the Second Division in 1961–62.

Two seasons later he played all 42 league games as Liverpool became champions of England. He was ever-present in the league again when they won the title in 1965–66.

## On the right wing

In that first Shankly team Callaghan played wide on the right, with Peter Thompson on the left. He gave the side speed and direct running, and his crosses supplied Roger Hunt and Ian St John. His most important cross came at Wembley.

On 1 May 1965 Liverpool met Leeds United in the FA Cup final. The game went to extra time. Hunt scored after three minutes of it and Billy Bremner equalised. In the 111th minute Callaghan crossed from the right and St John headed the winner. Liverpool won 2–1 and lifted the FA Cup for the first time.

Of everything he won, Callaghan later named that afternoon as his favourite memory. "First has to be Wembley '65 and Liverpool lifting the FA Cup for the first time," he told LFChistory.

## England

Callaghan won four England caps. The first came against Finland on 26 June 1966, a 3–0 friendly win. The second came at the World Cup, on 20 July 1966, when he played the full 90 minutes of a 2–0 group win over France. He was not used in the quarter-final, the semi-final or the final, but he was part of the winning squad. His winner's medal was presented in June 2009.

His next cap did not come until 7 September 1977, a 0–0 draw with Switzerland. The gap was 11 years and 49 days, still the longest between two England caps. England had played 108 matches in between. His fourth and last cap followed on 12 October 1977, a 2–0 win over Luxembourg.

## A new position

In 1970–71 a cartilage operation kept him out for about four months. Brian Hall took his place on the right, at a time when Shankly was replacing much of his first title-winning side. Callaghan returned to play in the 1971 FA Cup final, which Liverpool lost to Arsenal.

Shankly moved him into central midfield. He no longer had to beat a full-back and cross from the touchline. LFChistory says the change extended his Liverpool career by seven years.

The appearance figures show how much he was used. In 1972–73, when Liverpool won the league and the UEFA Cup, he played 66 competitive games, including all 42 in the league. In 1973–74 he played 61, again including every league game. They were the two highest season totals of his career, and both came after the operation and the change of role.

In 1974 he became the first Liverpool player to be named Footballer of the Year by the Football Writers' Association. On 4 May that year he started the FA Cup final against Newcastle United. Kevin Keegan scored twice and Steve Heighway once, and Liverpool won 3–0. Nine years after his cross against Leeds, Callaghan had a second FA Cup winner's medal.

He was appointed MBE in the 1975 New Year Honours.

## Paisley and Rome

Under Paisley, Liverpool won the league in 1976 and 1977 and the UEFA Cup in 1976. Callaghan appeared in four FA Cup finals for the club in all. He lost two of them, in 1971 and in 1977. In the second, against Manchester United on 21 May 1977, he came on for David Johnson in the 63rd minute and Liverpool lost 2–1.

Four days later, on 25 May 1977, he started the European Cup final against Borussia Mönchengladbach at the Stadio Olimpico in Rome. Liverpool used no substitutes, so he played the whole match. They won 3–1. He was 35.

He was the only member of that side who had played in Liverpool's first European tie, against KR Reykjavik on 17 August 1964. Liverpool won that first leg 5–0, and Callaghan set up one of the goals.

## The last season

Graeme Souness joined in January 1978 and took his place. On 22 March 1978 Callaghan played in the League Cup final replay against Nottingham Forest at Old Trafford, which Liverpool lost 1–0. He was booked, the only booking of his Liverpool career.

A week later, on 29 March, he played in the first leg of the European Cup semi-final at Mönchengladbach, a 2–1 defeat. It was his 857th and last game for Liverpool. When Liverpool retained the European Cup against Club Brugge at Wembley on 10 May 1978, winning 1–0, he was a named substitute and did not come on.

## After Liverpool

He spent the summer of 1978 on loan with Fort Lauderdale Strikers. He left Liverpool on 14 September 1978 and joined John Toshack's Swansea City that month. He helped them win promotion from the Third Division in 1978–79.

In an interview with James Mossop of the Sunday Express in December 1979, Callaghan said Paisley had offered him an improved contract and wanted him to stay. He had decided he needed to move to play regularly.

He joined Cork United in January 1981 and then Crewe Alexandra. His last professional game was for Crewe at Halifax Town on 20 March 1982, three weeks before his 40th birthday. He retired that year.

His Liverpool record stands at 857 games and 68 goals, including 640 league games and 49 league goals. The 857 counts every competitive first-team game, including the Charity Shield and the European Super Cup, and it is still the club record.

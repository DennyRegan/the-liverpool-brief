Albert Stubbins scored 84 goals in 178 games for Liverpool, according to LFChistory. He arrived from Newcastle United in September 1946 for a club-record fee and scored 24 league goals in his first season, the same number as Jack Balmer, as Liverpool won the league for the first time since 1923. His goal at Molineux on the last day of their programme gave them the 2–1 win they needed. Three years later he led the attack in Liverpool's first Wembley final.

## Wallsend and Newcastle

Stubbins was born in Wallsend in July 1919. He trained as a draughtsman and worked in that role in the shipyards during the war. He was an amateur with Sunderland before joining Newcastle in 1936.

He scored more than 230 wartime goals for Newcastle, though those came in regional competitions rather than the peacetime league. When league football returned in 1946, Newcastle were in the Second Division. Stubbins asked for a transfer that summer because he wanted to play in the First Division.

## The coin toss

George Kay had managed Liverpool since 1936. He had brought Billy Liddell and Bob Paisley to the club before the war and took his squad on a tour of the United States and Canada before the 1946–47 season. Early that season Liverpool beat Chelsea 7–4 at home, then lost 5–0 to Manchester United four days later. They still needed a centre-forward.

Liverpool and Everton both agreed terms with Newcastle. When the clubs came for him, Stubbins was at the Newcastle News Theatre, and, as the Daily Mail reported, messages were flashed on cinema screens asking him to go to St James' Park. By his own account, he tossed a coin to decide which club to speak to first: heads Liverpool, tails Everton. It came down heads. He met Kay and the chairman, Billy McConnell, agreed to join, and never spoke to Everton.

The transfer was completed on 12 September 1946. The fee was a club record, usually given as £13,000, though some accounts say £12,500.

## An immediate return

Stubbins made his debut two days later at Burnden Park. Liverpool led Bolton Wanderers through Berry Nieuwenhuys, Bolton equalised, and Stubbins scored in the 82nd minute. Balmer scored in the last minute to make it 3–1.

Before his first Anfield game, a derby against Everton, the Liverpool Echo warned that the Everton centre-half Tommy Jones "may have to gallop a little more than normal to hold Stubbins in check". The match finished 0–0.

Years later he said the supporters stood by him: "Even if I had a bad game the crowd would never crucify me like they would some players."

## Birmingham, 1947

On 1 March 1947 Liverpool beat Birmingham City 4–1 in the FA Cup quarter-final at Anfield, on an icy pitch. Stubbins scored a hat-trick. His second goal came from a Liddell cross, which he met with a diving header past Gil Merrick. Liddell later said Stubbins met the ball with his head when parallel with the ground and that it went in like a rocket. Stubbins remembered the cost: "both of my knees were lacerated and bleeding but it was certainly worth it".

The goals came from a plan. The Daily Mirror reported that Liverpool sent their attacks through the middle, with Balmer playing close to Stubbins. Ray Lambert explained afterwards that this always gave them a spare man in the centre, while Birmingham still had to watch Willie Fagan on the right.

Liverpool met Burnley in the semi-final. The first game at Ewood Park finished 0–0, and Burnley then beat Liverpool 1–0 in the replay at Maine Road.

## Molineux and the title

The league run-in went better. Liverpool won six and drew one of their last seven league games, including wins over Manchester United and Arsenal. They went to Wolverhampton Wanderers on 31 May 1947 with 55 points, one behind Wolves, and needed to win.

Balmer scored after 21 minutes. Stubbins made it 2–0 in the 38th minute from Bob Priday's pass. It was his 24th league goal of the season. Jimmy Dunn pulled one back for Wolves in the 65th minute, and Liverpool held on with Cyril Sidlow in goal.

The title was still not settled. Stoke City could take it on goal average by winning their last game, at Sheffield United, on 14 June. Liverpool were playing Everton in the Liverpool Senior Cup final that day, and won 2–1. Stoke lost 2–1, and Liverpool were champions for the fifth time.

## Scoring on, and a contract dispute

Stubbins scored even more league goals in 1947–48: 25 in 40 games, and 27 in all competitions. Balmer was next with 15. On 6 March 1948 Stubbins scored all four goals in a 4–0 win over Huddersfield Town at Anfield.

In 1948–49 he played only 18 games in league and cup and scored six league goals in 15 league appearances. He delayed signing his contract, and injuries also kept him out. Accounts of the disagreement differ. Club histories describe a dispute over his wish to live and train in the North-East while Liverpool wanted him on Merseyside. In his own later explanation, Stubbins pointed to an arrangement McConnell had made for him to write a weekly column in the Football Echo. After the chairman's death, he said, the board did not consider itself bound by the promise, and the uncertainty delayed his signing. Stubbins himself later described the matter as "all very amicable".

## Wembley, 1950

He played 28 league games in 1949–50 and scored ten league goals. Liverpool went their first 19 league games unbeaten. In a 0–0 derby at Goodison Park on 27 August, Leslie Edwards of the Liverpool Daily Post called one Stubbins run "the best sixpenny piece dribble he has ever produced". It set up a chance for Liddell, who hit a post.

In the FA Cup fifth round at Stockport County on 11 February 1950, Fagan scored first and Stubbins added the second in the 70th minute. Stockport scored in the last minute, and Liverpool won 2–1. In the semi-final at Maine Road on 25 March, Stubbins wore the No. 9 shirt as Liverpool beat Everton 2–0, with goals from Paisley and Liddell. In a souvenir profile that year, Stubbins said that next to the Everton semi-final, Molineux in 1947 was his most enduring memory.

It was the club's first Wembley final. Arsenal won 2–0 on 29 April, with both goals from Reg Lewis. Stubbins played at centre-forward. Paisley was left out despite his goal in the semi-final, and Stubbins encouraged him not to make any hasty decision about leaving.

## The last seasons

His appearances fell after that: 24 in 1950–51, 12 in 1951–52 and five in 1952–53. Kay retired through illness in January 1951, and Don Welsh became manager. Stubbins's last game for Liverpool was a 3–1 defeat at Stoke City on 3 January 1953. It was Sammy Smyth's debut.

## After Liverpool

Stubbins left Liverpool in September 1953 for a brief spell with the North-East non-league club Ashington. He later coached the New York Americans in the International Soccer League in 1960 and worked as a sports journalist in the North-East, writing for the Sunday People from November 1960 and working in local radio.

In 1967 his photograph appeared on the cover of the Beatles' album Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band, the only footballer on it. He was told of his inclusion through the Beatles' Apple organisation, and Paul McCartney later sent him a signed copy of the album.

He died in Cullercoats on 28 December 2002, aged 83. The Liverpool Daily Post's obituary remembered him as the first Kop idol of the post-war era. His Liverpool record stands at 178 appearances and 84 goals, with one league title.

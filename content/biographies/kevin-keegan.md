Kevin Keegan scored 100 goals in 323 competitive games for Liverpool in six seasons. He arrived from Scunthorpe United in 1971 and left in 1977 as a European champion. In between he won three league titles, two UEFA Cups, the FA Cup and the European Cup. He scored twice in the 1974 FA Cup final, twice in the first leg of the 1973 UEFA Cup final and in both legs of the 1976 final.

## From Scunthorpe

Keegan was born in Armthorpe on 14 February 1951 and began his senior career with Scunthorpe. Liverpool signed him on 3 May 1971, when he was 20, for £33,000, according to LFChistory.

He had played in midfield at Scunthorpe. Bill Shankly is said to have seen him first as a replacement for Ian Callaghan on the right, but he was soon playing further forward alongside John Toshack.

It worked from the start. On his competitive debut, against Nottingham Forest at Anfield on 14 August 1971, Keegan scored after 12 minutes, set up by Toshack. Liverpool won 3–1.

## Mönchengladbach, 1973

The partnership mattered most in the 1973 UEFA Cup final against Borussia Mönchengladbach. A waterlogged pitch forced the first leg at Anfield to be abandoned. Shankly brought Toshack into the side for the rearranged match, and his height troubled the German defence. Keegan scored in the 21st and 32nd minutes, and Larry Lloyd added a third in a 3–0 win.

Liverpool lost the return 2–0, with both goals from Jupp Heynckes, but won 3–2 on aggregate. It was the club's first European trophy. Liverpool were also league champions that season, and Keegan scored 22 goals in all competitions: 13 in the league, five in the League Cup and four in the UEFA Cup.

## Wembley, 1974

Keegan played in all 61 of Liverpool's competitive matches in 1973–74. He scored six times in the FA Cup run: two against Doncaster Rovers, one against Ipswich Town, one against Leicester City and two in the final.

The final, against Newcastle United, was goalless at half-time. Keegan opened the scoring with a shot from the edge of the penalty area. Steve Heighway added the second. Two minutes from time Tommy Smith fed the ball across the box and Keegan finished it to make it 3–0.

## Bremner and the ban

Shankly announced his resignation in July 1974. The next season started badly for Keegan. In the Charity Shield at Wembley on 10 August 1974, he and Leeds United's Billy Bremner were sent off after clashing around the hour mark. Both were banned for 11 games and fined £500. The game finished 1–1, and Liverpool won on penalties.

## Champions again under Paisley

Keegan stayed at the centre of Paisley's attack. In 1975–76 Liverpool won the league and the UEFA Cup again, and Keegan was named the Football Writers' Association Footballer of the Year.

He decided the European final as well. In the first leg at Anfield, Club Brugge led 2–0 after 15 minutes. Liverpool then scored three times in six minutes of the second half. Ray Kennedy and Jimmy Case brought them level, and Keegan's penalty made it 3–2.

In Belgium, Brugge scored first again, from a penalty after 11 minutes. Keegan equalised four minutes later. The game ended 1–1, and Liverpool won 4–3 on aggregate. He had scored the winner in the home leg and the goal that protected the lead in the return.

## Rome, 1977

His final season at Liverpool was 1976–77. He scored 20 goals in all competitions: 12 in the league, four in the European Cup and four in the FA Cup. Liverpool kept the league title and reached the FA Cup and European Cup finals.

Manchester United beat them in the FA Cup final at Wembley on 21 May. Four days later, on 25 May, Keegan played his last competitive game for Liverpool, against Mönchengladbach in Rome in the European Cup final.

Phil Neal later told UEFA what Paisley said to Keegan before the game: "Berti Vogts is going to mark you as tight as you've ever been marked before, so I want you to run over every blade of grass."

Keegan did not score. With Liverpool leading 2–1 and eight minutes left, he ran at the defence from about 40 yards, and Vogts brought him down in the penalty area. Neal scored the penalty, and Liverpool won 3–1 to take their first European Cup.

## Hamburg, England and after

Keegan left Liverpool for Hamburg on 3 June 1977. He won the Ballon d'Or in 1978 and 1979, and the Bundesliga with Hamburg in 1978–79. He later played for Southampton and Newcastle United.

He won 63 caps for England between 1972 and 1982, scored 21 goals and captained his country.

As a manager he took charge of Newcastle, Fulham, England and Manchester City. He managed England from 1999 to 2000.

He died on 20 July 2026, aged 75, after a battle with cancer.

Steve McManaman made 364 competitive appearances for Liverpool and scored 66 goals, according to LFChistory. Liverpool won two major trophies while he was in the first team, and he had a direct hand in both. In the 1992 FA Cup final he set up the opening goal against Sunderland and was named man of the match. In the 1995 League Cup final he scored both goals against Bolton Wanderers. He never won the league with Liverpool, and in 1999 he left for Real Madrid on a free transfer.

## An Everton fan at Anfield

McManaman was born in Liverpool on 11 February 1972. He grew up supporting Everton, but joined Liverpool as a schoolboy, aged 14, according to LFChistory.

Kenny Dalglish gave him his debut on 15 December 1990. He came on for Peter Beardsley in a 2–0 win over Sheffield United at Anfield. He was 18.

## Breakthrough under Souness

Graeme Souness made him a regular the following season. McManaman played 51 games and scored 11 goals in 1991–92, establishing himself before his twentieth birthday. His first senior goal was a diving header at Manchester City on 21 August 1991, though Liverpool lost 2–1.

## Sunderland, 1992

He was injured in the FA Cup semi-final against Portsmouth and missed a month with a cartilage problem. He came back for the final against Sunderland on 9 May 1992. John Barnes was ruled out injured.

The first half finished goalless. Two minutes after the restart, McManaman worked the ball down the right and Michael Thomas scored. Ian Rush added the second after 67 minutes, and Liverpool won 2–0. McManaman, aged 20, was named man of the match.

## A quieter spell

The goals did not keep coming. In 1993–94 he scored twice, and both came on the same day, 22 August 1993, in a 5–0 win over Swindon Town. Souness left in January 1994, and Roy Evans took over as manager.

## Bolton, 1995

On 2 April 1995 Liverpool met Bolton in the League Cup final. McManaman scored both Liverpool goals. For the first, after 37 minutes, he ran at the Bolton defence and beat several players before scoring. For the second, after 68 minutes, he ran at Bolton down the left, went past two defenders and scored again. Alan Thompson replied for Bolton a minute later, but Liverpool won 2–1. McManaman won the Alan Hardaker Trophy as man of the match.

## England and Euro 96

He made his England debut against Nigeria on 16 November 1994. He went on to win 37 caps and score three goals. At Euro 96 England reached the semi-finals, and McManaman was named in the Team of the Tournament.

## The 1996 FA Cup final

He was one of Liverpool's main attacking players in Evans's side, alongside Robbie Fowler. In 1995–96 he scored 10 goals in 53 games. The club's own retrospective also credits him with 20 assists that season.

The season ended at Wembley on 11 May 1996. Liverpool lost the FA Cup final 1–0 to Manchester United. Eric Cantona scored after 85 minutes.

## Celtic Park and a best season

On 16 September 1997 Liverpool were 2–1 down to Celtic in the UEFA Cup with a minute left. McManaman picked up the ball 20 metres inside his own half, carried it forward and curled a left-foot shot from near the D that went in off the post. The game finished 2–2.

He scored 12 goals in 46 games in 1997–98. It was his best return in a season for Liverpool. His next best was the 11 of 1991–92.

## The Barcelona move falls through

In August 1997 McManaman came close to joining Barcelona for £12 million. With less than two years left on his contract, Liverpool accepted Barcelona's offer and allowed him to talk to them. The deal collapsed over personal terms. Barcelona's vice-president, Joan Gaspart, said McManaman had asked for too much money, the Irish Times reported. His adviser, Simon Fuller, disagreed. "We asked for what we considered a reasonable wage in terms of European football," he said, adding that a club prepared to pay a £12 million fee must also be prepared to pay comparable wages.

McManaman stayed at Liverpool.

## The last season

Gérard Houllier was appointed joint manager alongside Evans on 16 July 1998. Evans left on 12 November, and Houllier took sole charge. McManaman's contract was still unresolved.

In January 1999 he confirmed that he would join Real Madrid when his contract ran out that summer. Under the Bosman ruling, Liverpool would receive no fee. The move, he said, "helps me further my ambition to test myself in another top European League."

He played on until the end of the season. He made 31 appearances in 1998–99, 28 of them in the league, and scored five goals. It was his ninth season in the first team.

## Madrid and Manchester City

He spent four seasons in Spain, from 1999 to 2003. Real Madrid won the Champions League in 2000 and 2002, and La Liga in 2000–01 and 2002–03. In the 2000 final against Valencia at the Stade de France on 24 May, he scored the second goal in a 3–0 win, a volley after a clearance fell to him on the edge of the box.

He joined Manchester City on a free transfer on 30 August 2003. He made 44 appearances in all competitions over two seasons and was released in May 2005.

His Liverpool honours were the 1992 FA Cup and the 1995 League Cup.

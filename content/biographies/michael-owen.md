Michael Owen scored 158 goals in 297 competitive games for Liverpool, 118 of them in 216 league games, according to LFChistory. He shared the Premier League Golden Boot in each of his first two full seasons. In 2001 he scored twice in the last seven minutes to win the FA Cup final, and that December he won the Ballon d'Or. He is the only player to have won it while at Liverpool. He left for Real Madrid in August 2004, aged 24.

## The youth team

Owen was born in Chester on 14 December 1979. In 1996 he and Jamie Carragher were in the side that won Liverpool's first FA Youth Cup, beating West Ham United 4–1 on aggregate in the final.

Roy Evans gave him his debut at Wimbledon on 6 May 1997. Liverpool were 2–0 down when Owen came on for Patrik Berger in the second half. He scored, reducing the deficit in a match Liverpool lost 2–1. He was 17, and at the time the youngest goalscorer in the club's history. Ben Woodburn broke the record in 2016.

## Two Golden Boots

In 1997–98, his first full season, Owen scored 23 goals in 44 games. Eighteen came in the league, enough to share the Golden Boot with Dion Dublin and Chris Sutton. In April 1998 he was named PFA Young Player of the Year.

On 14 February 1998 he scored a hat-trick against Sheffield Wednesday. He was 18 years and 62 days old, the youngest player to score a Premier League hat-trick. The record still stands.

He made his England debut against Chile that month. On 30 June 1998 he scored against Argentina in the World Cup round of 16 in Saint-Étienne, a 2–2 draw that England lost on penalties.

In 1998–99 he again scored 23 goals, in 40 games, with 18 in the league. This time he shared the Golden Boot with Dwight Yorke and Jimmy Floyd Hasselbaink. The management changed around him that season: Evans and Gérard Houllier were joint managers in 1998, and Houllier took sole charge that November.

## Leeds, April 1999

On Monday 12 April 1999 Owen suffered a serious hamstring injury at Leeds. It was his third hamstring problem of the season. "The scan has revealed damage to the hamstring and the tendon," Houllier said. He faced a long recovery, and hamstring trouble would recur through his career.

In 1999–2000 he scored 12 goals in 30 games.

## The treble

Emile Heskey arrived from Leicester City in March 2000 and became Owen's partner in attack. In 2000–01 Owen scored 24 goals in 46 games.

On 15 February 2001 he scored both goals in a 2–0 UEFA Cup win over Roma at the Stadio Olimpico, in the 46th and 71st minutes. Roma won the return leg 1–0 at Anfield, and Liverpool went through.

The FA Cup final against Arsenal was played at the Millennium Stadium in Cardiff on 12 May 2001. Arsenal were not given a penalty when Stéphane Henchoz handled the ball in the first half, and Sami Hyypiä made clearances to keep Arsenal out. Freddie Ljungberg put Arsenal ahead in the 72nd minute.

In the 83rd minute Gary McAllister took a free-kick, Markus Babbel headed it down and Owen fired past David Seaman. Five minutes later Berger played a long ball over the top. Owen held off Lee Dixon and Tony Adams and shot across Seaman with his left foot. Liverpool won 2–1.

Owen was 21. "If I could relive one day from my career, the FA Cup final of 2001 would be it," he said two decades later.

Four days later, on 16 May, Liverpool met Alavés in the UEFA Cup final in Dortmund. Owen set up Steven Gerrard's goal, and Liverpool won 5–4 after extra time with a golden-goal own goal by Geli. On 19 May they won 4–0 at Charlton Athletic to qualify for the Champions League. The season had brought the League Cup, the FA Cup and the UEFA Cup.

## Ballon d'Or

In the 2001 UEFA Super Cup in Monaco, Liverpool beat Bayern Munich 3–2. Owen set up John Arne Riise's opener and scored the third. On 1 September he scored a hat-trick for England in a 5–1 World Cup qualifying win over Germany in Munich.

On 17 December 2001 he was named European Footballer of the Year, France Football's Ballon d'Or. He received 176 points, ahead of Raúl on 140 and Oliver Kahn on 114.

## Houllier's last seasons

Owen scored 28 goals in 2001–02 and 28 again in 2002–03, his highest totals for the club. In 2001–02 Liverpool finished second in the league. Houllier fell ill during a 1–1 home draw with Leeds on 13 October 2001 and needed heart surgery, and Phil Thompson took charge while he recovered.

That season Liverpool reached the Champions League quarter-finals. On 9 April 2002 they lost 4–2 at Bayer Leverkusen and went out 4–3 on aggregate. Owen missed a one-on-one chance in the first half and hit the post from another early in the second.

On 2 March 2003 Liverpool beat Manchester United 2–0 in the League Cup final in Cardiff. Gerrard scored in the 39th minute, Jerzy Dudek made important saves and Owen added the second in the 86th. It was his last major trophy with Liverpool. In April 2003 he scored his 100th Premier League goal, against West Bromwich Albion. At 23 years and 133 days he was the youngest player to reach the mark.

He scored 19 goals in 2003–04, 16 of them in the league. His last game was at home to Newcastle United on 15 May 2004. Shola Ameobi scored for Newcastle, and Owen equalised in the 67th minute from Gerrard's pass. The game finished 1–1, and Liverpool finished fourth.

## Real Madrid

Houllier left that summer and Rafael Benítez became manager. Owen had a year left on his contract. "I was very happy with Michael and I wanted him to stay. The problem was he only had a year left on his contract," Benítez said.

Real Madrid unveiled him on 14 August 2004. Liverpool received £8 million and Antonio Núñez, and Owen signed a four-year deal. He had been an unused substitute in the 2–0 first-leg win over Grazer AK in Champions League qualifying, so the Newcastle game remained his last for Liverpool.

## After Liverpool

After one season in Madrid he joined Newcastle on 30 August 2005 for a reported £17 million. A return to Liverpool had been discussed but did not happen, according to his own later accounts. He injured his knee in the first minute of England's World Cup game against Sweden on 20 June 2006 and did not start again until 30 April 2007.

He joined Manchester United on a free transfer in 2009 and then moved to Stoke City. On 19 March 2013 he announced he would retire at the end of the season. In a Premier League interview he later said injuries had left him thinking about his game instead of playing on instinct, and that he was relieved to retire.

The RSSSF record of his international career lists 89 caps and 40 goals for England.

On 21 April 2016 Liverpool appointed him an international ambassador. Some supporters criticised the decision, pointing to his time at Manchester United. He is now a broadcaster and racehorse owner.

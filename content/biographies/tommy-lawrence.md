Tommy Lawrence played 390 games for Liverpool. He was in goal when Bill Shankly's side won the league in 1964 and 1966, and when the club won the FA Cup for the first time in 1965. He waited five years for his debut. Once he had the place, he missed only four league matches in six seasons. Supporters called him the Flying Pig.

## A long wait

Lawrence was born in Dailly, Ayrshire, on 14 May 1940. LFChistory records that he had a trial with Stockton Heath Albion as an outfield player and did not impress. He joined Liverpool as an amateur in April 1957 and signed professional forms that October. Liverpool were in the Second Division at the time, and Shankly had not yet arrived.

The club won promotion in 1962 without Lawrence making a senior appearance. His chance came in the first season back in the First Division, when the first-choice goalkeeper, Jim Furnell, was injured. Lawrence made his debut at The Hawthorns on 27 October 1962. West Bromwich Albion won 1–0 through a Derek Kevan goal, and Jimmy Melia had a penalty saved. Lawrence kept his place.

## The Flying Pig

The nickname was affectionate. It referred to his heavy build and to his spectacular saves. Roy Evans, who joined Liverpool in 1964, put the emphasis elsewhere. He said later that "his positional sense was great".

Shankly also asked Lawrence to play farther from his goal. When Liverpool pushed up, he moved to the edge of his penalty area, ready to intercept a ball played behind the defence.

Lawrence admitted the idea scared him. "At first I was frightened to death," he said. Supporters wanted him back on his line. The players practised it at Melwood before trying it at Anfield. Evans remembered a player who worked out for himself that he had to stay alert whenever the team pushed up the pitch. The club and later writers described him as one of the first sweeper-keepers.

## Champions in 1964

The 1963–64 season was Lawrence's first full one as Liverpool's established goalkeeper. It started badly, with early home defeats. Lawrence later picked out Easter as the decisive spell: wins over Tottenham either side of a win at Leicester. He also remembered how well balanced the team was.

The title was secured at Anfield on 18 April 1964, against Arsenal. Furnell, who had been sold to Arsenal, was in their goal. Liverpool were 1–0 up when George Eastham took a penalty, and Lawrence saved it. Liverpool went on to win 5–0. Frank McGhee's report in the Daily Mirror recorded the Kop singing about the save.

## Europe and Wembley

Lawrence was in goal for Liverpool's first competitive European match, against KR Reykjavík in Iceland on 17 August 1964. Liverpool won 5–0. That campaign took them to the semi-finals of the European Cup.

He played in every round of the 1965 FA Cup. Liverpool needed two games to get past Stockport County in the fourth round, and met Leicester City in the quarter-final. Leicester had knocked them out in the 1963 semi-final. This time the first game ended 0–0 at Filbert Street, and Roger Hunt scored the winner in the replay at Anfield.

The final against Leeds United was played at Wembley on 1 May 1965. It was goalless after 90 minutes. Hunt put Liverpool ahead in extra time, Billy Bremner equalised and Ian St John scored the winner. Liverpool won 2–1 and lifted the FA Cup for the first time.

## Inter Milan

Three days later Liverpool played Internazionale at Anfield in the first leg of the European Cup semi-final and won 3–1. They lost the second leg 3–0 in Milan. Inter's first goal came from a disputed free-kick. For the second, Joaquín Peiró took the ball from Lawrence as he bounced it and scored into the empty net. Liverpool protested, but the goal stood. They went out 4–3 on aggregate.

## A second title

Liverpool won the championship again in 1965–66. Lawrence was ever-present in the league that season, starting all 42 matches, and Liverpool conceded 34 goals. Across the six league seasons from 1963–64 to 1968–69 he missed four matches.

That season also brought a European final. On 5 May 1966 Liverpool met Borussia Dortmund in the Cup Winners' Cup final at Hampden Park. Siegfried Held put Dortmund ahead and Hunt equalised. Stan Libuda scored in extra time, and Liverpool lost 2–1.

## Clemence arrives

Ray Clemence joined Liverpool in 1967. Shankly had suggested he would get into the first team quickly, but Clemence later said that Lawrence was at the peak of his career. Clemence waited more than two years for a regular place.

Lawrence kept the shirt. In 1968–69 he was ever-present and Liverpool conceded 24 league goals, a club record at the time.

## Watford

The change came in 1969–70. Clemence had already made his league debut when Liverpool went to Watford, then a Second Division side, for an FA Cup quarter-final on 21 February 1970. Lawrence was in goal. Barry Endean scored in the second half and Watford won 1–0.

The defeat sped up Shankly's rebuilding of the team. Clemence played in the next league match, against Derby County, and became the regular goalkeeper.

Lawrence played once more for Liverpool. On 26 April 1971 Shankly picked a much-changed side for a game at Manchester City, between a league match at Nottingham Forest and the second leg of a Fairs Cup semi-final against Leeds. Lawrence, Ron Yeats and Evans all played in the 2–2 draw at Maine Road. The Football League fined Liverpool over the selection. It was Lawrence's 390th and last game for the club.

## After Anfield

He joined Tranmere Rovers in November 1971, aged 31, and made 80 league appearances for them before moving on to Chorley. He won three caps for Scotland. After football he went back to factory work in Warrington, as a quality controller.

Evans remembered Lawrence and the other senior players helping him when he was a young player. Later, at former players' get-togethers, he found Lawrence humble and happy-go-lucky.

Lawrence died in January 2018, aged 77. Liverpool held a minute's applause in his memory at Anfield before the home match against Manchester City.

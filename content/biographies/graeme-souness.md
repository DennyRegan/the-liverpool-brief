Graeme Souness played the pass that released Kenny Dalglish to win the 1978 European Cup final. Six years later he scored in the penalty shoot-out in another victorious final and lifted the trophy as Liverpool captain. He joined from Middlesbrough in January 1978 and left for Sampdoria in 1984 with 55 goals in 359 appearances in all competitions, five league titles, three European Cups and four League Cups.

## The route through Middlesbrough

Born in Edinburgh on 6 May 1953, Souness began at Tottenham Hotspur, where his only first-team appearance was as a substitute in a UEFA Cup tie against Keflavík in September 1971. After a spell with Montreal Olympique he moved to Middlesbrough. The Scottish Football Museum dates the £30,000 move to January 1973, and his debut came on 6 January.

At Middlesbrough he helped win the Second Division championship in 1973–74 under Jack Charlton, then played in the First Division. Paisley signed him for £352,000 on 10 January 1978, when Souness was 24.

His first Liverpool goal came against Manchester United in February, a volley in a 3–1 win at Anfield. Liverpool finished second to Nottingham Forest that season, but the European Cup was still to play for.

## Wembley and the title years

Liverpool had won the European Cup for the first time in Rome the year before. At Wembley on 10 May 1978 Club Brugge held out until the 64th minute, when Souness found Dalglish with the pass that left him one-on-one with the goalkeeper. Dalglish scored the only goal, and Liverpool kept the trophy.

Championships followed in 1978–79 and 1979–80. LFChistory records that Souness played 41 league games in each season, missing one in each.

He scored three times against CSKA Sofia at Anfield on 4 March 1981, in a 5–1 European Cup quarter-final first-leg win. The goals came in the 16th, 51st and 80th minutes, and the third was Liverpool's fifth. Liverpool won the second leg 1–0 for a 6–1 aggregate, and went on to beat Real Madrid in the final, Souness's second European Cup.

## Taking the captaincy

Liverpool lost 3–1 at home to Manchester City on Boxing Day 1981. Paisley then made Souness captain in place of Phil Thompson; his first match in the role was at Swansea on 2 January 1982, according to LFChistory. Liverpool's own account dates the appointment to January 1982.

Liverpool finished the season as champions and League Cup winners, then retained both trophies in Paisley's final season. At Wembley in March 1983, after a 2–1 extra-time win over Manchester United in the League Cup final, Souness gave up the captain's right to lift the trophy so that Paisley, who was retiring, could collect it.

## A final season and a departure

Under Joe Fagan in 1983–84, Souness captained Liverpool to the league title, League Cup and European Cup. His goal settled the League Cup final replay against Everton at Maine Road, after a goalless match at Wembley. He was also a hard player: LFChistory records that in the European Cup semi-final first leg against Dinamo Bucharest he broke an opponent's jaw.

His final Liverpool appearance came against Roma in their own stadium on 30 May 1984. The final finished 1–1 after extra time, and Souness scored in the shoot-out that Liverpool won 4–2. He left for Sampdoria that summer.

He later became Rangers player-manager and managed Liverpool from 1991 to 1994, winning the FA Cup in 1992. As a player he left Anfield as captain of a side that had just won the league, the League Cup and the European Cup in the same season.

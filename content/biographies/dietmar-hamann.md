Dietmar Hamann made 283 appearances for Liverpool between 1999 and 2006 and scored 11 goals. He started all three finals of the 2001 cup treble and came on at half-time in Istanbul with Liverpool 3–0 down to Milan. His last game for the club was the 2006 FA Cup final, in which he scored Liverpool's first penalty in the shoot-out. He sat in front of the defence and let others go forward. "He was my safety net," Steven Gerrard said at the launch of Hamann's book in February 2012.

## Bayern and Newcastle

Hamann was born in Waldsassen, Bavaria, on 27 August 1973. He joined Bayern Munich from Wacker Munich in 1989, at 16. At Bayern he won two Bundesliga titles, the German Cup and the UEFA Cup, and made 106 Bundesliga appearances.

Newcastle United signed him after the 1998 World Cup. He stayed one season and played in the 1999 FA Cup final, which Newcastle lost to Manchester United.

## A bad start

Gérard Houllier signed him for Liverpool on 22 July 1999 for £8 million. His debut, at Sheffield Wednesday on 7 August, lasted 24 minutes. He ruptured ankle ligaments and was replaced by David Thompson. Liverpool won 2–1.

He recovered to make 30 appearances that season, 28 of them in the league, and settled into the role he kept for the rest of his time at Anfield: the midfielder who stayed back, protected the defence and passed simply.

## The treble

In 2000–01 he made 53 appearances and started the League Cup, FA Cup and UEFA Cup finals.

The first, against Birmingham City on 25 February 2001, finished 1–1 after extra time. Hamann's kick in the shoot-out was saved by Ian Bennett, but Liverpool won 5–4 on penalties. In the FA Cup final against Arsenal he was replaced by Gary McAllister after an hour.

The UEFA Cup final against Alavés was played in Dortmund on 16 May. Liverpool won 5–4, settled by a golden own goal in extra time. Hamann later told UEFA that "to win it in my home country was unbelievable". He also remembered the end: "I remember all of us lined up together with the fans singing You'll Never Walk Alone."

## More than a holding player

He could do more than defend. When Liverpool drew 0–0 with Galatasaray at Anfield on 20 February 2002, UEFA's report called him "his side's main creative force". Faryd Mondragón tipped one of his curling shots on to the post.

In the 2003 League Cup final against Manchester United on 2 March, Gerrard's shot deflected off David Beckham to give Liverpool the lead. In the 86th minute Hamann put Michael Owen through to make it 2–0. It was his second League Cup winner's medal.

For Germany he won 59 caps and scored five goals between 1997 and 2005. On 7 October 2000 his free-kick beat England 1–0 in the last match at the old Wembley. He played in the 2002 World Cup final, which Germany lost 2–0 to Brazil.

Injuries interrupted him. On 30 July 2003 a shin problem returned and he needed further surgery, which kept him out until November. He still made 35 appearances in 2003–04. Houllier left at the end of that season.

## Benítez and Istanbul

Under Rafael Benítez he made 43 appearances in 2004–05. He did not play in the first leg of the Champions League semi-final against Chelsea but started the second, a 1–0 win at Anfield on 3 May 2005, and went off after 72 minutes.

The day before the final he described his job to UEFA: "My work is breaking up [our opponents'] attacks and then starting our own."

Against Milan in Istanbul on 25 May he was on the bench. "I was disappointed first of all not to start the game because I expected to start," he told the club later. Milan led 3–0 at half-time. Benítez brought Hamann on for Steve Finnan.

UEFA's technical report described what changed. Liverpool switched to a back three, and Gerrard moved up behind the forwards. Kaká's influence was reduced, and Gerrard's runs began to trouble Andrea Pirlo. Gerrard, Vladimír Šmicer and Xabi Alonso scored in the 54th, 56th and 60th minutes.

Hamann said his arrival let Gerrard play further up the field, but he also pointed to Milan's dip and his team-mates. "AC Milan took their foot off the gas," he said. "We had the belief and the team spirit to punish them for that."

The match went to penalties. Serginho missed Milan's first kick, and Hamann scored Liverpool's first. He had broken a bone in his foot, reported at the time as a broken toe, and did not realise until after the match. In June 2005 he agreed a new contract.

## Cardiff

Mohamed Sissoko arrived in 2005, and with Gerrard, Alonso and Sissoko ahead of him Hamann slipped down Benítez's pecking order. His league appearances fell from 30 to 17. He made 32 in all competitions.

The FA Cup final against West Ham on 13 May 2006 was his last game for the club. He came on for Peter Crouch midway through the second half, with Liverpool behind. The game finished 3–3 after extra time. Hamann took Liverpool's first penalty again and scored, and Pepe Reina's saves helped win the shoot-out 3–1. It was his second FA Cup winner's medal.

## Bolton and City

He left Liverpool on 11 July 2006, aged 32. He had agreed to join Bolton Wanderers but changed his mind. "I had agreed to join Bolton, but I quickly realised that going to them would not be the right move for me," he said. Bolton allowed him to talk to other clubs, and the next day he signed a two-year contract with Manchester City without playing a game for Bolton. City paid Bolton £400,000 in compensation.

Years later Bolton's chairman, Phil Gartside, claimed the club had never signed him. A Premier League investigation in 2013 found that Bolton had registered him.

Hamann spent three seasons at City and was released on 1 July 2009. He was player-coach at MK Dons in 2010–11 and managed Stockport County from July to November 2011.

His Liverpool honours were the Champions League in 2005, the UEFA Cup in 2001, the FA Cup in 2001 and 2006, the League Cup in 2001 and 2003, and the UEFA Super Cup in 2001 and 2005.

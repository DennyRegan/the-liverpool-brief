Adam Lallana made 178 appearances for Liverpool between 2014 and 2020 and scored 22 goals. He won the Premier League, the Champions League, the UEFA Super Cup and the Club World Cup. His best years came in Jürgen Klopp's first two seasons, when his pressing and his movement were a large part of how the new manager wanted Liverpool to play. By the time the trophies arrived, injuries had made him a squad player.

## Before Liverpool

Lallana was born in St Albans on 10 May 1988. He started at Bournemouth and joined Southampton's academy at the age of 12. Apart from a one-month loan back at Bournemouth in 2007, he stayed at Southampton until 2014. He was part of the side that won two promotions in a row, in 2010–11 and 2011–12, taking the club from League One to the Premier League, and he became captain in 2012.

In 2013–14 he was named in the PFA Premier League Team of the Year. He went to the World Cup with England that summer and played in all three group games. Liverpool signed him on 1 July 2014 for a reported £25m.

## The first season

Under Brendan Rodgers, Lallana played 41 times in 2014–15 and scored six goals. He had close control, could use either foot and was comfortable taking the ball with opponents close to him.

Klopp was appointed in October 2015. His Liverpool wanted to win the ball back high up the pitch and attack quickly once they had it. Lallana's work without the ball helped them do the first, and his technique helped with the second.

## Carrow Road

One of the first memorable nights of the Klopp era belonged to him. At Carrow Road on 23 January 2016, Roberto Firmino gave Liverpool an early lead. Norwich City scored three times to lead 3–1. Jordan Henderson and Firmino brought it level, James Milner put Liverpool 4–3 ahead, and Sébastien Bassong equalised in the second minute of stoppage time.

Lallana, who had come on as a second-half substitute, then scored the winner, also in stoppage time. Liverpool won 5–4. In the celebrations, Klopp's glasses were broken.

## Setting the press

Joe Gomez described Lallana's part in the press in an interview with the club's website in early January 2018. "He senses it; like a shark when they see blood, they attack," Gomez said. Lallana noticed when an opponent was about to lose control of the ball: "He sees when there's an iffy pass or the weight of pass is off or someone has a dodgy touch, he is going to punish you quickly."

He did not always win the ball himself, but Gomez said that "even if he doesn't win the ball, he is creating the possibility for someone else". He added: "That's what he does best and he starts it for us."

Gomez also praised his skill on the ball. "He can shift it and go whichever way he wants, dribble with his left or right."

That kind of work does not always show up in tackles or goals. Lallana would often start a move that someone else finished.

## 2016–17

His best season in front of goal came in 2016–17. He played 35 times and scored eight goals, all of them in the league, from 31 league games. The club also credits him with seven league assists that season.

He was playing as one of three central midfielders in Klopp's 4-3-3, but he was not there only to supply the forwards. In early September 2016 the Premier League's "The Scout" feature pointed out that he had already had eight attempts from inside the box, more than any other midfielder in the division. He was running beyond the ball from deep, which gave defenders another player to pick up.

In a piece on the club's website at the end of that season, Luis García said "This year we have seen his best." García said Lallana could "play a brilliant pass", "has got the ability to dribble past players" and could score a brilliant goal from nowhere.

The season ended at Anfield against Middlesbrough on 21 May 2017. A win would guarantee fourth place, and with Arsenal beating Everton the same day, a draw would not have been enough. Georginio Wijnaldum scored just before half-time and Philippe Coutinho added a free-kick six minutes after the break. In the 56th minute Wijnaldum headed the ball down and Lallana scored the third. Liverpool won 3–0 and finished fourth, which put them into the Champions League play-off round. They beat Hoffenheim to reach the group stage.

## Injuries

That summer his progress stopped. On 2 August 2017 he hurt his thigh in the Audi Cup final against Atlético Madrid in Munich. Klopp said the damage meant he was likely to be out "for a couple of months not weeks". He came back as a late substitute against Chelsea in November, then broke down again.

He never won back a regular place. According to LFChistory, he played 15 times in 2017–18 and 16 times in 2018–19, and did not score in either season.

He still played a part on the biggest nights. In the 2018 Champions League final in Kyiv he came on in the 31st minute for the injured Mohamed Salah. Real Madrid won 3–1. A year later, when Liverpool beat Tottenham 2–0 in Madrid on 1 June 2019 to win the trophy, he was an unused substitute.

## Old Trafford and the title

He had one more important goal to give. At Old Trafford on 20 October 2019, Liverpool were 1–0 down to a Marcus Rashford goal. Lallana came off the bench and, with about five minutes left, guided Andy Robertson's cross in at the far post. It was his first league goal since the Middlesbrough game in May 2017.

The 1–1 draw ended Liverpool's run of 17 straight Premier League wins, but they stayed unbeaten in the league that season. That campaign he made 15 league appearances, 22 in all competitions, and scored once. Liverpool won the title.

He left Liverpool on 27 July 2020. His final record was 178 appearances and 22 goals. In the league he made 128 appearances and scored 18.

## After Liverpool

Lallana joined Brighton on a free transfer the same day. He spent four seasons there and played 104 games, scoring four goals. On 14 June 2024 he went back to Southampton on a one-year deal, and he announced his retirement from playing on 25 June 2025.

He stayed at Southampton as a first-team coach under Will Still. When Still was sacked in November 2025, the Under-21 coach Tonda Eckert moved up to run the first team, and Lallana took charge of the Under-21s on an interim basis. On 30 January 2026 Southampton made the appointment permanent. He was still in the job when the club released its Premier League 2 fixtures in August 2026.

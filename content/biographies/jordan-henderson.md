Jordan Henderson played 492 games for Liverpool over 12 seasons and was captain for eight of them. He lifted the European Cup in 2019 and the league title in 2020, the club's first for 30 years. In the summer of 2012 his Liverpool career nearly ended after one season. Liverpool were willing to let him go to Fulham, and he refused.

## From Sunderland

Henderson was born in Sunderland on 17 June 1990. He came through at his home-town club, went on loan to Coventry City in 2009 and then established himself in Sunderland's first team. Kenny Dalglish signed him for Liverpool on 9 June 2011, a week before his 21st birthday.

He played 48 games in his first season, often on the right of midfield. He started the League Cup final against Cardiff City, which Liverpool won on penalties, and the FA Cup final, which they lost 2–1 to Chelsea. But he had not yet found his best position.

## Staying

Brendan Rodgers replaced Dalglish that summer. In August 2012 Rodgers proposed including Henderson in a deal for Fulham's Clint Dempsey. Henderson said no. Years later he said he had believed he still had plenty to give and wanted to win a place in Rodgers' team.

By 2013–14 he had done it. Playing in central midfield, he did much of the running for a side built around the goals of Luis Suárez and Daniel Sturridge. He started 35 of the 38 league games. He missed the other three because of a red card for a high challenge on Samir Nasri at the end of the 3–2 win over Manchester City on 13 April. The ban kept him out of the games against Norwich City, Chelsea and Crystal Palace, and Liverpool finished second. He accepted afterwards that he could not have expected any other decision.

## Captain

Henderson succeeded Steven Gerrard as captain on 10 July 2015. He was injured when Jürgen Klopp arrived that October. A knee injury in April 2016 then left him an unused substitute in the Europa League final in May.

Klopp moved him to the base of midfield, in front of the defence. Liverpool reached the Champions League final in 2018 with Henderson as captain and lost 3–1 to Real Madrid in Kyiv. The following season Fabinho's arrival gave Klopp another option. In April 2019 Henderson explained that he had talked to Klopp about playing further forward, a role that felt more natural to him with England. Fabinho could sit deeper, and Henderson could get forward more. On 5 April he came off the bench and scored late in a 3–1 win at Southampton.

A month later the new role mattered even more. Liverpool were 3–0 down to Barcelona after the first leg of the semi-final. Seven minutes into the second leg at Anfield, Henderson ran into the penalty area and forced a save from Marc-André ter Stegen, and Divock Origi scored the rebound. Liverpool won 4–0.

On 1 June 2019 they beat Tottenham 2–0 in Madrid, and Henderson lifted the European Cup, almost four years after he became captain. The Super Cup and the Club World Cup followed before the end of the year.

## Champions

In 2019–20 Liverpool won 26 of their first 27 league games and finished with 99 points. It was their first league title since 1990. The Football Writers' Association named Henderson its Footballer of the Year. During the pandemic he had also helped bring together the Premier League's captains to set up Players Together, which raised money for the NHS.

He was also versatile. In January 2021, with Liverpool's centre-backs injured, he played in central defence, and Klopp said he could do it because he understood the game so well. When Henderson left, Klopp singled out his technique and how he had developed tactically, and his ability to play in different positions.

He scored 33 goals for Liverpool, 29 of them in the league. Goals were never the main part of his game. Liverpool relied on his energy and his passing.

## Leaving

Liverpool won the League Cup and the FA Cup in 2022, and the Community Shield that summer. Henderson played 43 games in his final season, which took him to 492, including 360 in the Premier League. In total he won seven major trophies with Liverpool, plus the Community Shield.

In July 2023 he joined Al-Ettifaq in Saudi Arabia, who were managed by Gerrard. The move drew criticism. Henderson had spoken publicly in support of LGBT+ inclusion and the Rainbow Laces campaign, and Kop Outs, Liverpool's LGBT+ supporters' group, condemned the move and asked him to keep to those commitments.

He joined Ajax in January 2024, Brentford in July 2025 and Chelsea in August 2026. He had arrived at Liverpool as a young midfielder looking for a role, and left as the captain who lifted the European Cup and the league title.

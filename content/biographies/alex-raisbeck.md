Alex Raisbeck played 341 matches for Liverpool and scored 19 goals. He captained the club to their first league title in 1901 and their second in 1906. In between, Liverpool were relegated. Raisbeck stayed, and led them to the Second Division title at the first attempt and the First Division title the season after. He was a centre-half, a role that in his day was, in the club's words, closer to a modern central midfielder.

## From the pit to Larkhall

Raisbeck came from Wallacestone, near Polmont in Scotland. In a series of autobiographical newspaper articles published in 1915, he said he started work in a coal mine at twelve and learned his football at Larkhall Thistle. He began on the right wing before moving into the half-back line. He also wrote that every player's first duty was to make sure he had a trade to fall back on.

From Larkhall he went to Hibernian, and from there on a short loan to Stoke. Liverpool signed him from Hibernian for £350 in April 1898. The manager, Tom Watson, was getting a player with experience of both Scottish and English football. Raisbeck later recalled the welcome he had from his Scottish team-mates, among them John Walker, who became his particular friend.

## A first season of near misses

His debut came at Anfield on 3 September 1898, a 4–0 win over The Wednesday. Harry Storer was captain that day, and Raisbeck played centre-half. His first goal came in the FA Cup on 11 February 1899, in a 3–1 home win over Newcastle United.

That cup run ended in a long semi-final against Sheffield United. Liverpool drew twice, had a third meeting abandoned at an overcrowded Fallowfield, then lost 1–0 at Derby. The league also came down to one game. Level on points with Aston Villa but behind on goal average, Liverpool needed to win at Villa Park on the last day. They lost 5–0.

## Captain and champions

Raisbeck became captain for 1900–01 and held the job for nine seasons, according to LFChistory's record of the club's captains. He did not describe it as something he wanted. Writing in 1915, he said he had asked time and again to be relieved of the captaincy, because he disliked some of the duties.

The 1900–01 title was won with a late run. A 1–0 win at Sunderland started a 12-match unbeaten sequence in the league. Liverpool went to West Bromwich Albion for their final fixture needing a point to finish above Sunderland. On 29 April 1901 Walker scored the only goal at The Hawthorns, and Liverpool were champions of England for the first time, nine years after the club was founded. Raisbeck made 31 league appearances that season.

When the team came home, large crowds were waiting for them in Liverpool. Raisbeck recalled that he tried to keep out of the way so that he would not have to make a speech.

## How he played

A match programme portrait from September 1904 described a tireless player who broke up attacks, dropped back to help his full-backs and headed the ball again and again. It rated his defending above his ability to start attacks.

In 1924 Victor Hall of the Liverpool Echo looked back on Raisbeck's career. He singled out his speed, the way he could recover and tackle again after a forward had turned him, and the accuracy of his passing. Hall also remembered how seriously he took training and preparation.

In his memoir, Raisbeck contrasted club football, where much of a player's work was carried by an understanding with his team-mates, with international matches, where more was left to the individual. He won eight caps for Scotland between 1900 and 1907, starting with a 4–1 win over England on 7 April 1900.

## Relegation, and staying

The standard did not last. Liverpool finished 11th in 1901–02 and fifth in 1902–03, and were relegated in 1904. Raisbeck wrote in 1915 that he had thought about leaving during 1903–04. When Liverpool went down he changed his mind, because he could not leave the club in its difficulty.

In 1904–05 Raisbeck played 33 of Liverpool's 34 league matches. Ned Doig, brought in by Watson, was ever-present in goal, and Liverpool conceded only 25 league goals. A 4–0 home win over Manchester United in the penultimate game helped settle promotion. Liverpool finished two points above Bolton Wanderers as Second Division champions.

## Champions again

The return to the First Division started badly. Liverpool lost their first three league games of 1905–06, conceding 11 goals, and five of their first eight. Sam Hardy then replaced Doig in goal and results improved. Raisbeck played 36 of the 38 league matches.

Liverpool were top by the middle of December and stayed ahead of Preston North End to win the title. Everton ended their FA Cup run, winning the semi-final 2–0. Raisbeck had now captained a relegated side to the Second Division title and then straight to the First Division title.

## The last game

Injuries cut into his final seasons. He made 27 league appearances in 1906–07, 23 in 1907–08 and 15 in 1908–09, when a long absence kept him out for much of the campaign. By then Liverpool were fighting relegation again.

His last match for the club was at St James' Park on 30 April 1909, against the champions, Newcastle United. Jimmy Harrop played centre-half and Raisbeck was at left-back. A contemporary report described a close game in which Hardy made important saves before Ronald Orr scored the only goal. The 1–0 win left Liverpool two points clear of the relegation places. Manchester City and Leicester Fosse went down.

## After Anfield

Raisbeck returned to Scotland to play for Partick Thistle in 1909. He went on to manage Hamilton Academical, Bristol City, Halifax Town, Chester and Bath City, and later came back to Liverpool as a scout.

Watson died in 1915, while Raisbeck's memoir was running. Raisbeck paid tribute to him in it, writing about the manager's care for his players and the friendships he kept, and recorded that he had been one of the pall-bearers at the funeral.

Raisbeck died in Liverpool in 1949. He left the club with 341 appearances, 19 goals and two league titles as captain.

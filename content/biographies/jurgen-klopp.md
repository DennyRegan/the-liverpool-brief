In 2018–19 Liverpool took 97 points and finished second. It was the third-highest total in Premier League history at the time, and it was not enough. Jürgen Klopp's team got better. The next season they won the league with seven games to spare, Liverpool's first title since 1990.

That title and the European Cup won in Madrid in 2019 are the centre of Klopp's almost nine years at Anfield. In all he won seven major trophies: the league, the Champions League, the Super Cup, the Club World Cup, the FA Cup and two League Cups. He also won the Community Shield. He arrived in October 2015 and left in May 2024.

## Mainz and Dortmund

Klopp was born in Stuttgart in 1967. He spent 11 years as a player at Mainz and became their manager in 2001. He took them into the Bundesliga for the first time. They went down again, but his reputation was made, and in 2008 he moved to Borussia Dortmund.

Dortmund won the Bundesliga in 2011 and 2012, the second time as part of a league and German Cup double. In 2013 they reached the Champions League final and lost to Bayern Munich. Their pressing and fast attacking football was what Liverpool were buying when they appointed him on 8 October 2015.

## A first season of finals

He replaced Brendan Rodgers with Liverpool tenth in the Premier League. They finished eighth. They reached two finals and lost both: the League Cup final to Manchester City on penalties after a 1–1 draw, and the Europa League final 3–1 to Sevilla in Basel.

The run to Basel included one of Anfield's great European nights. In the quarter-final against Dortmund, his old club, in April 2016, Liverpool were 2–0 down, then 3–1 down. Philippe Coutinho and Mamadou Sakho brought them level, and Dejan Lovren headed the winner in stoppage time. Liverpool won 4–3 on the night and 5–4 on aggregate.

The first season also showed how far there was to go. In Basel, Liverpool led at half-time and fell apart after Sevilla equalised. Klopp said afterwards that they all had to learn to react better, and that this included him. Losing the final meant there would be no European football in 2016–17, and he pointed out that this left more time to train.

Sadio Mané, Joël Matip and Georginio Wijnaldum arrived that summer. Liverpool finished fourth and returned to the Champions League, though they had been second at the turn of the year and were still inconsistent.

## Building the team

Mohamed Salah arrived in 2017. With Mané and Roberto Firmino he formed the front three most associated with Klopp's Liverpool. Salah and Mané attacked from wide, Firmino dropped deep to link the play, and all three pressed defenders before they could settle.

Behind them were Andy Robertson, signed the same summer, and Trent Alexander-Arnold, who came through the academy. The two full-backs pushed on and supplied the forwards, so the width did not have to come from the front three. Over time the team found more ways to score. It could still press and counter, but it could also pin teams back, switch play with a long pass and create chances from crosses.

The defence changed most. Virgil van Dijk joined in January 2018. Alisson and Fabinho followed that summer. In 2018–19 Liverpool conceded 22 league goals.

Liverpool reached the Champions League final in 2018 and lost 3–1 to Real Madrid in Kyiv. Salah went off injured in the first half, Mané equalised after Karim Benzema's opener, and Gareth Bale came off the bench to score twice. The next season they lost one league game all year, at Manchester City, and finished with 97 points. City won their last 14 games and took the title with 98.

## Europe, then England

Barcelona won the first leg of the 2019 semi-final 3–0. At Anfield on 7 May, Liverpool won 4–0. Divock Origi scored first. Wijnaldum came on at half-time for the injured Robertson and scored twice in two minutes. Then Alexander-Arnold took a corner quickly before Barcelona had organised, and Origi scored the fourth. Alisson had kept them in the tie with saves in the first half.

In the final against Tottenham in Madrid on 1 June, Salah scored a penalty in the second minute and Origi added the second late on. In between, Liverpool had to defend, and Alisson made the saves. It was Liverpool's sixth European Cup and Klopp's first trophy at the club, after losing his first three finals.

The Super Cup followed in August, against Chelsea on penalties in Istanbul, and the Club World Cup in December, with a 1–0 extra-time win over Flamengo in Doha. Firmino scored the goal.

In the league, Liverpool won 26 of their first 27 games and drew the other. Then the pandemic stopped the season. When it resumed behind closed doors, the title was confirmed on 25 June 2020, when Chelsea beat City 2–1. No team had won the English top flight with so many games left to play. Liverpool finished with 99 points, a club record, 18 ahead of City. Supporters had waited 30 years and could not be in the ground to see it.

## Injuries, then another chase

The title defence in 2020–21 fell apart when Van Dijk, Joe Gomez and Matip all suffered long injuries. Liverpool recovered late, going unbeaten in their last ten league games, and beat Crystal Palace 2–0 on the final day to finish third. Mané scored both goals in front of 10,000 supporters at Anfield.

In 2021–22 they played 63 matches and reached the final of every cup they entered. They beat Chelsea in both domestic finals at Wembley, both after 0–0 draws: the League Cup 11–10 on penalties and the FA Cup 6–5, with Kostas Tsimikas scoring the winning kick. They beat Wolves 3–1 on the last day of the league season but finished a point behind City. In the Champions League final in Paris, Real Madrid won 1–0 with a goal from Vinícius Júnior, and Thibaut Courtois kept Liverpool out. Outside the stadium, supporters were caught in dangerous crushes, and police used tear gas. In February 2023, after an independent review, UEFA apologised to them and for the messages that had wrongly blamed Liverpool fans for the delayed kick-off.

## Rebuilding

Liverpool won the Community Shield in July 2022, beating City 3–1 at Leicester. The league season was poor. They finished fifth, the only time in Klopp's full seasons that Liverpool failed to qualify for the Champions League.

The team was changing. Diogo Jota, Luis Díaz and Darwin Núñez had arrived up front, and Firmino left in 2023. That summer Jordan Henderson and Fabinho went too, and Alexis Mac Allister, Dominik Szoboszlai, Wataru Endo and Ryan Gravenberch came into midfield.

On 26 January 2024 Klopp announced that he would leave at the end of the season. He had told the club in November. He said he was running out of energy.

There was one more trophy. On 25 February, Liverpool beat Chelsea 1–0 after extra time in the League Cup final. Van Dijk headed the winner in the 118th minute. Caoimhín Kelleher kept them in the game, and by the end Klopp had academy players Bobby Clark, James McConnell, Jayden Danns and Jarell Quansah on the pitch.

Liverpool finished third. His last game was a 2–0 win over Wolves at Anfield on 19 May 2024, with goals from Mac Allister and Quansah. It was his 491st match in charge.

## After Liverpool

Liverpool City Council had given him the Freedom of the City in 2022, recognising his charity and community work as well as the trophies. The UK later made him an honorary CBE for services to football and the community in Liverpool.

In January 2025 he became Red Bull's head of global soccer. On 24 July 2026 the German FA appointed him head coach of Germany on a contract to 2030, and he started on 15 August, with his old assistant Peter Krawietz alongside him.

At Liverpool he had taken a team that was tenth in the league and made it European champions within four years and English champions within five. When 97 points were not enough, the team improved again.

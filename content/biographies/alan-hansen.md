Alan Hansen played 620 games for Liverpool between 1977 and 1990 and scored 14 goals. He was a centre-half who carried the ball out of defence and into midfield. He won eight league titles and three European Cups, captained the club to its first league and FA Cup double in 1986, and was captain again when Liverpool won the title in 1990.

## From Sauchie to Anfield

Hansen was born in Sauchie on 13 June 1955. LFChistory records that he represented Scotland at junior level in golf, volleyball and squash. According to the National Football Museum, he turned down a contract at Hibernian and joined his brother John at Partick Thistle. He made his first-team debut in his second season, aged 19, and played more than 100 league games for the club. In 1976 Partick won the First Division, then the second tier, and promotion to the new Premier Division.

Bob Paisley signed him on 5 May 1977. The Liverpool club site says Celtic's manager Jock Stein had recommended him. The fee is given as £100,000 by LFChistory and the club, and as £110,000 by the Scottish and National Football Museums.

His debut came on 24 September 1977, a 1–0 home win over Derby County. He made 18 league appearances that season, 26 in all. On 10 May 1978 he started the European Cup final against Club Brugge at Wembley. Kenny Dalglish scored in the 64th minute and Liverpool won 1–0.

## Thompson, Lawrenson and the ball

In 1978–79 Hansen took the centre-half place from Emlyn Hughes, the club's own history records. Liverpool won the league that season and conceded 16 league goals.

LFChistory describes him dribbling forward from the back; the club's own account has him carrying the ball into midfield before passing it on. Paisley's verdict, as LFChistory quotes it, was: "He has given me more heart attacks than any player I have ever known." His first regular partner was Phil Thompson, and LFChistory records that Hansen considered Thompson the partner he understood best.

On 1 April 1981 Liverpool met West Ham United at Villa Park in the replay of the League Cup final. The first game at Wembley, 18 days earlier, had finished 1–1 after extra time. Paul Goddard put West Ham ahead and Dalglish equalised. Then Hansen headed the ball against Billy Bonds's knee and it went past Phil Parkes, in the 25th minute by the club's account. Liverpool won 2–1 and took the League Cup for the first time.

Eight weeks later, on 27 May 1981, he started the European Cup final against Real Madrid at the Parc des Princes in Paris. Alan Kennedy scored late on and Liverpool won 1–0.

Mark Lawrenson replaced Thompson as his partner in 1982–83. In November that season Hansen set up Ian Rush's first goal in a 5–0 win at Goodison Park. In 1983–84 Hansen played 67 games, including all 42 in the league, as Liverpool won the league, the League Cup and the European Cup. He partnered Lawrenson in the final against Roma in Rome on 30 May 1984. It finished 1–1 after extra time, and Liverpool won the shoot-out 4–2.

## Captain and the Double

In 1985 Dalglish, the new player-manager, made Hansen captain in place of Phil Neal, according to the club. He played 41 league games in 1985–86 as Liverpool won the title. On 10 May 1986 he captained the side against Everton in the FA Cup final at Wembley. Gary Lineker scored first, Ian Rush equalised, Craig Johnston made it 2–1 and Rush scored again. Liverpool won 3–1 and had the club's first league and FA Cup double.

## The knee

LFChistory records that knee problems limited his attacking game from 1985–86 and that he needed cortisone injections to play. He missed most of 1988–89 with a knee injury and played eight games, six of them in the league. Ronnie Whelan was captain in his absence.

He came back to captain Liverpool to the title in 1989–90, playing 31 league games and 42 in all. His last appearance came against Queens Park Rangers at Anfield on 28 April 1990. He played no first-team games in 1990–91 and announced his retirement in March 1991, shortly after Dalglish stood down as manager in February.

## Scotland

Hansen's 26 caps came between 19 May 1979, a 3–0 defeat by Wales, and 18 February 1987, a 1–0 defeat by the Republic of Ireland. He played three games at the 1982 World Cup in Spain, against New Zealand, Brazil and the Soviet Union. He was left out of the squad for the 1986 World Cup in Mexico, the summer he captained Liverpool to the Double.

## After Liverpool

He later became a television pundit, best known on the BBC's Match of the Day. LFChistory lists him in the PFA Team of the Year six times. He arrived in time to start the 1978 European Cup final and was captain when Liverpool won the league in 1990. His record stands at 620 appearances and 14 goals in all competitions, including 434 league appearances and eight league goals.

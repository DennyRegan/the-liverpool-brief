Alan Kennedy scored the goal that won Liverpool the European Cup in Paris in 1981 and the penalty that settled the final in Rome three years later. He made 359 appearances for the club and scored 20 goals. He won five league championship medals before leaving in 1985, and in the treble season of 1983–84 he played in every one of Liverpool's 67 matches.

## Newcastle

Kennedy was born in Sunderland on 31 August 1954 and established himself at Newcastle United. He was 19 when he started at left-back against Liverpool in the 1974 FA Cup final, which Liverpool won 3–0. Four years later Bob Paisley brought him to Anfield for £330,000.

Left-back had been a problem position for Paisley. Kennedy offered speed and a habit of going forward. "He will be a first-class capture," Paisley said when he signed. "He is fast, likes to move up to attack and should fit into our side very quickly."

## Two titles in two seasons

His debut came against Queens Park Rangers at Anfield on 19 August 1978, a 2–1 win. He made 37 league appearances that season and another 37 in 1979–80, and Liverpool won the championship both times.

The first of those titles was built on defence. Liverpool conceded 16 goals in 42 league matches and finished eight points ahead of Nottingham Forest. The second was closer, with Manchester United two points behind.

## The 1981 League Cup

In March 1981 he almost won Liverpool their first League Cup at Wembley. The final against West Ham United was goalless until the 118th minute, when Kennedy scored. Ray Stewart equalised from the penalty spot at the end of extra time. Liverpool won the replay 2–1 at Villa Park, with goals from Kenny Dalglish and Alan Hansen.

## A broken wrist

His place in that season's European Cup final was in doubt. Kennedy broke his wrist in the goalless first leg of the semi-final against Bayern Munich at Anfield. Recalling it later on the club's website, he said: "The club doctor told me I would be out of action for about 10 weeks and it would be difficult to make the final."

Richard Money played at left-back in the return in Munich. Paisley picked Kennedy for the final. "I'm glad Bob took a chance with me and I will be forever grateful for that," Kennedy said.

## Paris

On 27 May 1981 Liverpool met Real Madrid at the Parc des Princes. With nine minutes left and no goals, Ray Kennedy took a throw-in on the left and found his namesake. Alan controlled it on his chest, ran into the penalty area and hit a left-footed shot past Agustín. Liverpool won 1–0. "The ball seems to take a long time to hit the back of the net but I can't describe the feeling when it did," he said later.

It was Liverpool's third European Cup in five seasons. Paisley became the first manager to win the competition three times with one club.

## Wembley again

He scored in another League Cup final in 1983. Norman Whiteside had put Manchester United ahead, and Kennedy equalised with a shot from distance 15 minutes from the end of normal time. Ronnie Whelan scored the winner in extra time. Liverpool had won the competition for a third season running, and it was Paisley's last cup final as manager.

## Every game of the treble

Joe Fagan took over for 1983–84. Kennedy played in all 67 competitive matches, including all 42 league games, as Liverpool won the league, the League Cup and the European Cup.

## Rome

The last of those 67 games was against Roma at the Stadio Olimpico on 30 May 1984. Phil Neal put Liverpool ahead and Roberto Pruzzo equalised. It was still 1–1 after extra time, and for the first time a European Cup final went to a penalty shoot-out.

Steve Nicol missed Liverpool's first kick. Bruno Conti and Francesco Graziani then missed for Roma, which left Kennedy to take Liverpool's fifth penalty with the chance to win it. He beat Franco Tancredi, and Liverpool won the shoot-out 4–2. "What a relief it was," he said.

The penalty does not count in his goals total. LFChistory credits him with no European goals that season.

## Leaving

Kennedy won two England caps, both in 1984. At Liverpool, Jim Beglin's emergence challenged his place. Kennedy started the first eight league matches of 1985–86 and joined Sunderland in September 1985.

He left Liverpool with 359 competitive appearances and 20 goals. In the league he made 251 appearances and scored 15 times.

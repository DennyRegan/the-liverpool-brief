Ronnie Moran joined Liverpool in 1949 and retired from the coaching staff in 1998. In between he made 379 appearances and scored 17 goals, captained the club in the Second Division and played 35 league games as Liverpool won the title in 1963–64. He then coached under Bill Shankly, Bob Paisley, Joe Fagan, Kenny Dalglish, Graeme Souness and Roy Evans, and twice took charge of the first team himself. At Anfield he was known as "Bugsy".

## Crosby and the Second Division

Moran was born in Crosby on 28 February 1934. He joined Liverpool in 1949 as a part-timer while working as an apprentice electrician, and played for the club's 'C' team. He signed professional forms on 7 January 1952.

His debut came on 22 November 1952 at the Baseball Ground, a 3–2 defeat by Derby County. Liverpool were relegated at the end of 1953–54. Moran established himself at left-back over the next two seasons and became a regular in the Second Division side.

He was club captain when Shankly arrived in December 1959, and he led the team 47 times in all. Ron Yeats remembered him as strong, good in the air and a defender who used the ball well, according to the club's profile.

## Promotion and the title

On 1 October 1960 Moran was injured, and LFChistory records that he was out for about 14 months. He returned to play 16 league games as Liverpool won the Second Division in 1961–62.

Two years later he was a regular in the side that won the First Division. He played 35 of the 42 league games in 1963–64 and missed seven. He was 30. It was Liverpool's first league title since 1947.

That season also brought a setback. On 29 February 1964 Swansea Town won an FA Cup quarter-final at Anfield 2–1. Moran missed a penalty with Liverpool 2–1 down, and with it went their chance to draw level.

## The end of his playing career

The following season Chris Lawler took right-back and Moran lost his place. He missed the 1965 FA Cup final win over Leeds United, but played in both legs of the European Cup semi-final against Inter Milan. The second leg, at San Siro on 12 May 1965, was his last first-team appearance.

LFChistory gives his final record as 379 appearances: 342 in the league, 32 in the FA Cup, four in the European Cup and one in the Charity Shield. He scored 17 goals.

## The coaching staff

Shankly offered him a coaching role before the 1966–67 season. Moran worked with the youth teams and played on in the reserves for about two more years, formally retiring as a player in 1968–69.

He became reserve-team manager in 1971. Carl Clemente, writing for the club, credits him with Central League titles in 1973 and 1974. When Paisley succeeded Shankly in July 1974, he made Moran first-team trainer, with physiotherapy duties as well. In the summer of 1979 Moran was promoted to chief coach, and Roy Evans took over his old job.

According to the club, he kept detailed diaries of training, matches and injuries. Liverpool won the European Cup in 1977, 1978, 1981 and 1984, all after he had joined the first-team staff.

## On the training ground

Ian Rush said that when he arrived, Moran focused on the things he did wrong. If Rush shot, Moran told him he should have passed. If he passed, he should have shot. Rush recalled the standard as five good games out of six; three out of six was not good enough. Rush said Moran treated everyone the same way, from Rush as a teenager to Dalglish and Souness. He also remembered Moran taking shy players aside to tell them what they had done well.

In a tribute published by Liverpool after Moran's death, Rush said: "He made me the player I was, it's as simple as that."

An extract from the biography *Mr Liverpool*, published by the club, shows Moran in the dressing room before matches, massaging players' legs while telling them whom to mark. Alan Hansen remembered that he never gave long speeches, only sharp reminders of what the players had to do. Roy Evans said Moran "made sure everyone fell in line". He was called the "sergeant-major", Evans said, but he was "much more than that".

## Caretaker manager

Dalglish resigned in February 1991, and Moran took charge from 22 February to 13 April. He lost his first three games: 3–1 at Luton Town, the FA Cup second replay against Everton and a league game against Arsenal. Later in the spell Liverpool won 7–1 at Derby County, and his last game, on 13 April, was a 5–4 win at Leeds United. LFChistory records his ten matches, nine in the league and one in the FA Cup, as four wins, one draw and five defeats. Souness was appointed manager that April.

In April 1992 Moran took over again while Souness had heart surgery. He was in charge for the final seven league games and the FA Cup semi-final replay against Portsmouth. Souness returned for the final against Sunderland at Wembley and asked Moran to lead the team out. Liverpool won 2–0.

## Afterwards

Moran retired in 1998, 49 years after he first joined the club. He went on visiting Melwood on Tuesdays and Fridays at the invitation of Sammy Lee. Ian St John said he stayed within the club all his life.

He died on 22 March 2017, aged 83. His son Paul announced the news that morning.

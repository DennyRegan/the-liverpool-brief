Bill Shankly took over Liverpool in the Second Division in December 1959 and retired in July 1974. In between, the club and LFChistory record 784 matches under him. He won promotion in 1962, the league in 1964, 1966 and 1973, the FA Cup in 1965 and 1974, and the UEFA Cup in 1973. He did it with two different teams. When the first grew old, he built a second.

## Glenbuck and Preston

Shankly was born in Glenbuck, a mining village in Ayrshire, on 2 September 1913. His four brothers all played professional football, and he followed them. He joined Carlisle United in 1932 and moved to Preston North End the following year.

He was a right-half. He helped Preston win promotion to the First Division and played in two FA Cup finals, losing to Sunderland in 1937 and beating Huddersfield Town in 1938. He played for Scotland. During the war he served in the RAF, and by the time League football resumed in 1946 he was in his thirties.

## Learning the job

His management career began at Carlisle in 1949. They finished third in the Third Division North in 1951. He moved on to Grimsby Town and then Workington, a small club where he handled much more than team selection, including administration, scouting and fundraising.

At Huddersfield Town he worked first under Andy Beattie, coaching the reserves and young players. Denis Law was among them. He succeeded Beattie as manager in November 1956. He was frustrated by the lack of money to strengthen the team.

## A club in the Second Division

Liverpool appointed Shankly on 1 December 1959. He arrived at Anfield on 14 December. His first match, at home to Cardiff City on 19 December, ended in a 4–0 defeat.

He wanted better players and had to persuade the board to pay for them. His first signing, Sammy Reid from Motherwell, never played for the first team. The signings that mattered came in 1961: Ian St John from Motherwell and Ron Yeats from Dundee United. St John played up front and Yeats at centre-half.

Some of what he needed was already at the club. Roger Hunt and Gerry Byrne were there. So were the coaches Bob Paisley, Joe Fagan and Reuben Bennett, and Shankly kept them on. Their work together became known as the Boot Room.

## Melwood and the working day

The training ground at Melwood was in a poor state. The club's own account of it uses the phrase "a sorry wilderness". The pavilion was made over and ground staff were sent to fix the pitches.

Shankly changed the routine too. Players reported to Anfield, changed there, travelled to Melwood together and came back afterwards. He wanted a cooling-off period between training and a bath and a meal.

Sessions followed a set pattern of warm-ups, circuits, ball work and small-sided games. Players worked the ball against boards, and the sweatbox tested both technique and fitness.

## Promotion

Liverpool had been in the Second Division for eight years. In 1961–62 they scored 99 league goals and won it with 62 points, eight clear of Leyton Orient. Hunt scored 41 goals in 41 league games.

Promotion was secured on 21 April 1962 with a 2–0 win over Southampton at Anfield. Kevin Lewis, standing in for the suspended St John, scored both goals.

## Champions

Liverpool finished eighth in their first season back. Shankly signed Peter Thompson from Preston to play on the left wing, and in 1963–64 they won the league. They clinched it with a 5–0 win over Arsenal at Anfield on 18 April 1964, with three games left. It was the club's first title since 1946–47.

The all-red kit was first worn on 25 November 1964, against Anderlecht. Yeats had tried it on the day before. "Christ, the players looked like giants. And we played like giants," Shankly said.

## The first FA Cup

Liverpool had never won the FA Cup when they met Leeds United at Wembley on 1 May 1965. It was 0–0 after 90 minutes. Hunt scored early in extra time and Billy Bremner equalised. St John headed the winner from Ian Callaghan's cross for a 2–1 win.

Three days later Liverpool beat Internazionale 3–1 at Anfield in the first leg of the European Cup semi-final. Hunt, Callaghan and St John scored. Inter won the second leg 3–0 in Milan.

## A second title, and two European defeats

Liverpool won the league again in 1965–66, using only fourteen players in the league. They also reached the European Cup Winners' Cup final at Hampden Park on 5 May 1966. Hunt equalised against Borussia Dortmund, but Dortmund won 2–1 after extra time.

The next European Cup campaign ended against Ajax. Liverpool lost 5–1 in fog in Amsterdam on 7 December 1966 and drew 2–2 at Anfield a week later.

## Watford and the rebuild

The side that won those titles grew old together. Loyalty to those players complicated the task of replacing them, and Bob Paisley later called it Shankly's one failing. Liverpool did not win the league again until 1973.

On 21 February 1970 they lost 1–0 at Second Division Watford in the FA Cup quarter-final. Barry Endean scored. Shankly treated it as the turning point. "The breaking-up process began there," he said. The change was already under way. Hunt had left for Bolton in December 1969, and Emlyn Hughes and Ray Clemence were already at the club.

Clemence, Hughes, Larry Lloyd, Steve Heighway and Brian Hall became important to the new side. Tommy Smith, Chris Lawler and Callaghan carried over from the old one. John Toshack arrived from Cardiff City in November 1970, and Kevin Keegan from Scunthorpe United in May 1971. Toshack's work in the air set up chances for Keegan.

The new team came close first. Arsenal beat them 2–1 after extra time in the 1971 FA Cup final. In 1971–72 Liverpool finished one point behind the champions, Derby County.

## League and UEFA Cup

In 1972–73 Liverpool won the league and the UEFA Cup. They were the first English club to win the league and a European trophy in the same season. Their route to the final went through Eintracht Frankfurt, AEK Athens, Dynamo Berlin, Dynamo Dresden and Tottenham Hotspur.

The first leg of the final, against Borussia Mönchengladbach at Anfield, was abandoned because of rain. Shankly brought Toshack into the starting line-up for the replayed match the next day, in place of Hall. Keegan scored twice, both from Toshack's assists, and Lloyd added a third. Clemence saved a penalty from Jupp Heynckes. Liverpool lost the second leg 2–0, with Heynckes scoring both, and won 3–2 on aggregate.

The second team gave Shankly another FA Cup in 1974. Liverpool beat Newcastle United 3–0 at Wembley. Keegan scored twice and Heighway once.

## Shankly and the supporters

Shankly gave his time to the people who watched the team. Callaghan remembered him talking to fans and signing autographs, and he wrote letters to supporters. His socialism and his Glenbuck upbringing shaped his belief in collective effort.

## Retirement

Chairman John Smith announced Shankly's retirement at Anfield on 12 July 1974. Shankly was 60. His wife, Ness, wanted the family to see more of him.

Paisley succeeded him. Shankly led the team out once more, for the Charity Shield against Leeds at Wembley on 10 August 1974. It finished 1–1 and Liverpool won on penalties. It is recorded as his final game.

He regretted retiring within weeks. His relationship with the club became strained, and he never found a lasting formal role there.

Shankly died on 29 September 1981, aged 68. Ness first unlocked the Shankly Gates at Anfield on 26 August 1982. His statue was unveiled outside the ground in December 1997.

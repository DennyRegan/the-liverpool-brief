Ray Kennedy's most important Liverpool goal came with his right foot. In Munich in April 1981, he moved forward to meet David Johnson's pass and scored the away goal that took Liverpool into another European Cup final. It was a centre-forward's intervention from a man whose position had changed. Bob Paisley had moved Kennedy into midfield more than five years earlier, giving him a new starting point without taking away his ability to finish.

## An established goalscorer

Born in Seaton Delaval on 28 July 1951, Kennedy had already helped Arsenal win major trophies before Liverpool bought him. In the 1970 Inter-Cities Fairs Cup final, he scored late in the first leg at Anderlecht, reducing Arsenal's defeat to 3–1. They overturned that deficit with a 3–0 victory at Highbury. A year later came the league and FA Cup Double. [UEFA's career account](https://www.uefa.com/news-media/news/026f-13d8da938bba-3985044a685d-1000--english-football-mourns-european-cup-winner-ray-kennedy/) records those early European and domestic successes.

Kennedy's header from George Armstrong's cross [secured the championship at Tottenham](https://www.standard.co.uk/sport/football/ray-kennedy-arsenal-bob-wilson-b969375.html) in May 1971. Arsenal won 1–0 at White Hart Lane, with their young striker supplying the goal that settled the title. He was therefore an experienced winner when Liverpool paid £180,000 for him on 12 July 1974. The transfer coincided with Bill Shankly's announcement that he was retiring. Shankly had made the signing; Paisley inherited the player.

There was an encouraging beginning. Kennedy scored in his first three Liverpool games, but the early promise did not become a settled place in attack. John Toshack and Kevin Keegan remained a formidable partnership, and Kennedy finished his first season with ten goals in all competitions. Liverpool had acquired a forward of proven quality without yet finding his best use.

## A different place in the team

The opening came in November 1975, when an injury to Peter Cormack created a vacancy on the left of midfield. Kennedy took that position against Middlesbrough, then scored twice in Liverpool's next match, a 6–0 win over Real Sociedad. The [LFChistory profile](https://lfchistory.net/players/344) traces the change from an uncertain experiment to a regular role.

Moving back gave Kennedy more of the field to work with. His passing could help build attacks, while his experience up front remained useful when he went beyond the forwards. Liverpool gained a midfielder who could arrive in scoring positions rather than wait in them. His strength and shooting were still part of his game; they now served a wider purpose. The conversion gave Paisley another source of goals without requiring him to break up his established attack.

Its value was clear in the 1976 UEFA Cup final. Brugge led 2–0 at Anfield, leaving Liverpool with a substantial recovery to make. Kennedy scored in the 59th minute. Jimmy Case equalised two minutes later and Keegan's penalty completed the turnaround. Three goals in six minutes changed the tie, and a 1–1 draw in Belgium secured a 4–3 aggregate victory. [UEFA's final account](https://www.uefa.com/uefaeuropaleague/news/004e-0e6a07b4a2b7-2cb62806e092-1000--1975-76-keegan-lifts-liverpool-again/) places Kennedy at the beginning of that recovery. Liverpool also won the league that season: his new position had become part of Paisley's first championship side.

## Goals from the left

Kennedy started the European Cup final victories of 1977 and 1978. On the way to the second, he scored the opening goal in Liverpool's 3–0 semi-final win over Borussia Mönchengladbach at Anfield, helping reverse a first-leg defeat. These were substantial contributions to the European record, made before the more famous goal in Munich.

His attacking instincts were equally visible at home. In the 7–0 defeat of Tottenham on 2 September 1978, Kennedy headed Liverpool's third goal at the far post. The match is often remembered for Terry McDermott's spectacular finish at the end of a sweeping move, but Kennedy's earlier header offered another example of the threat Liverpool carried from midfield. The club's [analysis of the match](https://www.liverpoolfc.com/news/first-team/121297-analysis-longest-90-minutes) records his part in a commanding first half.

Liverpool won the league in 1979 and retained it in 1980. Kennedy's contribution rested on availability as well as decisive moments: across the five seasons from 1976–77 to 1980–81, he missed only five league games. Personnel changed around him, but the left side of midfield remained remarkably settled.

## Munich and Paris

The 1981 semi-final required Liverpool to survive a difficult return leg after Bayern Munich had drawn 0–0 at Anfield. Kenny Dalglish went off injured early in the match. The score remained goalless until Kennedy's finish in the 83rd minute; Bayern equalised, but the 1–1 draw sent Liverpool through on away goals. His run and shot had supplied precisely what the tie demanded.

In Paris, Kennedy contributed to the winning goal again. His throw-in found Alan Kennedy, who drove forward and scored against Real Madrid. Liverpool won 1–0, giving Ray his third European Cup. The [1981 campaign record](https://www.lfchistory.net/articles/2437) connects the two interventions: the goal that secured the final and the throw that began its decisive attack.

Ronnie Whelan's emergence eventually challenged his place. Kennedy left for Swansea City in January 1982, having made 15 league appearances that season, enough to receive a fifth Liverpool championship medal. The [club profile](https://www.liverpoolfc.com/past-players/ray-kennedy) records 393 appearances and 72 goals. His later career included Hartlepool United and a spell as player-manager of Pezoporikos in Cyprus, as [Swansea's tribute](https://www.swanseacity.com/news/swansea-city-saddened-learn-passing-former-player-ray-kennedy) records.

Kennedy died in November 2021, aged 70. His Liverpool career remains an unusually clear example of a manager recognising what a player could become. Paisley changed where Kennedy played; Kennedy made the change last through three European Cup-winning teams.

Trent Alexander-Arnold played 354 games for Liverpool and scored 23 goals. By the club's count he also provided 86 assists. He won eight major trophies, including two Premier League titles and the Champions League, with the club he had joined as a six-year-old. In 2018–19 he set a Premier League record for assists by a defender, and his quick corner against Barcelona that season led to one of the best-known goals in Liverpool's history.

## From West Derby to the first team

Alexander-Arnold was born in Liverpool on 7 October 1998 and grew up in West Derby, close to the club's Melwood training ground. He was a midfielder in the Academy before moving to right-back. Liverpool's account of his development credits his youth coaches, including Alex Inglethorpe, with seeing the position as his quickest route into the first team. LFChistory instead credits Jürgen Klopp with making him a right-back.

He had already played there for the senior side before Klopp arrived. On 2 August 2015, under Brendan Rodgers, he spent almost an hour at right-back in a 2–1 friendly win at Swindon Town.

Klopp gave him his competitive debut on 25 October 2016, in a 2–1 League Cup win over Tottenham at Anfield. He was 18 and played 68 minutes. Nathaniel Clyne was still the established right-back, but a back injury before the 2017–18 season gave Alexander-Arnold an opening. He and Joe Gomez shared the position before he made it his own.

His first Liverpool goal came on his European debut, away to Hoffenheim on 15 August 2017, in the first leg of the Champions League play-off. He curled a free-kick round the wall in a 2–1 win. "The free-kick is no coincidence," Jordan Henderson said afterwards. "He has been doing that every day in training for the last few weeks."

## A new kind of right-back

By the end of that season he was starting a Champions League final. Liverpool lost to Real Madrid in Kyiv, but returned to the final a year later. Alexander-Arnold was still only 20 when they beat Tottenham 2–0 in Madrid on 1 June 2019.

Liverpool created from both full-back positions. In the 2018–19 Premier League, Andy Robertson supplied 11 assists and Alexander-Arnold 12. According to the Premier League, that was the most by a defender in a single season, beating the 11 of Robertson, Leighton Baines and Andy Hinchcliffe. With Mohamed Salah, Sadio Mané and Roberto Firmino ahead of them, the full-backs were a large part of the attack.

The semi-final against Barcelona that season is the best-known example. Liverpool had lost the first leg 3–0. At Anfield on 7 May, Divock Origi scored in the seventh minute, and Georginio Wijnaldum came on at half-time and scored twice in just over two minutes. That made it 3–3 on aggregate. In the 79th minute Alexander-Arnold took a corner quickly, before Barcelona had organised themselves, and found Origi unmarked. Origi scored, and Liverpool won 4–0.

## The title season

On Boxing Day 2019, Liverpool went to second-placed Leicester five days after winning the Club World Cup in Qatar. They won 4–0, and Alexander-Arnold was involved in every goal.

For the first, Robertson's corner was only partly cleared, and Alexander-Arnold, temporarily on the left, crossed for Firmino to head in. The win gave Liverpool a 13-point lead at the top.

He played in all 38 league games in 2019–20, scoring four goals and providing 13 assists, beating his own record for a defender. Only Kevin De Bruyne supplied more. Liverpool won the league for the first time in 30 years, and Alexander-Arnold was named the first Premier League Young Player of the Season.

## More finals, and a move inside

Liverpool's title defence faltered in 2020–21. By the time Aston Villa visited on 10 April 2021, they had lost six Premier League games in a row at Anfield. Alexander-Arnold scored in stoppage time to win the game 2–1 and end the run. On 2 June he tore a thigh muscle playing for England against Austria and missed the delayed European Championship.

Liverpool won the FA Cup and the League Cup in 2022 and reached another Champions League final. In Paris on 28 May, Real Madrid won 1–0 through Vinícius Júnior. It was Alexander-Arnold's third start in a Champions League final, and he was still only 23.

In 2022–23 Klopp changed his role. When Liverpool had the ball, Alexander-Arnold moved from right-back into central midfield, which let him pass from inside the pitch as well as from the flank. He made six assists in six starts in April 2023, including Firmino's equaliser against Arsenal and two in the win at Leeds, and was named the club's Player of the Month.

On 31 July 2023 he was made vice-captain to Virgil van Dijk. Liverpool won the League Cup again in Klopp's final season, although injury kept Alexander-Arnold out of the final.

## A second title, then departure

He stayed for Arne Slot's first season and won another Premier League title in 2024–25. On 20 April 2025, back from an ankle injury, he came off the bench at Leicester and scored the only goal with a volley in the 76th minute. The win left Liverpool one victory from the title.

On 5 May 2025 he announced that he would leave when his contract ended. "I've been here 20 years now," he said in his message to supporters. He said he needed "a new challenge for me as a player and as a person."

Six days later, in a 2–2 draw with Arsenal at Anfield, some supporters booed him and others applauded. Slot spoke about both reactions afterwards. "A few of them showed it in a way that they booed him," he said. "And a few of them clapped."

His last game for Liverpool was on 25 May 2025, the final day of the title season. On 30 May Liverpool announced that they had accepted a fee from Real Madrid, reported as £10m, so that he could join before his contract expired at the end of June. The transfer went through on 1 June, which allowed him to play for Madrid at the Club World Cup.

## The honours

His eight major trophies with Liverpool were two Premier League titles, the Champions League, the FA Cup, two League Cups (2022 and 2024, although he missed the 2024 final), the UEFA Super Cup and the Club World Cup. He also won the Community Shield in 2022.

He is still at Real Madrid, where he wears the number 12 shirt.

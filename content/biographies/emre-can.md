Emre Can made 167 appearances for Liverpool in four seasons and scored 14 goals, according to LFChistory. His goal total rose every season: one, then two, five and six. He played in three finals for the club: the League Cup and Europa League finals in 2016 and the Champions League final in 2018. His overhead kick at Watford in May 2017 was voted the Premier League's Goal of the Season. He played in midfield and in defence, wherever he was needed.

## Bayern, Leverkusen and Liverpool

Can was born on 12 January 1994. Bayern Munich signed him from Eintracht Frankfurt in 2009 and sold him to Bayer Leverkusen in August 2013. Liverpool signed him on 3 July 2014, aged 20, for £9.75m when they activated a release clause, according to LFChistory.

Brendan Rodgers was the manager. He said Can had "charisma on the football pitch and courage to want the ball and make things happen", and that Liverpool would be the next phase of his development as a player.

At Liverpool he played in defence as well as in defensive midfield. His first goal came on 8 November 2014, when he opened the scoring at home to Chelsea. Liverpool lost 2–1. He made 40 appearances that season.

## Klopp's first season

Jürgen Klopp arrived in October 2015, and Can became an important part of his side. He made 49 appearances in 2015–16, more than in any other season. Liverpool reached two finals that season. In the League Cup final at Wembley on 28 February 2016 he started, and Liverpool lost to Manchester City on penalties after a 1–1 draw.

On 14 April 2016 Can damaged ankle ligaments in the quarter-final second leg against Borussia Dortmund at Anfield, which Liverpool won 4–3. UEFA reported that he "did not have a single day off" during his recovery and trained at Melwood for seven or eight hours a day.

He came back ahead of schedule. On 5 May, exactly three weeks after the injury, he started the semi-final second leg against Villarreal. Liverpool had lost the first leg 1–0. They won 3–0 at Anfield to reach the final. UEFA pointed to what his return would give Liverpool: the ability to break up counter-attacks and to start their play from in front of the defence.

He started the final against Sevilla at St. Jakob-Park in Basel on 18 May 2016. Daniel Sturridge put Liverpool 1–0 up after 35 minutes, but Sevilla scored three times in the second half and won 3–1.

## Burnley, Watford and the top four

In 2016–17 Liverpool were trying to finish in the top four and return to the Champions League. Can scored five goals that season, and two of them won matches.

On 12 March 2017 Burnley led at Anfield through Ashley Barnes after seven minutes. Georginio Wijnaldum equalised just before half-time, and Can won the game 2–1 with a low drive from distance on 61 minutes.

The Watford goal came on Monday 1 May 2017 at Vicarage Road. In first-half stoppage time Lucas Leiva chipped the ball forward into the penalty area. Can had run in behind the Watford defence. He said afterwards that he had seen the space and run behind. With his back to goal, he met the ball with an overhead kick that went into the top corner past Heurelho Gomes.

It was the only goal of the game. The win kept Liverpool third, four points clear of Manchester United in fifth. They finished the season in the top four and qualified for the Champions League play-off round. Can's strike was named the 2016–17 Premier League Goal of the Season.

## Hoffenheim and Brighton

The play-off round, in August 2017, was the last step before the group stage. Liverpool won the first leg 2–1 in Germany. In the second leg at Anfield on 23 August, Mohamed Salah scored after 18 minutes and Can made it 2–0 and 3–0 soon afterwards, his second goal coming on 21 minutes. Roberto Firmino added a fourth. Hoffenheim scored twice through Mark Uth and Sandro Wagner, but Liverpool won 4–2, 6–3 on aggregate.

Klopp was pleased with him. "He is a great player, it was an excellent game," he said. He added that "it was also useful that we kept the penalty box full of players". Can was now getting into the box more often, and he scored six goals that season, his best total for the club.

He still filled gaps when needed. Before the game at Brighton on 2 December 2017, Joel Matip was injured and Joe Gomez and Ragnar Klavan were ill. Klopp picked a makeshift back three of Can, Wijnaldum and Dejan Lovren. Can headed the opening goal after 30 minutes, from a set piece that LFChistory credits to Philippe Coutinho, and Liverpool won 5–1.

## The last season

A back injury cost him the end of his final season. He had the problem in the week before Liverpool's home game against Watford on 17 March 2018, and it forced him off in the first half. He did not play again for two months, and in April Klopp said his season was likely over.

He was fit enough to be on the bench for the Champions League final against Real Madrid in Kyiv on 26 May 2018. He came on for James Milner after 83 minutes, and Liverpool lost 3–1. It was his last game for the club.

His contract ran until 30 June 2018, and on 8 June Liverpool confirmed that he would leave.

Can left a year before Klopp's team began winning trophies. He was not part of the side that won the Champions League in 2019 or the Premier League in 2019–20.

## After Liverpool

Juventus announced his signing on 21 June 2018, on a four-year contract that began on 1 July. He joined Borussia Dortmund on loan in January 2020, and the move became permanent that summer. He later became Dortmund's captain.

On 28 February 2026 he tore the anterior cruciate ligament in his left knee in a 3–2 home defeat by Bayern Munich. In March Dortmund extended his contract by a year, to June 2027. Their sporting director, Lars Ricken, said: "Emre is our captain and a true role model."

Gerry Byrne made 333 appearances for Liverpool and scored four goals. He played every league game of the 1961–62 promotion season, won the First Division in 1963–64 and 1965–66, and started the club's first European final. In the 1965 FA Cup final he broke his collarbone early in the match, stayed on through extra time and crossed for Roger Hunt's opening goal. Liverpool won the FA Cup for the first time. His whole senior career was spent at Anfield.

## From schoolboy football

Byrne was born in Liverpool on 29 August 1938. By his own account, the club's scout Tosh Moore saw him on a trip to Dublin with Liverpool Catholic Schoolboys. He joined Liverpool as an amateur at 15 and turned professional in August 1955.

His competitive debut came under Phil Taylor on 28 September 1957, away to Charlton Athletic. Liverpool lost 5–1 at The Valley, and Byrne scored a late own goal. It was his only appearance that season. He played once the following season, and after three seasons he had made seven appearances in all. John Molyneux and Ronnie Moran held the full-back places.

## Shankly

Byrne had been on the transfer list before Bill Shankly arrived in December 1959. "I was on the transfer list, to be honest," he said later. He stayed, and under the new manager he got into the team at right-back. He later became known as a left-back.

On 18 April 1960 Liverpool beat Rotherham 3–0 at Anfield, with Byrne at right-back, Moran on the left and Ian Callaghan on the right wing. The News Chronicle and Daily Dispatch report singled out the two young players. It called Callaghan the "latest sensation" who "has solved the outside-right problem", and described Byrne as "a scientific defender".

Bob Paisley later said Byrne's right foot was his best but that he was good on either side. Paisley also remembered him as the quiet one in the cup-winning side.

## Promotion

In 1961–62 Byrne played all 42 league games and all five FA Cup ties, 47 matches in all. Liverpool won the Second Division and returned to the top flight.

He scored his first goal for the club that season, at home to Brighton on 3 February 1962. The visitors led at half-time before Byrne equalised early in the second half. Roger Hunt and Ian St John then scored to complete a 3–1 victory.

## Champions and Europe

Liverpool won the First Division in 1963–64, two seasons after promotion, and the title brought European football. On 25 November 1964 they beat Anderlecht 3–0 at Anfield in the European Cup, with Byrne at left-back. Shankly called it "the best full-back display Europe has ever seen". Byrne later confirmed the praise. "I cut a lot of balls out. I could read the game well," he said.

## Wembley, 1965

Liverpool met Leeds United in the FA Cup final at Wembley on 1 May 1965. They had never won the trophy. Early in the match Byrne collided with Bobby Collins and broke his collarbone. Substitutes were not allowed, so going off would have left Liverpool with ten men. He stayed on and played the full match, 120 minutes including extra time.

The score was 0–0 after 90 minutes. Three minutes into extra time, Byrne crossed from the left and Hunt headed Liverpool in front. Billy Bremner equalised nine minutes later. In the 111th minute St John headed in a cross from Callaghan, and Liverpool won 2–1.

## Another title

Byrne played every league game again in 1965–66, and 53 matches in all competitions. Liverpool won the league for the second time in three seasons.

On 5 May 1966 he started the Cup Winners' Cup final against Borussia Dortmund at Hampden Park, the club's first European final. Hunt equalised, but Liverpool lost 2–1 after extra time.

## England

Byrne won his first England cap against Scotland on 6 April 1963. His second came against Norway on 29 June 1966. Alf Ramsey named him in the squad for the 1966 World Cup, but he did not play in the tournament. He received a World Cup winner's medal in 2009.

## The knee

On 20 August 1966, in the opening league game after the World Cup, against Leicester City, Byrne was replaced by Gordon Milne after 24 minutes. He had twisted his knee with his studs caught in the turf. "There was nobody near me," he said later.

He made 12 appearances in 1966–67. He came back to play 36 times in 1967–68, 27 of them in the league, but never again played a full season.

## The last game

He made three appearances in 1968–69. The last was a 1–0 home win over Wolverhampton Wanderers on 5 April 1969, with Hunt scoring in the first half. Byrne was 30. He retired in 1969, having played for no other club.

Liverpool gave him a testimonial in April 1970, and around 40,000 people attended. Shankly called him "the best professional I worked with". He also described Byrne as "hard and skilful" and "totally honest, which is the greatest quality of all".

Byrne died on 28 November 2015, aged 77.

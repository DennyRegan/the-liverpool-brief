Tom Watson ran Liverpool's football from 1896 until his death in May 1915. In that time the club won its first league title, in 1901, went down in 1904, won the Second Division in 1905 and the First Division in 1906. No club had won the two divisions in successive seasons before. He also took Liverpool to their first FA Cup final, in 1914. The club's records credit him with 742 matches, 329 of them won. Only Bill Shankly, with 784, has managed more.

## From Newcastle to Sunderland

Watson was born in Newcastle upon Tyne in 1859. He worked for both of the city's early clubs, East End and West End, before becoming Sunderland's secretary in 1889, according to LFChistory. Sunderland joined the Football League the following year and were champions in 1892, 1893 and 1895.

## A new job at Anfield

Liverpool appointed him in the summer of 1896, when he was 37. The club had been relegated at their first attempt in the First Division and had just won promotion back. John McKenna moved into the boardroom as Watson arrived.

The title was secretary, but the job covered far more than administration. Watson arranged fixtures, kept records and dealt with the newspapers. He also chose, signed and prepared the players. He brought in a regulated diet and exercise routine.

His first league match was against The Wednesday on 1 September 1896. Liverpool wore red shirts and white shorts for the first time and won 2–1. The promoted side finished fifth and reached the FA Cup semi-final, where Aston Villa beat them 3–0.

## Recruiting

Watson wrote about recruitment in 1899, in an article for the Leicester Chronicle and Leicestershire Mercury. He recalled hazardous trips into Scotland for players in his earlier career, and the resistance he met from Scots who did not want English clubs taking them. At Liverpool, he wrote, players were now secured through the transfer system.

He relied on contacts. Writing in the Liverpool Echo in 1924, Victor Hall described a network of friends who would tell Watson about a player's experience and availability. "He touched the wire and back came the information," Hall wrote.

Liverpool finished ninth in 1897–98, a season that included their first league win over Everton, 3–1 at Anfield. Watson kept signing. Jack Cox came from Blackpool, Tom Robertson and John Walker from Hearts, and Alex Raisbeck from Hibernian.

## Close in 1899

By 1898–99 Liverpool were chasing the league and the FA Cup. In March the Evening Express visited the squad at St Anne's-on-the-Sea before the semi-final. The routine was walking, sprinting, baths supervised by the trainer James Chapman and quiet evenings. Watson told the reporter the players did not see a football during the week.

The semi-final against Sheffield United went to four matches. Two draws were followed by a game abandoned at an overcrowded Fallowfield, and Liverpool lost the fourth at Derby. In the league they went to Aston Villa for the final match level on points. Villa had the better goal average, so only a win would do. Liverpool lost 5–0.

The next season started with eight straight league defeats, and Liverpool finished tenth. George Allan, a forward in the 1899 side, died in October 1899. In January 1900 Watson signed Sam Raybould from New Brighton Tower.

## Champions in 1901

Liverpool won their first three games of 1900–01 and beat Aston Villa 5–1 at Anfield in November. Raybould and Robertson were prominent in that game.

A 1–0 win at Sunderland in February began a 12-match unbeaten league run. By the last day Sunderland had finished their fixtures, and Liverpool needed a point at West Bromwich Albion. On 29 April 1901 Walker scored the only goal at The Hawthorns. Liverpool finished two points ahead of Sunderland, Watson's old club, and were champions for the first time.

Bill Perkins played every league game in goal, and Billy Dunlop was at full-back. When the team came home, a fife and drum band welcomed them.

## Relegation and back

Liverpool finished 11th in 1901–02 and were relegated in 1904. Watson kept Raisbeck at the centre of the team and signed Ned Doig, the goalkeeper he had worked with at Sunderland.

Doig played every league match in 1904–05. Liverpool conceded only 25 goals in 34 league fixtures, beat Manchester United 4–0 in the penultimate match and finished two points above Bolton Wanderers to win the Second Division.

## Champions again in 1906

The return to the First Division started badly. Liverpool lost their first three league games, 3–1, 3–1 and 5–0. Jack Parkinson broke his wrist in the opening defeat at Woolwich Arsenal, and Dunlop twisted his knee. A 2–1 win at Sunderland brought the first points, but Liverpool lost five of their first eight games and were 16th.

Watson had signed Sam Hardy from Chesterfield, and Hardy replaced Doig after those eight games. Liverpool won 3–2 at Newcastle and 3–0 at home to Aston Villa, and by mid-December they were top.

Everton beat them 2–0 in the FA Cup semi-final. In the league, Preston North End were the nearest challengers. On 16 April 1906 Liverpool lost 3–2 at Bolton, but Preston lost 2–0 at Sunderland the same day and could no longer catch them. Liverpool finished on 51 points, four ahead of Preston.

At the club's meeting that summer, Watson spoke about Liverpool's ups and downs and urged further progress. The Kop was unveiled at Anfield in 1906.

## No third title

Liverpool were runners-up to Aston Villa in 1909–10, when Parkinson scored 30 First Division goals. In 1911–12 they were fighting relegation, and Watson gave the goalkeeping place to Kenneth Campbell, who was 20. Liverpool won three of their last four games, with three clean sheets from Campbell, and stayed up.

## The 1914 final

In 1914 Liverpool beat Aston Villa 2–0 in the FA Cup semi-final at White Hart Lane, with a goal in each half from Jimmy Nicholl. It was their first final. In the league that season they finished 16th.

The final was at Crystal Palace on 25 April 1914. Harry Lowe, the regular captain, was ruled out after a fitness test that morning. Burnley won 1–0 with a second-half goal from Bert Freeman.

Four days later the two finalists met again at Anfield in a charity match Watson had proposed. Liverpool won 1–0 and the Cup was paraded before the crowd.

## Death

The First World War had started by Watson's last season. His last match in charge was on 24 April 1915. He died of pneumonia on 6 May 1915, aged 56.

The Liverpool Echo's report of his funeral recorded former players, including Raisbeck, Doig and Arthur Goddard, among the bearers, and representatives of many clubs in attendance. He was buried at Anfield Cemetery.

He won five league titles, three with Sunderland and two with Liverpool. His grave had been unmarked. In May 2015, on the 100th anniversary of his death, Liverpool and Sunderland unveiled a headstone that both clubs had helped pay for.

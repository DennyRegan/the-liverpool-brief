Ray Clemence had already won a league title, a UEFA Cup and an FA Cup with Liverpool when Bob Paisley became manager in 1974. Paisley did not need to find a goalkeeper, and through the changes he made elsewhere Clemence remained.

His Liverpool career covered 665 competitive appearances and 323 clean sheets.

## From Skegness to Anfield

Born in Skegness on 5 August 1948, Clemence joined Scunthorpe United in 1965. He made his first-team debut in April 1966, while still 17. Liverpool bought him for £18,000 in June 1967, after 48 league appearances for Scunthorpe.

Anfield required another apprenticeship. Tommy Lawrence was the established goalkeeper, and Clemence had to wait. His senior debut came against Swansea in the League Cup in September 1968, and his first league appearance followed in January 1970. In 1970–71 he made 41 appearances and was the regular choice.

In 1973 he saved a penalty in the first leg of the UEFA Cup final against Borussia Mönchengladbach. Liverpool were 3–0 up when Jupp Heynckes took it. The return ended in a 2–0 defeat, leaving Liverpool winners by a single goal on aggregate; had the penalty gone in, the tie would have been level. According to Liverpool's own account of the final, Clemence had watched Heynckes take a penalty on television and used that to choose his direction.

## The goalkeeper Paisley inherited

Paisley's first league championship came in 1975–76, together with another UEFA Cup. Clemence kept goal as Liverpool beat Club Brugge 4–3 on aggregate in the final.

Liverpool's records give Clemence 456 league appearances out of a possible 462 between 1970 and 1981. Liverpool retained the league title in 1976–77, then lost the FA Cup final to Manchester United four days before the European Cup final in Rome.

In Rome, Terry McDermott put Liverpool ahead and Allan Simonsen equalised for Mönchengladbach. With the score at 1–1, about an hour into the match, Uli Stielike was through on goal and Clemence dived at his feet to save, as LFChistory records. Tommy Smith scored for Liverpool in the 64th minute and Phil Neal added a penalty to make it 3–1. Before Liverpool won their first European Cup, Clemence had kept the match level.

## Wembley, Paris and a record season

The following year's European campaign ended at Wembley against Brugge. Liverpool had overturned a first-leg semi-final defeat by Mönchengladbach with a 3–0 victory at Anfield. In the final, Kenny Dalglish scored the only goal. Clemence finished with a clean sheet, and Liverpool retained the trophy. The scoring names had changed since Rome; the goalkeeper had not.

In 1978–79 Clemence played all 42 league matches as Liverpool won the championship, conceding just 16 goals. He kept 28 league clean sheets. Phil Neal, Phil Thompson, Alan Hansen and Alan Kennedy formed the defence associated with that side, with Clemence behind them.

Another title followed in 1979–80. In 1981 Liverpool won their first League Cup and reached a third European Cup final under Paisley. They eliminated Bayern Munich after a goalless first leg at Anfield and a 1–1 draw in Munich, progressing on away goals. Against Real Madrid in Paris, Alan Kennedy scored the winner. Clemence kept another clean sheet in the 1–0 victory. It was his last Liverpool appearance.

## After Liverpool

He joined Tottenham in August 1981 and won the FA Cup in his first season. Spurs won the UEFA Cup in 1984, but Clemence did not play in the final; he was an unused substitute. Tottenham's records give him 330 appearances before he retired in 1988.

For England, competition with Peter Shilton limited the opportunities available to either goalkeeper. Clemence nevertheless won 61 caps between 1972 and 1983. He later coached at Tottenham, managed Barnet and joined England's coaching staff in 1996, remaining with the FA until 2013. He died on 15 November 2020, aged 72.

The three European Cup finals remain a clear measure of his Liverpool years: three starts, three victories, one goal conceded.

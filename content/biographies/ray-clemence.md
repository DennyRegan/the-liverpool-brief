Ray Clemence had already helped Liverpool win a championship, a UEFA Cup and an FA Cup when Bob Paisley became manager in 1974. There was no need for the new manager to find a goalkeeper or decide whether the one he had could manage the biggest occasions. Bill Shankly had settled that question. Through the changes Paisley made elsewhere, Clemence remained.

His Liverpool career eventually covered 665 competitive appearances and 323 clean sheets. Those figures describe both an exceptional goalkeeper and the teams around him. Clemence had to remain ready for an opponent's chance after long stretches when Liverpool controlled the game. Having little to do did not make the next save less important.

## From Skegness to Anfield

Born in Skegness on 5 August 1948, Clemence joined Scunthorpe United in 1965. He made his first-team debut the following April, while still 17. The next season brought a chastening afternoon when Scunthorpe conceded seven at Grimsby, but he retained his place. Liverpool bought him for £18,000 in June 1967, after 50 Scunthorpe appearances. [Scunthorpe's own account](https://www.scunthorpe-united.co.uk/news/2021/november/remembering-ray) records that swift progress from an inexperienced teenager to a Shankly signing.

Anfield required another apprenticeship. Tommy Lawrence was the established goalkeeper, and Clemence had to wait. His senior debut came against Swansea in the League Cup in September 1968; his first league appearance followed in January 1970. By the following season he was the regular choice. The delay matters in a career remembered for its longevity: he did not arrive fully formed and immediately take possession of the Liverpool goal.

By 1973 his value was plain. In the first leg of the UEFA Cup final against Borussia Mönchengladbach, he saved Jupp Heynckes's penalty to preserve Liverpool's 3–0 lead. The return ended in a 2–0 defeat, leaving Liverpool winners by a single goal on aggregate. Clemence later explained that he had watched Heynckes take a penalty on television and used that preparation to choose his direction. The save belonged to the work done before the match as well as his reactions during it. [Liverpool's anniversary account](https://www.liverpoolfc.com/news/announcements/460577-50-years-ago-today-shankly-s-reds-clinch-club-s-first-european-trophy) sets out both the preparation and the narrow margin of victory.

## The goalkeeper Paisley inherited

Paisley's first league championship came in 1975–76, accompanied by another UEFA Cup. Clemence was in goal as Liverpool beat Club Brugge over the two legs of the final. These were familiar responsibilities under a different manager, with the goalkeeper carrying experience from Shankly's rebuilding into Paisley's expansion of the team's ambitions.

Availability made that continuity unusually dependable. Liverpool's records give Clemence 456 league appearances out of a possible 462 between 1970 and 1981. A manager could change partnerships, introduce a defender or adjust the players ahead of the back four without routinely having to replace the man behind them.

The European Cup final in Rome in 1977 provided the clearest single example of what his dependability could mean. Liverpool had led through Terry McDermott, but Allan Simonsen equalised for Mönchengladbach. With the score still 1–1, Uli Stielike had an opportunity to put the German side ahead. Clemence saved. Tommy Smith's header and Phil Neal's penalty subsequently gave Liverpool a 3–1 victory.

[UEFA's account of Liverpool's finals](https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/0275-15201acb75e7-c57250f46a12-1000--liverpool-and-real-madrid-s-finals/) places that intervention between the equaliser and Liverpool's recovery. It is a useful corrective to remembering the evening only through the goals: before Liverpool could win their first European Cup, Clemence had helped keep the match level.

## Retaining the standard

The following year's European campaign ended at Wembley against Brugge. Liverpool had overturned a first-leg semi-final defeat by Mönchengladbach with a 3–0 victory at Anfield. In the final, Kenny Dalglish scored the only goal. Clemence finished with a clean sheet, and Liverpool retained the trophy. The scoring names had changed since Rome; the goalkeeper had not.

Then came the sustained achievement of 1978–79. Clemence played all 42 league matches as Liverpool won the championship while conceding just 16 goals. He kept 28 league clean sheets. Phil Neal, Phil Thompson, Alan Hansen and Alan Kennedy formed the defence associated with that side, with Clemence behind them. It was a collective performance maintained over an entire season, requiring organisation and concentration as well as the saves that could be isolated in a match report.

Another title followed in 1979–80. In 1981 Liverpool won their first League Cup and reached a third European Cup final under Paisley. They eliminated Bayern Munich after a goalless first leg at Anfield and a 1–1 draw in Munich, progressing on away goals. Against Real Madrid in Paris, Alan Kennedy scored the winner. Clemence kept another clean sheet in the 1–0 victory. It was his last Liverpool appearance.

## A career beyond Liverpool

He joined Tottenham in August 1981 and won the FA Cup in his first season. Spurs also won the UEFA Cup in 1984, but Clemence was injured and an unused substitute for the final; that distinction belongs alongside the honour. He made 330 appearances for Tottenham before retiring in 1988.

For England, competition with Peter Shilton limited the opportunities available to either goalkeeper. Clemence nevertheless won 61 caps between 1972 and 1983. He later coached at Tottenham, managed Barnet and joined England's coaching staff in 1996, remaining with the FA until 2013. He died on 15 November 2020, aged 72.

The three European Cup finals remain an unusually clear measure of his Liverpool years: three starts, three victories, one goal conceded. Paisley's sides could change around him and still find the same goalkeeper ready for the next final.

Ray Clemence made 665 appearances for Liverpool, 470 of them in the league. He won five league championships and three European Cups, and LFChistory credits him with 323 clean sheets. Two of his most important saves came against Borussia Mönchengladbach, four years apart. The first helped Liverpool to their first European trophy. The second came with the score 1–1 in their first European Cup final.

## The wait

Clemence was born in Skegness on 5 August 1948. Bill Shankly signed him from Scunthorpe United in June 1967 for £18,000, when he was 18. Tommy Lawrence was the established goalkeeper, and Clemence had to wait.

His debut came on 25 September 1968, a 2–0 League Cup win over Swansea Town. It did not bring him regular football. His league debut followed at Nottingham Forest in January 1970, and by the end of that season he had displaced Lawrence as part of Shankly's rebuilt side.

Once he was in, he stayed in. From September 1972 to March 1978 he played 336 consecutive first-team matches, according to LFChistory.

## The first trophies

Liverpool lost the 1971 FA Cup final to Arsenal, and Clemence had to wait until 1973 for his first major honours. That season Liverpool won the league and the UEFA Cup.

In the first leg of the UEFA Cup final, Liverpool beat Mönchengladbach 3–0 at Anfield. Clemence saved a penalty from Jupp Heynckes to keep the margin at three, and denied the Germans an away goal. In Germany, Heynckes scored twice before half-time to cut the aggregate lead to one. Liverpool held on to win 3–2 overall.

A year later Liverpool beat Newcastle United 3–0 in the FA Cup final. Clemence's long kick-out started the move for Steve Heighway's goal. It was the last major trophy of Shankly's time as manager. Under Bob Paisley, Clemence stayed first choice, and Liverpool won another league and UEFA Cup double in 1976.

## A voice behind the defence

When Phil Thompson moved from midfield to centre-back alongside Emlyn Hughes, Clemence helped him make the change. Thompson later recalled, in an account published by the club, that Clemence talked him through matches and gave him instructions throughout. The young defender had someone behind him who could see danger coming and tell him where it was.

## Rome and Wembley

In the 1977 European Cup final in Rome, Mönchengladbach had drawn level at 1–1 when Uli Stielike had a chance to put them ahead. Clemence stopped him. Liverpool went on to win the European Cup for the first time.

A year later Kenny Dalglish scored the only goal against Club Brugge at Wembley, and Clemence had a second European Cup winner's medal.

## Sixteen goals

In 1978–79 Clemence played all 42 league matches. Liverpool conceded 16 goals and won the championship. They kept the title the following season, which gave him his fifth.

His last season at Liverpool brought the League Cup and a third European Cup. On 27 May 1981 Alan Kennedy scored the only goal against Real Madrid in Paris. It was Clemence's 665th appearance for the club, and he kept a clean sheet. He joined Tottenham Hotspur that summer.

## Tottenham

On 15 May 1982 Clemence returned to Anfield with Tottenham. A win would secure the league title for Liverpool, and they were losing at half-time. When Clemence came out for the second half and went to the Kop end, the crowd gave him a standing ovation. Liverpool won the match 3–1. "It's probably the most emotional I have ever been at a football ground," he said.

He made 330 appearances for Spurs and won the FA Cup with them in 1982. He was part of their 1984 UEFA Cup-winning squad, but injury kept him out of the final, in which he was an unused substitute.

## England

Clemence won 61 England caps between 1972 and 1983. Competition with Peter Shilton limited his chances. He was selected for the 1982 World Cup squad but did not play in the tournament.

## After playing

He retired in 1988 and moved into coaching and management, including a spell at Barnet. He joined England's coaching staff in 1996 and retired from the Football Association in 2013. He died on 15 November 2020, aged 72.

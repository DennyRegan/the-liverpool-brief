Bob Paisley bought Kenny Dalglish in August 1977, when Liverpool were already European champions and Kevin Keegan had left for Hamburg. Liverpool needed a forward who could replace Keegan without disturbing a winning side. Dalglish scored 31 goals in all competitions in his first season, and the most important was the winner in the European Cup final nine months after he signed. He left in 1990 with 172 goals in 515 appearances in all competitions, according to Liverpool's records.

## From Glasgow to Anfield

Born in Glasgow on 4 March 1951, Dalglish joined Celtic in 1967, made his first-team debut the following year and became captain in 1975. The Scottish Football Museum records four Scottish league titles and four Scottish Cups by the time Paisley signed him, so Liverpool were buying a proven winner rather than a prospect.

The £440,000 fee was a record between British clubs. Dalglish signed on 10 August 1977 and played against Manchester United in the Charity Shield three days later, a goalless draw. His first league game, at Middlesbrough, brought a goal after seven minutes. LFChistory records that he scored again on his Anfield debut, in a 2–0 win over Newcastle United.

## The goal at Wembley

His first season ended with Liverpool facing Club Brugge in the European Cup final on 10 May 1978. Liverpool had beaten Brugge in the 1976 UEFA Cup final, and the match at Wembley was goalless into the second half.

In the 64th minute, Graeme Souness played the pass that released Dalglish inside the penalty area. Dalglish lifted the ball over the advancing Birger Jensen and into the net. Liverpool won 1–0 and became the first British club to retain the trophy.

## League titles and the League Cup

The championship followed in 1978–79. Dalglish scored 21 league goals as Liverpool collected 68 points under the system of two points for a win, conceding only 16 goals in 42 matches. The football writers named him Footballer of the Year.

That September, Liverpool beat Tottenham 7–0 at Anfield. Dalglish scored twice, in the 8th and 20th minutes, and LFChistory credits Jimmy Case with the pass for both goals.

Liverpool retained the league title in 1980. The following season brought the League Cup: in the final replay at Villa Park, West Ham took the lead, Dalglish equalised and Alan Hansen scored the winner, a 2–1 victory that gave Liverpool the trophy for the first time. On 27 May 1981 Alan Kennedy's goal beat Real Madrid 1–0 in the European Cup final in Paris, Dalglish's second European Cup.

Liverpool won the league and League Cup in both 1982 and 1983. In the latter season Dalglish scored 18 league goals and won both the PFA Players' Player of the Year and the Football Writers' Association award. Paisley retired that summer, with Dalglish, then 32, still central to the side.

## Player-manager

Under Joe Fagan, Dalglish won another league title, League Cup and European Cup in 1984. He became player-manager in 1985, and his most important goal in that role came on the field. At Chelsea on 3 May 1986, in the 23rd minute, he chested down Jim Beglin's flick and volleyed past Tony Godden. The 1–0 win secured the championship. A week later he played in the FA Cup final victory over Everton, completing Liverpool's first Double.

His appearances became less frequent as management took precedence. His final Liverpool game was against Derby County at Anfield on 1 May 1990, when he came on as a substitute in a 1–0 win. He was 39.

Dalglish arrived in 1977 to replace Keegan and made his last appearance 13 years later. In between came three European Cups as a player and the 1986 Double as player-manager.

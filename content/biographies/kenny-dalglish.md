Bob Paisley bought Kenny Dalglish in August 1977, when Liverpool were already European champions. Kevin Keegan had left for Hamburg. Liverpool needed a forward who could take his place in a winning team without making the team begin again. Dalglish scored 31 goals in his first season, including the one that kept the European Cup at Anfield. Over the following years, his passing became as important to Liverpool as his finishing. [Liverpool's player profile](https://www.liverpoolfc.com/info/kenny-dalglish) records 172 goals in 515 appearances; those figures describe only part of his contribution.

## From Glasgow to Anfield

Born in Glasgow on 4 March 1951, Dalglish joined Celtic in 1967 and made his first-team debut the following year. He established himself in the side in 1971 and became captain in 1975. By the time Paisley signed him, he had won four Scottish league titles and four Scottish Cups. The [Scottish Football Museum's account](https://www.scottishfootballmuseum.org.uk/exhibitions/hall-of-fame/sir-kenny-dalglish/) makes clear that Liverpool were buying an accomplished international, rather than a prospect who needed to learn senior football.

The £440,000 fee was a record between British clubs. Dalglish signed on 10 August 1977 and played against Manchester United in the Charity Shield three days later. That match finished goalless. His first league appearance, at Middlesbrough, brought his first goal after seven minutes; he then scored against Newcastle United on his Anfield debut. [LFChistory's record](https://www.lfchistory.net/players/287) sets out that rapid introduction.

## The goal at Wembley

His first season ended with Liverpool facing Club Brugge in the European Cup final on 10 May 1978. Brugge had already lost to Liverpool in the 1976 UEFA Cup final. This time they tried to restrict the holders at Wembley, and the game remained goalless into the second half.

In the [64th minute](https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/0065-0e6a08688dbf-39a54804ad3c-1000--1977-78-dalglish-keeps-reds-on-top/), Graeme Souness supplied the pass that released Dalglish inside the penalty area. Dalglish lifted the ball over the advancing Birger Jensen and into the net. Liverpool won 1–0 and became the first British club to retain the trophy. Within nine months of arriving, Dalglish had supplied the decisive finish on Europe's biggest club stage.

## Winning the league

The championship followed in 1978–79. Dalglish scored 21 league goals as Liverpool collected 68 points under the system of two points for a win, conceding only 16 goals in 42 matches. He and David Johnson gave Paisley a productive attacking partnership. The football writers named Dalglish their Footballer of the Year.

There was a particularly clear demonstration of Liverpool's attacking range in the 7–0 defeat of Tottenham at Anfield that September. Dalglish scored twice. He also took part in the passing sequence that produced Johnson's goal, combining with Johnson and Souness as Liverpool moved through the defence. The club's [retrospective analysis of the match](https://www.liverpoolfc.com/news/first-team/121297-analysis-longest-90-minutes) describes the involvement of nine Liverpool players in that move. Dalglish could finish an attack or help others finish it; the two jobs belonged together.

Liverpool retained the league title in 1980. The following season brought a different kind of success. In the League Cup final replay at Villa Park, West Ham took the lead before Dalglish equalised and Alan Hansen scored the winner. Liverpool had won the competition for the first time. The club's [account of the final](https://www.liverpoolfc.com/news/first-team/115971-league-cup-final-memories-1981) records a 2–1 victory. A second European Cup for Dalglish followed against Real Madrid in Paris.

## With Ian Rush

The emergence of Ian Rush gave Dalglish a new partner and Paisley another way forward. Rush could attack the space beyond defenders while Dalglish supplied him from deeper positions. Dalglish remained a scorer, but Liverpool could now use his passing to release a younger forward whose movement offered a different threat.

Liverpool won the league and League Cup in both 1982 and 1983. In the latter season, Dalglish scored 18 league goals and won both the PFA Players' Player of the Year and the Football Writers' Association award. He was 32 when Paisley retired. The manager was leaving a side in which his 1977 signing was still central.

## The player who became manager

Under Joe Fagan, Dalglish added another league title, League Cup and European Cup in 1984. He became player-manager in 1985, but his decisive intervention the following spring came on the field. At Chelsea on 3 May 1986, he controlled Jim Beglin's lifted pass on his chest and volleyed past Tony Godden. The [1–0 victory secured the championship](https://www.liverpoolfc.com/news/first-team/118981-chelsea-memories-league-title-1986). A week later he played in the FA Cup final victory over Everton, completing the Double.

His appearances became less frequent as management took precedence. His final Liverpool game was against Derby County on [1 May 1990](https://www.lfchistory.net/games/1740), when he came on as a substitute at Anfield. He was 39.

Dalglish's playing career had connected Paisley's European champions to the side he eventually managed himself. The lasting footballing image is of a player with choices: able to take the shot, hold the ball or supply the runner. Liverpool benefited from all three.

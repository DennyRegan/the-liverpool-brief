Phil Neal was Bob Paisley's first signing as Liverpool manager and the only player to start all four of the club's European Cup final wins, in 1977, 1978, 1981 and 1984. He made 650 appearances and scored 59 goals for Liverpool and played for England 50 times. He scored in the finals of 1977 and 1984, and in 1984 he also converted a penalty in the shoot-out.

## From Northampton to Goodison

Born in Irchester, Northamptonshire, on 20 February 1951, Neal came through at Northampton Town, who were then in the Fourth Division. Sporting Heroes counts 186 league games for the club before Liverpool paid £66,000 for him in October 1974, when he was 23.

His debut came on 16 November 1974, a goalless derby at Goodison Park. He spent about 18 months mostly at left-back, according to Liverpool's own account of his career, before establishing himself on the right. Dave Bowen, who had spotted him as Northampton's manager and later backed the move as club secretary, drove him to Liverpool, as recorded by the Cobblers Past history site.

## The ever-present

Neal made 365 consecutive league appearances between December 1974 and September 1983, a club record. A separate run of 417 consecutive matches in all competitions began in October 1976 and ended in the same month, and LFChistory also lists it as a club record.

In December 1977 his penalties were the only goals in home league wins over Queens Park Rangers, on 17 December, and Wolverhampton Wanderers, on 27 December. In 1978–79 he played all 42 league matches as Liverpool won the championship and conceded just 16 goals.

## Rome, twice

Neal's part in the first European Cup began before the final. In the semi-final first leg at Zürich on 6 April 1977 he scored twice, once from the penalty spot, in a 3–1 win. Steve Heighway scored the other goal. A 3–0 win at Anfield two weeks later completed the tie.

In the final against Borussia Mönchengladbach, Terry McDermott put Liverpool ahead, Allan Simonsen equalised and Tommy Smith restored the lead. Neal's penalty made it 3–1 in the 82nd minute by LFChistory's count; UEFA's match report gives the 83rd.

He started the finals of 1978 and 1981, both won 1–0. When Liverpool reached Rome again in 1984, Joe Fagan had succeeded Paisley, but the right-back was still there. Neal opened the scoring after 14 minutes, and Roberto Pruzzo equalised in the 44th. The match finished 1–1 after extra time. In the shoot-out Steve Nicol missed Liverpool's first kick, Neal scored the second, and Liverpool won 4–2.

## Honours and Bolton

LFChistory credits Neal with eight league titles, four European Cups, four League Cups, the UEFA Cup and the European Super Cup. The eighth league title came in 1985–86, when he made 13 league appearances before leaving.

He joined Bolton Wanderers as player-manager in December 1985. According to Bolton's own account, he played regularly in his first two seasons there and concentrated increasingly on management from 1987–88. No other player started all four of Liverpool's European Cup final wins, and Neal scored in the first and the last of them.

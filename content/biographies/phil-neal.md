Phil Neal was Bob Paisley's first signing as Liverpool manager. He became the one player who appeared in all four of the club's European Cup-winning finals between 1977 and 1984. That connection between the beginning of Paisley's management and its lasting achievements gives Neal a particular place in Liverpool's history. He was present for the great occasions, but his contribution depended just as much on the matches between them: defending, supporting attacks and making himself available again.

## From Northampton to Goodison

Born in Irchester, Northamptonshire, on 20 February 1951, Neal began his senior career with Northampton Town in 1968. He had substantial Football League experience before Liverpool bought him at 23. Northampton were then in the Fourth Division, a considerable distance from the European football he would soon encounter. The move was a promotion in standards and expectations, rather than an introduction to the demands of first-team football.

Liverpool paid £66,000 in October 1974. His debut came the following month, in a goalless Merseyside derby at Goodison Park. He initially played mainly at left-back, taking the place previously occupied by Alec Lindsay, before establishing himself on his preferred right. [LFChistory's career record](https://lfchistory.net/players/387) traces that early adjustment. The player remembered for Liverpool's number two shirt had first demonstrated that he could serve the team on either flank.

His Northampton education mattered. Dave Bowen, who had managed him there, recalled in [Neal's testimonial brochure](https://cobblerspast.com/north-from-northampton/) recognising his promise as a schoolboy and later backing his readiness to move. Neal was therefore a player whose development had been watched over several years. Paisley was buying a footballer with a grounding in senior competition, even if his name carried little of the prestige attached to an established First Division signing.

## A place Liverpool could depend on

The two great records of Neal's durability measure different things. He made 365 consecutive league appearances between December 1974 and September 1983. Within that longer league sequence came a run of 417 consecutive matches in all competitions, from October 1976 to September 1983. [Liverpool's profile](https://www.liverpoolfc.com/info/phil-neal) records the first; the club's [50th-anniversary account of his signing](https://www.liverpoolfc.com/news/50-years-ago-story-begins-liverpool-fcs-most-decorated-player) confirms the second.

The value of such continuity was practical. Paisley could change players elsewhere without repeatedly rebuilding the right side of his defence. Neal still had to merit selection: a long run required more than fitness. His place endured as Liverpool moved from their first successes under Paisley to a team with different forwards, midfielders and central defenders. Availability helped make him a constant; his usefulness kept him there.

There was an attacking dimension too. In December 1977, his penalties supplied the only goals in home league victories over Queens Park Rangers and Wolverhampton Wanderers. These were small, decisive contributions to ordinary league afternoons, recorded in [Sporting Heroes' career account](https://www.sporting-heroes.net/football/liverpool-fc/phil-neal-9748/biography-of-his-playing-career-at-liverpool-football-club_a11981/). In 1978–79 he played all 42 league matches as Liverpool won the championship while conceding just 16 goals. The full-back who took responsibility from the spot remained part of an exceptionally effective defence.

## Two goals on the way to Rome

Neal's part in the first European Cup extended beyond the final. In the semi-final first leg at Zürich in April 1977, he scored twice, once from the penalty spot, in Liverpool's 3–1 victory. Steve Heighway scored the other goal. A 3–0 win at Anfield completed the job, but Neal's contribution away had already given Liverpool a substantial advantage. The [campaign record](https://www.lfchistory.net/articles/2435) places him among the important scorers on the road to Rome.

Against Borussia Mönchengladbach in the final, Terry McDermott put Liverpool ahead, Allan Simonsen equalised and Tommy Smith restored the lead. Neal's penalty made it 3–1. [UEFA's account](https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/01d9-0e7236247719-6a5bb4786213-1000--neal-remembers-1977-victory-over-borussia/) records the kick in the 82nd minute. It gave Liverpool breathing space near the end of the match that brought their first European Cup. A defender had supplied the last goal of the campaign, just as he had supplied two in its semi-final.

Neal returned for the successful finals of 1978 and 1981. When Liverpool reached Rome again in 1984, Joe Fagan had succeeded Paisley, but the right-back was still there. This time Neal opened the scoring against Roma from close range. Roberto Pruzzo equalised, and the match finished 1–1 after extra time. Neal then converted Liverpool's second kick in the shoot-out, following Steve Nicol's miss. Liverpool won it 4–2. [UEFA's final retrospective](https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/00ad-0e6a0b25af45-1d23225afb81-1000--1983-84-kennedy-spot-on-for-liverpool/) records the fourth triumph: Neal had contributed both a goal during play and a successful penalty afterwards.

## Beyond Paisley's team

His Liverpool totals reached 650 appearances and 59 goals. His honours included eight league titles, four European Cups, four League Cups, the UEFA Cup and the European Super Cup. The eighth came in 1985–86, a season he began at Liverpool before leaving for Bolton. He also represented England 50 times.

Neal joined Bolton Wanderers as player-manager in December 1985. According to [Bolton's account](https://www.bwfc.co.uk/news/2020/may/from-player-to-manager-phil-neal), he played regularly during his first two seasons before increasingly concentrating on management from 1987–88. The later work took him beyond the role for which he remains best remembered. At Liverpool, Paisley's first purchase had become a player successive teams could rely upon, with a place in four European triumphs that belonged to him alone.

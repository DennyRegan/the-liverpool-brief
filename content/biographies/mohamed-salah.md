Mohamed Salah scored 257 goals in 442 games for Liverpool. Only Ian Rush and Roger Hunt scored more. The club also credits him with 120 assists. He was the leading scorer in the team that won the Champions League in 2019 and the league in 2020. Then, under a different manager, he was the outstanding player in the team that won the league again in 2025.

## The long way round

Salah was born in Basyoun, Egypt, on 15 June 1992. He started at El Mokawloon, moved to Basel and then to Chelsea, where he hardly played. He rebuilt his career in Italy, first on loan at Fiorentina and then at Roma, where he scored 29 Serie A goals in 65 games over two seasons. Liverpool announced the deal on 22 June 2017, and it went through on 1 July. Roma said the fee was €42 million, with up to €8 million more in add-ons. He was 25.

Jürgen Klopp put him on the right of a front three with Roberto Firmino in the middle and Sadio Mané on the left. Salah started wide but played like a striker. He ran into the space behind defenders, and when they dropped off he came inside onto his left foot and shot. Defenders knew what he wanted to do, but he did it so quickly that they could rarely stop it.

## The first season

He scored 44 goals in all competitions in 2017–18, more than any Liverpool player had managed in his first season and second only to Rush's 47 in 1983–84. His 32 league goals were a Premier League record for a 38-game season at the time.

The Champions League semi-final first leg against his old club, Roma, on 24 April 2018 showed everything he could do. Firmino set up both of his goals: a curled shot into the top corner and a chip over Alisson. Salah then made goals for Mané and Firmino in a 5–2 win.

The season ended badly. In the final against Real Madrid in Kyiv he hurt his shoulder in a challenge with Sergio Ramos and went off in the first half. Liverpool lost 3–1.

## Champions of Europe, champions of England

He scored 22 league goals in 2018–19 and shared the Golden Boot with Mané and Arsenal's Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang. Liverpool finished with 97 points and were still second to Manchester City. Concussion, suffered at Newcastle, kept him out of the 4–0 win over Barcelona in the semi-final second leg. In the final against Tottenham in Madrid he scored a penalty in the second minute, and Divock Origi's late goal made it 2–0. It was Liverpool's sixth European Cup.

The Super Cup and the Club World Cup followed in 2019. In 2019–20 he scored 19 league goals as Liverpool reached 99 points and won the title for the first time in 30 years.

He kept scoring when the team struggled. Liverpool's title defence in 2020–21 was wrecked by injuries, but he scored 22 league goals and they recovered to finish third.

## October 2021

On 3 October 2021, against Manchester City at Anfield, he ran at the defence and set up Mané for the opener. Later he took the ball on the right, dribbled past several players and finished across Ederson with his right foot. The game ended 2–2, and the goal was voted the Premier League's goal of the season. Three weeks later he scored a hat-trick in a 5–0 win at Old Trafford, the first visiting player to score three there in the Premier League.

Liverpool chased four trophies that season and won two, the League Cup and the FA Cup. Salah's 23 league goals earned him a share of the Golden Boot with Son Heung-min, and his 13 assists won him the Playmaker award. Liverpool finished on 92 points, one behind City. In the Champions League final in Paris, Thibaut Courtois kept him out several times, and Real Madrid won 1–0.

## Records

The team declined in 2022–23 and missed out on the Champions League, but Salah still scored 30 goals. At Ibrox in October he came off the bench and scored a hat-trick in six minutes and 12 seconds, the fastest in Champions League history, as Liverpool beat Rangers 7–1. In March his two goals in the 7–0 win over Manchester United took him past Robbie Fowler as the club's record Premier League scorer.

In 2023–24 he injured a hamstring playing for Egypt at the Africa Cup of Nations. He came back and scored at Brentford in February, then picked up another muscle injury and missed the League Cup final, which Liverpool won against Chelsea. Klopp left that summer.

## Slot's champions

Under Arne Slot in 2024–25 he had a season to rival his first. He scored 29 league goals and set up 18 as Liverpool won the title. His 47 goals and assists equalled the Premier League record, set by Andy Cole and Alan Shearer in 42-game seasons. He won his fourth Golden Boot, matching Thierry Henry, and became the first player to win the Golden Boot, the Playmaker award and the Player of the Season award in the same season. He signed a new contract in April 2025.

He was the first player to win the PFA Players' Player of the Year award three times, in 2018, 2022 and 2025, and he was the Football Writers' Footballer of the Year in the same three years. He scored at least 20 goals in eight consecutive seasons, from 2017–18 to 2024–25, a club record. He scored more goals in Europe than any other Liverpool player and more against Manchester United too. CAF named him African Player of the Year in 2017 and 2018.

## The end

His last season was much harder. He fell out publicly with Slot and scored 12 goals, his lowest total for the club. On 18 March 2026 he scored his 50th Champions League goal, against Galatasaray, the first African player to reach the mark. Six days later the club announced that he would leave that summer, a year before his contract ran out.

His last game was at home to Brentford on 24 May 2026. He set up Curtis Jones's goal in a 1–1 draw. It was his 93rd Premier League assist for Liverpool, one more than Steven Gerrard's club record. Liverpool finished fifth, enough for a Champions League place. In August he joined Trabzonspor in Turkey on a two-year contract.

He left as Liverpool's leading Premier League scorer, with 191 goals, and their leading provider of Premier League assists. He won eight major honours: two league titles, the Champions League, the Super Cup, the Club World Cup, the FA Cup and two League Cups.

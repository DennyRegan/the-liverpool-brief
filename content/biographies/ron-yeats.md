Ron Yeats made 454 appearances for Liverpool and scored 16 goals. More than 400 of those games were as captain. Bill Shankly signed him in July 1961, when Liverpool were still in the Second Division. Within four years Yeats had led them to promotion, the championship of England and the club's first FA Cup. He was the first Liverpool captain to receive the trophy. In 1986 he came back to Anfield as chief scout and stayed for 20 years.

## Aberdeen and Dundee United

Yeats was born in Aberdeen on 15 November 1937. He came through Aberdeen Lads Club and served an apprenticeship as a slaughterman. He joined Dundee United late in 1957.

He did his national service while he was at United. The manager, Jerry Kerr, secured his release from military duties whenever possible, and Yeats travelled back to play. That arrangement mattered in 1959–60, United's promotion season, when Yeats played in almost every match. He followed it with a season in Scotland's top division.

## Shankly's "colossus"

Shankly signed him in July 1961, the same summer Ian St John arrived from Motherwell. The two Scots gave the team strength at either end of the pitch. Shankly's description of Yeats as a "colossus" became attached to his name for good.

Promotion had eluded Liverpool for several seasons before they arrived. Yeats played 41 league games in 1961–62 and took over the captaincy during that first season. Liverpool won the Second Division championship. In 1963–64 they won the First Division, four points ahead of Manchester United.

Yeats was modest about his own game. In a later interview with LFChistory he talked about his left foot, his ability to read the game and the way he and Tommy Smith complemented each other. Smith supplied the right side. "He was my right foot and I was his left," Yeats said.

## Europe and the all-red kit

The title took Liverpool into European football. Shankly wanted to change the team's appearance, and Yeats modelled the red shorts. Liverpool wore them with red shirts and white socks against Anderlecht at Anfield on 25 November 1964. Yeats was among the scorers in a 3–0 win. Red socks completed the kit for the return match in Belgium on 16 December.

The European Cup quarter-final against Cologne went to a third game. After two goalless matches, a replay in Rotterdam in March 1965 finished 2–2 after extra time, and the tie was settled by a toss. Yeats recalled that the first attempt left the coin standing in the turf and that the second fell Liverpool's way. Accounts differ over the exact object used. Liverpool went through to the semi-finals in their first European season.

## Wembley, 1965

Liverpool had lost their two previous FA Cup finals, in 1914 and 1950. On 1 May 1965 they played Leeds United at Wembley. It was goalless after 90 minutes. In extra time Roger Hunt scored, Billy Bremner equalised and St John headed in Ian Callaghan's cross to make it 2–1. Yeats became the first Liverpool captain to receive the FA Cup, four years after joining a Second Division club.

## Champions again, and Hampden

Yeats played in all 42 league matches in 1965–66 as Liverpool won the title again. He won two Scotland caps, in 1964 and 1965.

On 5 May 1966 he captained the side against Borussia Dortmund in the Cup Winners' Cup final at Hampden Park. Hunt cancelled out Siegfried Held's opening goal, and the match went to extra time. Reinhard Libuda's shot hit the post and went into the net off Yeats as he tried to get back. Most records credit Libuda with the goal. Dortmund won 2–1, and Liverpool's first European final ended in defeat.

## The later years

No major trophy followed in the next four seasons, but Yeats made at least 48 competitive appearances in each of them.

The FA Cup defeat at Watford in February 1970 showed that the team needed rebuilding, and Yeats was among the established players who were gradually replaced. He had back trouble, and Larry Lloyd offered a younger option in central defence. Yeats played only 16 times in 1970–71.

## Tranmere and beyond

He left for Tranmere Rovers late in 1971, with 454 appearances and 16 goals for Liverpool. At Tranmere he became player-manager. His side beat Arsenal 1–0 at Highbury in the League Cup in October 1973.

Later playing spells took him into non-league football and to the United States.

## Chief scout

In 1986 Kenny Dalglish brought Yeats back to Liverpool as chief scout. He held the job until 2006.

John Aldridge later said of him: "He actually scouted me when I was at Oxford United."

Sami Hyypia was another of his recommendations. Yeats explained that he went to watch the defender after a tip, liked his performance and reported that "either the boss or a coach should go over and see this man."

Yeats died on 6 September 2024, aged 86, after living with Alzheimer's disease.

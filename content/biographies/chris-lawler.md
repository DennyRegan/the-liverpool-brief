Chris Lawler played 549 games for Liverpool and scored 61 goals, most of them as a right-back who did not take penalties or set pieces. He won the FA Cup in 1965, the league in 1966 and 1973, and the UEFA Cup in 1973. Between October 1965 and April 1971 he played 316 consecutive matches. He was known as the Silent Knight, and he played through both Bill Shankly's first great side and the one that replaced it.

## A local boy

Lawler was born in Liverpool on 20 October 1943, into a family of Liverpool supporters. He had represented England Schoolboys, and in 1959 he joined the club's groundstaff, a few months before Shankly arrived as manager. The job meant work around the stadium, with training alongside the amateurs in the evenings.

According to LFChistory, Shankly asked him about his day and changed the arrangement. "Tomorrow morning you're going training at ten o'clock with the first team," he told him. Lawler signed professional forms on his 17th birthday, 20 October 1960.

## Behind Ron Yeats

He started as a centre-half, which meant waiting behind the captain, Ron Yeats. His debut came on 20 March 1963, a 2–2 draw with West Bromwich Albion at Anfield. He wore No. 5, with Gerry Byrne and Ronnie Moran at full-back. A match report of the time praised his heading and his passing.

He made six league appearances that season and six more in 1963–64, when Liverpool won the title. He saw no future behind Yeats and asked Shankly for a transfer. Shankly's answer was to move him. With Moran's career near its end, Byrne switched to left-back and Lawler was tried on the right.

His first goal came in a 5–1 win at Burnley on 5 December 1964. It was Liverpool's fifth of the afternoon. Lawler wore No. 2, Byrne No. 3 and Yeats was still at centre-half.

## Wembley and Hampden

By the end of 1964–65 he was a regular. On 1 May 1965 he started the FA Cup final against Leeds United at Wembley. The game was goalless after 90 minutes. Roger Hunt scored in extra time, Billy Bremner equalised, and Ian St John headed the winner from Ian Callaghan's cross. Liverpool won 2–1 and lifted the FA Cup for the first time.

Lawler played 40 league games as Liverpool won the title in 1965–66. That season also brought the European Cup Winners' Cup final against Borussia Dortmund at Hampden Park on 5 May 1966. Siegfried Held scored for Dortmund, Hunt equalised, and Stan Libuda won it 2–1 in extra time. Lawler started in defence.

## How he played

Yeats later remembered him as a good passer, good in the air and a player who read the game very well.

His goals came from timing. He said he only went forward when Liverpool needed a goal, usually for corners when they were losing. When he did, Callaghan or Tommy Smith dropped back to cover him. Callaghan recalled that he could turn up anywhere opponents did not expect. Bob Paisley said he had the "brain of a striker". His best season was 1969–70, with 11 goals in all competitions.

He was also nearly always available. The 316-game run lasted from 2 October 1965 to 24 April 1971. In the eight league seasons from 1965–66 to 1972–73 he missed three league games in total.

## The derby

Liverpool waited seven years between the titles of 1966 and 1973. In that time Yeats, St John, Hunt and Tommy Lawrence gave way to Ray Clemence, Larry Lloyd, Emlyn Hughes, Steve Heighway, John Toshack and Kevin Keegan. Lawler stayed in the side with Smith and Callaghan.

On 21 November 1970 Everton led 2–0 at Anfield through second-half goals from Alan Whittle and Joe Royle. Heighway pulled one back and Toshack, with his first goal for the club, made it 2–2. In the closing stages Alec Lindsay took a free-kick, Toshack headed it on, and Lawler struck the winner past Andy Rankin. Liverpool won 3–2. The *Liverpool Echo* reported that Everton's penalty area was suddenly full of red shirts.

## Arsenal, and England

He started the 1971 FA Cup final against Arsenal at Wembley on 8 May. Heighway put Liverpool ahead early in extra time, but Eddie Kelly equalised and Charlie George scored the winner. Arsenal won 2–1.

Four days later, on 12 May 1971, Lawler made his England debut against Malta at Wembley. England won 5–0 and he scored the fifth goal. He won four caps, all in 1971, and that was his only international goal.

## Champions again

In 1972–73 Liverpool won the league and the UEFA Cup, the club's first European trophy. Lawler played in all 66 of their competitive matches.

The UEFA Cup final was played over two legs against Borussia Mönchengladbach. At Anfield on 10 May 1973, Keegan scored twice and Lloyd once in a 3–0 win, and Clemence saved a penalty from Jupp Heynckes. In the second leg on 23 May, Heynckes scored twice before half-time. Liverpool held on to win the tie 3–2, and the club's own account of the final singles out Lawler's defending. He had played in Liverpool's defeat at Hampden seven years earlier and now had a European winner's medal.

## Injury

His last Liverpool goal came against Red Star Belgrade at Anfield on 6 November 1973, in the second leg of a European Cup second-round tie. Red Star led through Vojin Lazarević before Lawler, set up by Toshack, equalised in the second half, but the visitors scored again and won 2–1. Liverpool went out 4–2 on aggregate.

Later that month he injured his knee against Queens Park Rangers. He waited five weeks for a cartilage operation and did not get back to his old fitness. Shankly named him as substitute for the 1974 FA Cup final against Newcastle United, but he did not come on. Smith played at right-back, Keegan scored twice and Heighway once, and Liverpool won 3–0. Lawler received a winner's medal.

## Leaving Anfield

Shankly retired that summer. Under Bob Paisley, Lawler made 16 appearances in 1974–75 and one in 1975–76. That last game was a 1–0 defeat by Hibernian at Easter Road in the UEFA Cup on 17 September 1975. Phil Neal also played.

In October 1975 he joined Portsmouth, managed by his former team-mate St John. He later had a loan spell with Miami Toros and played for Stockport County and Bangor City. In 1980 he became player-manager of Raufoss in Norway.

His record for Liverpool stands at 549 appearances and 61 goals, including 406 league games and 41 league goals. He came back to the club in 1982 to coach the reserve team.

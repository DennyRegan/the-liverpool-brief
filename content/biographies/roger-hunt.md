Roger Hunt scored 285 goals in 492 competitive games for Liverpool. He scored 41 in 41 league matches as they won promotion from the Second Division in 1961–62, then helped win the First Division twice and the club's first FA Cup. He was Liverpool's leading scorer in eight successive seasons. In 1966 he played in all six of England's games at the World Cup and won a winner's medal. Supporters called him "Sir Roger", though he was never knighted.

## From Stockton Heath

Hunt was born near Warrington on 20 July 1938. Bill Jones, a former Liverpool player, spotted him playing amateur football for Stockton Heath. Phil Taylor signed him in July 1958, initially as an amateur, and he turned professional the following year.

His debut came at Anfield on 9 September 1959, against Scunthorpe United in the Second Division. Liverpool won 2–0 and Hunt scored. Bill Shankly arrived as manager a few months later and rebuilt the side. Hunt was one of the players he inherited and kept.

Hunt was strong, quick and willing to work. He set out to improve from the start. "From the first day, I threw myself into training, ran and tackled for everything," he told LFChistory. He also practised his ball skills at every opportunity.

## Promotion with St John

Ian St John joined in 1961, and the two became Liverpool's best-known attacking partnership of the decade. In 1961–62 Hunt scored 41 goals in 41 league games, and Liverpool won the Second Division title.

That season began a run in which he was Liverpool's leading scorer eight seasons in a row, from 1961–62 to 1968–69. It covered promotion, the trophies that followed and the later years of Shankly's first great team.

## Champions

Liverpool won the First Division in 1963–64, two seasons after promotion. Hunt scored 31 league goals. When they won it again in 1965–66, he scored 29, which made him joint top scorer in the First Division with Willie Irvine. Both figures are league goals only; his goals in the cups came on top.

On 22 August 1964, at the start of the 1964–65 season, Liverpool beat Arsenal 3–2. Hunt scored the opening goal, the first ever shown on the BBC's Match of the Day.

## Wembley, 1965

Liverpool had never won the FA Cup. In the final at Wembley on 1 May 1965, they and Leeds United were goalless after 90 minutes. Three minutes into extra time, Hunt broke the deadlock with a stooping header from Gerry Byrne's cross. Billy Bremner equalised for Leeds, and St John headed the winner. Liverpool won 2–1.

## Hampden, 1966

A year later Liverpool reached a European final, in the Cup Winners' Cup against Borussia Dortmund at Hampden Park. Dortmund scored first and Hunt equalised. Dortmund won 2–1 in extra time. Liverpool's first European trophy came after Hunt had left.

## England

Hunt won his first England cap under Walter Winterbottom on 4 April 1962, while Liverpool were still in the Second Division. He scored in a 3–1 win over Austria at Wembley. He went to that summer's World Cup as a squad member but did not play.

Four years later Alf Ramsey made him a regular. Hunt played in all six games as England won the 1966 World Cup and scored three goals, all in the group stage. Against Mexico, the goalkeeper pushed away a shot from Jimmy Greaves and Hunt scored from the rebound to make it 2–0, which was the final score. He finished his England career with 18 goals in 34 appearances.

## The club record

Gordon Hodgson's total of 241 goals had been the club record. Hunt scored his 242nd against Manchester United on 11 November 1967. He finished with 285, 244 of them in the league, according to the club. Ian Rush overtook his overall total on 18 October 1992.

## Leaving Anfield

On 3 March 1969, in an FA Cup fifth-round replay against Leicester City at Anfield, Hunt was taken off with 17 minutes left for Bobby Graham and threw his shirt towards the dugout. During 1969–70 he lost his regular place.

In December 1969 he joined Bolton Wanderers, the club he had supported as a boy. Nat Lofthouse signed him. The move was still hard. His daughter Julie later told the club that in his first few weeks at Bolton he would have given anything to go back. Liverpool supporters kept writing to him after he left.

Hunt spent three seasons at Bolton and scored 24 goals in 76 league games. He was their leading scorer in 1970–71, the season they were relegated to the Third Division. He also had a brief loan spell with Hellenic in South Africa.

## The testimonial

Hunt came back to Anfield for his testimonial on 11 April 1972, when Liverpool played an England XI. More than 55,000 attended, and Liverpool won 8–6. Julie recalled that he had worried the supporters might have forgotten him, and that he was amazed and choked up at how many turned out.

## After football

Hunt worked in the family haulage business, Hunt Brothers, with his brother Peter. He was made an MBE in 2000, and in 2006 he was inducted into the National Football Museum's Hall of Fame. He died on 27 September 2021, aged 83.

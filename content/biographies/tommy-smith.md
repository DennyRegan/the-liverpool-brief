Tommy Smith made 638 appearances for Liverpool and scored 48 goals. He played in the team that won the club's first FA Cup in 1965. He was captain when Liverpool won their first European trophy in 1973. In Rome in 1977 he headed the goal that put them back in front in their first European Cup final. He won four league titles and two FA Cups. He was known as the "Anfield Iron", which became the title of his autobiography. Shankly is said to have remarked that Smith "wasn't born, he was quarried".

## From the groundstaff

Smith was born in Liverpool on 5 April 1945 and joined the club in 1960. He made his first-team debut against Birmingham City at Anfield on 8 May 1963, replacing the injured Jimmy Melia in the side. Liverpool won 5–1.

He did not play a senior game the following season, when Liverpool won the league. At the start of 1964–65 he played up front, scoring twice in his first five games, before moving to centre-half in December 1964. He made 25 league appearances that season.

## Wembley, 1965

Liverpool reached the FA Cup final against Leeds United on 1 May 1965. Smith was 20. Ron Yeats was captain, and Roger Hunt and Ian St John were in the attack. Gerry Byrne played on with a broken collarbone.

The game was goalless after 90 minutes. Hunt scored early in extra time, Billy Bremner equalised for Leeds, and St John's header won it 2–1. It was Liverpool's first FA Cup.

## Ever-present

In 1965–66 Smith played all 42 league games as Liverpool won the title. He was ever-present in the league again in 1966–67 and 1968–69, and played 36 league games in the season between.

## Captain

As Shankly rebuilt the side, Smith was made captain in March 1970.

He captained Liverpool against Arsenal at Wembley on 8 May 1971. Steve Heighway put them ahead early in extra time, but Eddie Kelly and Charlie George scored for Arsenal, who won 2–1.

Two years later Liverpool won the league and the UEFA Cup. They beat Borussia Mönchengladbach 3–0 at Anfield in the first leg of the final. In the second leg, on 23 May 1973, Jupp Heynckes scored twice before half-time, but Liverpool held on to win 3–2 on aggregate. Smith became the first Liverpool captain to lift a European trophy.

## Losing the armband

In November 1973 Smith was left out of the team to face Arsenal at Highbury. He argued with Shankly before kick-off, and soon afterwards Emlyn Hughes replaced him as captain.

He stayed in the side and played more at full-back. In the 1974 FA Cup final against Newcastle United on 4 May he wore the No. 2 shirt, with Hughes as captain. He and Ian Callaghan were the only players in the starting line-up who had also started the 1965 final.

Kevin Keegan scored in the 57th minute and Heighway added a second in the 75th. For the third goal, two minutes from time, Smith advanced on the right and sent the ball across for Keegan to finish. Liverpool won 3–0.

## Under Paisley

Shankly retired in July 1974 and Bob Paisley took over. Liverpool won the league and the UEFA Cup again in 1975–76. Smith made 24 league appearances that season.

In 1976–77 he played only six games between August and March and had planned to retire at the end of the season. When Phil Thompson was injured in March, Smith came back into the side and played every remaining game. Liverpool won the league. On 21 May they lost the FA Cup final 2–1 to Manchester United, four days before the European Cup final.

## Rome

Liverpool met Borussia Mönchengladbach in Rome on 25 May 1977. Smith was 32. Terry McDermott scored in the first half and Allan Simonsen equalised after the break. In the 64th minute Heighway took a corner from the left and Smith headed it in to make it 2–1. Phil Neal scored a penalty eight minutes from time, and Liverpool won 3–1 to become European champions for the first time.

Two days later Smith's testimonial was played at Anfield, and the European Cup was paraded before the crowd, according to LFChistory. He put off his retirement.

## The last season

Smith stayed for 1977–78 and made 22 league appearances, according to LFChistory. Liverpool beat Hamburg 7–1 on aggregate in the European Super Cup and reached the European Cup final again. Smith missed it. He broke a toe in an accident at home in April 1978. Liverpool beat Club Brugge 1–0 at Wembley without him.

## Swansea and England

Smith left Liverpool in August 1978 and joined his former team-mate John Toshack at Swansea City. In 1978–79 he made 43 appearances and scored twice as Swansea won promotion from the Third Division. He retired in 1979.

He won one England cap, in a 0–0 draw with Wales at Wembley on 19 May 1971.

## Later years

In his 2016 autobiography, 61 Minutes in Munich, Howard Gayle said Smith had racially abused him in training.

Smith died on 12 April 2019, aged 74, after living with dementia.

Kenny Dalglish said Smith and Graeme Souness had looked after him and his wife, Marina, and helped them settle when he moved to Liverpool. Phil Thompson remembered watching Smith before they were team-mates. "I loved him from the Kop, because of his passion, commitment and never-say-die attitude," he told Sky Sports.

Smith's Liverpool honours were four league titles, in 1966, 1973, 1976 and 1977, two FA Cups, two UEFA Cups and the European Cup.

Ian St John scored 118 goals in 425 appearances for Liverpool. He joined from Motherwell in May 1961, when Liverpool were in the Second Division, for a club-record £37,500. In ten years at Anfield he won promotion, two league titles and the FA Cup. His header against Leeds United at Wembley in 1965 won that cup for the first time in the club's history.

## Motherwell

St John was born in Motherwell on 7 June 1938. As a boy he watched his home-town club win the Scottish Cup in 1952, beating Dundee 4–0. He later played for them as one of the Ancell Babes, the young side built by Bobby Ancell, and scored 80 goals in 113 league games.

In 1959 he scored a League Cup hat-trick against Hibernian in about two and a half minutes. The same year, on 6 May, he won his first Scotland cap, against West Germany at Hampden Park.

## Shankly's signing

By St John's own account, Bill Shankly had seen him and Ron Yeats play while managing Huddersfield Town. Liverpool signed St John on 2 May 1961. The fee was more than double the club's previous record, the Scotsman later noted. Yeats followed on 22 July.

A week after signing, on 9 May, St John scored all three Liverpool goals in the Liverpool Senior Cup final at Goodison Park. Everton won 4–3. His league debut came on 19 August 1961.

Shankly was sure of him. "He's not just a good centre-forward, he's the only centre-forward in the game," he said, according to LFChistory.

## Promotion and the title

St John scored 18 league goals in his first season, and 22 in the league and FA Cup together. Liverpool won the Second Division. He played alongside Roger Hunt, and the pair shared the goals while St John also made chances for others.

He was not a tall centre-forward, but he was strong in the air. His timing let him outjump much taller defenders.

He had a temper too. In an interview with LFChistory he said: "I had a quick temper which was a bad thing." He recalled being sent off at Coventry and at Fulham. LFChistory records three league sendings-off in total.

In the First Division he kept scoring. He got 19 league goals in 1962–63 and 21 in 1963–64, when Liverpool won the championship.

## Wembley, 1965

Liverpool met Leeds United in the FA Cup final on 1 May 1965. It was 0–0 after 90 minutes. Gerry Byrne played on with a broken collarbone and crossed for Hunt to score early in extra time. Billy Bremner equalised. Late in extra time Ian Callaghan crossed and St John headed past Gary Sprake to win the match 2–1. It was Liverpool's first FA Cup.

## Europe

Liverpool played in the European Cup for the first time in 1964–65, and St John scored five goals in the competition. Liverpool won the first leg of their opening tie 5–0 in Reykjavik on 17 August 1964. He did not score that night. He scored twice in the 6–1 second leg against KR Reykjavik at Anfield on 14 September, the club's first home European tie. He also scored against Anderlecht and Cologne.

Three days after Wembley, on 4 May 1965, Liverpool beat Inter Milan 3–1 at Anfield in the first leg of the semi-final. St John scored the third goal, according to This Is Anfield, after Hunt's shot hit the goalkeeper. Liverpool lost the second leg 3–0 in Milan and went out.

In 1965–66 Liverpool were champions again, and St John scored ten league goals. They also reached the European Cup Winners' Cup final, where they lost 2–1 after extra time to Borussia Dortmund at Hampden Park on 5 May 1966.

## A deeper role

In the later 1960s St John dropped back into midfield. He stayed a regular through the late 1960s, with 49, 56, 50 and 36 appearances in the four seasons from 1966–67.

Shankly eventually dropped him without telling him to his face. The Scotsman dates it to autumn 1969. The game was at Newcastle. St John was talking to Jackie Milburn, by then a journalist, when Milburn was handed the team sheet, the Irish Times reported. The rift never fully healed, according to the Scotsman. "He let me down and he did it with other players as well," St John told LFChistory.

He made only three appearances in 1970–71. The last was on 23 January 1971, and he scored in it. It was his final Liverpool goal. He left the club on 18 May 1971.

## After Anfield

St John played for Hellenic in South Africa in 1971, then joined Coventry City that August. He moved on to Tranmere Rovers, where Yeats was manager. He won 21 Scotland caps by LFChistory's count and scored nine goals. The Scottish FA counts 20, leaving out a substitute appearance.

He went into management with Motherwell in 1973. There he brought Bobby Graham back from Liverpool and gave the young Willie Pettigrew his debut. He left for Portsmouth after little more than a year.

From 1985 to 1992 he presented *Saint and Greavsie* on ITV with Jimmy Greaves.

In May 2015 Liverpool gave St John and Yeats lifetime achievement awards at the club's Players' Awards. Looking back that year, St John said: "The team we came down and joined were then made out of kids from the reserves."

He died on 1 March 2021, aged 82.

Bob Paisley managed Liverpool for nine seasons, from 1974 to 1983, and won six league championships, three European Cups, the UEFA Cup and three League Cups. He was the first manager to win the European Cup three times. By the time he took charge he had already spent 35 years at Anfield, as a player, trainer, physiotherapist and coach. He took over a team built by Bill Shankly, replaced its most important players and kept winning.

## Durham to Anfield

Paisley was born in Hetton-le-Hole, County Durham, on 23 January 1919. He joined Liverpool from Bishop Auckland in May 1939. The war interrupted his career before he could establish himself, and he served with the Royal Artillery in North Africa and Italy.

He played at left-half. He was a regular in Liverpool's title-winning season of 1946–47, making 33 league appearances by LFChistory's count. In 1950 he scored against Everton in the FA Cup semi-final but was left out of the side for the final against Arsenal. LFChistory records that he later said the experience helped him when he had to make hard selection decisions himself.

His playing career ended in 1954, the year Liverpool were relegated. He made 277 competitive appearances and scored 13 goals, with wartime football counted separately.

## Trainer and coach

He stayed on as reserve-team trainer, qualified as a physiotherapist and was made chief coach in 1957. He became first-team trainer in 1959, the year Bill Shankly arrived as manager. He was one of the Boot Room coaches, with Joe Fagan and Ronnie Moran.

When Shankly retired in 1974, Paisley needed persuading to take over. He was 55, and it was his first senior managerial job. Liverpool finished second in his first league season.

## Ray Kennedy

One of his most important decisions involved Ray Kennedy, Shankly's final signing. Kennedy had arrived from Arsenal as a forward. Paisley moved him to the left of midfield, and Kennedy played in all three of Paisley's European Cup-winning teams.

## The first title

The first league title came in 1976. Liverpool trailed at Wolves in their final league game, on 4 May, before goals from Kevin Keegan, John Toshack and Kennedy gave them a 3–1 win. They also beat Club Brugge 4–3 over two legs in the UEFA Cup final. In his second season Paisley had matched Shankly's league and UEFA Cup double of 1973.

## Rome, 1977

Liverpool retained the league title in 1977 but lost the FA Cup final 2–1 to Manchester United. Four days later they played Borussia Mönchengladbach in the European Cup final in Rome. Terry McDermott, Tommy Smith and Phil Neal scored in a 3–1 win. Keegan's running tormented Berti Vogts throughout the game and won the penalty from which Neal completed the scoring. It was Liverpool's first European Cup.

## Replacing Keegan

Keegan then left for Hamburg. Paisley signed Kenny Dalglish from Celtic to replace him. Alan Hansen also arrived in 1977, and Graeme Souness joined from Middlesbrough in January 1978. The three Scots became central to Liverpool's next side.

Two of them settled the 1978 European Cup final. Souness made the pass and Dalglish lifted his finish over the Brugge goalkeeper. Liverpool won 1–0 at Wembley and kept the trophy.

Paisley later explained that continental teams had learned to intercept the ball, and that Europe's best sides taught Liverpool how to build attacks from the back. "The pace of their movement was dictated by the first pass," he said. He also called the five-a-side games in training "key".

## 1978–79

The 1978–79 league season showed what that football could produce. Liverpool scored 85 goals, conceded 16 and collected 68 points from 42 matches, with two points for a win. They finished eight points ahead of Nottingham Forest.

There were defeats too. Forest knocked the holders out of the European Cup in the first round that season, and Dinamo Tbilisi did the same the following season. Paisley never won the FA Cup as manager.

## Paris, 1981

Liverpool retained the league title in 1980. In 1981 they returned to the European Cup final. Ray Kennedy's away goal against Bayern Munich took them through the semi-final, and in Paris Alan Kennedy scored the only goal against Real Madrid. That season also brought Liverpool's first League Cup, won against West Ham United in a replay.

## Rebuilding again

On Boxing Day 1981 a 3–1 home defeat by Manchester City left Liverpool 12th. Paisley then changed the captaincy, giving it to Souness in place of Phil Thompson, who had lifted the European Cup in Paris only months earlier.

Younger players were taking on more. Ronnie Whelan succeeded Kennedy on the left of midfield, and Ian Rush established himself in attack. In the 1982 League Cup final Whelan scored twice and Rush once as Liverpool beat Tottenham Hotspur 3–1 after extra time. In the league they won 20 of their last 25 matches and finished four points ahead of Ipswich Town.

Neal described the pressure in an interview with LFChistory. "Whatever you won you knew somebody was going to come in and take your place," he said.

## The last season

Paisley's final season, 1982–83, brought a sixth league title, won by 11 points, and a third League Cup in a row. After Manchester United were beaten 2–1 at Wembley, Souness asked Paisley to go up and collect the trophy.

He handed over to Fagan in 1983. His 13 major honours were six league titles, three European Cups, the UEFA Cup and three League Cups, and he also won the 1977 European Super Cup. The club's records and LFChistory credit him with 534 matches in charge. Under Fagan the following season, Liverpool won the league, the European Cup and the League Cup.

## Afterwards

In retirement Paisley advised Dalglish, who became player-manager in 1985, and also served as a director of the club. His later years were affected by Alzheimer's disease. He died on 14 February 1996, aged 77.

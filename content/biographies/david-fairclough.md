David Fairclough scored 55 goals for Liverpool between 1975 and 1983. By LFChistory's count he made 154 appearances, starting 92 and coming off the bench 62 times. Eighteen of his goals came as a substitute, including the late winner against Saint-Étienne in March 1977 that put Liverpool into the European Cup semi-finals. He became known as "Supersub". He won league titles in 1975–76, 1976–77 and 1979–80, and started the 1978 European Cup final. But 37 of his goals came in games he started, and he spent much of his Liverpool career wanting more of those.

## Signing and debut

Fairclough was born in Liverpool on 5 January 1957. He joined the club in 1972 and signed as a professional on 9 January 1974, according to LFChistory. Bill Shankly was still manager when he arrived, but it was Bob Paisley who gave him his first-team chance.

He made his debut on 1 November 1975, starting a 1–0 win at Middlesbrough. Three days later he came on against Real Sociedad at Anfield and scored his first goal for the club in a 6–0 UEFA Cup win.

Kevin Keegan and John Toshack were the established strikers. Toshack's Achilles tendon problems, as Fairclough later recalled them, opened the door for him.

## The 1976 run-in

In 1975–76 Fairclough made 14 league appearances, nine of them from the bench, and scored seven league goals. In all competitions he had eight goals in 19 games.

Most of them came in the run-in. On 27 March 1976 he came on against Burnley and scored twice in a 2–0 win. A week later, on 3 April, Grand National day, Liverpool played Everton at Anfield. Fairclough replaced Toshack, won the ball on the halfway line, ran through the Everton defence and beat Dai Davies with about two minutes left. Liverpool won 1–0. The club's official profile records it as Fairclough's own favourite goal.

Liverpool won the league that season, with Fairclough playing 14 times. He was also involved in the UEFA Cup final against Club Brugge. He started the first leg on 28 April 1976, a 3–2 win, and came on in the second leg on 19 May, a 1–1 draw that won Liverpool the trophy.

## Saint-Étienne

The goal he is remembered for came on 16 March 1977, in the European Cup quarter-final second leg at Anfield. Saint-Étienne, the French champions, had won the first leg 1–0. Keegan scored for Liverpool in the second minute, Bathenay equalised in the 50th and Ray Kennedy made it 2–1 in the 59th. That still left Saint-Étienne through on away goals.

With around a quarter of an hour left, Paisley replaced Toshack with Fairclough. In the 84th minute Kennedy flicked a ball from midfield beyond the French defence. Fairclough ran on to it and slid it past Ivan Ćurković. Liverpool won 3–1 on the night and 3–2 on aggregate.

## Left out in May

Fairclough started both FA Cup semi-finals against Everton at Maine Road that April, a 2–2 draw on 23 April and a 3–0 win in the replay four days later. He was left out of the final against Manchester United. According to LFChistory, he said Paisley had told him he would play.

He was then an unused substitute in Rome on 25 May 1977, when Liverpool won the European Cup for the first time. "I was very disappointed," he told LFChistory years later.

He played 30 times that season, 20 of them in the league, as Liverpool retained the title.

## His busiest season

In 1977–78 Fairclough made 42 appearances, 34 of them starts, and scored 15 goals. It was the most games he played in any season for Liverpool.

He scored in both legs of the European Super Cup against Hamburg, starting each time: in a 1–1 draw on 22 November 1977 and in a 6–0 win on 6 December. Liverpool won the trophy.

He still had his moments from the bench. On 26 October 1977 Derby County came to Anfield in the League Cup. Fairclough came on with 20 minutes left and the score 0–0, and scored twice within five minutes. Liverpool won 2–0.

On 10 May 1978 he started the European Cup final against Club Brugge at Wembley. Kenny Dalglish scored the only goal, and Liverpool kept the trophy.

## Injuries and Ian Rush

The next season Steve Heighway started ahead of him at No. 9. Fairclough came on against Everton on 13 March 1979, was injured in a 1–1 draw, and did not play again that season.

In 1979–80 he scored 13 goals in 26 appearances, 14 of them as a substitute, as Liverpool won the league again.

Injuries then took most of 1980–81 from him. He was out until mid-January, and in February 1981 a knee injury ended his season. He made no first-team appearances in 1981–82 as Ian Rush established himself in attack, scoring 30 goals in 49 games. Fairclough spent the season in the reserves and scored 14 goals in 29 games for them. In the summer of 1982 Liverpool loaned him to Toronto Blizzard in the North American Soccer League, to keep his fitness up, according to LFChistory. He returned to the Liverpool first team in October 1982.

## "A lot more to offer"

In a 2009 interview with LFChistory, Fairclough talked about how Paisley used him after Saint-Étienne. Paisley, he said, decided to play him as a substitute or leave him out. "I had a lot more to offer," he said.

## After Liverpool

Fairclough left Liverpool on 4 July 1983 and joined the Swiss club Luzern, where he stayed until 1985. He then played for Norwich City, Oldham Athletic, the Belgian club Beveren from 1986 to 1989, Tranmere Rovers and Wigan Athletic, before finishing in non-league football with Knowsley.

In 2015 he published his autobiography, written with Mark Platt. It was called *Supersub: The Story of Football's Most Famous Number 12*.

His Liverpool record stands at 154 appearances and 55 goals by LFChistory's count, including 34 goals in 98 league games.

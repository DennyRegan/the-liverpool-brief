Steve Heighway made 475 appearances for Liverpool and scored 76 goals between 1970 and 1981. He scored in two FA Cup finals, won league titles and UEFA Cups, and set up two of Liverpool's three goals when they won their first European Cup in Rome in 1977. Eight years after he left as a player, he came back to run the club's youth development. Robbie Fowler, Jamie Carragher, Michael Owen and Steven Gerrard all came through in his time.

## From Warwick to Anfield

Heighway was born in Dublin on 25 November 1947. He reached professional football by an unusual route. He studied economics and politics at the University of Warwick while playing for non-league Skelmersdale United, and graduated in 1970. He signed for Liverpool as an amateur in 1970 and turned professional that summer, aged 22.

Bill Shankly was rebuilding his side, and a place opened quickly. Bobby Graham broke his leg against Chelsea in early October 1970, and Heighway moved into the team. He made 49 appearances in his first season and scored seven goals. He also played international football for the Republic of Ireland, winning 34 caps between 1970 and 1981, according to LFChistory and RSSSF.

## Everton and Arsenal

He played mainly on the left. He could beat a full-back and cross, or carry on towards goal himself.

On 21 November 1970 Everton led 2–0 at Anfield after 63 minutes. Heighway scored in the 65th minute. Six minutes later he made the equaliser for John Toshack, Toshack's first goal for Liverpool. Chris Lawler scored the winner in the 76th minute, and Liverpool won 3–2.

That season ended at Wembley against Arsenal in the FA Cup final. In the first minute of extra time Heighway cut in from the left and beat Bob Wilson with a low shot at his near post. Arsenal came back. Eddie Kelly was credited with the equaliser and Charlie George scored the winner. Heighway's first professional season ended with a Wembley goal and a losers' medal.

## Trophies under Shankly

Liverpool won the league and the UEFA Cup in 1973. A year later they were back at Wembley, against Newcastle United in the FA Cup final.

Kevin Keegan gave Liverpool the lead. Heighway scored the second. Ray Clemence kicked long, Toshack flicked it on, and Heighway ran through to finish low into the far corner. Two minutes from the end he passed into the right side of the area for Tommy Smith, whose ball across goal gave Keegan his second. Liverpool won 3–0.

Shankly resigned that summer.

## Paisley and Rome

Under Bob Paisley, Heighway stayed in the side. Liverpool won the league and UEFA Cup again in 1976, and he started both legs of the final against Club Brugge. He was still best known on the left, but he played more in central areas as time went on.

In May 1977 Liverpool lost the FA Cup final 2–1 to Manchester United. Four days later they played Borussia Mönchengladbach in the European Cup final in Rome. Heighway played Terry McDermott through for the first goal. Allan Simonsen equalised early in the second half. Heighway then took a corner from the left and Smith headed it in at the near post to make it 2–1. Phil Neal scored a penalty after Berti Vogts fouled Keegan, and Liverpool won 3–1. They finished the season as champions of England and of Europe.

He won a second European Cup a year later, coming on for Jimmy Case against Club Brugge at Wembley. By then his place was less secure. He made 28 league appearances in the 1978–79 title season, nine in 1979–80 and six in 1980–81.

He left Liverpool in April 1981. His record was 76 goals in 475 matches, and 50 goals in 331 league games. He went to the United States, playing for Minnesota Kicks and then Philadelphia Fever, and began coaching there, later becoming director of coaching at Clearwater Chargers in Florida.

## Youth development officer

In 1989 Kenny Dalglish brought him back to Liverpool as youth development officer. He led the club's youth development for the next 18 years.

The job began in a small youth department at Melwood. It grew into responsibility for the purpose-built academy at Kirkby, which opened in 1998. Heighway said the new base allowed better planning. Before it, youth sessions could be disrupted whenever the reserves needed players. By 2007, he said, under-18 coach John Owens and reserve manager Gary Ablett spoke most days. Bringing boys towards the first team took several coaches, not one director.

He wanted high standards and close contact with each player. In 2016 he told the Telegraph: "My mantra is engage, confront, challenge and inspire." He meant confronting a youngster with what he was doing, challenging him to be better, then inspiring him. He wanted discipline and respect, and said knowing how tough to be had to start early, but that it was a balance with being able to inspire. He also spoke out against boys being overplayed across school, Sunday league and club football, and against recruiting youngsters from far away when it disrupted their schooling and family life.

Hughie McAuley, one of his coaches, recalled Heighway taking promising younger boys, Gerrard and Owen among them, on trips and to cup finals with older players. The aim was that the big occasions would feel familiar when their own chance came. McAuley named Dave Shannon as part of the work on Gerrard's development.

## Youth Cups

Liverpool won the FA Youth Cup for the first time in 1996, beating West Ham United 4–1 on aggregate with Owen and Carragher in the team. They won it again in 2006, against Manchester City, and in 2007, against Manchester United. After the 2007 final, won on penalties, Heighway said the staff had left the players to decide their own penalty takers.

Cups did not settle the harder question of who reached the first team. Heighway drew a line between a talent like Gerrard, obvious from the start, and players like Carragher who improved through senior opportunities. The academy could prepare a player, he argued, but could not show how good he would become without those chances. Rafael Benítez, then Liverpool manager, was calling in 2007 for more competitive football for 18-to-21-year-olds, proposing that big clubs' reserve sides play in the Football League.

## Leaving and returning

Heighway left in 2007. After the Youth Cup final he said it was his choice and that the club had never suggested he should go. In 2016 he gave a fuller account. He said Benítez had set up a mini-academy at Melwood, which he believed cut the chances for the Kirkby players. There was no conflict, he said, but the two sides did not communicate. He also said that by 2007 he was ready for a break.

He returned to the academy part-time in January 2015 and became a full-time consultant later that year. Under academy director Alex Inglethorpe he worked mainly with 15- and 16-year-olds, individually and in groups, without running a team. He could also advise parents and coaches. Barry Lewtas, then the under-16s coach, said exchanging ideas with Heighway had helped his own development as a coach.

Liverpool announced his retirement from his academy coaching role on 2 December 2022, a week after his 75th birthday. Inglethorpe said he would still be at the academy to watch games and share his expertise.

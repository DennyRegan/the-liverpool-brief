Gary McAllister played 87 games for Liverpool and scored nine goals. He arrived on a free transfer in the summer of 2000, aged 35, and stayed two seasons. In the first of them, 2000–01, Liverpool won the League Cup, the FA Cup and the UEFA Cup. In the UEFA Cup final against Alavés in Dortmund he had a hand in four of Liverpool's five goals and was named man of the match. He was 36.

## Motherwell to Coventry

McAllister was born in Motherwell on 25 December 1964 and began his career with his hometown club. He moved to Leicester City in August 1985 and spent five seasons there. In July 1990 he joined Leeds United, and in 1991–92 he won the First Division in a midfield with Gordon Strachan, David Batty and Gary Speed. He won 57 caps for Scotland and scored five goals.

He joined Coventry City in July 1996. In his final season before joining Liverpool, he scored 13 goals, 11 of them in the league, and was Coventry's player of the season.

## The signing

McAllister passed his Liverpool medical on 25 May 2000 and joined on 1 July, when his Coventry contract expired. Gérard Houllier wanted him to link defence and attack, make good decisions and help the young players develop. He was happy to take on that role, and later said the influence ran both ways and that he had been inspired by seeing so many good young players.

Steven Gerrard was one of them. "I was thinking he's going to come and take my place and I wasn't going to play," Gerrard said later. Training changed his mind, and he came to see McAllister as someone to learn from.

The start was poor. McAllister made his debut as a substitute for Gerrard in a 1–0 home win over Bradford City on 19 August 2000. Two days later he started at Arsenal and was sent off in the 37th minute for a mistimed tackle on Patrick Vieira. Vieira and Hamann were also sent off, and Liverpool lost 2–0.

His first Liverpool goal came against Coventry at Anfield on 12 November, in a 4–1 win. He also set up Gerrard's goal that day.

## The first trophy

On 25 February 2001 he came on for Gerrard in the League Cup final against Birmingham City. The game finished 1–1 after extra time. McAllister took Liverpool's first penalty in the shootout and scored, and Liverpool won it 5–4. He later called the win "a massive catalyst to win the next two finals".

## Goodison

Liverpool lost 2–1 at home to Leeds on 13 April. Three days later they went to Everton. Liverpool led twice, through Emile Heskey and Markus Babbel, and Everton equalised twice, the second time from a David Unsworth penalty. Robbie Fowler missed a penalty and Igor Bišćan was sent off.

In the fourth minute of added time Liverpool won a free-kick 44 yards out, in the left channel. Earlier in the match he had taken a free-kick from the same area, which Sami Hyypiä had headed narrowly wide. This time McAllister watched the goalkeeper, Paul Gerrard, step across to cover the cross, and shot at the near post. The ball went in and Liverpool won 3–2.

It was a habit from free-kicks closer to goal. "I would always keep my head down and see where the keeper's feet were," he told LFChistory. Phil Thompson, Houllier's assistant, said of the goal: "We were down to 10 men and we thought everything was going against us and that moment flipped it the other way."

## Five in a row

The derby began a run of goals in five successive games. On 19 April Liverpool played Barcelona at Anfield in the UEFA Cup semi-final second leg, after a 0–0 draw in the first. McAllister scored a penalty just before half-time and Liverpool won 1–0. He then scored a penalty against Tottenham Hotspur, and free-kicks against Coventry and Bradford.

## Cardiff

Houllier left him on the bench for the FA Cup final against Arsenal on 12 May, on a very hot day in Cardiff, against a physical side. McAllister had grown up in Scotland watching the English cup final and was badly disappointed. He came on for Hamann after an hour.

Freddie Ljungberg put Arsenal ahead in the 72nd minute. McAllister's free-kick from the left was not cleared, Babbel diverted it to Michael Owen, and Owen equalised in the 83rd minute. Patrik Berger then supplied the pass for Owen's winner. In the celebrations Houllier tapped McAllister on the shoulder and told him to get ready to start in Dortmund.

## Dortmund

The UEFA Cup final was four days later, on 16 May. In the fourth minute McAllister's free-kick found Babbel for the first goal, and Gerrard made it 2–0. Alavés replied through the substitute Iván Alonso before McAllister scored a penalty to make it 3–1 before half-time. Javi Moreno scored twice after the break.

McAllister's pass set up Fowler for 4–3 in the 73rd minute. Jordi Cruyff headed an equaliser a minute from time. In extra time Alavés had two players sent off, and in the 117th minute McAllister's free-kick was headed into his own net by Delfí Geli. Under the golden-goal rule the game ended there, 5–4.

Three days later Liverpool won 4–0 at Charlton Athletic and finished third, which brought Champions League football. McAllister ended the season with 49 appearances and seven goals.

## The second season

In August 2001 he scored a penalty in the second minute as Liverpool beat Manchester United 2–1 in the Charity Shield. Twelve days later Liverpool beat Bayern Munich 3–2 in Monaco to win the UEFA Super Cup, their fifth trophy of the calendar year.

He played less in 2001–02 and made 38 appearances, scoring twice. Liverpool finished second with 80 points and went out of the Champions League to Bayer Leverkusen in the quarter-finals, 4–3 on aggregate. Looking back, he said he had been given a job to do. "I had a job brief and I'd come in and done my job," he told LFChistory.

His last appearance came at Anfield on 11 May 2002, as a late substitute in a 5–0 win over Ipswich Town.

## Coaching

He went back to Coventry as player-manager that summer and later said combining the two had been too much. He managed Leeds United in 2008, coached at Middlesbrough and was reunited with Houllier as his assistant at Aston Villa. Liverpool appointed him first-team coach under Brendan Rodgers on 11 July 2015. When Jürgen Klopp arrived that October he left the first-team staff and took an ambassadorial role. He later worked as Steven Gerrard's assistant at Rangers from 2018 to 2021, where they won the 2020–21 Scottish title, and then at Aston Villa from 2021 to 2022.

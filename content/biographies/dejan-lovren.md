Dejan Lovren made 185 appearances for Liverpool in six seasons and scored eight goals, according to LFChistory. One of them was the stoppage-time header that beat Borussia Dortmund 4–3 at Anfield in 2016. He was in the squad for three European finals: he started the 2016 Europa League final and the 2018 Champions League final, and was an unused substitute in the 2019 Champions League final. He was part of the squad that won the Champions League in 2019 and the Premier League in 2019–20.

## Before Liverpool

Lovren was born in Zenica on 5 July 1989. He came through the youth system at NK Karlovac and then Dinamo Zagreb, with a loan spell at Inter Zaprešić. He moved to Lyon in January 2010.

Southampton signed him for the 2013–14 season, his only one there. He made 31 league appearances and scored two goals. One of them came at Anfield on 21 September 2013, a header in the 53rd minute that gave Southampton a 1–0 win.

## Signed by Rodgers

Brendan Rodgers brought him to Liverpool on 27 July 2014 for a reported £20m. He was the third player to arrive from Southampton that summer, after Rickie Lambert and Adam Lallana.

He made 38 appearances in his first season. It was a difficult one for the team. Liverpool had finished second in 2013–14 and dropped to sixth. When Jürgen Klopp was appointed in October 2015, he inherited Lovren.

## Dortmund

The night Lovren is best remembered for came in Klopp's first season. Liverpool played Dortmund at Anfield in the second leg of the Europa League quarter-final on 14 April 2016. The first leg in Dortmund had ended 1–1.

Dortmund scored twice in the first nine minutes, through Henrikh Mkhitaryan and Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang. Divock Origi pulled one back after half-time, but Marco Reus made it 3–1 in the 57th minute. Philippe Coutinho scored in the 66th minute and Mamadou Sakho in the 78th. In the first minute of stoppage time Lovren headed the winner. Liverpool won 4–3 on the night and 5–4 on aggregate, as UEFA's report recorded.

The run ended in the final. Lovren started against Sevilla in Basel on 18 May 2016, and Liverpool lost 3–1.

## Back into the Champions League

In 2016–17 he made 32 appearances and scored two goals. The first came at Stamford Bridge on 16 September 2016. He opened the scoring against Chelsea in the 17th minute and Jordan Henderson added a second. Diego Costa replied, and Liverpool won 2–1. They finished the season in the top four and returned to the Champions League.

## Wembley, October 2017

His hardest afternoon came against Tottenham at Wembley on 22 October 2017. Harry Kane scored in the fourth minute after Lovren stepped out and failed to head the ball clear. In the 12th minute he misjudged a long ball from Hugo Lloris, Kane got through and Son Heung-min scored. Mohamed Salah pulled a goal back, but Klopp took Lovren off after 31 minutes and sent on Alex Oxlade-Chamberlain. Tottenham scored twice more after he left the pitch, and Liverpool lost 4–1.

Klopp did not put the defeat on one player. Of the second goal, he said that if the same ball came another 500 times, he was pretty sure Lovren would win it. "I really don't want to blame players and Dejan was no worse, to be honest, than Joel," he said, referring to Joël Matip. He explained that the substitution was a tactical change: "we needed more protection in the half-space."

## Kyiv

Lovren stayed in the side. He made 43 appearances in all competitions in 2017–18, 14 of them in Europe, his busiest season at Liverpool, according to LFChistory. Virgil van Dijk arrived in January 2018, and the two played together in the centre of defence.

Liverpool knocked out Porto, Manchester City and Roma on the way to the Champions League final in Kyiv on 26 May 2018. Lovren started beside Van Dijk, and Real Madrid won 3–1.

Two months later he was in another final. He played in every match of Croatia's run at the 2018 World Cup, which ended in a 4–2 defeat by France in the final.

## A smaller role

Over his last two seasons his place in the team became less secure. The club's profile points to injuries, Matip's form and the emergence of Joe Gomez. He made 18 appearances in 2018–19 and 15 in 2019–20, 33 in all.

He still contributed. On Boxing Day 2018 he opened the scoring against Newcastle United in the 11th minute, with a half-volley after Jamaal Lascelles headed the ball out. Liverpool won 4–0, as the Premier League's report recorded. In the 2019 Champions League final against Tottenham in Madrid he was among the unused substitutes, and Liverpool won.

On 27 November 2019 Napoli came to Anfield in the group stage. Early in the game Lovren blocked a shot from Dries Mertens. Mertens scored in the 21st minute. In the 65th Lovren headed in a corner from James Milner to make it 1–1. The draw meant Liverpool needed only a point at Salzburg on 10 December to be sure of going through.

He made ten league appearances, and 15 in all competitions, as Liverpool won the title in 2019–20.

## Leaving

His move to Zenit St Petersburg was announced on 27 July 2020, six years to the day after he signed. "It was emotional from the beginning until the end," he told the club. "That was a big journey for me that I will always carry forever in my heart."

## After Liverpool

He won the Russian league with Zenit in each of his first two seasons there and returned to Lyon in January 2023. He retired from international football in February 2023, with 78 caps and five goals for Croatia.

He joined PAOK in September 2024. In June 2026 the Greek club announced that he was leaving at the end of his contract, after 27 appearances. By early November 2026 no new club or retirement had been announced.

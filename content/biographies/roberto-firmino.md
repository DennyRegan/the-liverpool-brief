Roberto Firmino made 362 appearances for Liverpool and scored 111 goals. He also set up 72 goals, according to LFChistory. He was a centre-forward who kept leaving the centre. He would drop into midfield to take the ball, pull a defender with him and find a runner going into the space he had left. With Mohamed Salah and Sadio Mané either side of him, that movement was central to how Jürgen Klopp's team attacked. He won the Champions League and the club's first league title for 30 years.

## From Maceió to Hoffenheim

Firmino was born in Maceió, Brazil, on 2 October 1991. He made his name at Figueirense and signed for Hoffenheim at the end of 2010. In 2013–14 he scored 16 Bundesliga goals in 33 games. Liverpool agreed a fee of £29m in June 2015, when Brendan Rodgers was manager. The club's own profile described him at the time as nominally a No. 10 who could also play wide, deeper or as a striker.

## Learning to be a No. 9

His first weeks in England were unsettled. Klopp arrived in October, and on 21 November Firmino scored his first Liverpool goal in a 4–1 win at Manchester City. His low ball across had already forced Eliaquim Mangala into an own goal, and he had put Philippe Coutinho through for the second. Coutinho then set up Firmino's goal. Liverpool were 3–1 up at half-time.

Klopp played him as a false nine: a centre-forward who drops into midfield to help build attacks. He had to learn the role. After Firmino scored twice in a 3–3 draw with Arsenal on 13 January 2016, Klopp said they were working on when he should be in the box and when he should not, and when to push the defensive line back. There was not enough time between games, Klopp added, to see the big steps. A few days later, Michael Cox of FourFourTwo noted that the system worked well against big sides who played a high line, and less well against teams who sat back.

He also showed he could work alongside a conventional striker. With Daniel Sturridge leading the line in the Europa League semi-final second leg against Villarreal on 5 May 2016, Firmino was involved in all three goals of a 3–0 win. His low ball forced an own goal, his flicked pass sent Sturridge through for the second, and his cut-back from the byline led to Adam Lallana's third.

## The first defender

In 2016–17 he played with Coutinho and the newly signed Mané. After a 6–1 win over Watford in November, Jordan Henderson praised the three of them for their defending and for pressing and winning the ball back high up the pitch. Klopp called Firmino "a connecting player, a finisher, a fighter, a defender, the first defender." He started 34 league games that season, and Liverpool qualified for the Champions League.

Salah arrived in 2017. While Salah and Mané ran at goal, Firmino linked midfield and attack. He also helped win the ball back. In February 2018 the Premier League analyst Adrian Clarke pointed out that he was statistically the squad's third-most successful tackler.

He scored, too: 27 goals in all competitions in 2017–18, his best season for the club, including 11 in the Champions League (one of them in the play-off round against Hoffenheim). In the 5–2 semi-final first-leg win over Roma on 24 April 2018 he set up both of Salah's goals and scored twice himself. Liverpool lost the final to Real Madrid in Kyiv.

A year later they won it. A muscle injury kept Firmino out of the second leg against Barcelona, but he was fit to start the final against Tottenham in Madrid on 1 June 2019. Liverpool won 2–0, with goals from Salah and Divock Origi.

Some nights he took games on himself. On 29 December 2018 he scored a hat-trick in a 5–1 win over Arsenal at Anfield. For the second goal he weaved past defenders and rolled the ball into the bottom corner.

## Qatar and the title

In December 2019 Liverpool went to Qatar for the Club World Cup. Firmino came off the bench in the semi-final against Monterrey and scored the winner in stoppage time. Three days later, in the final against Flamengo, he took Mané's pass in the box, sent the goalkeeper and a defender the wrong way and scored in extra time. It was the only goal, and it gave Liverpool the trophy for the first time.

He played in all 38 league games as Liverpool won the title in 2019–20. His goals won away games at Crystal Palace, Tottenham and Wolves, and he scored twice in the 4–0 win at Leicester on Boxing Day. His first home league goal of the season did not come until the last home game, a 5–3 win over Chelsea on 22 July 2020.

## The last seasons

A serious hamstring injury interrupted his 2021–22 season, and Diogo Jota and Luis Díaz gave Klopp other options. He made 20 league appearances, compared with 38 two seasons earlier. Liverpool won the League Cup and the FA Cup that year, though he missed the League Cup final through injury.

He still had big moments. Two goals in the 9–0 win over Bournemouth on 27 August 2022 took him to 100 for the club. In March 2023 it became known that he would leave when his contract ran out that summer. Two days later he came off the bench and scored the seventh in the 7–0 win over Manchester United.

His last game at Anfield was against Aston Villa on 20 May 2023. He equalised in the 89th minute from Salah's cross. Afterwards his team-mates formed a guard of honour for him and the other departing players. Supporters had sung "Si Señor" for years, not only when he scored.

Eight days after the Villa game he scored in a 4–4 draw at Southampton, his final match for Liverpool. He joined Al-Ahli in Saudi Arabia in July 2023, was named the most valuable player as they won the AFC Champions League Elite in 2025, and moved to Al Sadd in Qatar that July. He is still playing there, on a contract that runs to June 2027.

Firmino rarely topped Liverpool's scoring charts. In Klopp's front three he did the work others did not, and he still scored 111 goals.

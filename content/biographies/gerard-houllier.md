Gérard Houllier managed Liverpool from 1998 to 2004. In 2001 his team won the League Cup, the FA Cup and the UEFA Cup, then added the Charity Shield and the UEFA Super Cup, five trophies in one calendar year. A year later Liverpool finished second in the league with 80 points, his highest placing. LFChistory records 307 games with him in sole charge, of which Liverpool won 160. He rebuilt the defence, made Steven Gerrard captain and won another League Cup in 2003, but he could not take the team on to the title.

## A teacher on the Kop

Houllier was born in Thérouanne, France, on 3 September 1947. He knew Liverpool long before he managed the club. While teaching in the city in 1969, he watched from the Kop as Liverpool beat Dundalk 10–0 in September that year. Years later he recalled the support of the crowd, and a team that kept looking for goals when the game was already won.

## France

His coaching career was built in France. After spells at Nœux-les-Mines and Lens, he led Paris Saint-Germain to the French title in 1986. He then managed the national team, a job that ended when France failed to qualify for the 1994 World Cup. He stayed in the federation's technical work and led France's under-18s to the European title in 1996.

## Joint manager, then sole charge

Liverpool appointed him joint manager alongside Roy Evans in July 1998. The arrangement lasted 18 competitive matches. After a League Cup defeat at home to Tottenham, Evans left on 12 November and Houllier took sole control. Phil Thompson returned as his assistant. Steven Gerrard made his senior debut later that month. Liverpool finished seventh.

## Rebuilding the defence

In 1999 Houllier signed Sami Hyypiä, Stéphane Henchoz and Dietmar Hamann. Hyypiä and Henchoz became the central defensive partnership, with Hamann protecting them from midfield. Sander Westerveld replaced David James in goal. Paul Ince left, and Vladimír Šmicer arrived to replace Steve McManaman, who left for Real Madrid.

The effect was immediate. Liverpool conceded 30 league goals in 1999–2000, the fewest in the division, and collected 67 points, 13 more than the season before. They finished fourth, but a poor finish cost them a Champions League place. Houllier said afterwards that injuries to Robbie Fowler, Michael Owen and Šmicer had limited his attack, and that his team had struggled to break down packed defences, particularly at Anfield.

The changes went beyond the team. Thompson later recalled that discipline was one of the main things Houllier wanted. The manager was also central to the redevelopment of Melwood, begun in 2001. Danny Murphy credited him with changing his mentality, and Šmicer remembered being given time to adapt after a difficult first season.

## The treble

Gary McAllister and Markus Babbel joined in 2000, and McAllister was particularly influential in the closing months. Liverpool played 63 competitive matches in 2000–01 and scored 127 goals.

Houllier asked his forwards to share the defensive work, with Owen's pace the outlet when Liverpool won the ball. In the UEFA Cup semi-final first leg in Barcelona, which ended 0–0, Emile Heskey played deep, blocking the gaps Luis Enrique usually exploited, rather than simply waiting alongside Owen. Houllier praised his defending but admitted Liverpool had been too cautious with the ball. Heskey, Patrik Berger and Murphy, he said, were meant to give Owen more support.

The League Cup came first, in February, when Birmingham City were beaten on penalties. In the FA Cup final Arsenal had much of the game and led through Freddie Ljungberg. Owen equalised in the 83rd minute and, five minutes later, ran on to Berger's pass to win it 2–1.

In the later rounds of the UEFA Cup Liverpool knocked out Roma, Porto and Barcelona before meeting Alavés in the final in Dortmund. They won 5–4 after extra time, when Delfí Geli headed into his own net for a golden goal.

Third place in the league secured a place in the Champions League qualifiers. The Charity Shield and the Super Cup followed in August.

## Illness

On 13 October 2001 Houllier was taken ill during Liverpool's home match against Leeds United. He needed life-saving surgery to repair a dissection of the aorta. It was not a heart attack. Thompson took temporary charge while he recovered.

Houllier returned to the dugout for the Champions League match against Roma in March 2002. Liverpool won 2–0, with goals from Jari Litmanen and Heskey, and reached the quarter-finals. There they beat Bayer Leverkusen 1–0 at Anfield but lost the second leg 4–2. Litmanen's late goal briefly put Liverpool ahead in the tie before Leverkusen scored again.

In the league they finished second with 80 points, behind Arsenal.

## Gerrard's captaincy

In October 2003 Houllier made Gerrard captain in place of Hyypiä. He said he had spotted Gerrard's leadership qualities very early in his career, and had waited for his personality and game to mature. Houllier believed Hyypiä had at times been taking too much responsibility upon himself and that the change would help him.

## The last two seasons

The signings of summer 2002, El Hadji Diouf, Salif Diao and Bruno Cheyrou, did not bring the improvement Liverpool needed. The 2002–03 season began with nine wins and three draws in the first 12 league games. An 11-match run without a win followed. Liverpool finished fifth and went out in the first group stage of the Champions League. They did beat Manchester United 2–0 to win the League Cup.

In 2003–04 they finished fourth, 30 points behind Arsenal, and qualified for the Champions League again. It was not enough for the board. Houllier's departure was announced on 24 May 2004. The chief executive, Rick Parry, described Champions League qualification as a minimum standard and said change was needed if Liverpool were to challenge realistically for the title. Houllier had wanted to carry on.

## After Liverpool

Houllier won two French league titles with Lyon and later returned to England to manage Aston Villa. He died on 14 December 2020, aged 73.

Jamie Carragher made 737 appearances for Liverpool, more than anyone except Ian Callaghan. He spent 16 years in the first team, served six managers and scored five goals. He played at right-back, at left-back and, from 2004, at centre-back, where he partnered Sami Hyypiä in the side that won the Champions League in Istanbul. His 150 European appearances are a club record. The club's records show nine seasons in which he played more than 50 games, six of them in a row from 2004 to 2010.

## Bootle and the academy

Carragher was born in Bootle on 28 January 1978. The club describes him as "originally an Evertonian". He joined Liverpool in 1987, aged nine.

In 1996 he was part of the team that won the club's first FA Youth Cup, beating West Ham United 4–1 on aggregate. Michael Owen played in the same side. Carragher signed professional terms on 9 October 1996.

Roy Evans gave him his debut on 8 January 1997, in a League Cup fifth-round tie at Middlesbrough. He came on for Rob Jones after 65 minutes, and Liverpool lost 2–1. Ten days later he made his first start, against Aston Villa at Anfield in the Premier League. He played in midfield, and in the 50th minute he headed in a Stig Inge Bjørnebye corner in a 3–0 win. He scored only four more goals for Liverpool.

## Full-back under Houllier

Under Gérard Houllier he became a regular. He started at right-back and moved across to the left when Markus Babbel arrived, with Hyypiä and Stéphane Henchoz in the middle.

There were bad days. On 11 September 1999 he scored two own goals against Manchester United at Anfield, in the third and 44th minutes, and Liverpool lost 3–2.

In 2000–01 he played 58 matches, mostly at left-back, as Liverpool won the League Cup, the FA Cup and the UEFA Cup. The League Cup final against Birmingham City finished 1–1 after extra time. The shoot-out reached 4–4 and went to sudden death, and Carragher scored Liverpool's sixth penalty. Sander Westerveld then saved from Andrew Johnson, and Liverpool won 5–4. It was the club's first trophy since the 1995 League Cup.

In the UEFA Cup semi-final Liverpool kept Barcelona out over two legs: 0–0 away, then 1–0 at Anfield through a Gary McAllister penalty. Owen scored twice in the last seven minutes to beat Arsenal 2–1 in the FA Cup final. Four days later, on 16 May 2001 in Dortmund, Liverpool beat Alavés 5–4 in the UEFA Cup final with a golden own goal in extra time.

On 27 January 2002, in an FA Cup fourth-round defeat at Highbury, Carragher threw a coin back into the crowd after it had been thrown at him. He was sent off. He apologised, Liverpool fined him £40,000 and he served a three-match ban. He also received a formal police warning.

On 13 September 2003 a tackle by Lucas Neill at Ewood Park broke his leg, and he was out for about four months. Danny Murphy later said Carragher "actually tried to get back on the pitch with a broken leg". Murphy also credited him with "keeping me in the right place and doing the right thing".

## Centre-back

Rafael Benítez arrived in 2004 and moved Carragher into the middle beside Hyypiä. He had formed his view of him while facing Liverpool as Valencia manager. "Sami was very good in the air, and Carra was always controlling the defence," Benítez said.

In the 2005 Champions League semi-final second leg against Chelsea, Luis García scored in the fourth minute and Liverpool held on to win 1–0. UEFA's report said Carragher "led Liverpool's resistance with commendable assurance".

In the final in Istanbul, AC Milan led 3–0 at half-time. Steven Gerrard, Vladimír Šmicer and Xabi Alonso brought Liverpool level. Carragher suffered cramp in extra time and kept playing. Before the shoot-out, by Jerzy Dudek's account, Carragher told the goalkeeper: "Remember Bruce, remember Bruce Grobbelaar." Dudek thought of Grobbelaar in the 1984 final and borrowed his wobbling legs. Liverpool won on penalties for their fifth European Cup.

A year later Carragher scored an own goal in the 21st minute of the FA Cup final against West Ham, and Dean Ashton made it 2–0 seven minutes later. Djibril Cissé and Gerrard pulled it back, Paul Konchesky put West Ham ahead again, and Gerrard equalised in the 90th minute. Liverpool won the shoot-out 3–1 after a 3–3 draw.

On 1 May 2007, in the Champions League semi-final second leg against Chelsea at Anfield, Carragher played his 90th European game and passed Callaghan's club record of 89. Liverpool reached the final in Athens, where Milan won 2–1. Filippo Inzaghi scored twice and Dirk Kuyt scored for Liverpool.

In 2008–09 Liverpool finished second with 86 points, four behind Manchester United. He never won the league.

He won 38 England caps between 1999 and 2010.

## The last seasons

Benítez left in 2010. Roy Hodgson followed, then Kenny Dalglish, then Brendan Rodgers, his last Liverpool manager.

In September 2010 Carragher's testimonial against Everton drew a crowd of 35,682, and the proceeds went to local charities through his 23 Foundation.

In the 2012 League Cup final against Cardiff City, Daniel Agger started and Carragher came on to replace him. Liverpool drew 2–2 after extra time and won the shoot-out 3–2. It was their first trophy since 2006.

On 7 February 2013 he announced that he would retire at the end of the season. "I don't want the manager or the club to be answering questions on my future," he said. Gerrard admitted he had tried to talk him into playing on for another year. By the spring he was back in the side and playing well, but that only confirmed his decision. "It's made me think it is the right time," he told reporters at Melwood before his last game, and he named the Melwood staff among the things he would miss.

His last match was against Queens Park Rangers at Anfield on 19 May 2013. With Gerrard absent, he captained the side. Both teams formed a guard of honour, and the Kop held up a "JC 23" display. Liverpool won 1–0, and Carragher's long-range volley hit the woodwork. Afterwards Callaghan presented him with a gift.

He left with the Champions League (2005), the UEFA Cup (2001), two FA Cups (2001, 2006), three League Cups (2001, 2003, 2012) and two UEFA Super Cups (2001, 2005).

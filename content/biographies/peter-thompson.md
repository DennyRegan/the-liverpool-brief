Peter Thompson made 416 appearances for Liverpool and scored 54 goals. He played on the left wing for Bill Shankly, opposite Ian Callaghan on the right, and won the league in 1963–64 and 1965–66. On 27 March 1965 he scored the first goal of the FA Cup semi-final against Chelsea at Villa Park, and Liverpool went on to win the cup for the first time. In his first seven seasons he missed only ten league games.

## Preston

Thompson was born in Carlisle on 27 November 1942. He made his senior debut for Preston North End at 17, against Arsenal on 30 August 1960, in a 2–0 win at Deepdale. Tom Finney had just retired, and Thompson was compared with him. He later said he felt the pressure of that comparison.

Preston were relegated in 1960–61. In February 1962 they met Liverpool in the fifth round of the FA Cup. The first game at Anfield and the replay at Deepdale both finished 0–0. In the second replay, at Old Trafford on 26 February, Thompson scored in the 60th minute and Preston won 1–0.

## Liverpool

Liverpool, Second Division champions in 1961–62, signed him on 14 August 1963, about 18 months after that tie. He was 20, and the fee was £37,000, according to LFChistory.

He made his debut at Blackburn on 24 August 1963, wearing No. 11 in a 2–1 win. He played all 42 league games that season and scored six league goals. Liverpool won the title with 57 points, four more than Manchester United. It was the club's first championship since 1946–47.

## Two wingers

Callaghan and Thompson played the position differently. In Thompson's account, Callaghan went direct and crossed early, while Thompson held the ball and tried to beat his man. Against some full-backs they swapped wings.

Thompson was right-footed. "At Melwood I used to spend hours just pushing the ball down the wing with my right foot and crossing it with my left," he said.

He was rarely absent. From 1963–64 to 1969–70 he played 284 of a possible 294 league games, by LFChistory's season tables.

## Brazil, 1964

Thompson won his first England cap in a 4–3 win over Portugal in Lisbon on 17 May 1964. His fourth, fifth and sixth caps came that summer at the Jubilee Tournament in Brazil, known as the "Little World Cup", against Brazil, Portugal and Argentina. "After the tournament I was the 'White Pelé'," he said. He did not accept the name. "I was no Pelé."

## The 1965 FA Cup

The semi-final against Chelsea was goalless at half-time. Thompson scored in the 63rd minute, and Willie Stevenson added a penalty in the 79th for a 2–0 win.

The final against Leeds United at Wembley on 1 May went to extra time. There were no substitutes, and Thompson played the whole match. Roger Hunt headed in Gerry Byrne's cross in the 93rd minute and Billy Bremner equalised in the 102nd. Ian St John headed the winner from Callaghan's cross in the 111th. Liverpool won 2–1.

## Another title and a European final

Liverpool won the league again in 1965–66. Thompson played 40 of the 42 league games and 50 matches in all.

On 5 May 1966 he started the Cup Winners' Cup final against Borussia Dortmund at Hampden Park, Liverpool's first European final. Dortmund scored first, and Thompson set up Hunt's equaliser. Stan Libuda scored in extra time and Dortmund won 2–1.

## England

Thompson was named in the initial squads for the 1966 and 1970 World Cups and left out of the final squad both times. Alf Ramsey's England increasingly played without orthodox wingers, which narrowed the opportunities for a player of Thompson's type. He won 16 caps without scoring. The last was a 0–0 draw with Scotland at Hampden Park on 25 April 1970.

## A new role

On 6 August 1968 the Liverpool Echo's Michael Charters reported that Shankly planned to give Thompson more freedom to leave the wing and attack through the middle. His display at Hull shortly before the new season had offered an encouraging example of the intended change.

## The last seasons

In 1970–71 he made 39 appearances, 32 starts and seven as a substitute. In every earlier season at Liverpool he had played between 47 and 58 games.

He came on for Alun Evans after 68 minutes of the 1971 FA Cup final against Arsenal on 8 May. In the 92nd minute he made the pass for Steve Heighway's goal. Eddie Kelly equalised in the 101st and Charlie George won it for Arsenal in the 111th.

His last game for Liverpool was a 0–0 draw at Wolves on 22 January 1972. He had two operations on his left knee. In his own account, his relationship with Shankly broke down while he was injured. "He treated me like a son for about nine years," he said. Afterwards, "He wouldn't speak to me."

## Bolton

Jimmy Armfield took him to Bolton Wanderers on loan in December 1973. LFChistory records that he left Liverpool on 5 January 1974, and the move became permanent for £18,000. He played for Bolton until April 1978 and retired as they won the Second Division title.

After football he ran a caravan park, then an inn in the Lake District, then a hotel in Harrogate. He later moved to Portugal. He died on 30 December 2018, aged 76.

John Toshack scored 96 goals in 247 games for Liverpool. He joined from Cardiff City in November 1970 for a club record £110,000 and stayed until early 1978. He won the league three times, the UEFA Cup twice and the FA Cup once. His partnership with Kevin Keegan was the attacking base of the side Bill Shankly built after his first great team broke up, and many of Toshack's best moments were goals he set up for someone else.

## From Cardiff

Toshack was born in Cardiff on 22 March 1949. Cardiff's Welsh Cup wins took them into the Cup Winners' Cup, and he played in Europe from the age of 18. Liverpool signed him on 11 November 1970, when he was 21. The club's annual described the fee as a "club record fee of £110,000".

He arrived as Shankly was replacing the team of the 1960s. Roger Hunt had already gone, and Ian St John, Tommy Lawrence and Ron Yeats were being phased out. Ray Clemence, Emlyn Hughes and Larry Lloyd were in the side.

His debut was at home to Coventry City on 14 November 1970, a 0–0 draw. A week later Everton came to Anfield and led 2–0 after goals from Whittle and Royle early in the second half. Steve Heighway pulled one back in the 65th minute. Six minutes later Heighway set up Toshack, who headed the equaliser. It was his first goal for Liverpool, in his second game. In the 76th minute he set up Chris Lawler for the winner, and Liverpool won 3–2.

## Keegan

Kevin Keegan joined from Scunthorpe United in May 1971. Toshack later spoke of a chemistry that was there from the start. Keegan's chances came from reading where Toshack's headers would fall, according to the club's own account of the partnership.

Trophies took time. Toshack started the 1971 FA Cup final against Arsenal on 8 May. Heighway scored in the second minute of extra time, but Kelly and George replied and Arsenal won 2–1. A year later Liverpool went to Highbury on 8 May 1972 needing a win to take the title. It finished 0–0, Derby County were champions and Liverpool were third, one point behind.

## The first double

Liverpool won the league in 1972–73, their first major trophy since 1966. Injuries cost Toshack 20 league games that season, according to LFChistory, and he made 22 league appearances.

He was left out of the first leg of the UEFA Cup final against Borussia Mönchengladbach at Anfield. The match was abandoned on 9 May because the pitch was waterlogged and was played again the next day. Shankly changed his team to include Toshack, and UEFA's account says his height troubled the German defence. He set up both of Keegan's goals, in the 21st and 33rd minutes. Lloyd added a third. Clemence saved a penalty from Jupp Heynckes, and Liverpool won 3–0.

They needed the margin. In the second leg on 23 May, Heynckes scored twice before half-time. Liverpool held on to lose 2–0 and won 3–2 on aggregate. It was the club's first European trophy.

## Wembley and Leicester

Toshack scored three goals in the 1974 FA Cup run, including in the quarter-final at Bristol City and the semi-final replay against Leicester City. In the final against Newcastle United on 4 May, Keegan scored first. Then Clemence kicked long, Toshack flicked it on with his head and Heighway ran through to make it 2–0. Keegan scored again late on after a move involving Heighway and Smith. Liverpool won 3–0.

It was Shankly's last trophy. He resigned on 12 July 1974, the same day Liverpool signed Ray Kennedy from Arsenal for £180,000. Bob Paisley took over.

In November 1974 Liverpool accepted a £160,000 bid from Leicester. The transfer collapsed when Toshack failed a medical because of a long-standing thigh problem. He went back to Liverpool. In an interview reproduced by LFChistory, he said he "had a falling out with Bob" before the move.

## His best season

In 1975–76 Toshack scored 23 goals in 50 games, 16 of them in the league. It was his highest total for the club. Six came in the UEFA Cup: a hat-trick against Hibernian and goals against Real Sociedad, Śląsk Wrocław and Barcelona.

Liverpool played at the Camp Nou for the first time on 30 March 1976, in the semi-final first leg. Johan Cruyff and Johan Neeskens played for Barcelona. After 13 minutes Keegan set up Toshack, who beat goalkeeper Pere Mora. Liverpool won 1–0. In the second leg at Anfield on 14 April, Phil Thompson scored and Rexach equalised, and Liverpool went through 2–1 on aggregate.

The league was decided at Molineux on 4 May. Queens Park Rangers had finished their fixtures and were a point clear. Wolves led through Kindon's 13th-minute goal. In the 76th minute Toshack knocked the ball down for Keegan to equalise. Toshack scored in the 85th minute and Kennedy in the 89th, and Liverpool won 3–1. It was Paisley's first league title as manager.

In the UEFA Cup final first leg against Club Brugge at Anfield on 28 April, Liverpool were 2–0 down after 15 minutes. Paisley took Toshack off at half-time and sent on Jimmy Case. Kennedy scored in the 59th minute, Case in the 61st and Keegan from a penalty in the 65th. Liverpool won 3–2. In Belgium on 19 May it finished 1–1, and Liverpool won 4–3 on aggregate. They had won the league and the UEFA Cup again.

## Rome

Toshack scored 13 goals in 1976–77, the last of them on 19 February 1977. Liverpool kept the league title and reached their first European Cup final. He missed it. He had an Achilles tendon injury and watched from the stands as Liverpool beat Mönchengladbach 3–1 in Rome on 25 May. He later called missing the game "devastating". It was Keegan's last match for Liverpool before his move to Hamburg.

Toshack played less the following season. His last appearance was at home to Bristol City on 19 November 1977, a 1–1 draw in which he set up Kenny Dalglish's goal. Liverpool released him on a free transfer on 27 February 1978.

## Swansea, Spain and Wales

He became Swansea City's player-manager on 1 March 1978. Swansea won promotion in 1977–78, 1978–79 and 1980–81, three in four seasons, and rose from the Fourth Division to the First.

On 3 October 1981 he brought Swansea to Anfield, soon after Shankly's death in September. He wore a Liverpool shirt during the minute's silence for his former manager. He later called it "just my personal tribute to somebody I owed everything to". The match finished 2–2.

He won 40 caps for Wales between 1969 and 1980 and scored 13 goals. Twenty-six of those caps and eight of the goals came while he was at Liverpool. His managerial career took him to Sporting in Portugal and then to Spain. He won the Copa del Rey with Real Sociedad in 1987, and in 1989–90 his Real Madrid side won La Liga and scored 107 goals. He managed Wales for one match in 1994 and again from 2004 to 2010.

At Liverpool he made 247 appearances and scored 96 goals: 74 in the league, eight in the FA Cup, three in the League Cup, ten in Europe and one in the Charity Shield.

Elisha Scott made 468 competitive appearances for Liverpool between 1913 and 1934, 430 of them in the league. He won two league titles. In 1922–23, when Liverpool retained the championship for the first time, he played all 42 league games and kept 21 clean sheets. When the club put him up for sale in 1934 and Everton were ready to sign him, supporters protested until Liverpool changed their minds.

## Belfast and a trial at Everton

Scott was born in Belfast on 24 August 1893. He played Boys' Brigade football, then for Broadway United and Linfield. His elder brother Billy was already an established goalkeeper who had played for Everton and Ireland, and Billy's recommendation helped Elisha on his way.

Everton gave him a trial but did not sign him; later accounts put it down to his youth or his size. Liverpool, then managed by Tom Watson, signed him in September 1912, into a squad in which Kenneth Campbell was the established goalkeeper.

## Waiting for Campbell

His debut came at St James' Park on 1 January 1913. He was 19, Liverpool drew 0–0 with Newcastle United, and the Echo welcomed the performance. It did not win him the place. He played one league game that season and four the next.

In January 1914 a newspaper report had him in contention for a cup tie because Campbell was doubtful. The same report said Liverpool had turned down an offer from Newcastle for him. The club wanted to keep him, even without a regular place to give him.

In 1914–15 he finally had a run of 23 league games. Then the war stopped the Football League. Scott played wartime football for Liverpool and then went home to Belfast, where he turned out for local clubs including Belfast Celtic. Those games do not count in his Liverpool record.

He came back into the side late in 1919–20, and Campbell moved on. The place was his.

## Two titles

Under David Ashworth, Liverpool won the league in 1921–22. Scott played 39 of the 42 league games. Liverpool conceded 36 goals and finished six points clear of Tottenham Hotspur. The defence in front of him included Ephraim Longworth, Donald McKinlay, Tommy Lucas and Ted Parry.

The Athletic News, reviewing the champions on 24 April 1922, praised the defenders' judgement, their tackling and their kicking, as well as Scott. It also credited Tom Bromilow and Walter Wadsworth in midfield, and Harry Chambers and Dick Forshaw for the goals.

Two days earlier, on 22 April 1922, Liverpool lost 2–0 at Cardiff City, with both goals coming in the opening minutes. The same paper thought the score flattered Liverpool, and still named Scott the best player on the field. Its reporter described him being tested with shots of all speeds, at all heights and from varying angles, and pulling down fast, rising shots from Cardiff's inside forwards.

In 1922–23 Liverpool were top from mid-September to the end of the season and won the title by six points. Scott was ever-present. He kept 21 clean sheets, and the goals conceded fell from 36 to 31. It was the first time Liverpool had won the league in consecutive seasons.

## How he kept goal

Scott was small for a goalkeeper. He relied on quick reactions and on reading where an attack was going.

He was also loud. Liverpool's own profile of him records that he shouted at the players in front of him, often in language that was not printable.

A Daily Mail assessment in September 1931 stressed that he never took his eye off the ball and that when he left his line he came away with it.

## Dixie Dean

Everton's Dixie Dean was his great Merseyside opponent, and the two were friends away from the pitch. Dean later said of him: "Elisha was the greatest I've ever seen."

## Ireland

Scott won 31 caps for Ireland, and his international career carried on after he left Liverpool. In a newspaper piece published under his name in 1929, he remembered the nerves of his first post-war representative games and how hard it was to face a strong Scotland attack.

## Riley and the later years

Liverpool won no more major trophies while he was at the club. Arthur Riley became a serious rival for his place and Scott's appearances fell. He recovered to play 37 league games in 1931–32, but managed only ten in 1933–34.

His last game was at Stamford Bridge on 21 February 1934. Chelsea won 2–0. Scott was 40.

## The move to Everton

That spring Liverpool placed him on the transfer list for £250, and a move to Everton appeared close. Supporters wrote to the local press in protest, and the club changed its mind.

Scott did not play for Liverpool again. Before the home game against Manchester City on 2 May 1934, a microphone was set up in the Main Stand and he thanked the Kop for its support. Liverpool won 3–2.

## Belfast Celtic

In 1934 he went home to become player-manager of Belfast Celtic. He stopped playing in 1936 and stayed on as manager until 1949. In that time the club won six Irish League titles and six Irish Cups.

## His standing

In February 1939 the Liverpool match programme reported a vote by supporters on the club's most famous players. Scott came first, ahead of Alex Raisbeck.

Scott died in Belfast on 16 May 1959. He was 65.

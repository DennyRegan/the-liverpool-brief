Jan Mølby made 292 competitive appearances for Liverpool and scored 61 goals. He joined from Ajax in 1984 and stayed for more than eleven years. His best season was 1985–86, when he played 58 games, scored 21 goals and helped Liverpool win the league and FA Cup double for the first time. In the FA Cup final against Everton, Liverpool trailed at half-time. Mølby set up two of their three goals and started the move for the other. He also scored 42 of the 45 penalties he took for the club.

## Kolding and Ajax

Mølby came through at his hometown club, Kolding, in Denmark, and moved to Ajax in 1982. He was already a Danish international when Liverpool signed him.

## A hard first season

Graeme Souness had left Liverpool for Sampdoria in the summer of 1984. Joe Fagan's side needed a midfielder, and Liverpool signed Mølby from Ajax in August for £200,000. He offered close control, a hard shot and passes that could switch the point of attack in one ball.

His competitive debut came at Carrow Road on 25 August 1984, a 3–3 draw with Norwich City. Alan Hansen, Ronnie Whelan and Kenny Dalglish started alongside him. He made 24 appearances that season and scored once.

He lost his place and was put in the reserves. Denmark were going to the 1986 World Cup, and Mølby was told he would not go to Mexico unless he was playing for Liverpool. He later said he had struggled with a lot of aspects of the English game in that first season.

## Dalglish's faith

Dalglish became player-manager in June 1985. Mølby credited him with turning things round. "Kenny had seen something in me – and I think that saved my time at Liverpool," he told the club.

The 1985–86 season brought 58 appearances and 21 goals, figures he never matched again. He was now a regular.

## The lost goal

The best known of those goals came against Manchester United in the Milk Cup fourth round at Anfield on 26 November 1985. Liverpool were behind to a Paul McGrath goal. Mølby intercepted the ball inside his own half, ran towards the Kop and shot from the edge of the penalty area past Gary Bailey. Sixty seconds later he scored a penalty, and Liverpool won 2–1.

A television dispute meant the match was not broadcast, so for years the goal lived on mainly in the memories of those who were there. Footage filmed by Manchester United for their own analysis surfaced years later, and supporters can now see it.

## The double

Mølby later picked out the 2–1 win at West Bromwich Albion in April as the most nervous game of the run-in.

In the FA Cup final on 10 May 1986, Gary Lineker put Everton ahead after 27 minutes, and they led 1–0 at half-time. In the 57th minute Mølby's pass set up Ian Rush for the equaliser. Six minutes later his cross gave Craig Johnston the second. In the 84th minute Mølby's pass sent Whelan away, and Whelan set up Rush for the third. Liverpool won 3–1 and completed the double.

## From the spot

Mølby converted 42 of his 45 penalties for Liverpool. On 26 November 1986 he scored three of them in a League Cup fourth-round replay against Coventry City at Anfield, a 3–1 win.

Steven Gerrard was in the crowd. "Jan's hat-trick was the first ever game that I went to see. My dad took me," he later told the club.

## Injury and prison

A serious injury in pre-season training disrupted Mølby's 1987–88 season. He played only eight competitive matches as Liverpool won the league.

In October 1988 he was sentenced to three months in prison for reckless driving. He served about six weeks. "I know I did wrong," he said later. After his release the club's chairman, John Smith, told him Liverpool had decided to keep him, and Dalglish backed him. He returned as a substitute at Old Trafford on 1 January 1989, a 3–1 defeat by Manchester United.

## Back in the side

He played 20 games in all competitions in 1989–90, when Liverpool won the league again. That rose to 34 in 1990–91 and 40 in 1991–92.

In November 1990 he believed he was about to join Barcelona. Dalglish then told him the clubs had not agreed a fee, and the move did not happen.

Dalglish sometimes used him as a sweeper, because he could start attacks from the back. Mølby preferred midfield.

## Wembley again

Dalglish left in 1991 and Souness returned as manager. Mølby started the 1992 FA Cup final against Sunderland on 9 May, six years after his first. It was 0–0 at half-time. Steve McManaman had been switched to the right a few minutes before the interval, and he set up Michael Thomas for the opener two minutes into the second half. Rush added the second, and Liverpool won 2–0. Contemporary reports said Mølby was forced deep in the first half, then in the second "swept the ball from one side of the field to the other".

## The last years

His appearances fell away. He played 13 games in 1992–93, 13 in 1993–94 and 16 in 1994–95.

His last competitive game for Liverpool was against Coventry at Anfield on 14 March 1995. He scored a penalty in the 62nd minute of a 3–2 defeat. He went on loan to Barnsley and Norwich City, and left Liverpool on 22 February 1996 to become player-manager of Swansea City. Swansea were relegated that season and lost the Third Division play-off final to Northampton Town a year later.

In April 2009 the Lord Mayor of Liverpool made him an honorary Scouser.

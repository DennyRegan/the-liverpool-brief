Rafael Benítez's side had beaten Manchester United and Chelsea on the way to the 2006 FA Cup final, and had come from 3–1 down to beat Luton 5–3 in the third round. Their opponents in Cardiff were West Ham United, newly promoted and managed by Alan Pardew.

It was the last FA Cup final at the Millennium Stadium before the new Wembley opened. Liverpool had won the first one played there, against Arsenal in 2001. The Aberdeen Press and Journal counted this as Liverpool's 62nd game of the season.

Benítez picked Pepe Reina; Steve Finnan, Jamie Carragher, Sami Hyypiä, John Arne Riise; Steven Gerrard, Momo Sissoko, Xabi Alonso, Harry Kewell; Peter Crouch, Djibril Cissé. West Ham had Shaka Hislop in goal, Anton Ferdinand in defence, Nigel Reo-Coker and Yossi Benayoun in midfield, and Dean Ashton and Marlon Harewood up front.

* * *

West Ham started better and took the lead after 21 minutes. Ashton played Lionel Scaloni in on the right, Scaloni crossed, and Carragher, stretching to cut it out, turned the ball into his own net.

Seven minutes later it was 2–0. Matthew Etherington shot, Reina could not hold it, and Ashton was there to put in the rebound.

Liverpool got back into the game before half-time. On 32 minutes Gerrard picked out Cissé at the back post, and Cissé volleyed in.

Reina saved from Harewood seconds into the second half. Then, nine minutes after the break, Crouch headed a ball down and Gerrard drove it in. It was 2–2.

West Ham went ahead again on 64 minutes. Paul Konchesky, the left-back, crossed from the left, and the ball looped over Reina and in. The Evening Herald in Dublin wrote that it seemed to change its mind in mid-flight.

Pardew had played for Crystal Palace in the 1990 final, when they were minutes from beating Manchester United and ended up drawing. This time, he said afterwards, he was three minutes short.

* * *

The fourth official held up the board for stoppage time. Crouch said later that when it went up the players thought their chance had gone.

Riise put a ball into the box from the left. West Ham's tired defenders cleared it only as far as Gerrard, about 35 yards out. He hit it first time, low and hard, past Hislop.

It was 3–3. Gerrard had scored twice and set up the other.

In extra time players on both sides went down with cramp, Gerrard among them. West Ham had the last chance. In the final minutes Konchesky swung in a free-kick from the left, Reina turned Reo-Coker's header onto the post, and Harewood, barely able to run, put the rebound wide.

* * *

The shoot-out was taken at the West Ham end. Didi Hamann, on as a substitute, scored first. Reina saved from Bobby Zamora. Hislop saved from Hyypiä. Teddy Sheringham scored. Gerrard, who had needed treatment for cramp, scored. Reina saved from Konchesky. Riise, who had missed in the shoot-out in Istanbul, scored. Reina saved from Ferdinand.

Liverpool had won 3–1 on penalties. Riise told reporters he had burst into tears after scoring, and called it closure. The Aberdeen Press and Journal noted that Reina had a reputation in Spain for saving penalties.

* * *

Benítez said the team should enjoy it and then keep going over the summer, because the aim was to challenge for the league. Liverpool had won the Super Cup and the FA Cup that season, he said, and had closed the gap on the top teams.

Pardew said it had taken a 35-yard strike to stop his side. Reo-Coker said he was gutted, because he felt West Ham had deserved to win. Crouch said it was always good to have a player like Gerrard in the team.

The Evening Herald's verdict was that the final belonged to Gerrard. In 2015 a BBC Sport poll named his equaliser the best goal in an FA Cup final in the previous 50 years.

Liverpool's first season in Europe had already been eventful. They had beaten KR Reykjavík and Anderlecht, and needed three matches to get past Cologne, the third a 2–2 draw in Rotterdam settled on the toss of a coin. That put them in the semi-final of the European Cup against Internazionale of Milan.

Inter were the reigning European and world club champions. Helenio Herrera's team had won the European Cup in 1964 and the world club championship that September. They had Giacinto Facchetti, the attacking left-back, Tarcisio Burgnich and Armando Picchi in defence, Luis Suárez in midfield, and Sandro Mazzola, Jair, Joaquín Peiró and Mario Corso in attack.

The first leg was at Anfield on Tuesday 4 May 1965, three days after Liverpool had beaten Leeds United at Wembley to win the FA Cup for the first time. That final had gone to extra time, and the Wembley pitch had been heavy. Liverpool's players were expected to be tired.

Shankly was without Gordon Milne and Gerry Byrne, both injured. He picked Tommy Lawrence; Chris Lawler, Ronnie Moran; Geoff Strong, Ron Yeats, Willie Stevenson; Ian Callaghan, Roger Hunt, Ian St John, Tommy Smith, Peter Thompson.

* * *

Before kick-off Shankly arranged things carefully. The Inter players were allowed to take the field first. Then Byrne, his arm in a sling, and Milne came out carrying the FA Cup and walked it round the ground. Tommy Smith later told the Liverpool Echo: "If the Italians thought they had heard noise in the San Siro I can tell you they had never experienced anything like the noise from the Anfield fans." The rest of the Liverpool team followed.

Hunt scored after four minutes. Callaghan crossed from the right and Hunt volleyed it in from about 14 yards. The Scotsman's report called it a brilliant goal and said it set the game alight.

Inter showed early on how good they were. The Dundee Courier wrote that they had mastered all the football arts and played them at amazing speed. After ten minutes Facchetti played a long ball through, Peiró ran on to it and pulled it back, and Mazzola equalised with a fine shot. Lawrence then had to come out and save at the feet of Jair.

Liverpool steadied, and the Courier gave much of the credit to Smith, playing as a sweeper behind the defence. After 34 minutes they went back in front with a free-kick routine: Stevenson played it quickly, Hunt helped it on, and Callaghan darted in to score.

* * *

In the second half Liverpool did most of the attacking. The Courier described them playing ideal continental football, falling back into shape like the pieces of a jigsaw whenever Inter tried to break.

The third goal came after 75 minutes. Smith broke forward and lobbed the ball through for Hunt, who looked offside. The referee let play go on, Hunt shot, Giuliano Sarti pushed it out, and St John was there to put in the rebound.

Sarti kept the score at 3–1, with saves from St John, Hunt and Strong late on. At the final whistle some supporters slipped past the police to congratulate the players. The Courier and the Scotsman both called it the finest game seen at Anfield for years.

Shankly said he was proud of every one of his players. They had proved fitter than the Italians, he said, and just as able to adapt to any style of football. "We played so well that we should be going to Milan with at least a four-goal advantage," he added.

* * *

The second leg was played at the San Siro eight days later. Inter won 3–0 and went through 4–3 on aggregate.

Two of the goals came in the first ten minutes. Corso scored from what Liverpool insisted was an indirect free-kick, and Peiró put the ball in after kicking it out of Lawrence's hands as he prepared to clear. Facchetti scored the third in the second half. Shankly wrote in his autobiography that Liverpool did not think two of the goals were legal. In the 1970s a Sunday Times investigation by Brian Glanville and Keith Botsford alleged that referees had been bribed in Inter's European Cup semi-finals of 1964 and 1965, this one included.

Inter went on to beat Benfica in the final and keep the trophy. Liverpool would not reach another European Cup semi-final for 12 years.

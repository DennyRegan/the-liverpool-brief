In January 1996 the Premier League title looked settled. By the 20th of that month Kevin Keegan's Newcastle were 12 points clear of Manchester United. By the end of March the lead was gone. United had won 1–0 at St James' Park in early March, and on 23 March Newcastle lost 2–0 at Arsenal. Going into April, United led the table on 67 points from 32 games; Newcastle had 64 from 30.

The Scotsman counted only four points from Newcastle's previous 15. They needed to win at Anfield on Wednesday 3 April to stay in touch.

Roy Evans's side were third, eight points behind United with a game in hand, and the Scotsman noted they had lost only one of their previous 22 matches. That one defeat had come at Nottingham Forest on the same day Newcastle lost at Highbury. Three days before Newcastle's visit they had beaten Aston Villa 3–0 in the FA Cup semi-final.

The match had originally been scheduled for the Saturday, and was moved to the Wednesday night for Sky because of the FA Cup.

Evans played the three-man defence he had used through the season: David James; Mark Wright, John Scales, Neil Ruddock; Jason McAteer and Rob Jones as wing-backs; Jamie Redknapp and John Barnes in central midfield; Steve McManaman behind Robbie Fowler and Stan Collymore.

Keegan picked Pavel Srníček; Steve Watson, Steve Howey, Philippe Albert, John Beresford; Peter Beardsley, David Batty, Rob Lee, David Ginola; Les Ferdinand, Faustino Asprilla.

* * *

Liverpool scored inside two minutes. Wright moved the ball out to the right, Fowler spread it wide and on to Redknapp, whose long diagonal pass was knocked on first time by Jones. Collymore got past Watson and crossed deep, and Fowler's downward header bounced into the net.

Newcastle were level after ten minutes. Asprilla cut the ball back and Ferdinand turned and shot. Martin Lipton's report put the blame squarely on James, as the ball brushed past his shoulder and through his hands.

Four minutes later Newcastle were in front. Ferdinand sent Ginola clear, Wright having mistimed his attempt to catch him offside, and Ginola finished. It was 2–1 to Newcastle with 14 minutes gone, and it stayed that way to half-time. Steve Harkness replaced Wright at the interval.

* * *

Ten minutes into the second half Fowler equalised. McAteer found McManaman on the right, and McManaman, losing his balance, still played the ball square along the edge of the area for Fowler to drive in low.

The scores were level for less than two minutes. Lee played Asprilla in, James came out to meet him, and Asprilla clipped the ball past him with the outside of his right foot. It was 3–2 to Newcastle.

Lipton wrote that the travelling supporters were by now beginning to think of drawing level with United, with a game in hand. Then, with 22 minutes left, McAteer curled a cross between Srníček and the defenders, and Collymore got there first. It was 3–3.

Late on, Howey went off injured and Darren Peacock replaced him, and Evans brought on Ian Rush for Jones.

In the second minute of stoppage time Liverpool attacked again. Barnes and Rush exchanged passes on the edge of the area, and nobody had picked up Collymore's run in from the left. Barnes found him, and Collymore drove the ball past Srníček at the Kop end.

The television cameras caught Keegan slumped over the advertising hoardings in front of the dugout.

* * *

The Yorkshire Post's report described Newcastle as broken and heavy-hearted, and noted that their supporters applauded them off. It suggested the biggest winner of the night might be the Manchester United manager.

"We carry on playing this way or I go," Keegan told reporters. He called the game a classic, said that even in defeat he was not as disappointed as he should have been, and joked that he did not know what Liverpool charged for admission but did not think anyone would be asking for a refund. He also said Liverpool had kept at it to the end like the Liverpool of 20 years earlier.

Collymore said the result kept the title race wide open. McManaman, voted man of the match, said Liverpool had given away bad goals but shown great resilience, and that Newcastle were a great team.

Evans was more critical. He said the entertainment value was up there with the best, but admitted that no side could win the championship defending like that.

Newcastle never got back on top. They finished second, four points behind United, and Keegan left the club the following January. Liverpool finished third. Less than six weeks after the Newcastle game, United beat them 1–0 in the FA Cup final.

In 2003 the game was named the Premier League's match of the decade. And when Newcastle came back to Anfield in March 1997, the score was 4–3 again.

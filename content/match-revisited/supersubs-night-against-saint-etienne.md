In March 1977 Liverpool had not reached a European Cup semi-final for 12 years. The last time, in 1965, they had gone out to Inter Milan. Bob Paisley's side were league champions and UEFA Cup holders, and this was the club's fourth attempt at the European Cup.

Their quarter-final opponents were the French champions. Saint-Étienne had reached the final the previous May and lost 1–0 to Bayern Munich in Glasgow. They had Dominique Rocheteau, the young winger, Jean-Michel Larqué in midfield, Christian Lopez in the centre of defence and Ivan Ćurković, the Yugoslav, in goal.

The first leg in France on 2 March went their way. Dominique Bathenay scored 12 minutes from time, and Saint-Étienne won 1–0. A 1–0 win at Anfield would only take the tie to extra time. If Saint-Étienne scored, Liverpool would need to win by two.

Paisley picked Ray Clemence; Phil Neal, Joey Jones, Tommy Smith, Ray Kennedy, Emlyn Hughes; Kevin Keegan, Jimmy Case, Steve Heighway, John Toshack, Ian Callaghan. David Fairclough, the 20-year-old the Kop already called "Supersub", was on the bench.

* * *

The official attendance was 55,043, and Horace Yates of the Liverpool Daily Post reported that thousands more were locked out. He also noted that the referee had started the match two minutes early, so some supporters were still struggling to their seats when Liverpool scored.

The goal came inside two minutes. Heighway took a short corner, and Keegan ran forward a few yards and chipped the ball towards goal. Yates described the shot as a deliberate chip over Ćurković, who was standing off his line, with a helpful wind carrying it in. The Scotsman's reporter thought it was a cross the goalkeeper misjudged. Either way, it was 1–0, and the aggregate score was level.

Yates described a defence that began at halfway and caught Liverpool time and again in no-man's-land. Rocheteau and Larqué caused problems going forward, and the Scotsman credited Clemence with three spectacular saves. Late in the first half Smith went on a solo run through the massed French defence and the move ended with Toshack heading over, but Liverpool led only 1–0 at the interval.

* * *

Six minutes into the second half, Heighway lost the ball, and Bathenay, who had scored in the first leg, picked it up 30 yards out and beat Clemence with a left-footed shot.

Saint-Étienne now had an away goal. Liverpool needed two more just to go through.

They got the first within ten minutes. Callaghan crossed, Toshack's presence unsettled the defence, and the ball came to Kennedy on the edge of the penalty area. His shot went in, by LFChistory's account off Toshack's shin. It was 2–1 on the night, 2–2 on aggregate, and Saint-Étienne were still ahead on away goals.

With 17 minutes left Paisley made his change. He took off Toshack and sent on Fairclough. Yates was careful to say this was no criticism of Toshack. It was a gamble on Fairclough's speed.

Six minutes from the end, Kennedy won the ball in midfield and sent a high ball forward. Fairclough ran on to it, held off Lopez, and with Ćurković coming out, put the ball past him into the net.

Yates wrote that he had seldom seen excitement to match it. The Kop, by his account, became a sea of unbroken red, and the crowd sang that they were going to Italy, where the final would be played in May.

* * *

Fairclough had a simple explanation. People said he was lucky, he told reporters, and perhaps he was, but he had reckoned he would get at least one chance while he was on the pitch, and he had had plenty of time to decide where to put it.

Paisley praised the team's character in coming back to score twice. "They never know when they are beaten," he said, and called it a tremendous team performance against a very good side.

Lopez blamed himself. He told the French press that he had been too hesitant, that he should have attacked the ball as it hung in the air, and that he had given Fairclough a tug as he went past. The next day Le Figaro wrote that Fairclough had stabbed Les Verts, and L'Équipe said Saint-Étienne had played intelligently against an enterprising side who had turned the tie around.

Liverpool were through to the European Cup semi-finals for the second time in their history. They beat FC Zürich 6–1 on aggregate, and on 25 May, in Rome, they won the European Cup for the first time.

By the spring of 1981 some critics thought Liverpool's time had passed. Bob Paisley's side finished fifth in the First Division, and the only way back into the European Cup the following season was to win it. They had won the League Cup for the first time, beating West Ham after a replay, but the league form had been patchy all year.

The road to Paris had been hard on the squad. In the semi-final against Bayern Munich, Liverpool drew 0–0 at Anfield and went through on away goals after a 1–1 draw in Munich, where Ray Kennedy scored late. Alan Kennedy broke his wrist in the first leg. Phil Thompson, the captain, missed the second. Kenny Dalglish was injured after nine minutes in Munich and had not played since.

Alan Kennedy, Thompson and Dalglish were all in the side for the final. The Liverpool Daily Post's Nick Hilton noted that Alan Kennedy had been out for six weeks and his chances of playing had once looked remote. Paisley picked him anyway, to counter Real's right winger Juanito.

Liverpool lined up: Ray Clemence; Phil Neal, Phil Thompson, Alan Hansen, Alan Kennedy; Sammy Lee, Terry McDermott, Graeme Souness, Ray Kennedy; Kenny Dalglish, David Johnson.

Real Madrid were six-time European champions, coached by Vujadin Boškov. They had Uli Stielike in midfield, José Antonio Camacho at left-back, Juanito and Santillana in attack, and Laurie Cunningham, the English winger, back from a long injury. Their first-choice goalkeeper was out, so Agustín played. Hilton reported that Boškov had predicted Real would run Liverpool's old men off their feet.

* * *

The game was played at the Parc des Princes in front of 48,360 people. Real's tackling was ferocious from the start. In the first minute, Bob Driscoll of the Daily Star reported, a two-footed tackle from Camacho tore a hole in Neal's sock. The Scotsman's reporter called Real's tackling in the opening spell not so much hard as intimidating, and noted that Souness, whom they targeted, could look after himself.

Liverpool had the better of the first 20 minutes. Alan Kennedy tested Agustín with a low drive. In the tenth minute Dalglish sent Lee running forward and McDermott shot just over. Two minutes later Dalglish turned in a tight space in the area and Agustín had to block. Just before half-time Dalglish held the ball up on the edge of the box and laid it into Souness's path, and the goalkeeper needed two attempts to stop the shot.

Real came into it as the half went on, sending men down the flanks. Cunningham ran hard and often at Neal and Alan Kennedy, the Scotsman wrote, and only his final pass let him down. Just after half-time Camacho wriggled away from Hansen on the edge of the area and, with Clemence off his line, lobbed just wide. It was the clearest chance Real had.

Hilton described an absorbing contest rather than a classic, between two accomplished sides with very different styles. Real man-marked, with Cortés on Dalglish. Liverpool kept their shape and gave Real's forwards little room.

* * *

With eight or nine minutes left, Ray Kennedy took a throw-in on the left. Alan Kennedy ran forward from left-back, took the ball on his chest, went past Cortés and, from a narrow angle on the left of the area, hit a shot that went in high past Agustín. He kept running towards the Liverpool supporters behind the goal.

Afterwards Kennedy told John Keith of the Daily Express that he thought he had mis-kicked it. He had been left in space with defenders backing off, he said, and where in the past he might have passed, this time he decided to have a go. The ball seemed to take a long time to reach the net.

Dalglish limped off with four minutes to go and Jimmy Case replaced him. At the end Thompson, the captain, lifted the trophy.

* * *

Liverpool had become the first British club to win the European Cup three times. Paisley, who had replaced Bill Shankly seven years earlier, now had ten major trophies.

Paisley called it a triumph for the team's character. They had started with three players short of match practice, Dalglish, Thompson and Alan Kennedy, he said, and Souness had been hurt soon after kick-off. He also admitted that the game had not been the showpiece the occasion deserved.

Thompson, who had just been left out of the England squad, said Liverpool had joined the greats and beaten two of them, Bayern and Real, to do it.

It was the last match for Liverpool for two of the side. Clemence joined Tottenham that summer, and Case went to Brighton.

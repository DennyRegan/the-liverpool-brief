On 1 May 2019, in the first leg of the Champions League semi-final, Liverpool played well at the Camp Nou and lost 3–0. Luis Suárez scored against his old club in the first half, and Lionel Messi added two late goals, the second a free-kick from long range. Jürgen Klopp said afterwards that his side could hardly have played much better.

It still meant Liverpool needed to win the second leg 4–0, or score three without reply to take it to extra time, against a side with Messi, Suárez, Philippe Coutinho, Jordi Alba and Ivan Rakitić.

And they would have to do it without two of their front three. Mohamed Salah had a concussion from the league game at Newcastle the previous Saturday, and Roberto Firmino had a muscle injury. Naby Keïta was also out. Liverpool were in a title race with Manchester City as well, which was not settled until the final day.

Klopp named Alisson; Trent Alexander-Arnold, Joel Matip, Virgil van Dijk, Andy Robertson; Fabinho, Jordan Henderson, James Milner; Xherdan Shaqiri, Divock Origi, Sadio Mané.

A year earlier, Barcelona had beaten Roma 4–1 at home in the Champions League quarter-final and then lost the second leg 3–0 in Rome.

* * *

Liverpool scored after seven minutes. Alba's weak header fell to Mané, who played in Henderson, and Henderson ran into the area and his shot was parried by Marc-André ter Stegen. Origi was there to put in the rebound.

For the rest of the first half Barcelona had the chances. Alisson saved from Messi, held an effort from Coutinho and blocked Alba at close range after a pass from Messi. Liverpool led only 1–0 at half-time, 3–1 down on aggregate, and Robertson did not come out for the second half. Georginio Wijnaldum replaced him, with Milner moving to left-back.

On 54 minutes Alexander-Arnold crossed from the right, and Wijnaldum met it with a low drive that went under ter Stegen. Two minutes later Shaqiri crossed from the left and Wijnaldum headed in. It was 3–0 on the night, 3–3 on aggregate.

* * *

Liverpool still needed one more, and Barcelona still had Messi and Suárez. Alisson saved a low drive from Suárez. Ter Stegen blocked a backheeled volley from Van Dijk.

Then, after 79 minutes, Liverpool won a corner on the right. Alexander-Arnold placed the ball and started to walk away as Shaqiri came across to take it. Barcelona's players, assuming there would be a delay, turned their backs and began to organise.

Alexander-Arnold turned back and played the ball low into the six-yard area. Barcelona had not reacted. Origi had, and he swept it first time into the top corner. Klopp said later that he saw the ball go in without knowing who had taken the corner or who had scored.

A teenage ball boy, Oakley Cannonier, an academy player, had sent a second ball to Alexander-Arnold quickly. The ball boys had been told before the game to return the ball as fast as they could.

Liverpool held on through eleven minutes and stoppage time. At the end the players stood in front of the Kop and sang "You'll Never Walk Alone" with the supporters. Milner was seen in tears on the pitch.

* * *

Klopp's verdict, given to BT Sport straight after the match, was that his players were "mentality giants". He said that winning was difficult enough, and that he did not know how they had done it with a clean sheet. He also said he thought Liverpool had been better than the 3–0 in Barcelona, and that Barcelona had probably been better than the 4–0 at Anfield.

Ernesto Valverde, the Barcelona coach, called it a terrible result for his club's supporters and his players, but said credit was due to Liverpool.

Liverpool lost the title to City by a single point, finishing with 97. On 1 June in Madrid they beat Tottenham 2–0 in the Champions League final. Origi scored the second.

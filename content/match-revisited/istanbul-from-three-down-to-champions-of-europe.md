Liverpool's first season under Rafael Benítez was uneven. They finished fifth in the Premier League. In Europe they needed a late Steven Gerrard goal to beat Olympiacos and get out of their group, then knocked out Bayer Leverkusen, Juventus and Chelsea to reach the Champions League final in Istanbul on 25 May 2005.

Their opponents were AC Milan, European champions two years earlier and second in Serie A. Carlo Ancelotti's side had Dida in goal; Cafu, Jaap Stam, Alessandro Nesta and Paolo Maldini in defence; Gennaro Gattuso, Andrea Pirlo, Clarence Seedorf and Kaká in midfield; and Andriy Shevchenko and Hernán Crespo up front.

Liverpool had last won the European Cup in Rome in 1984, 21 years earlier. Benítez's side was Jerzy Dudek; Steve Finnan, Jamie Carragher, Sami Hyypiä, Djimi Traoré; Luis García, Xabi Alonso, Steven Gerrard, John Arne Riise; Harry Kewell, Milan Baroš.

* * *

Milan scored after 52 seconds. Pirlo's free-kick came in from the right and Maldini, the Milan captain, volleyed it into the ground and past Dudek.

Kewell went off injured after 23 minutes and Vladimír Šmicer replaced him. García cleared off the line, and Shevchenko had a goal ruled out for offside.

Just before the goal that made it 2–0, Liverpool thought they should have had a penalty. García turned inside Nesta, who appeared to handle the ball, and the referee waved play on. Within seconds Milan were scoring at the other end. Kaká sent Shevchenko clear, and his cutback gave Crespo a tap-in.

A minute before half-time Kaká played a pass through Liverpool's defence that left Carragher stretching, and Crespo ran on and lifted the ball over Dudek. It was 3–0 at the interval.

* * *

Benítez changed the shape. Dietmar Hamann came on for Finnan, Liverpool went to three at the back, and Gerrard was given licence to go forward. According to the Dundee Courier, the Liverpool supporters chanted that they were going to win 4–3 as the teams came back out.

Dudek made a fine save from Shevchenko early in the half. Then, on 54 minutes, Riise crossed from the left at the second attempt and Gerrard looped a header over Dida. He ran back towards the halfway line, waving his arms at the supporters.

Two minutes later Šmicer shot from about 25 yards and the ball slipped past Dida inside the post. It was 3–2.

Three minutes after that Gerrard ran on to a pass from Baroš inside the area and was brought down by Gattuso. Alonso took the penalty. Dida dived to save it, and Alonso followed up and smashed the rebound into the roof of the net. Liverpool had scored three times in six minutes. It was 3–3.

Traoré cleared off the line, Carragher made last-ditch tackles on Shevchenko and Kaká, and, by the Courier's account, Stam headed wide. Djibril Cissé replaced Baroš. In extra time Carragher was suffering from cramp. Gerrard said later that they were running on empty and, if he was honest, playing for penalties.

In the last minutes of extra time Shevchenko had a header from close range, Dudek saved it, and the ball came straight back to Shevchenko a yard or two out. Dudek got a hand to that as well and the ball went over the bar.

* * *

Before the shoot-out, Carragher went to Dudek and told him to put the Milan takers off as Bruce Grobbelaar had done in Rome in 1984. Dudek said afterwards that Grobbelaar had been his inspiration.

Serginho shot over the bar. Hamann, who had been playing with a broken toe, scored. Dudek saved from Pirlo. Cissé scored. Jon Dahl Tomasson scored for Milan. Dida saved from Riise. Kaká scored. Šmicer, in his last game for the club, scored.

That left Shevchenko needing to score to keep Milan in it. He went down the middle. Dudek, moving to his right, kept it out with his left hand.

* * *

Gerrard, who lifted the trophy, called it the greatest night of his life. At half-time, he said, he had thought it was impossible, and he admitted that Milan had deserved to be three goals up.

The captain also used the night to address his future. "How can I leave after a night like this and all the nights that I have experienced?" he asked reporters.

Ancelotti spoke of six minutes of madness in which his team had thrown away a strong position. Benítez said the early goal in the second half had changed everything, because the players started believing and the supporters lifted them further.

Because they had finished fifth, Liverpool had not qualified for the following season's Champions League through the league. Gerrard and Benítez said the holders should be allowed to defend the trophy, and the Aberdeen Press and Journal reported that Ancelotti said the same. UEFA later let them enter at the first qualifying round. Having won the competition for the fifth time, Liverpool kept the trophy for good.

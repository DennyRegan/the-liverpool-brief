In the summer of 1977 Kevin Keegan left for Hamburg, and Bob Paisley replaced him with Kenny Dalglish from Celtic. A year later Liverpool were back in the European Cup final, defending the trophy they had won in Rome.

As holders, they had a bye in the first round. They then beat Dynamo Dresden 6–3 on aggregate and Benfica 6–2, and in the semi-final met Borussia Mönchengladbach again, losing 2–1 in Germany before winning 3–0 at Anfield.

The final was at Wembley on Wednesday 10 May 1978. The Daily Record's Alex Cameron pointed out that Liverpool had not won on their three previous Wembley visits, in 1977 and 1978: they had lost the 1977 FA Cup final to Manchester United, drawn with United in the Charity Shield, and drawn the League Cup final with Nottingham Forest before losing the replay at Old Trafford.

Their opponents were Club Brugge, the first Belgian side to reach a European Cup final, coached by the Austrian Ernst Happel. Liverpool had beaten them in the 1976 UEFA Cup final. Brugge had knocked out Atlético Madrid and then Juventus, after extra time, to get to Wembley. But they were without Raoul Lambert, their striker, and Happel said afterwards that injuries to two players had handicapped his side.

Liverpool had problems too. Tommy Smith had broken a toe after dropping a pick-axe on his foot, and David Johnson had damaged knee ligaments. Paisley picked Ray Clemence; Phil Neal, Phil Thompson, Alan Hansen, Emlyn Hughes; Jimmy Case, Terry McDermott, Graeme Souness, Ray Kennedy; David Fairclough, Kenny Dalglish.

* * *

The Dundee Courier's report said Brugge back-pedalled continuously, and Liverpool did nearly all the attacking. Souness, by the Courier's account, spent the night taunting the Brugge defence with passes that split it open.

The problem was Birger Jensen, the Brugge goalkeeper. He blocked Fairclough when he ran through, tipped a header from Hansen over the bar before half-time, and saved from McDermott early in the second half. The Courier said he was in inspired form.

Paisley sent on Steve Heighway for Case to give the side more width on the right. A couple of minutes later Liverpool scored. Souness picked the ball up about six yards outside the area, worked his way past a defender and rolled it into the area to Dalglish on the right. The Courier reported that the Brugge defenders stopped for a moment, looking for an offside flag. Dalglish went on, and as Jensen came out to narrow the angle, he lifted the ball over him from a sharp angle and into the net.

The papers could not agree on the minute: the Courier said 61, the Daily Record and the Daily Mirror 65. LFChistory's records give 64.

* * *

The Courier thought the margin could have been much larger, but the one real scare came at the other end. With about ten minutes left Hansen played a poor back-pass and Jan Sørensen got to it first. Clemence came out and blocked his shot, the ball ran to Jan Simoen, who shot at what looked an open goal, and Thompson stuck out his left foot on the line to clear. Cameron noted it was Hansen's one slip on an otherwise impressive night.

It stayed 1–0. Liverpool were the first British club to win the European Cup twice, and the first to keep it.

At the end Dalglish put on a supporter's cap and joined the lap of honour. He said it was wonderful to win his first European Cup medal with a club like Liverpool, and that the victory was a team effort. Paisley said Liverpool had controlled the game from start to finish and that the Brugge goalkeeper had made some excellent saves. Smith, who had missed the final, was scathing about Brugge's approach: "It was a pathetic attitude. You never win anything like that."

Happel was not impressed either. He called it a weak final and said he had been disappointed with Liverpool, who were a shadow of the side he had faced two years earlier. Still, he accepted that they had deserved to win.

Three years later Liverpool won the European Cup again, in Paris.

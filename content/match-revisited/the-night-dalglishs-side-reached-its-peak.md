In April 1988 Liverpool played Nottingham Forest three times in 11 days. On Saturday 2 April Forest beat Liverpool 2–1 at the City Ground, only the second league defeat of Liverpool's season. A week later the two met again in the FA Cup semi-final, and Liverpool won 2–1, both goals scored by John Aldridge. On Wednesday 13 April, four days after the semi-final, Forest came to Anfield for the league fixture.

Brian Clough's side were a good team and a young one. LFChistory puts the average age of the Forest eleven that night at under 23, against more than 27 for Liverpool's, and the Liverpool Daily Post noted that many people considered Forest the next season's title contenders. Across the first two meetings, the Derby Evening Telegraph observed, Forest had proved themselves Liverpool's equal.

Liverpool, though, were close to the title. They had gone the first 29 league matches of the season unbeaten, a run that equalled the Football League record and ended only with a defeat at Goodison Park in March. By the Daily Post's reckoning, a win against Forest would leave them two points from the championship.

This was a new side. Kenny Dalglish, now picking himself only occasionally, had rebuilt it in a year. John Barnes had arrived from Watford and Peter Beardsley from Newcastle the previous summer. Aldridge had come from Oxford United to replace Ian Rush, who had left for Juventus, and Ray Houghton had followed him from Oxford in the autumn. Barnes would be the PFA's player of the year that season.

Dalglish named the same eleven that had won the semi-final: Bruce Grobbelaar; Gary Gillespie, Gary Ablett, Steve Nicol, Nigel Spackman, Alan Hansen; Peter Beardsley, John Aldridge, Ray Houghton, John Barnes, Steve McMahon.

* * *

For the first quarter of an hour Forest held their own. The Derby Evening Telegraph's reporter thought it looked like being another close duel that would hang in the balance all night.

Liverpool were already pressing. Philip McNulty, reporting for the Daily Post, had Barnes forcing Steve Sutton into a low save after 11 minutes, and Sutton in action again less than a minute later, turning a fierce shot from Nicol over the bar.

The first goal came after 17 or 18 minutes. Houghton and Barnes exchanged passes inside the penalty area, and Houghton ran on to slide the ball past Sutton.

After that, wrote McNulty, Liverpool carved out chance after chance. Barnes came close twice. Then Beardsley split Forest's central defenders with a pass, and Aldridge ran clear and lifted the ball over Sutton as he came out. It was Aldridge's 26th goal of the season. Within a minute Beardsley had skipped past two tackles on the edge of the box and hit the crossbar.

Liverpool led 2–0 at half-time. The Daily Post called the first 45 minutes devastating, even by Liverpool's standards, and said Forest had been left battered and bewildered.

* * *

Forest's problems got worse at the interval. Des Walker, the centre-half, had aggravated an ankle injury early in the game, and he did not come out for the second half. Darren Wassall replaced him.

Sutton was the only thing keeping the score down. Just short of the hour he made a fine save from Beardsley, and from the corner that followed Gillespie volleyed in with his left foot from about 12 yards. It was 3–0.

McNulty noted that Hansen, the captain, passed up a chance to score after 67 minutes by trying to set up Aldridge instead. Then Barnes left two defenders behind near the byline and squared the ball for Beardsley to drive past Sutton for the fourth, after 79 minutes. The Derby Evening Telegraph, writing for readers who followed Forest, judged that Beardsley, at his England best, had a hand in all four goals after the first.

With two minutes left Beardsley and Spackman worked another opening, and Aldridge scored his second. Liverpool had won 5–0, their biggest win of the season, in front of 39,535 at Anfield.

* * *

The Derby Evening Telegraph noted that it equalled the heaviest defeat Forest had suffered under Clough. It also gave the young side credit for keeping going to the end without once resorting to a dubious tackle, and said Sutton had saved them from greater embarrassment.

Maurice Roworth, the Forest chairman, was the most generous of all. "I don't think there is another side in Europe who could have competed against them," he told reporters.

Dalglish was pleased, though more measured. He called it a tremendous performance and said that every goal had been a great goal. He told the Daily Post that it would have been easy for the players to become complacent after the semi-final, and that their hard work was sometimes overlooked because of how well they played. "We have got six games to win the title and we need two points," he said.

The players spoke more freely. Barnes, whom the Daily Star named the night's outstanding performer, told the paper it was easily the best Liverpool had played all season, and in agency reports said he would like to think they could get better still. Beardsley said it was the best they had played since he arrived, especially as it came against one of the three best teams in the country. Hansen, according to the Star, said that when players like Barnes and Beardsley hit their peak, nobody in the country could match them.

Two days later Tommy Lawton, the former England centre-forward, used his column in the Nottingham Evening Post to agree with his old England team-mate Tom Finney, who had watched the match and judged Liverpool the best side since the war. Finney's verdict on the night itself, as LFChistory records it, was that it was the finest exhibition he had seen in all his time playing and watching the game.

* * *

Liverpool drew 0–0 at Norwich the following Wednesday, and on 23 April Beardsley scored the only goal against Tottenham at Anfield to confirm the club's 17th league championship with four games to spare. Liverpool finished with 90 points, nine clear of Manchester United, and lost only two of their 40 league matches.

They did not win the double. On 14 May Wimbledon beat them 1–0 in the FA Cup final.

Years later Barnes told LFChistory that the match stood out above any other in his career, because it was the best team performance he had been part of.

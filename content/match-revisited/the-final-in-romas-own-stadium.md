Joe Fagan had been on Liverpool's coaching staff for more than 20 years when he replaced Bob Paisley as manager in the summer of 1983. In his first season the side won the league and the League Cup, beating Everton in a replay. On 30 May 1984 they played in the European Cup final for the fourth time, chasing a treble.

The final was in Rome, at the Stadio Olimpico, where Liverpool had won the trophy for the first time in 1977. This time the stadium was also their opponents' home ground. AS Roma, coached by the Swede Nils Liedholm, had reached the final after beating Dundee United in the semi-final, and the bookmakers made them favourites. They had two Brazilians, Falcão and Toninho Cerezo, in midfield, and Bruno Conti and Francesco Graziani, both World Cup winners with Italy in 1982.

Fagan picked Bruce Grobbelaar; Phil Neal, Mark Lawrenson, Alan Hansen, Alan Kennedy; Craig Johnston, Sammy Lee, Graeme Souness, Ronnie Whelan; Kenny Dalglish, Ian Rush. Alex Cameron of the Daily Record estimated 10,000 Liverpool supporters in a crowd of 69,693.

* * *

The Daily Express's reporter described Liverpool settling at once into a patient, disciplined pattern after a nervous start, with Souness controlling midfield. They went ahead after about a quarter of an hour. Johnston sent a high cross into the area, and the Roma goalkeeper Franco Tancredi went up for it with Whelan and dropped it. A Roma defender's attempted clearance struck the grounded goalkeeper and ran loose, and Neal, the only survivor of the 1977 final, stabbed it in. Tancredi thought he had been fouled. Cameron noted that the referee, Erik Fredriksson, had a clear view and gave the goal.

Rush almost added a second, but Tancredi dived to keep out his left-footed shot from 12 yards. Shortly before half-time Roma equalised. Conti crossed from the left, and Roberto Pruzzo, facing away from goal, guided the ball with the back of his head over Grobbelaar and into the net. The Scotsman's agency reporter rated it among the finest goals of that season's competition.

* * *

In the second half Liverpool slowed the game down. Steve Nicol came on for Johnston after 72 minutes. Dalglish forced a spectacular one-handed save from Tancredi, but he was tiring, and early in extra time Michael Robinson replaced him. Cameron thought Liverpool looked the more likely winners as players tired, but nobody scored, and the final went to penalties.

Souness, the captain, chose Nicol, 22, to take the first. He shot over the bar. Agostino Di Bartolomei scored for Roma. Neal scored. Conti shot over. Souness scored. Ubaldo Righetti scored. Rush scored.

That left Graziani needing to score. As he placed the ball, Grobbelaar wobbled his legs on the goal-line as if they had turned to jelly. Graziani's kick went over the bar. Cameron, in the Daily Record, saw it clip the woodwork on the way.

Alan Kennedy, who had scored the winner against Real Madrid in 1981, took the fifth. He sent Tancredi the wrong way and Liverpool had won 4–2.

* * *

The Express's reporter described Souness on his knees in tears at the end. Liverpool had won a treble, and their fourth European Cup.

Fagan was sympathetic to Roma. He said a replay would have been a fairer way to settle it, but the competition's rules required a winner. "It just turned out to be us," he told reporters.

Liedholm said it had been a great game, against a stronger team than he had expected.

It was Souness's last game for Liverpool before his move to Sampdoria.

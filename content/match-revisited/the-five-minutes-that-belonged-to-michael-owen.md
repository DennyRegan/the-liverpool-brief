The 2001 FA Cup final was the first ever played outside England. Wembley had closed for rebuilding, so the final went to the Millennium Stadium in Cardiff, where Liverpool had already won the League Cup that season, beating Birmingham City on penalties in February.

Gérard Houllier's side were chasing a treble no English club had managed: the League Cup, the FA Cup and the UEFA Cup in one season. Four days after the FA Cup final they were due to play Alavés in the UEFA Cup final in Dortmund, and the Saturday after that they needed to beat Charlton on the last day of the league season to finish third and reach the Champions League. The Cardiff final was the 61st game of a 63-game season.

Arsenal were the league runners-up and the stronger side on paper. Arsène Wenger had Patrick Vieira and Gilles Grimandi in midfield, Thierry Henry and Sylvain Wiltord up front, Robert Pires and Freddie Ljungberg wide, and Tony Adams, Martin Keown, Lee Dixon and Ashley Cole in defence, with David Seaman in goal.

Houllier named Sander Westerveld; Markus Babbel, Stéphane Henchoz, Sami Hyypiä, Jamie Carragher; Danny Murphy, Steven Gerrard, Dietmar Hamann, Vladimír Šmicer; Emile Heskey, Michael Owen. Gary McAllister, Robbie Fowler and Patrik Berger were on the bench. Hyypiä captained the side, with Jamie Redknapp, the club captain, injured.

* * *

It was a hot, sunny afternoon, and Stephen Halliday of the Scotsman described a carnival atmosphere in Cardiff from early morning. The first half did not live up to it. Liverpool, Halliday wrote, were extremely cautious, concentrating on stopping Vieira, and the Arsenal supporters responded with ironic chants of "boring, boring Scousers".

Arsenal had the one real chance of the half, and it should have brought a penalty. After 17 minutes Henry went round Westerveld and shot, and Henchoz blocked the ball on the line. Replays showed it had struck his arm. The referee, Steve Dunn, and his assistant missed it, and did not even give a corner.

* * *

The second half was different, and for most of it Arsenal were on top. Hyypiä cleared off the line more than once. Then, after 72 minutes, Westerveld's clearance went straight to Grimandi, the ball was worked through Pires, and Ljungberg ran on to it, rounded the goalkeeper and scored.

Arsenal could have settled the game within two minutes. Henry checked back inside Henchoz and was through again, but Westerveld saved point-blank and Hyypiä cleared the rebound. Andrew Smith of Scotland on Sunday wrote that Arsenal dominated and were punished for not taking their chances.

Houllier had already changed things. McAllister had come on for Hamann on the hour, and with 13 minutes left Fowler and Berger replaced Šmicer and Murphy. Liverpool now had four attacking players on the pitch.

The equaliser came from McAllister. With seven minutes left he swung in a free-kick, Arsenal's defence failed to clear it, Babbel nodded it back, and the ball dropped to Owen about eight yards out. He turned and volleyed it past Seaman with his right foot. Mark Bradley of the Aberdeen Press and Journal said the Arsenal defence, which had looked so solid, was caught flat-footed.

Extra time looked likely. Instead, with two minutes left, Berger hit a long pass into the space behind Arsenal's defence. Owen beat Dixon and Adams for pace and, from the left side of the area, shot low across Seaman with his left foot into the far corner.

The Bristol Evening Post called it daylight robbery. Owen had broken Arsenal's hearts, its reporter wrote, in the space of seven pulsating minutes.

* * *

Houllier was emotional at the end, and frank about how the match had gone. Arsenal, he admitted to Scotland on Sunday, had more skill and ability than his side in some areas. He had told his players before the game that the ability to win mattered, but that the will to win mattered more.

He was also clear about where the win had come from. Owen had been outstanding in his finishing and always a threat, Houllier said, and if he signed a new contract he was going to cost the club a lot more money.

Wenger said his side had lost a number of games in the last quarter of an hour that season, and that this defeat was very hard to accept.

The goals took Owen to 23 for the season, according to the Press and Journal, and eight in his last four games. The Duke of York presented the Cup, and Redknapp lifted it alongside Fowler. It was Liverpool's first FA Cup since 1992.

Owen was already looking ahead. Even on Wednesday night in Dortmund, he told reporters, the players would have one eye on the Charlton game. Liverpool beat Alavés 5–4 in the UEFA Cup final, completing a treble of cups, and three days later won 4–0 at The Valley to finish third.

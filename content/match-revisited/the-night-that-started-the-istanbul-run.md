Rafael Benítez's first Champions League group as Liverpool manager came down to the last night. Monaco were away to Deportivo La Coruña. Olympiacos, who led the group, were at Anfield. Liverpool were third and had to beat them.

The arithmetic depended on how they won. Olympiacos had beaten Liverpool 1–0 in Piraeus in September, so a 1–0 win at Anfield would be enough, but if Olympiacos scored, Liverpool would need to win by two clear goals.

The Olympiacos side included Rivaldo, 32, the former Barcelona forward and World Cup winner with Brazil. Before the match, Paul Walker of the Press Association noted, the Greek club had never scored in a European match in England and had never won an away match in the Champions League.

Benítez picked Chris Kirkland; Steve Finnan, Jamie Carragher, Sami Hyypiä, Djimi Traoré; Antonio Núñez, Xabi Alonso, Steven Gerrard, John Arne Riise; Harry Kewell, Milan Baroš. Dietmar Hamann was suspended. There had been reports before the game that Gerrard might leave Liverpool.

* * *

Liverpool attacked from the start. Baroš had a goal ruled out early on, and the Liverpool players were already celebrating it before they realised the Spanish referee, Manuel Mejuto González, had blown for a foul on the goalkeeper. Gerrard flicked an Alonso free-kick against the foot of a post.

After 26 minutes Rivaldo picked the ball up near halfway and ran at the Liverpool defence. Hyypiä brought him down about 20 yards out. Henry Winter of the Daily Telegraph described the free-kick going through a gap that opened as the wall shifted, and past Kirkland. By half-time Monaco were 3–0 up in La Coruña.

Liverpool now needed three goals. At half-time, Benítez said afterwards, he decided the answer was his substitute strikers. He took off Traoré, moved Kewell wide and sent on Florent Sinama Pongolle.

* * *

In the second half Liverpool were kicking towards the Kop. Two minutes after the restart Kewell went round his marker and cut the ball back, and Sinama Pongolle scored from close range. Gerrard had another goal disallowed.

Benítez sent on his second striker, Neil Mellor, for Baroš. Within a few minutes, Sinama Pongolle crossed, Núñez headed towards goal, and the Olympiacos goalkeeper Antonios Nikopolidis could only push it out. Mellor hit the rebound into the net. It was 2–1, which was still not enough.

With four minutes left, Carragher chipped the ball into the area. Mellor headed it back towards the edge of the box, where Gerrard was waiting, 20 to 25 yards out. He hit it first time on the half-volley, low into the corner of the Kop goal.

* * *

Monaco had won 5–0 in Spain and topped the group. Liverpool and Olympiacos both finished on ten points, and Liverpool went through on their head-to-head record: 3–2 on goals across the two matches between them.

Gerrard admitted afterwards that he had thought they were out. At half-time, he said, it had looked like a mountain to climb, and the players had heard that Monaco were three up. Benítez said the goal mattered most of all for Gerrard, as captain, because he wanted to win things with the club.

Liverpool beat Bayer Leverkusen, Juventus and Chelsea in the knockout rounds. The final, against AC Milan, was in Istanbul on 25 May 2005.

Kenny Dalglish became Liverpool's player-manager in the summer of 1985, at 34, with no coaching experience. A year later his side were one game from the league and FA Cup double, which Liverpool had never won.

The first part was done a week before Wembley. On 3 May Liverpool won 1–0 at Chelsea, Dalglish scoring the goal himself, and took the title by two points from Everton. The second part meant beating Everton too, in the first FA Cup final between the two Merseyside clubs.

Everton were the champions of the previous season and had finished two points behind Liverpool this time. Gary Lineker, signed from Leicester City the summer before, had been named Footballer of the Year by the football writers. Everton were without their regular goalkeeper, Neville Southall, who had been injured playing for Wales, and Bobby Mimms played instead.

Liverpool lined up: Bruce Grobbelaar; Steve Nicol, Jim Beglin, Mark Lawrenson, Ronnie Whelan, Alan Hansen; Kenny Dalglish, Craig Johnston, Ian Rush, Jan Molby, Kevin MacDonald. Steve McMahon was substitute. The Wolverhampton Express and Star pointed out that there was not an Englishman in the starting XI. The only one on the team sheet, McMahon, was on the bench. The crowd was given as 98,000.

* * *

The best early chance fell to Liverpool. After 13 minutes Beglin met a Nicol cross with a free header from 12 yards and put it well wide. Everton appealed for a penalty when Nicol challenged Graeme Sharp, and gradually they took control.

After 27 minutes Peter Reid sent a long ball over the Liverpool defence and Lineker ran on to it ahead of Hansen. Grobbelaar blocked his first shot, but Lineker reached the rebound first and put it in. It was his 40th goal of the season.

Everton led at half-time and almost doubled it soon after the restart. Paul Hetherington of the Sunday Sun described Kevin Sheedy, a former Liverpool player, coming close twice in a minute. Sheedy shot just wide after hesitation in the Liverpool defence, then Grobbelaar turned a Sheedy free-kick round the post. In the middle of it, Grobbelaar and Beglin had a heated exchange in front of the crowd.

* * *

Just before the hour Gary Stevens, the Everton right-back, gave the ball away. Molby threaded a pass through for Rush, who went round Mimms and scored.

Grobbelaar then turned a Sharp header over the bar, and soon afterwards Liverpool were ahead. Molby went to the left byline and pulled the ball back low. Dalglish let it go past him and Johnston, unmarked at the far post, scored from six yards.

Howard Kendall, the Everton manager, took off Stevens and sent on Adrian Heath with about 20 minutes left, leaving three at the back. Liverpool scored again with six or seven minutes to go. Molby found Whelan, Whelan crossed, and Rush scored with a first-time shot from close range. In the final minute he broke clear again for a hat-trick, but his attempted lob was saved by Mimms.

Hetherington named Molby man of the match. He had a hand in all three goals.

* * *

Liverpool were the fifth club to win the English league and cup double, after Preston, Aston Villa, Tottenham and Arsenal. They had done it in a season in which Manchester United had been ten points clear of them in November.

Dalglish said he was delighted with every member of the team and how they had supported him. Kendall said a lot of clubs would be happy with second in the league and runners-up in the cup. "But we are not one of them," he added.

Rush's two goals made it 33 for the season. That summer he agreed a move to Juventus, but spent one more season at Liverpool on loan. Lineker went to Barcelona after the World Cup, having spent one season at Everton.

Liverpool needed replays to get past Doncaster Rovers and Carlisle United on the way to the 1974 FA Cup final, and another to beat Leicester City in the semi-final. They had finished second in the league, behind Leeds United.

Newcastle United's sixth-round tie against Nottingham Forest had been replayed twice. Their centre-forward, Malcolm Macdonald, known as Supermac, had scored both goals in their semi-final, and in the days before the final he talked freely about what he would do to Liverpool. Bill Shankly, the Liverpool manager, pinned his comments up in the dressing room.

Newcastle's record at Wembley was also on their side. They had played in five FA Cup finals there and won them all. Liverpool had won the cup once, in 1965.

* * *

There was one upset in the Liverpool camp. Phil Boersma had expected to be substitute, and when Chris Lawler was given the place instead, he left Wembley. Alan Hoby of the Sunday Express reported that he said he was going home to Liverpool. The Daily Mirror's version was that he took a taxi back to the team hotel.

Shankly picked Ray Clemence; Tommy Smith, Phil Thompson, Emlyn Hughes, Alec Lindsay; Ian Callaghan, Brian Hall, Peter Cormack; Kevin Keegan, John Toshack, Steve Heighway. Thompson, 20, was given Macdonald to mark.

Hoby described the noise before the start as a contest between "You'll Never Walk Alone" and "Blaydon Races". Stuart Barnes of the Grimsby Evening Telegraph thought the Newcastle supporters were louder at first. The crowd was 100,000.

The first half was tight and goalless, though Liverpool had more of the ball.

* * *

In the second half Liverpool attacked instead of containing. Shortly after the restart Lindsay, the left-back, had a fierce shot ruled out for offside against Keegan. Television replays later suggested the last touch had come off a Newcastle defender.

With Newcastle playing only two men up front, Smith and Lindsay had space to push on down the flanks, and after 57 minutes Smith played the ball in from the right. The Liverpool Daily Post described Hall letting it run, and Keegan beat the Newcastle goalkeeper, Willie (Iam) McFaul.

The second came with about 15 minutes left. Clemence kicked long, Toshack flicked the ball on with his head, and Heighway ran on to it and shot past McFaul. Heighway had also scored Liverpool's goal in the 1971 final, which they lost to Arsenal. The Birmingham Daily Post's reporter thought this was the best of the three goals, and the one that settled the match.

The third, two minutes from time, came at the end of a long passing move. Smith played the ball across the six-yard box and Keegan put it in from close range.

Macdonald had two late shots with his left foot, and both went yards wide. Barnes described the Newcastle midfield as giving him almost no supply all afternoon, while Thompson and Hughes shut out him and John Tudor.

* * *

Hoby reported that at the final whistle two Liverpool supporters got past the security, found Shankly in front of the royal box, knelt and kissed his feet. Shankly said afterwards that he was more pleased for the supporters than for himself.

Barnes wrote that many Newcastle supporters had left before Keegan's second goal, and that one fan on the train home told him he wouldn't have minded so much if they had gone down fighting. Hoby called the 3–0 win the most one-sided final for many years. The Birmingham Daily Post went further, calling it the most conclusive since Wolves beat Blackburn in 1960.

The next day, the Sunday Express reported, half a million people were expected on the streets of Liverpool for the homecoming.

On 12 July Shankly announced his retirement. He led the team out at the Charity Shield in August, but Bob Paisley was manager by then, and the 1974 final was Shankly's last competitive match in charge.

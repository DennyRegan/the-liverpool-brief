Liverpool went into March 2009 in an odd state. In the Premier League they were chasing Manchester United and dropping points they should have won. Only ten days before Real Madrid came to Anfield they had lost 2–0 at Middlesbrough. Europe was going better.

A fortnight earlier, in the first leg of the Champions League last 16, they had won 1–0 at the Bernabéu with a late header from Yossi Benayoun. The Spanish press had not been kind to them beforehand. Henry Winter, reporting the second leg for the Daily Telegraph, referred to the "Liverpoor" jibes that had spilled out of Madrid, Benítez's home city.

For Benítez the tie carried extra weight. He had started his coaching career at Real Madrid, and the agency report of the second leg called him the club's old boy. Juande Ramos, the former Tottenham manager, was now in charge of Real, and his side included Iker Casillas, Sergio Ramos, Fabio Cannavaro, Pepe, Arjen Robben, Wesley Sneijder, Gonzalo Higuaín and their captain Raúl.

Liverpool had a weakness to fix. According to the Belfast Telegraph, before this match they had failed to score in the first 40 minutes of 11 consecutive games. Benítez's instruction for the second leg, as Ryan Babel described it afterwards, was to go at Real for the first 20 minutes, stay on top of them and see what happened.

Fernando Torres was fit again after the ankle injury that had forced him off at half-time in Madrid. He had grown up at Atlético Madrid, Real's neighbours and rivals. Steven Gerrard was making his 100th European appearance for the club.

Liverpool lined up: Pepe Reina; Álvaro Arbeloa, Jamie Carragher, Martin Škrtel, Fábio Aurélio; Javier Mascherano, Xabi Alonso; Dirk Kuyt, Steven Gerrard, Ryan Babel; Fernando Torres.

* * *

The agency report described Anfield as being at its European best, with the noise pouring down from the Kop. Winter thought it reached the levels of the great nights against Saint-Étienne, Olympiacos and Chelsea.

Liverpool did exactly what Benítez had asked. Inside the first few minutes Torres turned Cannavaro, a former European Footballer of the Year, to control a pass from Gerrard, and only Casillas's outstretched leg stopped him scoring. Soon afterwards Casillas stretched to his left to turn away a shot from Mascherano. Babel and Mascherano both had efforts deflected wide.

The goal came after 16 minutes. Carragher hit a long ball forward, Torres helped it on, Cannavaro and Pepe failed to deal with it, and Kuyt ran clear down the inside-right channel. He squared for Torres, who flicked the ball into the net and celebrated in front of the Real supporters.

Twelve minutes later it was 2–0. Gabriel Heinze was penalised for handball in the area, a decision Winter considered harsh on Real, and Gerrard sent Casillas the wrong way from the spot.

Casillas kept the score at two before half-time. He stopped a header from Škrtel and then beat away a shot from Gerrard after more good work by Torres on the left. The agency report said that without him Liverpool would have had a hatful in the first half alone.

* * *

Two minutes into the second half, Babel beat Sergio Ramos on the left and pulled the ball back, and Gerrard met it on the half-volley in front of the Kop. It was 3–0, and 4–0 on aggregate.

By then the Kop was singing about a final in Rome. That season's final was to be played there, where Liverpool had won the European Cup in 1977 and 1984.

Benítez took Gerrard off after 73 minutes, sending on the 20-year-old Jay Spearing. As Gerrard left the field, Winter noted, Raúl crossed to shake his hand.

Two minutes from the end Kuyt won the ball back, Babel and Mascherano worked it across, and Andrea Dossena, on as a substitute, scored his first goal for Liverpool. It was 4–0 on the night and 5–0 on aggregate. Winter's lasting image was of Casillas, who had saved Real from a far heavier defeat, leaving the pitch in tears.

* * *

It was the heaviest defeat Real Madrid had suffered in the Champions League, and it put Liverpool into the quarter-finals for the fourth time in five seasons. Ramos was gracious in defeat. "Liverpool were excellent and deserved to go through," he said. "They were too good."

Babel's reflections the next day were more mixed. He told reporters that Liverpool had all but given away the league title, but that the win gave them new belief for their trip to Old Trafford that Saturday.

The belief showed. Four days later Liverpool beat Manchester United 4–1 at Old Trafford, with Dossena scoring again. It was not enough to win the league: United held on, and Liverpool finished second, four points behind. In Europe the run ended in the quarter-finals against Chelsea, after a 4–4 draw at Stamford Bridge.

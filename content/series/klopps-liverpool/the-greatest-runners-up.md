The final in Kyiv on 26 May 2018 had ended with two goals conceded through Loris Karius's errors, and for a week the conversation about Liverpool's goalkeeper was about nerve. Then, on 4 June, a statement from two doctors in Boston changed its terms. Dr Ross Zafonte and Dr Lenore Herget had examined Karius on 31 May at Massachusetts General Hospital and Spaulding Rehabilitation Hospital, and had "concluded that Mr. Karius sustained a concussion during the match". His symptoms, they said, pointed to visual-spatial dysfunction that had probably begun immediately and could have affected his performance. They expected him to make a full recovery.

The explanation altered how Karius was judged. It did not alter what Liverpool did next. By the time the statement appeared, the club had already begun its summer's spending.

The first signing came two days after the final. On 28 May Liverpool announced that Fabinho, a defensive midfielder who could also play at right-back, would join from Monaco on 1 July. The Press Association understood the fee to be €45m with up to €5m in add-ons; other reports put it at £39m or £40m, and the difference is largely whether add-ons are counted. Klopp described a fantastic player who was "an equally fantastic person". Fabinho told the club's website he would "try to create my own history at this football club."

Naby Keïta arrived from RB Leipzig on the same date as Fabinho. A third midfielder, Nabil Fekir, very nearly followed. Liverpool agreed a fee and personal terms with Lyon, then told the French club on Friday 8 June that they would not proceed. Lyon announced the next evening that the negotiations "have not succeeded". A medical concern was widely reported, and Fekir had a history of knee trouble. Manuel Lanzini of West Ham, mentioned as an alternative, was ruled out for months by a cruciate ligament injury on international duty.

Mohamed Salah, whose final had ended with a shoulder injury after a challenge from Sergio Ramos, signed a new long-term contract on 2 July. Klopp said it showed "his belief in Liverpool and our belief in him." Salah's World Cup had been uncomfortable. Egypt were based in Chechnya, where Ramzan Kadyrov made him an honorary citizen at a banquet, and reports followed that Salah was angry enough to consider giving up international football; Egypt's football association denied that he had discussed it. In late July, during the club's American tour, Klopp called Ramos's conduct in the final "ruthless and brutal".

Xherdan Shaqiri came next, from Stoke City, who had just been relegated. Liverpool met the release clause in his contract, a fee Sky Sports understood to be just over £13.5m, and the deal was completed in mid-July. Klopp called it a "no-brainer". The Swiss forward, he said, had "the right amount of arrogance on the football pitch".

Then, on 19 July, the goalkeeper. Roma's official statement gave an initial fee of €62.5m for Alisson Becker, "the most expensive transfer fee ever paid for a goalkeeper", with performance clauses worth about €10m more. The Press Association broke it down as £56m guaranteed, rising to about £65m, and reported a six-year contract; Eurosport reported a five-year deal. Roma had reportedly asked for €90m in May (Sportsmole said £90m). The previous record had been the £35m Manchester City paid for Ederson a year earlier. Alisson's lasted 20 days: on 8 August Chelsea paid a reported £71.6m for Kepa Arrizabalaga. "The full package is just good," Klopp said. The day after Alisson's arrival, Danny Ward had been sold to Leicester for £12.5m. Karius stayed until after the English window shut and joined Beşiktaş on a two-year loan on 25 August.

What left was modest by comparison. Emre Can went on a free transfer at the end of his contract. Danny Ings joined Southampton on deadline day on loan, with a permanent move agreed for the following summer; Klopp said "the best chapters in his personal story are still to be written". Ragnar Klavan was sold to Cagliari after the window had closed in England. By the Liverpool Echo's calculation the club had spent £160.85m and recouped £35.75m, a net outlay of £125.1m on guaranteed fees alone.

It was a great deal of money. Liverpool had finished the previous season 25 points behind Manchester City, and when bookmakers cut their title odds from 5/1 to 9/2 on the day Alisson signed, City were still odds-on at 4/6. Klopp refused to make promises. "We cannot talk about winning something before we start the season," he told reporters in New York. "We have the highest ambitions, 100 per cent." Among the journalists the club canvassed for predictions, Chris Bascombe of the Telegraph picked Liverpool, and Henry Winter of the Times picked City and wrote that Liverpool would "push City all the way". After a 3-1 win over Torino at Anfield on 7 August, Klopp described the summer's signings as "icing on the cake".

\* \* \*

West Ham were beaten 4-0 at Anfield on 12 August, and Liverpool won their first six league games. Not all of them were comfortable. At Leicester on 1 September an error by Alisson gave Rachid Ghezzal Leicester's goal in a 2-1 win. At Tottenham a fortnight later Roberto Firmino scored the winner and suffered an eye injury after a clash with Jan Vertonghen.

The first dropped points came at Stamford Bridge on 29 September, where Daniel Sturridge equalised late on after Eden Hazard had given Chelsea the lead. Then, on 7 October, City came to Anfield. Mahrez had already shot wide and been saved by Alisson, and Salah had curled a shot straight at Ederson, when the game came to its one decisive moment. Late on, Virgil van Dijk lunged at the substitute Leroy Sané in the area and Riyad Mahrez shot the penalty over the bar. A City side who had not won at Liverpool in 15 years went home with a point. After eight matches City, Chelsea and Liverpool were level on 20 points, with Liverpool third on goal difference.

There were questions. Keïta, who had started the season in the side, was stretchered off at Napoli in the Champions League on 3 October and then struggled with a series of smaller injuries. Fabinho had not yet played a league minute.

Klopp's answer, given before the trip to Huddersfield on 20 October, was that the team had changed on purpose. Liverpool wanted to be stronger defensively, he said, and in a period of adaptation, "immediately you lose a bit of the fluency in something else". "It's a question of maturity and that makes absolute sense," he said. Fabinho made his league debut as a substitute that afternoon, a 1-0 win, and started the following week against Cardiff. By early December Klopp was saying it outright in an interview with Sky Sports: opponents had learned to defend against Liverpool's speed, so "now we have to control more games." He added: "I like it more now actually. It is more mature."

The numbers supported him. Liverpool kept twelve clean sheets in their first 20 league games and conceded eight goals.

The stretch around the turn of December tested the squad. Liverpool lost 2-1 at Paris Saint-Germain on 28 November, then played Everton, Burnley and Bournemouth in seven days before Napoli came to Anfield. The derby, on 2 December, was goalless into the sixth minute of stoppage time when van Dijk miscued a volley high into the air. Jordan Pickford tried to tip it over, it came back off the crossbar and Divock Origi, on as a substitute, headed in from close range. Klopp ran onto the pitch to embrace Alisson, accepted an FA misconduct charge for it, was reportedly fined £8,000 and promised not to repeat it, "Not in the next 14 years!" Of his run onto the pitch he said: "Not cool, but it happened." He said he had apologised to Marco Silva afterwards. "He didn't apologise to me," Everton's manager said.

Three days later Liverpool came from behind to win 3-1 at Burnley, and lost Joe Gomez, who had partnered van Dijk for much of the autumn, to a fractured lower leg after a tackle by Ben Mee. He would need surgery in February. Klopp said that before the injury he had been "in such fantastic shape". At Bournemouth on 8 December Salah scored a hat-trick in a 4-0 win. Three days later Salah's goal and a late save by Alisson from Arkadiusz Milik beat Napoli 1-0 and took Liverpool through their Champions League group.

The rest of December was the best month of the season. Shaqiri came off the bench to score twice in a 3-1 win over Manchester United on 16 December, both shots deflected; two days later José Mourinho was sacked, with United sixth and 19 points behind Liverpool. Van Dijk scored his first league goal for the club in a 2-0 win at Wolves. City lost at home to Crystal Palace on 22 December and at Leicester on Boxing Day, the day Fabinho scored his first Liverpool goal in a 4-0 win over Newcastle. On 29 December Arsenal took an early lead at Anfield and lost 5-1, Firmino scoring a hat-trick. Opta calculated that only Chelsea in 2005–06 and City in 2017–18 had ever taken more than Liverpool's 54 points from the first 20 games of an English top-flight season.

Klopp, asked about being top at Christmas, said: "Don't think that you've won something before you win something." On New Year's Eve Liverpool were seven points clear of City and nine clear of Tottenham, unbeaten after 20 matches. They were due at the Etihad Stadium three days later.

\* \* \*

In hindsight, the match on 3 January 2019 decided less than it seemed to at the time and more than anyone could have known. In the first half Vincent Kompany went in two-footed on Salah and was shown only a yellow card. "How on earth is that not a red card?" Klopp asked afterwards; Kompany called it "a great challenge". Sadio Mané hit the post, the rebound struck Ederson and John Stones cleared it off the line. Goal-line technology showed the ball had stopped about a centimetre short of crossing; Sky reported 11 millimetres, Jonathan Wilson in The Guardian 11.7. Sergio Agüero scored on 40 minutes, Firmino equalised just after the hour and Sané won it 18 minutes from time. It was the only league match Liverpool lost all season. The lead was four points.

Four days later Liverpool went out of the FA Cup at Wolves, 2-1. Five days after that a Salah penalty won the game at Brighton and the lead was seven again, with City having a game in hand. On 29 January City lost 2-1 at Newcastle. In hindsight, it was the last league game they lost that season.

On 30 January, at home to Leicester, Mané scored in the third minute and Liverpool did not score again; 1-1. On 4 February they drew 1-1 at West Ham. Two days later City won at Everton and went top on goal difference. Liverpool beat Bournemouth and briefly went back above them. At Old Trafford on 24 February Liverpool drew 0-0. On 3 March, at Goodison Park, they drew 0-0 again, and when the day was over City were top by a point.

In five weeks Liverpool had drawn four league games out of six and dropped eight points. In two of them they had not scored. The causes are easier to list than to weigh. Gomez was out. What is certain is the arithmetic. Over the rest of the season City dropped no more points at all.

\* \* \*

From 10 March Liverpool won every league game they played. Burnley were beaten 4-2. At Fulham on 17 March James Milner's penalty nine minutes from time made it 2-1 and put Liverpool two points clear, though City had a game in hand. On 31 March Tottenham were level at Anfield in the final minutes when Salah's header was spilled by Hugo Lloris and went in off Toby Alderweireld. Liverpool won 3-1 at Southampton on 5 April. From the middle of March the lead changed hands with almost every round of matches. Each time Liverpool won, City answered within a day or two.

On Sunday 14 April Liverpool played Chelsea at Anfield, the day before the 30th anniversary of the Hillsborough disaster. Both teams wore black armbands, the crowd observed a minute's silence for the 96, and the Kop and the Sir Kenny Dalglish Stand held up mosaics reading "96" and "30 years". Two years later the number changed: in July 2021, after Andrew Devine died from the injuries he had lived with for 32 years, the Liverpool coroner ruled that he had been unlawfully killed, the 97th victim of the disaster. Mané and Salah scored within a few minutes of each other early in the second half, and Liverpool won 2-0. City won at Crystal Palace on the same day.

Liverpool won at Cardiff on 21 April. On 26 April Keïta scored after 15 seconds against Huddersfield and Liverpool won 5-0. Two nights later van Dijk was named the PFA Players' Player of the Year. That same weekend, at Turf Moor, Agüero's shot was judged to have crossed the line by 29.51 millimetres, and City won 1-0. The title was being contested in measurements.

The penultimate weekend fell between the two legs of a Champions League semi-final against Barcelona. Liverpool went to Newcastle on 4 May three days after losing the first leg in Spain. Twice they led and twice Newcastle equalised. Salah was taken off on a stretcher in the second half after a collision with the goalkeeper Martin Dúbravka, and four minutes from time Origi, his replacement, headed in Shaqiri's free-kick. Liverpool were 94 points to City's 92, top with a game to play.

Two days later, at the Etihad, City were held by Leicester until the 70th minute, when Kompany struck a shot from 25 yards into the top corner. City had 95 points. Liverpool needed to beat Wolves at Anfield on the final day and hope Brighton could take something from City at the Amex.

On 12 May, for a little more than a minute, Brighton did. Glenn Murray headed in Pascal Gross's corner after 27 minutes. Agüero equalised within a minute, Aymeric Laporte headed City ahead on 38, and after half-time Mahrez and İlkay Gündoğan made it 4-1. At Anfield Mané scored twice and Liverpool won 2-0. City finished with 98 points, Liverpool with 97.

\* \* \*

Liverpool's record read: played 38, won 30, drawn seven, lost one. They scored 89 goals and conceded 22, the fewest of any Liverpool side in the Premier League era, and kept 21 clean sheets. They were unbeaten at home. Salah and Mané shared the Golden Boot with Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang on 22 goals each; Alisson won the Golden Glove; Trent Alexander-Arnold's 12 assists were a Premier League record for a defender, and Andrew Robertson, with 11, made them the first pair of defenders from one club to reach double figures in the same season. Over the 2018–19 season as a whole, Liverpool dropped points in eight matches. City dropped points in six.

So was it a failure? The case that it was rests on a single fact: they did not win the league. Everything else points the other way. On Opta's figures, 97 points would have won every previous Premier League title except one, City's 100 in 2017–18. It remains the highest total by a runner-up in the Premier League's history. It was 22 points more than Liverpool had managed the previous season. The team that beat them won their last 14 league matches, and from 1 January won 17 of 18.

The more useful question is what kind of improvement the 97 points represented. Using the Opta figures FBref published before it removed its advanced data in January 2026, Liverpool's expected goals in 2018–19 were 73.7, their opponents' expected goals were 28.8, and the goals they actually conceded numbered 22. Read alongside the summer's business, the pattern is hard to miss. Liverpool had spent much of their money on a goalkeeper and a defensive midfielder, built on the centre-back they had signed in January 2018, and become, as Klopp kept saying, a more controlled team. In hindsight, that is the clearest answer to what the season did to them: it turned a side that could beat anyone into one that rarely lost to anyone, and it showed that this version of Liverpool could keep up with City across 38 matches rather than over a few weeks. Some of that defensive record, on those numbers, was also good fortune or outstanding goalkeeping, since Liverpool let in about seven fewer than the quality of chances against them would predict.

Where the title was lost is clearer still. Carragher's view on the final day was that "you can't expect other people to take points off City if you don't do it yourselves", and it is true that Liverpool took one point from six against them. But a single point separated the clubs, and Liverpool dropped eight in the four winter draws.

Before the season ended, John Barnes told talkSPORT that nobody could convince him that 97 points and losing one game would be a failure.

There is one further piece of context that was not available at the time. On the final day Klopp had spoken of City's "quality" and "the financial power and that stuff". In September 2026, the Premier League announced that an independent commission had found Manchester City guilty of serious breaches of its financial rules over nine seasons, from 2009–10 to 2017–18. City have appealed and no sanction has been decided. The financial findings do not cover 2018–19, the season in which City beat Liverpool to the title.

Klopp's own verdict was given in the minutes after the Wolves game. "First and foremost, I have to say congratulations to City," he said. "It was brilliant, 97 points is incredible, and only because City are there it was not enough." He called his side "one of the best that ever played for Liverpool, 100 per cent", and said it was "not a finished article". There was still a Champions League final to play in Madrid three weeks later. The league, though, was over.

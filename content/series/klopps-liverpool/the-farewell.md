Five days after Jürgen Klopp told the world he was leaving, his team played Chelsea at Anfield. He had given his reason in the club's own interview on 26 January: after more than eight years, he was "running out of energy". The question that week was whether a squad told its manager was going could keep its focus through a four-month farewell. On 31 January Liverpool beat Chelsea 4–1 and went five points clear at the top of the table.

The player of the night was Conor Bradley, a 20-year-old right-back from Northern Ireland who was in the side only because Trent Alexander-Arnold had injured a knee earlier in the month. Klopp kept him there even though Alexander-Arnold was fit enough for the bench. Bradley set up Diogo Jota's opener, scored his first goal for the club and crossed for Dominik Szoboszlai's header. Darwin Núñez hit the woodwork four times, once from the penalty spot. The victory was Klopp's 200th in the Premier League.

It was a fair picture of the team he would spend the spring with. Since the previous summer Liverpool had rebuilt their midfield around Alexis Mac Allister, Szoboszlai, Ryan Gravenberch and Wataru Endo, and Klopp would later say he had called the project "Liverpool 2.0" from the start of the season. The new side was quick, aggressive and young in places. It also had very little spare.

* * *

That became clear within a week. On 4 February Arsenal won 3–1 at the Emirates and cut the gap to two points. Liverpool had equalised through Gabriel's own goal in first-half stoppage time, but Arsenal regained the lead in the 67th minute, when Virgil van Dijk hesitated over a long ball and Alisson ran out and missed it, leaving Gabriel Martinelli an empty net. Leandro Trossard's late third deflected off Van Dijk and through Alisson's legs; Ibrahima Konaté had been sent off by then. "I take full responsibility for the 2–1 goal," Van Dijk said, as Reuters reported.

Liverpool recovered with wins over Burnley and at Brentford, but the 4–1 at Brentford on 17 February cost them three more players. Jota was stretchered off with a knee injury, Curtis Jones left the ground on crutches and Núñez was taken off at half-time. Sky Sports counted nine senior players unavailable: Jota, Jones, Núñez, Joel Matip, Alexander-Arnold, Szoboszlai, Thiago, Alisson and Stefan Bajčetić. Three days later Klopp confirmed that Jota's injury would "obviously take months". Mohamed Salah, back from a hamstring injury picked up at the Africa Cup of Nations, had come on at Brentford and scored, but his fitness was again in doubt within days. Against Luton on 21 February Liverpool went a goal down, then won 4–1 with Caoimhín Kelleher in goal, Jarell Quansah at centre-back and five players on the bench who had never started a league game.

The League Cup final against Chelsea came four days later. By the club's own team news, eight players were already ruled out, and on the morning of the game Salah, Núñez and Szoboszlai all failed late fitness tests. The side that started at Wembley was Kelleher; Bradley, Konaté, Van Dijk, Andy Robertson; Endo, Mac Allister, Gravenberch; Harvey Elliott, Luis Díaz, Cody Gakpo. Gravenberch was injured in the first half. Chelsea had a Raheem Sterling goal ruled out for offside; Van Dijk had a header disallowed by VAR because Endo, standing offside, had blocked a Chelsea defender. During the second half, Klopp sent on three teenagers, Bobby Clark, James McConnell and Jayden Danns, the most to appear in a League Cup final since Arsenal in 2007, according to the Premier League's report. In the 118th minute Van Dijk headed in from a corner to win it, 1–0.

Klopp went up the Wembley steps with his captain to lift the trophy. Afterwards he put it above everything else he had won. "In more than 20 years it is easily the most special trophy I have ever won," he said, as the Guardian reported, and he turned an old line of Alan Hansen's about winning nothing with kids into a joke against himself. It was Liverpool's tenth League Cup, a record, and Chelsea's sixth successive defeat in a Wembley cup final.

Three days later the youngest players went again. In the FA Cup fifth round on 28 February Southampton hit the woodwork and were repeatedly denied by Kelleher, but Lewis Koumas scored on his debut and Danns, on as a substitute, scored twice. Trey Nyoni, at 16 years and 243 days, became the youngest player to appear for Liverpool in the FA Cup. Liverpool won 3–0 and drew Manchester United away in the quarter-final.

* * *

What followed in early March looked like the habits of a champion. At Nottingham Forest on 2 March, in their fourth game in 11 days, Liverpool were heading for a goalless draw until Núñez, back from injury and on as a substitute, headed in Mac Allister's cross in the 99th minute. "Most difficult game we played because of the circumstances," Klopp said. The win put them four points clear of Manchester City, who had a game in hand. Forest's anger afterwards was directed at the referee, Paul Tierney, who had restarted play with a dropped ball to Liverpool rather than to Forest minutes before the goal; the club's referee analyst said he had got it wrong.

Then City came to Anfield on 10 March, in what would be the last league meeting between Klopp and Pep Guardiola. John Stones scored in the first half. Five minutes after the break Ederson brought down Núñez and Mac Allister converted the penalty. In the second half Liverpool were the better side by a distance; Díaz missed a one-on-one, and Phil Foden and Jérémy Doku hit the woodwork for City. In stoppage time Doku's boot caught Mac Allister in the chest in the City area and VAR let play go on. Klopp was adamant that it was a penalty, but his view of the game itself was simpler. "The second half was the best we ever played against Manchester City," he said, as Reuters reported it. The draw left Arsenal top on goal difference, with Liverpool level on points and City one behind. Ten games remained.

The club, meanwhile, was rebuilding the structure that would choose Klopp's successor. Jörg Schmadtke, the sporting director appointed only the previous summer, had left at the end of January. On 12 March Fenway Sports Group announced that Michael Edwards, Liverpool's former sporting director, was returning as FSG's chief executive of football, with day-to-day authority over the club's football operations and a remit to acquire a second club. Richard Hughes was to join from Bournemouth as sporting director. On 29 March Xabi Alonso, widely seen as the leading candidate, said he would stay at Bayer Leverkusen. Klopp said he understood, having made a similar decision at Dortmund in 2013.

On the pitch the cup run was still alive. Sparta Prague were beaten 5–1 in Prague and 6–1 at Anfield, four of the second-leg goals coming in the first 14 minutes, for an 11–2 aggregate win that sent Liverpool into the Europa League quarter-finals. Three days later, on 17 March, they went to Old Trafford for the FA Cup quarter-final. They came back from Scott McTominay's early goal to lead 2–1 at half-time through Mac Allister and Salah, but missed a series of chances in the second half, and Antony equalised in the 87th minute. Elliott's deflected shot put them ahead again in extra time. Marcus Rashford, who had missed a clear chance to win it in normal time, made it 3–3, and in the last minute of extra time Amad Diallo finished a counter-attack from a Liverpool corner. United won 4–3. The talk of four trophies ended there. Asked afterwards whether his side had played too many games, Klopp turned on the reporter rather than answer.

* * *

The run that followed was the closest Liverpool came to the ending Klopp wanted. On 31 March they fell behind to Danny Welbeck's second-minute goal against Brighton, then won 2–1 through Díaz and Salah, who was making his first league start since New Year's Day. Arsenal and City drew at the Etihad the same afternoon. On 4 April Liverpool beat Sheffield United 3–1. After 30 games they had 70 points, two more than Arsenal and three more than City, and had lost only twice. Eight games remained.

They took 12 points from those eight games. The slide began at Old Trafford on 7 April. Díaz scored in the first half, and Liverpool had 28 shots in the match, but Bruno Fernandes scored from inside the centre circle after a Liverpool mistake and Kobbie Mainoo curled in a second. Salah's penalty six minutes from time rescued a 2–2 draw. "We should have won the game, that is clear," Klopp said, and he added that his side should have beaten United in both meetings. Arsenal went top on goal difference.

Four days later came the worst night of the spring. Klopp made changes for the home leg of the Europa League quarter-final, and Atalanta won 3–0 at Anfield, Gianluca Scamacca scoring twice and Mario Pašalić adding a third; Kelleher made the saves that stopped it being worse. It was Liverpool's first defeat at Anfield in almost 14 months. "It was a really bad game, oh my God," Klopp said, as Sky Sports reported. He promised a reaction against Crystal Palace on the Sunday. Instead Eberechi Eze scored after 14 minutes. Before half-time Endo hit the bar and Dean Henderson saved from Díaz; in the second half Núñez, Jota, Jones and Salah all missed chances, and Tyrick Mitchell blocked Salah's stoppage-time effort on the line. Palace won 1–0, Liverpool's first home league defeat since October 2022. "The first half was absolutely not good enough," Klopp told Sky. City, who had gone top by beating Luton the day before, stayed there.

In Bergamo on 18 April Salah's penalty won the second leg 1–0, which was not enough; Atalanta went through 3–1 on aggregate, and Klopp's last European match was over. Liverpool won 3–1 at Fulham on the Sunday, then went to Goodison Park three days later for a midweek derby. Jarrad Branthwaite and Dominic Calvert-Lewin, both from set pieces, gave Everton a 2–0 win, their first at home against Liverpool in almost 14 years and Klopp's first defeat there in eight visits. Everton's supporters sang that Liverpool had lost the league at Goodison Park. Klopp did not argue. "I can only apologise for today to the people," he said; asked about the title, he said Arsenal and City would need a very bad moment.

There was one more scene to come. At West Ham on 27 April, a Saturday lunchtime kick-off after the Wednesday derby, Salah was left on the bench. As he waited to come on, he and Klopp exchanged words on the touchline, and Núñez and Joe Gomez intervened. Liverpool had led 2–1; Michail Antonio's header made it 2–2, and that was how it finished. Klopp told reporters the matter was finished. Salah walked through the mixed zone without stopping. "There's going to be a fire today if I speak," he said, as the42 reported. The draw left Liverpool third, a point behind City, who had two games in hand.

The arithmetic had been severe from the start of the run-in. City won all eight of their games after 4 April, and Arsenal won seven of eight. To finish above City on points, Liverpool would have needed at least seven wins and a draw from their last eight games, which would have taken them to 92, one more than City's 91. The three weeks from Old Trafford to West Ham did not cost Liverpool a title they had in hand; it removed any chance that City's run would matter.

* * *

The last three weeks were a farewell rather than a race. Tottenham were beaten 4–2 at Anfield on 5 May. That weekend, in a Sky Sports interview reported by Reuters, Klopp gave his view of what he was handing on. "Liverpool 2.0 doesn't stop after I leave," he said. "It's just the start of another project with a really good squad." His successor was already known in all but name. Liverpool had reportedly agreed compensation with Feyenoord for their head coach, Arne Slot, at the end of April, a deal Sky Sports reported at £7.7m plus £1.7m in add-ons, and on 17 May Slot confirmed it himself. At Villa Park on 13 May, Klopp's last away game, Liverpool led 3–1 with five minutes left and drew 3–3, Jhon Durán scoring twice.

At his final pre-match press conference, on 17 May, Klopp was asked whether his teams should have won more. He did not pretend otherwise. "I know we could have won more but I can't change that," he said, as Sky Sports reported, before adding that they could also have won less, and that he could not have done more himself. He named this season's League Cup as his favourite final.

Two days later Liverpool beat Wolves 2–0 at Anfield, Mac Allister and Quansah scoring in the first half. Liverpool finished third with 82 points, nine behind City, who beat West Ham on the same afternoon to win a fourth successive title, and seven behind Arsenal. It was Klopp's 491st match in charge. Afterwards, on a stage on the pitch, he was presented to the crowd by John W. Henry, Tom Werner, the chief executive Billy Hogan and Sir Kenny Dalglish, and he spoke to the ground for the last time as its manager. He asked supporters to welcome the next man as they had welcomed him. "Change is good," he said, and then he led the crowd in a song for his successor, to the tune of the one they had sung about him: "Arne Slot, la la la la la." Near the end he told them: "I'm one of you now."

The club confirmed Slot's appointment the next day, on a three-year contract. He would be Liverpool's first head coach rather than manager, working in the structure FSG had built that spring: Edwards as chief executive of football, Hughes as sporting director, and Julian Ward, who had left as sporting director a year earlier, returning as FSG's technical director.

* * *

What did the run-in show about the team Klopp was leaving?

First, that it was a serious side. With eight games left, after a season in which its midfield had been rebuilt almost from nothing, it had lost two league games in 30 and was top. It had won a final with a goalkeeper who was officially the second choice, a 20-year-old right-back and three teenagers off the bench. The young players who came through that winter, Bradley, Clark, Danns and Koumas among them, had been brought into first-team work from pre-season, and Quansah had already broken into the side in August; but it was the injuries that turned occasional opportunities into regular football. That they coped was a real achievement, and it was evidence of good coaching and a strong academy as much as of a deep squad.

Second, that it did not finish its chances. The contemporary data is consistent on this. Sky Sports' analysis after Old Trafford found Liverpool leading the league for shots, big chances created and expected goals, but 14th for shooting accuracy and top for big chances missed; Núñez alone had missed 24 of 30. Opta's analysts calculated that Liverpool had scored only 0.2 goals more than their chances were worth, while Arsenal had outscored theirs by 12.7 and City by 7.0. Against Palace Liverpool created close to three expected goals, by Opta's model, and scored none. By the Premier League's own figures in mid-April, Liverpool had scored 72 league goals from chances worth 74.66, and the whole of that shortfall came in March and April.

Third, and less often said at the time, that the defence went as well. After the win on 4 April, Liverpool had conceded 28 league goals in 30 games. In the last eight they conceded 13. The Premier League's analysis in April noted that they had kept six clean sheets in 24 games in 2024 and, citing The Athletic, that they had used 31 different back-four combinations during the season. Injuries, the Thursday–Sunday rhythm of the Europa League, and a midweek derby followed by a lunchtime kick-off left little room for recovery; how much each contributed cannot be measured from the outside. What can be said is that the squad had no margin to absorb them, and that both ends of the pitch weakened at the same time.

Taken together, the spring answers the chapter's question in two parts. The first is that Klopp was leaving a team, not a ruin. Unlike the side of 2022–23, this one did not stop working; it ran out of fit players, form in front of goal and time, in that order, against a City side that did not drop a point after early April. The second is that the gaps were visible and specific. The attack needed a more reliable finisher, the squad needed senior cover in defence and midfield, and the club, which had spent that spring replacing almost everyone above the head coach, now had to decide how much of Liverpool 2.0 to keep.

Klopp's own verdict, in his last post-match press conference, was shorter. "Third," he said, "that's alright."

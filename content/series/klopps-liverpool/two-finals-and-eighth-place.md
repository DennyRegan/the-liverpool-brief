Jürgen Klopp's first match as Liverpool manager was a goalless draw, and the most telling number from it had nothing to do with goals.

At White Hart Lane on Saturday 17 October 2015, Liverpool covered 116 kilometres, their highest figure of the season and 1.2km more than Tottenham. They made 614 sprints, 140 above their average for the campaign. James Milner alone made 82 sprints and ran 13.1km, more than anyone else on the pitch. Sky Sports' analysis the next day noted that Liverpool were the first side that season to outrun Tottenham.

Running harder was something a new manager could ask of the players he found. Most other things would take longer, and by Tottenham he had already lost two of them. Danny Ings ruptured an anterior cruciate ligament in a routine training session at Melwood that week, and Joe Gomez, 18, suffered the same injury on England Under-21 duty. Ings was ruled out for the season. The rest of the squad was the one Brendan Rodgers had left, and Klopp had no transfer window for almost three months.

What followed over the next seven months was a long test of how far that squad could be pushed. The answer turned out to depend on the competition.

\* \* \*

Tottenham was the first of three draws, followed by 1–1 at home to Rubin Kazan in the Europa League and 1–1 at home to Southampton, before a 1–0 League Cup win over Bournemouth. Then, on 31 October, Liverpool went to Stamford Bridge.

Ramires put Chelsea ahead inside five minutes. Philippe Coutinho equalised just before half-time, scored again on 76 minutes, and Christian Benteke added a third seven minutes later. FourFourTwo's same-day analysis put the win down to shape as much as effort. Roberto Firmino played as a false nine, dropping deep to link with Coutinho and Adam Lallana, and leaving John Terry and Gary Cahill unsure whether to follow him. Liverpool's starting line-up was close to having no striker at all. They had 58 per cent of the ball and made 470 passes to Chelsea's 333. It was the first time since December 1989 that Liverpool had scored more than twice in a league match at Stamford Bridge. Much of the coverage was really about the home side: it was José Mourinho's sixth league defeat of the season, already matching his highest total in any previous Chelsea league campaign, and the Associated Press led on Mourinho's crisis.

After a 1–0 win in Kazan, the first defeat came at Anfield on 8 November. Crystal Palace led through Yannick Bolasie, Coutinho equalised before half-time, and Scott Dann headed the winner on 82 minutes. Klopp told reporters he had seen many people leave once the goal went in. He had turned round, he said, and felt "pretty alone". By AP's account he did not blame the supporters; he said it was the players' job to give them a reason to stay, and that "we decide when it is over". The line would be recalled later in the season.

Two weeks after that Liverpool won 4–1 at Manchester City. An Eliaquim Mangala own goal and strikes from Coutinho, Firmino and Martin Škrtel did it; Sergio Agüero's goal just before half-time was City's only reply. Frank Lampard, working for Sky, said Liverpool were "flying into the press". Sky's figures had them running 110.36km to City's 108.46km, with Lallana making 53 sprints and Firmino winning the ball back eight times. Even so, Liverpool were six points off the top four afterwards. A 1–0 win over Swansea, from a Milner penalty, lifted them to sixth, and on 2 December a 6–1 League Cup quarter-final win at Southampton brought two goals for Daniel Sturridge and a hat-trick for Divock Origi.

Chelsea and City were the evidence that something real had arrived. Against good opponents who wanted to play, Liverpool's pressing and Firmino's movement could be decisive. The weeks that followed showed the other side of it.

\* \* \*

Liverpool's December league programme began with a 2–0 defeat at Newcastle, who had started the day in the bottom three. A Škrtel own goal and a stoppage-time strike by Georginio Wijnaldum settled it. A goalless draw in Sion confirmed Liverpool as winners of their Europa League group, unbeaten in six. Klopp had taken charge for the last four of those matches.

At home to West Bromwich Albion on 13 December, Jordan Henderson put Liverpool ahead, a Simon Mignolet error helped Craig Dawson equalise, and Jonas Olsson put West Brom in front. Origi equalised in the fifth minute of stoppage time. Klopp then led his players, arms linked, towards the Kop to salute the crowd. Supporters of other clubs mocked it, and the West Brom winger James McClean called Klopp "a bit of an idiot". Klopp later called the reaction a big misunderstanding. He had wanted to thank the supporters, he said, and to show that players and crowd were one unit. Afterwards he also defended Mignolet: he wanted a goalkeeper who tried to get the ball. This Is Anfield thought that backing owed more to necessity than conviction.

A week later Liverpool lost 3–0 at Watford. Mignolet was injured, and his deputy Ádám Bogdan dropped the corner from which Nathan Aké scored in the third minute. Odion Ighalo scored the other two. After Watford, Liverpool remained without a league win in December, five points off the top four and with a negative goal difference. Benteke came off the bench to beat Leicester 1–0 on Boxing Day, and a 1–0 win at Sunderland four days later closed the year. In the league, Klopp's 2015 came to five wins, three draws and three defeats.

By then the injuries had become a story of their own. Origi went off after 38 minutes against Leicester with a hamstring problem, following Škrtel, Jordan Rossiter and Sturridge. Klopp's Boxing Day verdict came with an expletive: hamstring was his word of the year, and it was always hamstring, because of the intensity of the game and the fixtures. After the League Cup semi-final at Stoke on 5 January, Graeme Souness questioned on Sky whether Liverpool's training was contributing to the hamstring injuries, saying they could not be a coincidence. Klopp accepted responsibility as manager but challenged that explanation: with matches so close together, he said, the players were recovering between games rather than training. The physical demand that had produced Tottenham, Chelsea and City was now being questioned for what it cost.

\* \* \*

January brought nine matches. Liverpool lost 2–0 at West Ham, then won the League Cup semi-final first leg 1–0 at Stoke on 5 January through Jordon Ibe. A very young side drew 2–2 in the FA Cup at Exeter. Arsenal drew 3–3 at Anfield, Joe Allen equalising in the 90th minute, and Manchester United won there 1–0 through Wayne Rooney.

The signings were small. Marko Grujić joined from Red Star Belgrade, at a reported £5.1 million, and went straight back to Belgrade on loan. Steven Caulker came on loan from Queens Park Rangers. On 18 January Mignolet signed a new contract to 2021, despite a run of criticism for errors, although he had kept more Premier League clean sheets than any goalkeeper in calendar year 2015.

Then came Carrow Road on 23 January. Firmino put Liverpool ahead. Norwich then scored three times, through Dieumerci Mbokani, Steven Naismith and a Wes Hoolahan penalty. Henderson and Firmino brought it back to 3–3, and Milner put Liverpool in front after Russell Martin misjudged a back-pass. Sébastien Bassong equalised in stoppage time. Lallana then won it 5–4 from Emre Can's deflected cross. LFCTV's commentator John Bradley called it the most ridiculous game of football you will ever see. In the celebrations on the touchline Klopp's glasses were broken. He had a second pair, he said afterwards, but could not find them, because it is hard to find glasses without glasses.

Three days later Stoke won the second leg 1–0 at Anfield. Mignolet saved from Peter Crouch and Marc Muniesa in the shoot-out, and Allen scored the kick that sent Liverpool to Wembley, 6–5 on penalties. The FA Cup ended in a 2–1 extra-time defeat in a replay at West Ham on 9 February.

That month was a fair summary of the squad Klopp had. It could produce a match like Norwich. It could not see out a lead and keep its shape, even in a 5–4 win.

\* \* \*

February brought the club's first organised walkout. Under plans for the 2016–17 season, the most expensive general-admission ticket in the redeveloped Main Stand would rise from £59 to £77. Before kick-off against Sunderland on 6 February, black flags were waved on the Kop. In the 77th minute, with Liverpool 2–0 up through Firmino and Lallana, supporters led by Spirit of Shankly left their seats; the commonly reported estimate is more than 10,000. Sunderland, then in the relegation zone, scored through Adam Johnson on 82 minutes and Jermain Defoe on 89, and the match finished 2–2.

Four days later the owners gave way. In a letter signed by John W Henry, Tom Werner and Mike Gordon, Fenway Sports Group apologised for the distress caused by the pricing plan. The letter said, simply, "Message received." The top general-admission price would stay at £59, match categorisation was scrapped, and £9 tickets would be offered at every Premier League home game, more than 10,000 across the season, under a plan covering two seasons.

At the start of that month Manchester City had announced that Pep Guardiola would succeed Manuel Pellegrini in the summer. Klopp, who had faced Guardiola often in Germany, called him a really smart person and said City would not have the worst team in the world next year. "We will try to beat them," he added. On 14 February Liverpool won 6–0 at Aston Villa, with six different scorers, among them Kolo Touré. The next day Schalke confirmed that Joël Matip would join on a free transfer at the end of the season.

The League Cup final against City at Wembley on 28 February was even. Fernandinho scored four minutes after half-time, and Coutinho equalised on 83 minutes. Klopp said afterwards that City should not have scored. It finished 1–1 after extra time. In the shoot-out Can scored and Fernandinho hit the post, but Willy Caballero then saved from Lucas Leiva, Coutinho and Lallana, and City won 3–1 on penalties. "It was a great day until the last kick in the penalty shootout," Klopp said, adding that you can fall, but what matters is how you stand up. Three days later Liverpool beat City 3–0 at Anfield, with goals from Lallana, Milner and Firmino.

\* \* \*

The Europa League now carried the season. A Milner penalty in the fifth minute at Anfield, after a 0–0 draw in Germany, took Liverpool past Augsburg. The draw for the last 16 brought Manchester United, in what UEFA called the first European meeting between English football's two most successful clubs. At Anfield on 10 March, Sturridge scored a penalty after Nathaniel Clyne was brought down by Memphis Depay, and Firmino added a second 17 minutes from time. At Old Trafford a week later, Anthony Martial scored a penalty, but Coutinho's away goal on the stroke of half-time, a run onto Can's pass, past Guillermo Varela and over David de Gea, settled the tie at 3–1 on aggregate.

The league, meanwhile, drifted. Liverpool lost 3–2 at Southampton on 20 March and drew 1–1 with Tottenham on 2 April. Matt Ladson, writing for Bleacher Report at the end of the season, judged that this was the point at which Liverpool effectively gave up on the league and began fielding weakened sides.

The quarter-final drew Borussia Dortmund. In the first leg on 7 April, Klopp's first match back in Dortmund as an opponent, Origi scored after 36 minutes and Mats Hummels headed an equaliser just after half-time. Klopp said the quiet, respectful applause he received had been perfect. Henderson went off at half-time and was found to have damaged a lateral collateral ligament in his knee. He was expected to miss six to eight weeks, which meant almost certainly the rest of the club season.

The second leg was played at Anfield on Thursday 14 April, the eve of the 27th anniversary of the Hillsborough disaster. It had been reported beforehand that Dortmund's travelling supporters would hold up a yellow-and-black mosaic bearing the number 96, and the two sets of fans sang You'll Never Walk Alone together.

Henrikh Mkhitaryan scored after five minutes and Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang after nine. Origi pulled one back three minutes into the second half, from Can's pass, but Marco Reus made it 3–1 on 57 minutes, leaving Liverpool needing three goals. ESPN's report described a crowd that seemed deflated until Sturridge and Allen came on around the hour. Coutinho scored on 66 minutes and Mamadou Sakho headed in on 77. In the first minute of stoppage time Dejan Lovren headed the winner in front of the Kop, 4–3 on the night and 5–4 on aggregate. UEFA called it one of the most startling comebacks in Europa League history, and it ended Dortmund's 18-match unbeaten run in 2016. Afterwards ESPN, too, recalled what Klopp had said after Crystal Palace about deciding when it was over.

The following afternoon, 15 April, about 25,000 people attended the Hillsborough memorial service at Anfield. It was the last to be held there, as the families of the 96 had unanimously agreed. Klopp and his players were there, and so were Dortmund supporters. The 96 names were read aloud, and a minute's silence was held at 3.06pm, the time the semi-final against Nottingham Forest was stopped in 1989.

On 26 April, at Birchwood Park in Warrington, the jury in the inquests, six women and three men, concluded that all 96 had been unlawfully killed and that supporters had played no part in causing the disaster. By the club's account the verdicts came more than 27 years after the disaster, after more than two years of proceedings, and ended the longest-running inquest in UK legal history. The following evening thousands gathered at St George's Hall. In July 2021 a coroner ruled that Andrew Devine, who had been gravely injured at Hillsborough and died 32 years later, had also been unlawfully killed, making him the 97th victim.

\* \* \*

The squad was now thin in exactly the places the season had stretched. On 20 April Liverpool beat Everton 4–0 at Anfield. Origi and Sakho scored headers before half-time; Ramiro Funes Mori was sent off five minutes into the second half for a challenge on Origi, who was carried off on a stretcher; and Sturridge and Coutinho added the rest. Klopp confirmed the next day that the ankle was not broken, but the forward who had scored at Villa, twice against Stoke, at Dortmund and against Everton was sidelined for the weeks that followed.

On 23 April, the day of a 2–2 draw with Newcastle, Liverpool announced that UEFA was investigating a possible anti-doping rule violation by Sakho relating to the second leg at Old Trafford. The club said he was not suspended but had agreed not to be selected while the process ran. On 28 April UEFA imposed a provisional 30-day suspension, which covered the rest of the season. (In July UEFA's disciplinary body dismissed the case, and the club said it confirmed he had committed no doping violation; by then he had missed the final and Euro 2016.) That night Liverpool lost the semi-final first leg 1–0 in Villarreal to a late goal from Adrián López.

The league now came second. Three days after Villarreal, Liverpool lost 3–1 at Swansea. The return leg on 5 May was a different kind of night: Bruno Soriano turned the ball into his own net inside seven minutes to level the tie, Sturridge scored from a Firmino flick on 63 minutes, Víctor Ruiz was sent off for a second booking, and Lallana made it 3–0, 3–1 on aggregate. Liverpool were in a second final.

The Premier League season ended with a 2–0 win over Watford, a 1–1 draw with Chelsea at Anfield, rescued late by Benteke in Klopp's 50th match in charge, and a 1–1 draw at West Brom. Liverpool finished eighth with 60 points, two places and two points worse than the season before. That left one route into Europe for 2016–17. Under UEFA's rules, introduced the previous season, the Europa League winner was guaranteed at least a place in the Champions League play-off round.

\* \* \*

The final against Sevilla was played at St. Jakob-Park in Basel on 18 May. For 45 minutes it went the way Liverpool had hoped. About ten minutes before the break, played in by Firmino and Coutinho on the left of the area, Sturridge curled the ball with the outside of his foot around David Soria and into the far corner.

Kevin Gameiro equalised 18 seconds into the second half, turning in Mariano's cross from close range. Coke put Sevilla ahead on 64 minutes and scored again six minutes later; ITV's report noted an offside flag overruled by the referee, Jonas Eriksson, because the ball had come off a Liverpool player. Klopp brought on Origi and Benteke and moved to three at the back, without effect. Sevilla won 3–1, their third Europa League title in a row.

Klopp's explanation was unusually exact. At the moment of the equaliser, he said, his team "lost faith in our style of play", switching from quick and simple passing to something complicated, and losing their shape. Conceding straight after half-time was bad, he said, but there were 44 minutes left and the reaction had been the problem. It was his second lost final with Liverpool in three months, and his fifth in a row as a manager. He said he would try to reach the next final even knowing he could lose it.

Liverpool had missed the Champions League, and with it European football of any kind for the following season.

\* \* \*

The contemporary verdicts were mixed. Jack Lusby, grading the season for Bleacher Report, gave Klopp a B: a hugely positive impact, but hard to match October's euphoria, and still a work in progress. Phil Thompson, on Sky, credited Klopp with rejuvenating players and supporters and called two cup finals a very good season. After Basel the tone was sharper. As The Week summarised the morning's coverage, Henry Winter compared the collapse to Istanbul in reverse, Chris Bascombe argued that Klopp's emotional appeals were not enough and that he needed a team that inspired supporters on its own, and Jonathan Wilson wrote in the Guardian that Liverpool had lost control of a match they had dominated, and that chaos, however exciting, was not reliable.

The numbers backed both readings. Klopp's 30 league matches brought 48 points, an average of 1.6 a game. The eight under Rodgers had brought 12, an average of 1.5. In the cups, Liverpool reached two finals in a season of 63 matches, the club's most since 2000–01, and in April they produced the night against Dortmund. Liverpool used 22 different scorers, a club record, gave 19 players their debuts, and scored 18 goals from the bench. In Sky's end-of-season review, though, Liverpool had dropped 14 points from winning positions at home, joint-most in the division with Chelsea.

So what did the first season show? Some things changed quickly. The team ran further and pressed harder from the first match. Players who had looked uncertain under Rodgers looked better: contemporary assessments named Firmino, who had struggled in a wide role under Rodgers, along with Lallana, Can, Lovren, Origi and Sturridge when fit. The relationship with Anfield, tested after Palace and mocked after West Brom, produced the Dortmund night. Against good sides who left space, Liverpool could be formidable, as Chelsea, City twice, United and Dortmund found.

Other things did not change. The squad could not hold a lead or control a match from in front, at Norwich, against Sunderland or in Basel. Its depth gave way under the load of two cup runs: Henderson missed most of the run-in, returning only for the final league match and a place on the bench in Basel; Sakho remained unavailable; Origi had been injured; and the league had been left behind. Some of what Klopp inherited never fitted. Benteke, signed for £32.5 million the previous summer, started 20 times and came on as a substitute 22 times; six of his nine league goals came off the bench, and he said he found it hard to understand being overlooked by a coach who had once wanted to sign him. The goalkeeper Klopp had backed with a long contract in January now had a rival. Loris Karius signed from Mainz on 24 May for a reported £4.7 million and was given the No 1 shirt, which had been vacant all season; Mignolet kept his No 22.

At his first press conference Klopp had said Liverpool had to change "from doubter to believer". Eight months later, belief was the part he had largely delivered. A side good enough to justify it over a season was the part his inherited squad could not provide. He said after Basel that Liverpool would come back stronger, and he would now try to do it with fewer matches and a different set of players.

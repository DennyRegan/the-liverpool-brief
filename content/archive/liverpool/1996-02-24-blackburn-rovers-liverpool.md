Stan Collymore scored twice as Liverpool won 3-2 at Blackburn Rovers on 24 February 1996. Michael Thomas added the goal that ultimately separated the sides, with the visitors surviving a late Blackburn response to leave Ewood Park with three points.

Liverpool's first goal owed much to the pitch. Collymore's low shot appeared to be heading comfortably towards Tim Flowers, but bounced sharply off the surface and over the goalkeeper. The striker had already turned away from his effort before it found the net.

His second was a free-kick in the 21st minute, curled past the wall and beyond Flowers. Liverpool were two goals ahead, but the lead was soon reduced when Jason Wilcox headed in Stuart Ripley's cross.

Blackburn reached half-time only 2-1 behind. Ripley then went off injured early in the second half, with Paul Warhurst replacing him. The home side were still within one goal when Robbie Fowler supplied the pass for Thomas to score with around 20 minutes remaining.

That restored Liverpool's two-goal advantage. Tim Sherwood reduced it again in the 83rd minute, and Blackburn pressed for an equaliser. Their late appeals for a penalty for handball against John Barnes were unsuccessful.

The victory kept Liverpool third, on 52 points from 27 games. They were two points behind Manchester United and nine behind Newcastle, with all three clubs having played the same number of matches.

It was only Blackburn's second home league defeat of the season, their first since Manchester United's visit in August. Liverpool had also beaten the reigning champions 3-0 at Anfield in September. This return meeting was considerably closer: after scoring three times, Evans's side still had to defend a one-goal lead through the closing minutes.

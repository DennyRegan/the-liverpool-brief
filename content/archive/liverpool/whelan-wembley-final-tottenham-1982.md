Liverpool were three minutes from losing the League Cup. Ronnie Whelan scored twice before Tottenham could take it from them.

His equaliser in the 87th minute rescued Bob Paisley’s side. His second, deep into extra time, put Liverpool ahead before Ian Rush completed a 3–1 victory. At 20, Whelan had decided a Wembley final.

Tottenham arrived as FA Cup holders, having eliminated Manchester United and Nottingham Forest on their way to this final. Liverpool were defending the League Cup they had won for the first time the previous year. This was a meeting between two established cup-winning sides, and for most of the afternoon Spurs held the advantage.

Steve Archibald put Tottenham ahead after 11 minutes, breaking through to beat Bruce Grobbelaar. At the other end stood Ray Clemence, facing the club whose goal he had protected for so many years.

Clemence helped preserve Tottenham’s lead as Liverpool searched for an equaliser. The early goal remained the difference at half-time, and it was still separating the sides when Paisley brought David Johnson on for Terry McDermott in the 76th minute.

Johnson’s contribution proved decisive. With three minutes of normal time remaining, his cross reached Whelan, who swept the ball past Clemence. Liverpool had finally broken through.

Before extra time, Paisley insisted his players remain standing. Phil Neal later recalled that the manager would not allow them to show Tottenham they were tired. It was a small act of defiance after an afternoon spent trying to recover the match.

The winning goal came in the 111th minute. Kenny Dalglish supplied the pass and Whelan scored again, turning Liverpool’s late reprieve into a lead. Rush added the third in the 119th minute, with Johnson again providing the assist. Liverpool had retained the trophy.

After his second goal, Whelan had vaulted the advertising boards to celebrate near the Liverpool supporters. Years later, he could still recall the difficulty of getting back.

“I jumped over the barrier at the back and could hardly make it back over again!” he told Liverpool’s official website.

It was the response of a young player whose two goals had changed the final. A year later he would score the winner against Manchester United at Wembley, helping Liverpool secure a third consecutive League Cup. His reputation in the competition began with the afternoon he first saved the trophy, then won it.

Liverpool scored four times after the interval to beat Leeds United 5-0 at Anfield on 20 January 1996. Robbie Fowler and Neil Ruddock contributed two goals each, with Stan Collymore completing the scoring for Roy Evans's side.

The result lifted Liverpool into second place in the Premier League. They had 42 points from 23 matches, level with Manchester United but ahead on goal difference. Newcastle remained 12 points clear at the top.

Ruddock opened the scoring with a header in the 27th minute following a short-corner routine. Leeds had chances before half-time: David James saved efforts from Gary Speed, Rod Wallace and Gary McAllister to preserve Liverpool's narrow advantage.

The decisive change came around the hour. Gary Kelly brought down Rob Jones in the penalty area and was sent off. Fowler converted the spot-kick, then added Liverpool's third in the 68th minute, meeting Collymore's delivery at the far post.

That second goal took Fowler's total for the season to 23. England manager Terry Venables was watching, having left the striker out of the squad gathering announced that week.

Liverpool added two more goals late on. Collymore scored after Steve McManaman and Fowler combined, before Ruddock completed his double with a left-footed volley following a corner in stoppage time.

The margin was emphatic, although both Evans and Leeds manager Howard Wilkinson considered Kelly's dismissal harsh. Until the penalty, Liverpool had led by a single goal; afterwards, Leeds had to play the remainder a man short.

A crowd of 40,254 saw Liverpool secure their twelfth league victory of the campaign. The five goals also took their league tally to 46, one more than leaders Newcastle had scored at that stage.

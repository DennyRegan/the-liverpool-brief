Liverpool's defence of the League Cup ended at Anfield on 9 October 2001. Grimsby Town won the third-round tie 2–1 after extra time, overturning Gary McAllister's penalty with goals from Marlon Broomes and Phil Jevons.

Chris Kirkland made his Liverpool debut in goal for Gérard Houllier's side. In front of him, Sami Hyypia captained a team that also included Jamie Carragher, McAllister and Jari Litmanen. Liverpool had experienced players on the pitch, but their First Division opponents kept the match goalless through the opening 90 minutes.

Danny Coyne's saves and Grimsby's defending prevented Liverpool from settling the tie in normal time. Houllier brought on Emile Heskey and Jamie Redknapp during the second half, but the visitors continued to hold out. At the other end, Grimsby had a late penalty appeal after a challenge on Michael Boulding; none was awarded.

The breakthrough finally came in the first half of extra time. David Beharall was penalised for handball, and McAllister converted the resulting penalty. Liverpool had the lead, but not a place in the next round.

Broomes levelled from close range late in extra time. With the tie still undecided in its closing moments, Jevons beat Kirkland with a long-range shot. Grimsby had gone from trailing the holders to eliminating them, without needing a penalty shoot-out.

For Liverpool, the result was an early end to the defence of a trophy they had won the previous season. For Grimsby, the decisive action belonged to a player who had joined from Everton that summer: Jevons's late winner settled a cup tie that Liverpool had been unable to put beyond them.

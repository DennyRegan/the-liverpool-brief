Liverpool confirmed Jürgen Klopp as their new manager on Thursday 8 October 2015. The announcement was brief: the German had signed a deal, and a press conference at Anfield would introduce him the following morning.

The appointment came four days after Liverpool terminated Brendan Rodgers's contract. Rodgers had left on 4 October, following a 1–1 draw with Everton. The club's statement that day announced the search for his successor; by Thursday evening, Klopp's arrival was official.

At 48, Klopp brought seven seasons of experience at Borussia Dortmund. His team had won the German league in 2011 and 2012, adding the German Cup in the latter year to complete the club's first league-and-cup double. Dortmund had also reached the Champions League final in 2013. He had left the German club in May 2015.

Liverpool's fuller announcement, published on 9 October, confirmed that Klopp was taking immediate responsibility for the first team. It also named Zeljko Buvac and Peter Krawietz as senior members of his coaching staff. The appointment was therefore more than an agreement to begin work at some later point: the change of manager had taken effect.

In that next-day statement, Klopp outlined his intention to develop an energetic, attacking side and a close relationship with the supporters. Those were ambitions at the start of the job, not achievements already secured. The task was to put them into practice with the squad he had inherited.

The two dates mark different parts of the same arrival. Liverpool announced their decision on 8 October; the Anfield press conference was scheduled for 9 October. The appointment anniversary belongs to the Thursday evening, when the club confirmed who would take charge after Rodgers.

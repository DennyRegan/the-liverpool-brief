The 2005 UEFA Champions League final was played at the Atatürk Olympic Stadium in Istanbul on 25 May, the first time the competition's showpiece had been staged in Turkey and the fiftieth final in its history. Kick-off, according to UEFA's referee appointment notice, was set for 21:45 local time — 20:45 in Central Europe. Manuel Enrique Mejuto González of Spain was the referee.

Liverpool arrived under Rafael Benítez in his first season at the club, having gone out at the group stage was never a risk that materialised but progress through the knockout rounds had not been straightforward. AC Milan arrived as one of the strongest club sides in Europe: Carlo Ancelotti's team had been eliminated in the previous year's quarter-finals and now fielded a forward line and midfield that most observers rated ahead of Liverpool's on paper. Nobody disputed which side went into the final as favourites.

## Two teams, two systems

Liverpool lined up with Jerzy Dudek in goal; Steve Finnan, Jamie Carragher, Sami Hyypiä and Djimi Traoré across the back; Luis García, Steven Gerrard, Xabi Alonso and John Arne Riise in midfield; Harry Kewell just off Milan Baroš up front.

Milan's XI read Dida in goal; Cafu, Jaap Stam, Alessandro Nesta and Paolo Maldini in defence; Gennaro Gattuso, Andrea Pirlo and Clarence Seedorf in midfield; Kaká behind Andriy Shevchenko and Hernán Crespo.

Neither line-up needed introduction. What followed did.

## Three down inside forty-four minutes

Milan scored inside the first minute. Andrea Pirlo's free-kick was met by Paolo Maldini, who volleyed it in — UEFA sources vary between 51 and 52 seconds; either way, it stands as the fastest goal scored in a Champions League final. Liverpool had barely touched the ball.

The half settled after that, without settling in Liverpool's favour. Shortly before the half-hour, Liverpool had a penalty appeal for handball against Nesta waved away by Mejuto González. Milan broke immediately, and from the move that followed, Shevchenko squared for Crespo to tap in on 39 minutes. Five minutes before the interval, Kaká sent Crespo through again, and Crespo dinked the ball past Dudek for his second. Milan led 3-0 at half-time, and the gap between the two sides on the pitch looked, if anything, wider than the scoreline.

What Liverpool said to each other in that dressing room is not fully recorded, but two of those present later spoke about it in an oral history UEFA published in 2023. In UEFA's 2023 oral history, Dietmar Hamann said of the shoot-out: "I thought to myself at the start of the penalty shoot-out, 'If we don't beat them now, we never will.'" Steven Gerrard, in a separate UEFA retrospective, described his own state of mind more starkly: "I was sitting there and my head was in my hands: I thought it was over."

## Six minutes

Liverpool made a substitution at half-time — Hamann on for Finnan — and within nine minutes of the restart the match had changed shape entirely.

In the 54th minute, Gerrard headed in a Riise cross. Two minutes later, Vladimír Šmicer, on since the 23rd minute for Kewell, drove a low shot in from the edge of the area. And in the 60th minute, Gattuso fouled Gerrard in the box; Xabi Alonso's penalty was saved by Dida, but Alonso scored from the rebound. Three goals in six minutes had brought Liverpool level at 3-3, from three down at the interval.

Milan made changes of their own as the match wore on — Tomasson for Crespo in the 85th minute, Serginho for Seedorf a minute later — but the scoreline held through the rest of normal time. Liverpool made one further substitution, Djibril Cissé for Baroš in the 85th minute. Jamie Carragher was booked in the second half, in the 75th or 76th minute depending on the record consulted; Baroš was cautioned late on too, according to one match database, though that booking does not appear in the contemporary minute-by-minute reporting of the game.

## Extra time, and a save that is still shown back

Extra time settled into a tighter, more cautious match than the second half that preceded it. Milan brought on Rui Costa for Gattuso in the 112th minute and pushed for a winner that would have denied Liverpool the shoot-out altogether.

Late in extra time, Jerzy Dudek produced a double save from point-blank range to deny Andriy Shevchenko — the save most often replayed from the entire match, and the moment that kept the score at 3-3 through to penalties. UEFA's own report places it with two minutes left; other accounts have put it a few minutes earlier, in the 117th minute. What is not in dispute is what it prevented: a Milan winner that would have ended the final there and then.

Dudek later described part of what happened in the moments before it, recalling that Carragher had jumped on his back during the shoot-out preparations, telling him: "Jerzy, come on Jerzy! You need to put the pressure on them! More pressure! Come on… do something! Remember Bruce, remember Bruce Grobbelaar." Dudek said he had not wanted to make his teammates laugh — only to unsettle the opposition.

## The shoot-out

Milan shot first. Serginho put his effort over the bar; Dudek saved from Pirlo; Jon Dahl Tomasson and Kaká both scored; Dudek saved again, from Shevchenko, to end Milan's sequence.

Liverpool went 3-2 through the shoot-out with Hamann and Cissé both scoring, Riise's kick saved, and Šmicer converting — leaving Shevchenko needing to score to keep Milan in the final. He did not. Liverpool had won the European Cup for the fifth time, 3-2 on penalties after the match itself had finished 3-3.

Rafael Benítez later credited preparation as much as fortune for the outcome, saying: "When it went to penalties, that was a result of luck and hard work because of the five penalty takers Milan had, we knew about four of them very well and where they usually shot. We'd been compiling information and statistics on them for some time. That once again came down to our methodical nature."

## What the night settled

Steven Gerrard was named UEFA's Player of the Match. His header had started the fightback and his performance across the ninety minutes and extra time — much of it spent driving Liverpool forward from a position that had looked hopeless at half-time — was the individual display most closely associated with the result.

The attendance was recorded by UEFA's technical report as 69,600, though some secondary sources round the figure to 69,000. It was Liverpool's fifth European Cup, meaning the trophy itself was theirs to keep under UEFA's rules for five-time winners, and it made them the first side to lift the Champions League after trailing by three goals at half-time in a final.

None of that changes what the scoreline said at the interval. Milan had played the better football for forty-five minutes and led by three goals against a Liverpool side that had barely threatened. What happened afterwards is the reason the match is still talked about by people who were not born when it was played: not simply that Liverpool won, but that they won from there.

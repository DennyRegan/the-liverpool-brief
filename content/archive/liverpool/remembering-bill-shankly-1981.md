Bill Shankly died on 29 September 1981, aged 68, after suffering a heart attack. He had been retired for seven years, but the Liverpool side preparing for a European Cup match the following evening belonged to a club he had helped transform.

When Shankly arrived in December 1959, Liverpool were in the Second Division. Promotion came in 1962. His side won the league championship in 1964 and again in 1966, with the club's first FA Cup victory between those two titles. In 1973, Liverpool won both the league and the UEFA Cup, their first European trophy. A second FA Cup followed in 1974, before his retirement that summer.

That record spanned two distinct Liverpool teams. The side associated with Ron Yeats, Ian St John and Roger Hunt had been followed by one containing Kevin Keegan, Steve Heighway and Ray Clemence. Shankly had rebuilt a successful team as well as taking the club back into the First Division. Bob Paisley, part of his coaching staff, succeeded him as manager.

On 30 September 1981, Paisley's Liverpool faced Oulu Palloseura at Anfield in the second leg of a European Cup first-round tie. The fixture went ahead the day after Shankly's death. Supporters on the Kop sang his name during the second half, a tribute recalled in both the club's account and LFChistory's record of the night.

Liverpool won 7–0, completing an 8–0 aggregate victory. Ian Rush and Mark Lawrenson each scored their first goals for the club. Those milestones belong to the match's own history; they took place on an evening when the crowd was also remembering their former manager.

Shankly's death and that European match remain separate dates: 29 September was the day Liverpool lost their former manager; on 30 September an Anfield crowd gathered for a match and sang his name. His managerial honours explain the scale of his contribution. The tribute from the Kop records the connection that remained after he had stopped managing the team.

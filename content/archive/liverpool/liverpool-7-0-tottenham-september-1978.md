Tottenham Hotspur returned to the First Division for 1978-79 having spent the previous season in the Second, and they had used the summer to make a statement about their ambitions. In July 1978 they signed Ossie Ardiles and Ricky Villa, two members of Argentina's World Cup-winning squad from that summer, straight into a promoted side. Both started at Anfield on 2 September, in only Tottenham's fourth league match back in the top flight.

Liverpool had not needed the summer to make a point of their own. They opened 1978-79 with a 2-1 win over Queens Park Rangers at Anfield, followed it with a 3-0 win at Ipswich Town, and then won 4-1 at Manchester City. Three games, nine goals scored, two conceded. Tottenham arrived at Anfield as the visitors in Liverpool's fourth match of the campaign, with a front line built around two World Cup winners and a defence that had conceded only modestly on its early travels elsewhere. Neither fact would count for much by full time.

## Three up by half-time

Liverpool lined up with Ray Clemence in goal, Phil Neal, Phil Thompson, Emlyn Hughes and Alan Kennedy across the back, Jimmy Case, Graeme Souness and Terry McDermott in midfield, and Steve Heighway, Kenny Dalglish and Ray Kennedy in attack. Tottenham's side read Barry Daines, Don McAllister, Terry Naylor, John Lacy, Steve Perryman, Glenn Hoddle, Neil McNab, Peter Taylor, Ardiles, Villa and John Duncan.

Dalglish scored twice inside the first twenty minutes, in the 8th and 20th, and Ray Kennedy added a third in the 28th. Half-time score: 3-0. Whatever Tottenham had hoped to bring into English football's top division that summer, the first 45 minutes at Anfield gave them nothing to work with.

David Johnson had come on for Liverpool during the first half, replacing Hughes. Johnson replaced Hughes in the first half (11v11 records it at 25 minutes). What is clear is that Johnson was on the pitch in time to do serious damage after the interval.

## A second half that kept going

Three minutes into the second half, Johnson made it 4-0. Ten minutes after that, he scored again for 5-0. In the 64th minute Neal converted a penalty for 6-0. According to Liverpool's own historical archive, the penalty followed a foul on Heighway, and Neal's first attempt was ordered to be retaken after Daines was judged to have moved off his line before the kick was struck.

The seventh goal, in the 76th minute, is the one still talked about. Liverpool worked the ball through Dalglish and Johnson before it reached Heighway, who crossed first-time for McDermott to meet it with a diving header. Liverpool's official retrospectives are consistent on that sequence. Several retrospectives – including David Johnson's own recollection – say the move began from a Spurs corner, with McDermott racing from his own box to the far post.

Bob Paisley's assessment of the goal has been preserved in Liverpool's own retrospective features, though no contemporary source recording the remark at the time has been traced: he is quoted as saying it "must be the best goal Anfield has ever seen."

Seven goals, four different scorers, no reply. The match finished 7-0.

## What it meant

The attendance was 50,705, recorded in Tottenham's own club handbook for that season. It remains, by the club's own statistical record, the heaviest league defeat in Tottenham Hotspur's history, and their heaviest defeat at Anfield specifically.

For Liverpool, the scoreline extended a run rather than announcing one. Across their opening four league matches of 1978-79 — the wins over QPR, Ipswich and Manchester City, and now Tottenham — they had scored sixteen goals. Ardiles and Villa, brought in that summer as the signings that were meant to lift Tottenham back into the First Division with genuine substance, had started a match in which their side conceded seven without reply, in only their fourth league game back among England's elite.

None of that settled what kind of season either club would go on to have. It did settle, for Tottenham, which single afternoon they would spend decades being unable to point to as worse.

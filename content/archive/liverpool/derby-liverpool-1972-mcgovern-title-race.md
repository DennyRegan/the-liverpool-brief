John McGovern’s goal ended Derby County’s league season with a win over Liverpool. It did not settle the championship that evening, but it gave Brian Clough’s side the total that would eventually be enough.

Derby beat Liverpool 1–0 at the Baseball Ground on 1 May 1972. They had completed their forty-two matches with fifty-eight points. Liverpool, on fifty-six, still had one fixture left; Leeds United also remained capable of finishing above Derby.

The match was goalless at half-time. Its decisive move came in the sixty-second minute, when Archie Gemmill received Kevin Hector’s throw-in and worked towards the edge of the penalty area.

Gemmill laid the ball back to Alan Durban, whose dummy allowed it to run on to McGovern. The midfielder slipped as he struck, but his shot beat Ray Clemence. Derby had the lead they needed to preserve their chance of a first league title.

Clough had selected sixteen-year-old Steve Powell at right-back after Ron Webster was declared unfit. In Derby’s later account, Powell remembered learning that morning that he would play. The youngster was part of a defence that kept Liverpool out.

Bill Shankly brought on John McLaughlin for Steve Heighway after the goal, but Liverpool could not equalise. Their final opportunity would now come at Highbury a week later.

That distinction matters. Derby’s win was decisive in the eventual reckoning, yet Liverpool were not out of contention when they left the Baseball Ground. A victory over Arsenal, combined with the necessary result in Leeds’s remaining game, could still take them above Derby.

On 8 May, Leeds lost 2–1 at Wolverhampton. Liverpool could manage only a 0–0 draw at Arsenal. Derby became champions with fifty-eight points; Leeds, Liverpool and Manchester City each finished one point behind.

The defeat at Derby and the draw at Arsenal therefore belong together in Liverpool’s account of the season. At the Baseball Ground they lost ground to a direct rival. At Highbury they could not recover it.

McGovern’s shot had given Derby something to defend without playing another match. The final verdict took a further week.

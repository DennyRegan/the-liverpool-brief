Liverpool began 1959-60 as they had begun several recent seasons: in the Second Division, with no European football and no League Cup to play for, only the league and the FA Cup ahead of them. They lost the opening match 3-2 at Cardiff City, then won their first two games at Anfield, beating Bristol City 4-2 and Hull City 5-3. A fortnight later Roger Hunt made his first-team debut, scoring in a 2-0 win over Scunthorpe United. None of it looked like the prelude to anything unusual.

By November it was. In the space of three weeks Liverpool signed a forward from Everton, lost their manager, and appointed the man who would run the club for the next fifteen years.

## A manager leaves

Phil Taylor's side had been solid without being sharp — good home form, an inconsistent one on the road, a place in the upper half of the table without ever threatening the top two. On 6 November they signed Dave Hickson from Everton. Accounts differ on what Liverpool paid for him: contemporary Liverpool Echo reporting put the fee at around £12,000, while other records list £10,000. Hickson, greeting the move, said he was "very pleased indeed that I have signed for Liverpool after a week of uncertainty" and that his wife and family shared his feelings.

Eight days later Liverpool lost 4-2 at Lincoln City. It was Taylor's last match. He resigned as manager on 17 November 1959.

The club did not appoint a successor immediately. Two league matches were played under caretaker management — a 4-3 win over Leyton Orient at Anfield on 21 November and a 1-0 defeat at Huddersfield Town a week later. It is widely reported, though not confirmed in club records, that first-team trainer Bob Paisley took charge for those two games. In between, on 20 November, Liverpool signed the Scottish international Tommy Leishman from St Mirren, commonly reported at £10,000.

Liverpool appointed Bill Shankly as manager on 1 December 1959. Some accounts date the club's public announcement to the day before; the precise sequence of announcement, board decision and Shankly's arrival at Anfield is not given consistently across the sources, but the appointment itself is settled at the start of December.

## Shankly's first months

His first match in charge, at Anfield on 19 December, was a 0-4 defeat to Cardiff City — the same club that had beaten Liverpool on the season's opening day. Among those given a debut that afternoon was Allan Jones. Afterwards Shankly gave a verdict that read as measured rather than alarmed: "Naturally I'm disappointed but it's just as well that I've seen the team give an off-form display in my first match. I've learned quite a few things this way."

There was no immediate transformation. Liverpool beat Ipswich Town 3-1 and won at Bristol Rovers before Christmas, then lost 3-0 at Charlton Athletic on Boxing Day and beat the same side 2-0 two days later. They won at Hull City in the new year and went into the FA Cup with the league form neither collapsing nor lifting clear.

The FA Cup brought the season's biggest crowd. Liverpool beat Leyton Orient 2-1 at Anfield in the third round on 9 January, then lost 1-3 to Manchester United in the fourth round on 30 January in front of 56,736 — the highest attendance Anfield recorded that season. It ended Liverpool's interest in the competition for the year.

The league form through the spring was streaky rather than settling into any clear pattern: a 5-1 win over Stoke City, a 1-2 defeat at Portsmouth, a 2-2 draw with Huddersfield, a 4-4 draw at Aston Villa, a 1-3 home defeat to Lincoln City. On 16 April, Ian Callaghan made his debut in a 4-0 win over Bristol Rovers. He was 18, and remembered it decades later as "one of the highlights of my life really, making my debut for Liverpool." Home and away matches against Rotherham United followed within two days of each other — a 3-0 win, then a 2-2 draw — before a 1-0 win at Ipswich Town closed out the away fixtures. A Liverpool Senior Cup defeat to Everton at Goodison Park in late April had no bearing on the league campaign. Liverpool finished with a 3-0 win over Sunderland at Anfield on 30 April.

## Where it left them

Liverpool finished third in the Second Division with 50 points from 42 games — 20 wins, 10 draws, 12 defeats, 90 goals scored and 66 conceded — eight points behind Cardiff City in the second and final promotion place. It was a season spent, once again, on the outside of the division above.

Roger Hunt was the club's leading scorer, with 21 league goals and 23 in all competitions in his first full season. Dave Hickson, after his November move, scored 21 league goals of his own; between them the two forwards accounted for 42 of Liverpool's 90 league goals.

Judged purely on the table, 1959-60 changed little. Liverpool had finished outside the promotion places with a similar points total to recent seasons, under two different managers whose results, set side by side, look much the same. What had changed was who would be picking the team from now on, and that would not show itself in a single set of results. It would take the next two and a half years, and considerably more building, before it did.

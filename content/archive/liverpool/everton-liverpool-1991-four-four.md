Liverpool led four times at Goodison Park on 20 February 1991 and still could not settle their FA Cup fifth-round tie with Everton. Peter Beardsley scored twice, Ian Rush and John Barnes once each, but two goals apiece from Graeme Sharp and substitute Tony Cottee produced a 4–4 draw after extra time.

The clubs had drawn 0–0 at Anfield three days earlier. The replay began with Beardsley restored to Liverpool’s starting side, and he opened the scoring before half-time after Rush had broken through and Andy Hinchcliffe had intervened on the goal line.

Sharp headed Everton level early in the second half. Beardsley answered with a shot from outside the penalty area, only for Sharp to exploit another opening and make it 2–2. Liverpool’s second lead had lasted only a matter of minutes.

Rush then headed in Jan Mølby’s delivery to put Liverpool ahead for a third time. Howard Kendall introduced Cottee late in normal time, and the substitute combined with Stuart McCall to equalise before the whistle. A match Liverpool had repeatedly appeared close to winning would require another half-hour.

Barnes supplied the fourth Liverpool goal with a curling right-footed shot over Neville Southall. Cottee answered again in the second period of extra time, taking advantage when the ball escaped Liverpool’s defence. Everton had rescued the tie for a fourth time.

The draw meant another replay at Goodison rather than a penalty shoot-out. Before that match could take place, Dalglish resigned on 22 February. This became the final game of his first spell as Liverpool manager.

Ronnie Moran took temporary charge, and Everton won the second replay 1–0 on 27 February. The eight-goal match had left the cup contest unresolved; within a week, Liverpool had both a different manager and an exit from the competition.

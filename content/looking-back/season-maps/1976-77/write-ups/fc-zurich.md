# FC Zürich: the road to Rome

**FC Zürich 1 Liverpool 3. European Cup semi-final, first leg. Wednesday 6 April 1977, Letzigrund. Attendance 30,000.**
**Liverpool 3 FC Zürich 0. Second leg. Wednesday 20 April 1977, Anfield. Attendance 50,611. Liverpool won 6–1 on aggregate.**

After Saint-Étienne, the semi-final draw gave Liverpool the Swiss champions. It was only the second time Liverpool had reached the last four of the European Cup. The first had been in 1965, when Inter Milan beat them. In the other semi-final, Borussia Mönchengladbach played Dynamo Kyiv.

The first leg in Zürich began badly. With Phil Thompson injured, Tommy Smith was back in the side, and inside the first six minutes his tackle on Alfred Scheiwiler sent the ball over the byline. Frank McGhee of the Daily Mirror thought that in England it would have been worth no more than a corner. The Turkish referee, Doğan Babaçan, gave a penalty. Clemence got a hand to Peter Risi's kick but could not keep it out. A few minutes later Smith was booked for a foul on René Botteron, whom McGhee rated Zürich's most dangerous midfielder.

Liverpool were level in the 14th minute. Hilmar Zigerlig fouled Keegan, Ray Kennedy floated the free kick across, and Phil Neal arrived to score. McGhee gave some of the credit to Terry McDermott, whose decoy run had pulled the defence away. From there, by McGhee's account, Liverpool did what they always tried to do away from home in Europe: slow the game down and quieten the crowd.

The tie turned at the start of the second half. Heighway forced his way between three defenders to collect a pass and score. Midway through the half he was brought down in full flight, Babaçan gave Liverpool a penalty, and Neal scored his second. It finished 3–1.

Emlyn Hughes called the result "bloody marvellous", according to the agency report. Paisley praised Botteron but said that, as a team, Zürich did not compare with Saint-Étienne. Zürich's coach, Timo Konietzka, blamed the injuries that had plagued his side.

* * *

The second leg was a formality, and it was played like one. The Daily Mirror noted that Zürich played with only two men forward. Jimmy Case scored just after the half-hour, and Yates wrote that Liverpool could easily have had four by then.

The only worry came 20 minutes from the end, when Heighway went off with a knock on the knee and Alan Waddle came on. Then Liverpool scored twice more in the last 15 minutes. Case scored his second with a direct free kick, and Waddle made the third for Keegan. Yates thought the Swiss had never looked like causing an upset.

He also noted that Smith was the only Liverpool player on the pitch who had played in the 1965 semi-final against Inter. The gate receipts were the biggest in the club's history. Liverpool were the third English club to reach a European Cup final, after Manchester United and Leeds United. Gladbach beat Dynamo Kyiv the same night, so the final would be against the West German champions, in Rome on 25 May.

There was no time to enjoy it. Three days later Liverpool were at Maine Road for an FA Cup semi-final against Everton.

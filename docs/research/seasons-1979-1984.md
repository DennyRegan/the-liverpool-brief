Every competition entry is checked against the complete competitive fixture list. Aggregate scores are arithmetic from the two legs. European Cup third-round ties in 1982 and 1983 are quarter-finals. The 1981 Tokyo match is the Intercontinental Cup, distinct from the later FIFA Club World Cup. Key players are an editorial selection checked against competitive appearances, not a claimed objective ranking. Shootout kicks do not count as player goals.

## Conflicts and exclusions

- McDermott’s 1980–81 total is 22 including the Charity Shield, not 21. LFChistory and 11v11 agree. Other 11v11 player totals differ (notably the Kennedys and Neal); these are not published.
- The official Fagan biography compresses his two campaigns and places Souness’s departure alongside his initial appointment. Use it only for managerial succession and the first-year treble; use dated ledgers for transfers.
- Some official player-profile “Years” headings describe playing appearances, not registration. Whelan signed in 1979 but debuted in 1981; Grobbelaar signed in March 1981 and debuted in August. These are different events, not interchangeable dates.
- No unverified fees, attendance figures, dressing-room motives or quotations are included.

## Transfer boundaries for continuation

- Rush (April 1980), Money (May 1980), Grobbelaar (March 1981) and Johnston (April 1981) stay in the campaign in progress. They are not selected as key players before their competitive involvement.
- Clemence and Case’s August 1981 exits belong in 1981–82.
- Sheedy’s 26 May 1982 move to Everton follows that league season and is grouped with 1982–83, using the preceding ledger as its explicit source.
- Beglin’s May 1983 arrival is retained with Paisley’s final campaign, supported by the club profile identifying him as Paisley’s last signing. No precise day is asserted.
- Paul Walsh’s 17 May 1984 signing is allocated to the incoming 1984–85 campaign. Do not omit or duplicate it in the next batch. Souness’s later departure also belongs in 1984–85.

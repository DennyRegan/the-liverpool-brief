# Klopp's Liverpool, Part 4: The Rise of a Contender

*Working title. Suggested excerpt: In 2017–18 Liverpool scored more goals in a Champions League season than any club before them and reached their first European Cup final in eleven years. In October they had been ninth. This is the story of how the attack carried them to Kyiv, and what the final revealed.*

---

The apology to Southampton in early June closed one line of inquiry. Fifteen days later Liverpool opened another. On Thursday 22 June 2017 the club announced the signing of Mohamed Salah from Roma, effective from 1 July, on a five-year contract. Roma gave the fee as €42m, rising by up to €8m in add-ons. In sterling Sky Sports put the fee at up to £34.3m, which it said was just short of the club record, though some headlines called it a club record.

Salah was not an unknown quantity in England, which was part of the question around him. He had joined Chelsea in 2014 and made 13 appearances by Sports Illustrated's count (Sky Sports gave 19), scoring twice, before being loaned to Fiorentina and then Roma. Sports Illustrated's report of the signing noted that history and said he would be looking to prove he belonged. In Italy he had become one of Serie A's most productive wide forwards. Klopp, quoted on the club's website, said Salah had "the perfect mix of experience and potential" and called the signing really exciting.

The rest of the summer followed the pattern set the year before: a few deliberate purchases, several departures, and one major target pursued for longer than seemed reasonable. Andrew Robertson came from Hull City, who had just been relegated, for £8m rising to £10m. Klopp's verdict was brief: "This is a player who does not limit his ambition." Dominic Solanke arrived from Chelsea when his contract expired, with compensation left to be settled between the clubs. It took a year to agree. Lucas Leiva left for Lazio for £5m after ten years at the club, which he described as "a little bit like a rollercoaster: a lot of ups and downs".

The most significant agreement of the summer did not bring a player to Anfield at all. RB Leipzig had rejected two Liverpool bids for Naby Keïta in July, and a Leipzig official had said he would not be sold in that window. On 29 August Liverpool confirmed that they had met his £48m release clause, plus an undisclosed premium, and that he would join on 1 July 2018. Sky Sports reported it as a club record.

Two days later, on deadline day, Alex Oxlade-Chamberlain joined from Arsenal for a reported £35m, possibly rising to £40m. He was in the last year of his contract, had turned down a new deal at Arsenal reportedly worth £180,000 a week, and was thought to have chosen Liverpool over Chelsea. In the early hours of 1 September Mamadou Sakho, who had been sent home from the previous summer's tour, completed a move to Crystal Palace for £24m plus £2m in add-ons.

All summer one sale had been refused. After Neymar's world-record move to Paris Saint-Germain, Barcelona wanted Philippe Coutinho. On 11 August Fenway Sports Group issued a statement: "The club's definitive stance is that no offers for Philippe will be considered." Sky Sports understood that two Barcelona bids had been rejected by then, the latest worth up to £90.4m. Coutinho had signed a contract in January with no release clause. Later that month Barcelona signed Ousmane Dembélé from Dortmund for £97m. Coutinho stayed, though, as it turned out, the matter had been put off rather than settled.

\* \* \*

The season began with a warning. At Watford on 12 August Salah scored on his league debut, but Liverpool drew 3–3 when Miguel Britos equalised in the 93rd minute. Three days later they went to Hoffenheim for the first leg of the Champions League play-off. Fourth place had brought only a qualifying tie, not a guaranteed group place. Trent Alexander-Arnold, 18 and making his European debut, scored from a free-kick after 35 minutes. Klopp said afterwards that a teenager's nerve in taking it at all mattered more than anything that had gone wrong. Liverpool won 2–1 and finished the job at Anfield on 23 August, scoring three times in the first 21 minutes on the way to a 4–2 win and a 6–3 aggregate. Four days later Arsenal were beaten 4–0.

What came next undid that start. On 9 September at the Etihad, Sadio Mané went for a high ball and caught Ederson in the face with his boot. He was sent off on 37 minutes. Klopp said he did not think it was a red card because Mané had not seen the goalkeeper. The FA rejected Liverpool's appeal and Mané served a three-match ban. City won 5–0. Ten days later Liverpool went out of the League Cup, beaten 2–0 at Leicester. In the Champions League they drew 2–2 at home to Sevilla in their first group-stage match since 2014, and 1–1 in Moscow against Spartak.

Those results set the pattern of the autumn. Counting the Leicester tie, Liverpool won one of seven domestic matches between 9 September and 22 October. In Europe the attack was flourishing. On 17 October they won 7–0 in Maribor, which Sky Sports reported as Liverpool's biggest away win in Europe and, citing Opta, the biggest by any English club away from home in the European Cup or Champions League. At home the defence kept conceding.

The low point came at Wembley on 22 October. Tottenham won 4–1, and Klopp took off Dejan Lovren after 31 minutes. "The whole game, the whole result was very much our fault," Klopp said, in the words Sky Sports reported. Sky Sports' panel was harder on Liverpool. "They would have won a league, probably, in the last three or four years if they could defend," said Gary Neville. "I don't see leaders at Liverpool," said Jamie Redknapp. Sky noted that Liverpool had conceded 15 away goals in the league, more than any other side. It added an Opta figure: since August 2013 Simon Mignolet had made 13 errors leading to goals, more than any other Premier League player.

That night, from nine matches, Liverpool had 13 points and a goal difference of minus two. They were ninth in the table, 12 points behind City. Klopp had spent a second summer reshaping the attack, and on that evidence Liverpool were a long way from being contenders.

\* \* \*

The recovery was uneven at first. From 28 October, when they beat Huddersfield 3–0, Liverpool went 14 league games without defeat. In Europe they beat Maribor 3–0 at Anfield. In Seville on 21 November they led 3–0 at half-time through two goals from Roberto Firmino and one from Mané, then conceded three, the last in stoppage time, and drew 3–3. On 6 December they beat Spartak 7–0 at Anfield, Coutinho scoring a hat-trick, to win the group unbeaten with 23 goals in six matches.

That night Alberto Moreno, who had started the season at left-back, injured his ankle. Robertson took his place in the side and kept it. In February, reports quoted Jordan Henderson calling Robertson brilliant on and off the field. A player bought for £8m from a relegated club had become first choice.

By then the attack had a nickname. Commentators were calling Coutinho, Firmino, Mané and Salah the "Fab Four". In early December Klopp said he had no problem with the label but that it was disrespectful to the rest of the squad. In the Guardian, Jonathan Wilson questioned it from the other direction. After a 3–3 at Arsenal on 22 December, Wilson argued that the four were not actually very effective when they played together.

Salah, meanwhile, was scoring at a rate few had expected. His two goals against Leicester on 30 December took him to 23 for the season, equalling Roger Hunt's club record for goals before the new year. At the turn of the year Liverpool were fourth with 41 points from 21 matches. They had scored 48 league goals, but they were 18 points behind City.

Three days earlier the club had resolved the longest-running story of the year. On 27 December Liverpool agreed to sign Virgil van Dijk from Southampton. The fee was not disclosed. It was widely reported as £75m, and Southampton's statement said the deal "will set a new world record for a defender". Liverpool had chased him in June, been forced into an apology and a withdrawal, and then paid more than anyone had paid for a defender. Van Dijk had not played since 13 December. He was registered from 1 January, in time for the Champions League knockout stage. On 5 January, on his debut in an FA Cup tie against Everton at Anfield, he headed the winner from an Oxlade-Chamberlain corner six minutes from time.

The next day Coutinho was sold. Barcelona agreed to pay £106.5m up front and up to £35.5m in add-ons, a possible £142m in total, and gave him a contract with a buy-out clause of £354.5m. Klopp said Coutinho had made clear that it was his dream. "I am now convinced there is nothing left at our disposal to change his mind," he said. FourFourTwo wrote that the Fab Four had become a Fab Three. When the season ended, AFP's assessment was that about half of the Coutinho money had in effect paid for Van Dijk.

The first match without Coutinho in the league was against City at Anfield on 14 January. City had not lost a league game all season and had not won at Anfield since 2003. Oxlade-Chamberlain scored after ten minutes and Leroy Sané equalised before half-time. Between the 59th and 68th minutes Firmino, Mané and Salah scored. City pulled two goals back late through Bernardo Silva and İlkay Gündoğan, but Liverpool won 4–3 and ended a City run of 30 matches unbeaten. Liverpool rose to third.

Klopp was clear about what he thought the result showed. "It is important to show it is possible to play without him and we did that," he said. He also allowed himself a prediction: "Maybe this is the only game they will lose in the whole season." He was wrong about that, as Manchester United won 3–2 at the Etihad in April, but Liverpool were the first team to beat City in the league. That afternoon Loris Karius was in goal. Asked about him on television, Klopp confirmed that Karius was now his first-choice goalkeeper.

Within two weeks Liverpool had lost twice. At Swansea, Alfie Mawson scored the only goal on 22 January. Five days later Liverpool went out of the FA Cup at home to West Bromwich Albion, 3–2, in a match dominated by the video assistant referee. VAR ruled out a Craig Dawson header, gave Liverpool a penalty that Firmino hit against the woodwork, and was used again before a second Dawson effort went in off Joël Matip. Two days later Daniel Sturridge, who had started five matches all season, went on loan to West Brom.

\* \* \*

On 14 February, in Porto, Liverpool won the first leg of their last-16 tie 5–0. Mané scored a hat-trick and Salah and Firmino one each, and the second leg finished 0–0.

The spring also gave Liverpool's European nights a new song. According to an Irish Times account of its origins, the tune was an Italian disco hit from the 1980s that had reached Liverpool fans by way of Porto's supporters. Liam Malone, a supporter, had written new words for it in December. A few days after the Porto tie the singer Jamie Webster performed it in a pub near Anfield. By the end of the quarter-final, the account said, the whole ground was singing it.

In the league the run was steady rather than spectacular. Liverpool lost 2–1 at Old Trafford on 10 March, where Marcus Rashford scored twice before Eric Bailly's own goal. A week later they beat Watford 5–0. Salah scored four, taking him to 36 for the season and past Fernando Torres's club record of 33 for a debut season. At the end of March Liverpool were third.

The cost came alongside the results. At Crystal Palace on 31 March Matip injured his thigh and Klopp said his season was over. Adam Lallana, who had missed the start of the season with a thigh injury, broke down again in the same match. Emre Can, with a back injury, was a major doubt for the quarter-final first leg. Liverpool went into the quarter-finals with a thinner squad than they had started the year with.

The draw paired them with City. Before the first leg at Anfield on 4 April, City's team coach was attacked on its way to the ground. Liverpool condemned the behaviour. In June UEFA fined the club €20,000 over the incident and other offences by supporters that night.

The match itself was over in 31 minutes. Salah scored after 12 minutes, Oxlade-Chamberlain after 21 and Mané after 31. Wilson's Guardian analysis called Liverpool's pressing ferocious. Klopp declined to celebrate.

In Manchester six days later, with Henderson suspended, Gabriel Jesus scored after two minutes and City pressed for the goal that would make the tie competitive. Salah equalised early in the second half and Firmino scored again with 13 minutes left. Liverpool won 2–1 on the night and 5–1 on aggregate. City would finish the season as champions with 100 points. In the league the two clubs had won a match each; in Europe Liverpool had beaten them twice.

\* \* \*

The semi-final against Roma began with a crime outside the ground. Before the first leg on 24 April, Sean Cox, a 53-year-old Liverpool supporter from Dunboyne in County Meath, was attacked near Anfield and suffered catastrophic head injuries.

On the pitch, Salah scored twice against his former club before half-time and was involved in everything Liverpool did well. Mané and Firmino twice took the score to 5–0. In the same match James Milner made his ninth assist of the Champions League campaign, a new record for a single season, beating the eight made by Neymar in 2016–17 and Wayne Rooney in 2013–14. Two late goals from Roma, the second a penalty, made it 5–2 and left the tie open. Oxlade-Chamberlain left the game with knee ligament damage that ended his season and ruled him out of the World Cup.

Before the second leg Salah won the two main individual awards of the English season. On 22 April he was voted the PFA Players' Player of the Year, ahead of Kevin De Bruyne. On 1 May he was named Footballer of the Year by the Football Writers' Association, the first African player to win it.

In Rome on 2 May, Liverpool scored twice in the first 25 minutes through Mané and Georginio Wijnaldum. In between, Milner turned the ball into his own net. Roma scored three more, the last two in the final minutes, and won 4–2. Liverpool went through 7–6 on aggregate. Over the two legs against Roma they had passed the record for goals in a single Champions League season, Barcelona's 45 in 1999–2000. They reached the final with 46, or 40 without the Hoffenheim play-off. Salah and Firmino each had ten goals in the competition proper.

The league season ended without much drama. A 1–0 defeat at Chelsea on 6 May left Liverpool exposed in the race for the top four. Two days later it was reported that Joe Gomez needed ankle surgery and would miss the final. On the last day, at home to Brighton, Salah scored his 32nd league goal of the season, a record for a 38-game Premier League season, and Liverpool won 4–0. Liverpool finished fourth with 75 points. They had lost five league games and drawn 12, and they were 25 points behind City. Salah had 44 goals in all competitions and Firmino 27.

\* \* \*

Liverpool had not played in a European Cup final since 2007. Getting supporters to Kyiv was difficult and expensive. Some residents of the city offered visiting fans a place to stay free of charge. Two days before the match, masked men attacked Liverpool supporters in a restaurant in the city.

The final was played at the NSC Olimpiyskiy on 26 May 2018 before 61,561 spectators. Liverpool's team was the one that had beaten City and Roma, with the gaps filled: Karius; Alexander-Arnold, Lovren, Van Dijk, Robertson; Henderson, Milner, Wijnaldum; Salah, Firmino, Mané. ESPN noted that Real Madrid started with the same eleven that had won the 2017 final. Zinedine Zidane's side had Gareth Bale on the bench.

Midway through the first half Salah and Sergio Ramos went to ground together, Ramos holding Salah's arm as they fell, and Salah landed on his shoulder. He tried to carry on, then went down again and left the field in tears around the half-hour. Lallana replaced him on 31 minutes. Real's right-back Dani Carvajal went off injured soon afterwards.

Six minutes into the second half Karius rolled the ball out towards a team-mate. Karim Benzema stretched out a leg and the ball went off him into the empty net. Four minutes later Liverpool were level. Lovren headed a Milner corner across goal and Mané scored. Then, on 61 minutes, Zidane brought on Bale. Three minutes later Bale met Marcelo's cross with an overhead kick. With seven minutes left, he struck a shot from about 40 yards that went through Karius's hands. Real Madrid won 3–1.

At full time Karius, in tears, apologised to the Liverpool supporters. In a message to supporters he wrote: "I know that I messed it up with the two mistakes and let you all down." Klopp did not defend the errors and did not dwell on them. "The mistakes were obvious, we don't have to talk about that, it's all clear; he knows it, I know it, you all know it," he said. On Salah he was blunter, saying it was very bad for Mo, for the team and for Egypt. The next day he described the Ramos challenge as "like wrestling a little bit". Egypt's federation said Salah had sprained his shoulder ligaments, and its sports minister expected him to need about two weeks' treatment before the World Cup.

\* \* \*

The verdicts were quick. The AFP report the next morning said Liverpool's belief "seemed to drain away" once Salah had gone. Mark Lawrenson told the BBC: "I don't see any way back for him at Liverpool now." A Daily Mirror analysis on 29 May said Liverpool needed two goalkeepers and a partner for Keïta in midfield. In its view the club had to go from 13 or 14 good players to 22 or 23. Fabinho's signing from Monaco had been announced the day before.

On 4 June the story changed. Karius had been examined in the United States, where he was on holiday, at Massachusetts General Hospital in Boston, at the request of Liverpool's medical staff. The hospital's doctors concluded that he "sustained a concussion during the match May 26, 2018". They said the visual and spatial problems they had identified had probably begun immediately after the blow he received, and that it was possible those problems had affected his performance. The finding did not change the result. It did make the two errors that had defined the final harder to read as simple mistakes.

What, then, had the season gained? In August Liverpool had a front four built around Coutinho and a defence that could not hold a lead at Watford. By May they had a front three that had scored 30 goals between them in the Champions League proper, a striker who had broken the Premier League's scoring record for a 38-game season, and a press that had beaten Guardiola's league leaders twice in six days. They had the most expensive defender in the world, who had arrived in January, and a left-back who had cost a fraction of his fee. On the way to Kyiv the team scored 46 Champions League goals, a record, and its supporters had a new song for European nights.

The final showed how much of that rested on a few people. When Salah left, Liverpool were without the player who had scored 44 of their goals, and the replacement was a midfielder who had broken down with injury twice that season. When the attack slowed, the goalkeeper who had been first choice for four months made two errors, later attributed at least partly to concussion. The unused substitutes were Mignolet, Clyne, Klavan, Moreno and Solanke, and Can came on only in the 83rd minute. Oxlade-Chamberlain, Matip and Gomez were injured.

None of this made Liverpool a poor side. With Salah off the pitch they had drawn level with the European champions early in the second half and held them until a substitute of Bale's quality scored one spectacular goal and was handed another by a goalkeeper who, it later emerged, was concussed. But in October Neville had said Liverpool could not defend and Redknapp that they had no leaders. By May the criticism had moved on. The question was no longer whether Liverpool could defend. It was whether a team built to overwhelm opponents could survive losing the man at the centre of it. The season had answered the first criticism. It had not yet answered the second.

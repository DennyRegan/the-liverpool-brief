Fernando Torres scored twice in the second half as Liverpool beat Benfica 4–1 at Anfield on 8 April 2010, reversing a first-leg deficit to reach the Europa League semi-finals. The 5–3 aggregate victory was secured only after Óscar Cardozo had brought the Portuguese side back within a goal of changing the outcome.

Liverpool had left Lisbon with a 2–1 defeat and an away goal. Daniel Agger had put them ahead there, but Ryan Babel’s dismissal was followed by two Cardozo penalties. At Anfield, a single unanswered goal would be enough to advance; Rafael Benítez’s side eventually needed a more substantial margin.

Benfica, the Portuguese league leaders, began the return confidently. Ángel Di María sent a free-kick into the wall, while Liverpool took time to create openings. Torres headed one chance straight at Júlio César, with an offside flag already raised, then glanced another delivery wide.

The breakthrough arrived before the half-hour. Dirk Kuyt met Steven Gerrard’s corner with a close-range header, but celebrations were interrupted by the assistant referee’s flag. After consultation between the officials, referee Björn Kuipers awarded the goal. The aggregate score was level and Liverpool held the away-goal advantage.

Gerrard supplied the second as well. His pass released Lucas Leiva through the defence, and the midfielder went around Júlio César before rolling the ball into the empty net. Liverpool led 2–0 at half-time and 3–2 across the tie.

Torres increased that advantage with a counterattack after the break. Javier Mascherano found Yossi Benayoun on the left; Benayoun moved the ball across to Kuyt, whose first-time cross gave Torres the finish. Liverpool were three goals ahead on the night.

Cardozo then drove a low free-kick through the wall and past Pepe Reina. At 3–1, Liverpool still led 4–3 on aggregate, but another Benfica goal would have put the visitors ahead on away goals. Cardozo threatened again from a free-kick, his effort deflecting wide.

With eight minutes remaining, Mascherano sent Torres clear. The striker lifted his finish over substitute goalkeeper Moreira, who had replaced the injured Júlio César, to restore Liverpool’s three-goal match advantage.

That fourth goal settled the quarter-final. Liverpool would face Atlético Madrid, Torres’s former club, for a place in the Hamburg final.

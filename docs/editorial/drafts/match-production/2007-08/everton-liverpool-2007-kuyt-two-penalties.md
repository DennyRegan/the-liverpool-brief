Dirk Kuyt converted two penalties as Liverpool came from behind to win 2–1 at Goodison Park on 20 October 2007. Everton finished with nine players, and the winner followed Phil Neville's deliberate handball to prevent Lucas Leiva from scoring late in the match.

Everton had taken the lead through Sami Hyypiä's own goal in the 38th minute. Their threat from set pieces had troubled Liverpool, and the home side reached half-time with an advantage that survived until Steven Gerrard's run into the penalty area early in the second half.

Tony Hibbert brought Gerrard down and was sent off. Kuyt beat Tim Howard from the spot to level, giving Liverpool more than half an hour to seek a winner against ten men.

The equaliser did not remove the derby's disputes. Kuyt was booked for a high challenge on Neville, while Everton had penalty appeals rejected. Rafael Benítez then made an unusual change in the 71st minute, replacing Gerrard with Lucas while the score remained 1–1.

The substitute was involved in the incident that settled the match. With Liverpool pressing towards the end, Lucas struck a shot that Neville stopped with his hand on the line. The Everton captain was dismissed and Kuyt took responsibility for a second penalty, converting again to put Liverpool ahead.

Everton still appealed for a penalty after Jamie Carragher's challenge on Joleon Lescott, but referee Mark Clattenburg did not award one. An earlier appeal involving Steve Finnan and Lescott had also been unsuccessful.

Liverpool left with all three points after trailing at the interval, with both goals supplied by Kuyt from the penalty spot.

With six minutes of normal time left at Anfield, Arsenal were heading through on away goals. By the end of the Champions League quarter-final on 8 April 2008, Liverpool had scored twice more and secured a 4–2 victory, progressing 5–3 on aggregate.

The sides had drawn the first leg 1–1 in London. Arsenal began the return strongly and took the lead when Alexander Hleb found Abou Diaby, whose shot beat Pepe Reina at his near post. Liverpool were behind on the night and in the tie.

Sami Hyypiä brought them level on the half-hour. Steven Gerrard delivered a corner and the defender escaped Philippe Senderos to head in off the post. That goal restored parity and gave Liverpool a foothold after Arsenal's effective opening.

The next decisive finish came midway through the second half. Peter Crouch flicked on a long clearance from Reina, and Fernando Torres controlled the ball, turned and struck high into the corner. Liverpool now led 2–1 and had the aggregate advantage.

Arsenal's response came through substitute Theo Walcott. Collecting the ball in his own half, he carried it through Liverpool's retreating defence and delivered across goal for Emmanuel Adebayor to score. At 2–2 on the night and 3–3 across the two legs, Arsenal's two away goals placed them ahead of Liverpool's one.

The position lasted only briefly. Ryan Babel, who had replaced Crouch, drove into the penalty area and went down under Kolo Touré's challenge. Referee Peter Fröjdfeldt awarded a penalty. Gerrard converted it to restore Liverpool's lead, putting them back in front in the tie.

Even then, another Arsenal goal could have reversed qualification again. Babel settled the contest in stoppage time, breaking forward, outpacing Cesc Fàbregas and finishing past Manuel Almunia. Liverpool had their fourth goal and the two-goal aggregate margin that carried them through.

The result put Liverpool into another Champions League semi-final against Chelsea. Torres' finish had appeared to give them control, Walcott and Adebayor had briefly changed the destination of the tie, and Gerrard's penalty had changed it back. Babel's final run supplied the security that neither side had been able to establish during the preceding exchanges.

Fernando Torres' second-half goal at San Siro completed Liverpool's passage into the Champions League quarter-finals on 11 March 2008. A 1–0 win over Inter Milan added to the two-goal advantage established at Anfield, taking Rafael Benítez's side through 3–0 on aggregate.

The return was also Jamie Carragher's hundredth European appearance for Liverpool, a milestone no previous player at the club had reached. He and his defensive colleagues began with a lead to protect against opponents who needed to recover the two late goals conceded in the first leg.

Inter created opportunities to narrow that deficit before half-time. Pepe Reina saved a low effort from Julio Cruz and also stopped a backheeled attempt by the forward. Cruz sent another chance wide after a pass from Zlatan Ibrahimović. Liverpool threatened as well, with Torres forcing Júlio César into a save after an Esteban Cambiasso mistake.

The tie remained goalless on the night at the interval. Early in the second half, Inter were reduced to ten men for the second time across the two legs. Nicolás Burdisso, already booked following a challenge on Dirk Kuyt, received a second yellow card after fouling Lucas Leiva.

Liverpool made the numerical advantage count. Fábio Aurélio supplied Torres from the left, and the striker controlled the ball with his back to goal, turned and sent a low right-footed shot past Júlio César.

That away goal placed the tie beyond Inter's remaining response. Liverpool kept another clean sheet, winning both legs against the Italian champions and completing qualification without conceding.

They joined Arsenal, Chelsea and Manchester United in the quarter-finals, giving England four clubs in the last eight. For Liverpool, the Anfield goals had created the advantage; Reina's saves and Torres' finish preserved and extended it in Milan.

Mark Wright's late equaliser completed Liverpool's second recovery of an extraordinary League Cup tie at Anfield. Chesterfield had led 3–0 early in the second half and 4–2 with little more than twenty minutes remaining. The first leg finished 4–4, leaving Liverpool still in the contest after a night in which fourth-tier opposition had repeatedly exposed them.

Steve Norris put Chesterfield ahead in the seventh minute. Dave Lancaster added a second before half-time and scored again shortly after the restart. Difficult backpasses involving Wright had contributed to Liverpool's problems, with David James unable to deal cleanly with the danger before Lancaster's first goal. At the interval Liverpool were two down; within minutes of returning they faced a three-goal deficit.

Istvan Kozma, introduced for Phil Charnock at half-time, helped initiate the response. His cross from the right was met by Ronny Rosenthal's diving header. Don Hutchison then scored his first senior Liverpool goal, bringing the deficit back to one and changing the direction of the tie.

Chesterfield did not simply retreat. Norris broke through to score his second and restore their two-goal cushion. Liverpool now needed two goals merely to draw. This time they reached level terms. Mark Walters struck a low shot to make it 4–3. Wright then headed in another Kozma delivery for the equaliser with five minutes remaining.

The result preserved Liverpool's chance of progress without disguising how close they had come to taking a severe deficit into the return. They had needed four goals at home to draw with a club playing in the newly named Third Division, the fourth tier after the creation of the Premier League.

Liverpool won the second leg 4–1 on 6 October and advanced 8–5 on aggregate. That comfortable return completed the recovery begun by Rosenthal, Hutchison, Walters and Wright on a far less comfortable evening at Anfield.

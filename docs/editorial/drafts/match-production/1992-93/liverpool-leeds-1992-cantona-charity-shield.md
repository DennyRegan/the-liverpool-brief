Liverpool arrived at Wembley as FA Cup holders to face the league champions, Leeds United. The Charity Shield on 8 August 1992 offered Graeme Souness an early competitive outing for Paul Stewart, making his debut, and an examination of a side arranged with three central defenders. By the finish Liverpool had scored three times and still lost. Eric Cantona supplied the difference with a hat-trick.

Cantona opened the scoring after 26 minutes, meeting Rod Wallace's cutback. Liverpool's response came through a familiar scorer: Ian Rush headed in Ronny Rosenthal's cross from near the byline to make it 1–1. Yet the equaliser did not carry Liverpool into the interval on level terms. Tony Dorigo's free-kick took a deflection off Rosenthal and restored Leeds' advantage shortly before half-time.

Dean Saunders brought Liverpool back again in the second half. After Mark Walters' effort was blocked, Saunders finished on the half-volley to make it 2–2. Twice Liverpool had recovered; neither recovery produced a period in which they could make the score secure.

Cantona struck again following a Leeds free-kick, then completed his treble with a header from another Wallace cross. Bruce Grobbelaar came for the delivery but could not intercept it. A contest that had been level with little more than a quarter of an hour remaining had moved decisively towards Leeds.

There was still one further Liverpool goal. Mark Wright's effort following a corner went in off Gordon Strachan and was recorded as an own goal. It reduced the deficit to 4–3 but left Liverpool without time to find another equaliser.

The Shield went to Leeds. Liverpool's attacking response had kept the match open, with Rush and Saunders both scoring, but the champions repeatedly found routes through their defence. A week before Liverpool's first Premier League fixture, their three-goal return had been outweighed by the openings conceded at the other end.

Liverpool's defence of the FA Cup ended in the third round at Anfield on 13 January 1993. Bolton Wanderers, playing in the third tier, won the replay 2–0 through headers from John McGinlay and Andy Walker. The holders had recovered from two goals down to draw at Burnden Park; this time there was no recovery.

Bolton went ahead in the third minute. David Lee beat Mike Marsh on the flank and crossed for McGinlay to head in. The early goal gave the visitors an advantage they had the pace and organisation to protect, while Liverpool were again required to pursue a tie they had begun as favourites.

Lee's running continued to cause problems. Graeme Souness switched his full-backs in an attempt to contain him, but the threat did not disappear. At the other end Keith Branagan denied Ronny Rosenthal and Jamie Redknapp as Liverpool sought an equaliser. Their opportunities did not become a sustained, effective response to Bolton's lead.

Liverpool also lost Michael Thomas to injury early in the second half. As the match moved towards its conclusion, Bolton remained ahead and retained enough attacking purpose to settle it themselves. McGinlay crossed for Walker to head in the second goal after 78 minutes. The scorer of the opener had supplied the cross that removed Liverpool's remaining margin for recovery.

Bolton's victory was achieved by a club in the division then called the Second Division, renamed after the launch of the Premier League and representing the third level of English football. It ended Liverpool's involvement in the last domestic cup competition still open to them that season.

Spartak Moscow had already eliminated Liverpool from Europe, and Crystal Palace had ended their League Cup run in December. The FA Cup won at Wembley the previous May now passed out of Liverpool's hands at the first hurdle of their defence, with Bolton winning at Anfield on the strength of two decisive headers and a lead they never relinquished.

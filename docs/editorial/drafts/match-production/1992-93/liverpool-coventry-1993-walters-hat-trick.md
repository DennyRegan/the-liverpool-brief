Mark Walters scored Liverpool's first Premier League hat-trick in a 4–0 victory over Coventry City at Anfield on 17 April 1993. Two first-half goals and a penalty soon after the interval settled a match that also brought a long-range goal from David Burrows.

The meeting had an uncomfortable precedent for Liverpool. Coventry had beaten them 5–1 at Highfield Road in December, one of the heaviest defeats of a difficult winter. By the time of the return Liverpool were recovering league positions, and Walters gave them an early route to another win.

Don Hutchison supplied a low cross for the opening goal. Walters met it to put Liverpool ahead, then added a second with a lob over Jonathan Gould. The two finishes established a clear advantage before half-time and left Coventry needing to recover against a Liverpool side already producing chances through several different routes.

Walters completed his hat-trick early in the second half. Andy Pearce fouled Paul Stewart and Walters converted the penalty, taking the score to 3–0.

Burrows added the fourth after receiving a short pass from John Barnes. His shot from distance completed the scoring and gave Liverpool a four-goal margin without reply. The defender's contribution extended a result already shaped by Walters' finishing.

The victory lifted Liverpool to eighth in the immediate league table. They had lost heavily in the corresponding away fixture, but the Anfield return formed part of the spring improvement that was carrying them back into the top half. No cup competition remained for them to pursue; league results were now the measure of what could still be recovered from the campaign.

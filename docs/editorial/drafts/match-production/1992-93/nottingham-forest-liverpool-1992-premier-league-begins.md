Nottingham Forest won 1–0 at the City Ground on 16 August 1992, Teddy Sheringham scoring the only goal of Liverpool's first Premier League match. The visitors could not find an equaliser, despite an improved attacking performance after half-time.

The occasion also marked David James' competitive debut. With Bruce Grobbelaar away on international duty for Zimbabwe, the goalkeeper was immediately required to deal with Forest's movement through the middle. He saved early efforts from Roy Keane and Nigel Clough, and later kept out a Keane header. Those interventions kept Liverpool in the match before their own attacking play acquired greater purpose.

James could not stop Sheringham before the half-hour. Scot Gemmill supplied the pass and Sheringham moved in from the left before shooting across the goalkeeper into the far corner. Forest had repeatedly threatened the spaces around Liverpool's three-man defence; this time their approach ended in a goal.

Liverpool lost Ian Rush at half-time with a groin injury. Steve McManaman replaced him and gave the attack fresh impetus, but there was no equaliser. Michael Thomas had an opportunity that Mark Crossley stopped with his legs. The Forest goalkeeper's intervention preserved the advantage James' earlier saves had kept within Liverpool's reach.

The match was the Premier League's first live Sky broadcast, placing Liverpool's introduction to the competition within the wider change in English football's presentation. On the pitch, the result was a narrow defeat in which their debutant goalkeeper had already been asked to do substantial work.

Liverpool would record their first Premier League victory three days later, beating Sheffield United 2–1 at Anfield. Their opening afternoon belonged to Forest and Sheringham, with James' performance offering more encouragement than the score.

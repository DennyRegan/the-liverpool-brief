Ian Rush reached 300 competitive goals for Liverpool as the club ended its first Premier League season with a 6–2 victory over Tottenham Hotspur. Rush and John Barnes each scored twice at Anfield on 8 May 1993, though Liverpool needed a late burst to turn a narrow lead into an emphatic score.

Rush opened the scoring after 21 minutes to reach his landmark. Barnes headed in Mark Walters' cross shortly before the interval, giving Liverpool a 2–0 lead. Ronnie Moran was directing the side from the dugout while Graeme Souness watched Coventry City's match against Leeds United on a scouting trip.

Tottenham reduced the deficit immediately after half-time through Teddy Sheringham. Liverpool quickly restored their two-goal advantage when a ball from Steve Harkness went in off Stuart Nethercott. Contemporary reports initially credited Harkness, but the goal was subsequently recorded as an own goal.

Steve Sedgley made it 3–2 with thirteen minutes remaining. Tottenham were again within one goal, and the eventual margin was still far from established. Liverpool's fourth came from the penalty spot after Nethercott fouled Rush. Walters converted, then Rush added his second from Jamie Redknapp's cross near the byline. Barnes completed Liverpool's scoring by meeting another Walters delivery.

There was further work for Bruce Grobbelaar even with Liverpool well ahead. After Rob Jones fouled Danny Hill, Grobbelaar saved Sheringham's penalty and kept out the follow-up. The match finished with eight goals, and Liverpool had supplied six of them.

The win briefly placed Liverpool fifth in the table. Once the remaining fixtures were completed, their final position was sixth with 59 points. That was a considerable recovery from their lower-half position during the season, although it did not bring a trophy. Rush's 300th goal and the late sequence against Tottenham gave the campaign a prolific ending, with Barnes, Walters and Rush central to the final afternoon.

Liverpool began the second leg against Spartak Moscow knowing that a 2–0 victory would overturn their first-leg defeat on away goals. Instead, the visitors won by that score at Anfield on 4 November 1992, completing a 6–2 aggregate success and ending Liverpool's European Cup Winners' Cup campaign in the second round.

The 4–2 defeat in Moscow had left Liverpool with both a difficult task and a changed goalkeeper. Bruce Grobbelaar was suspended following his dismissal in the first leg, so Mike Hooper started. Graeme Souness was also serving a touchline suspension. Liverpool needed to recover the tie without conceding the away goal that would make their position substantially worse.

They did not find the early breakthrough. Their attempts included hurried shots from distance, while Spartak retained the capacity to threaten when Liverpool committed men forward. The visitors had no need to chase the match, and the first half passed without Liverpool reducing the aggregate deficit.

After the hour, Spartak scored the goal that put the tie beyond Liverpool's original recovery target. Vladimir Beschastnykh supplied the cross and Dmitri Radchenko finished. Liverpool now needed three unanswered goals to advance on away goals, an imposing requirement against opponents who had already contained them for most of the evening.

Their difficulties increased when Mike Marsh was sent off late in the match for a foul on Radchenko. Andrei Piatnitski then added Spartak's second goal, completing a victory in both legs. Liverpool finished with ten men and without a goal at Anfield.

The contrast with the previous round was sharp. Liverpool had beaten Apollon Limassol 8–2 on aggregate, including Ian Rush's four-goal performance at home. Against Spartak they conceded six across the tie and could not score in the return. Their European season was over before the winter, leaving the domestic competitions as the remaining routes to a trophy.

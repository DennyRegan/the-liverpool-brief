Ronny Rosenthal supplied the goal that had eluded Liverpool for almost the entire Merseyside derby at Anfield on 20 March 1993. Introduced in place of Steve McManaman during the second half, he finished from Ian Rush's pass near the end to secure a 1–0 victory over Everton.

The clubs had begun level on 42 points, although Liverpool had played two fewer matches. Their shared total reflected the difficult position of both teams rather than a contest near the top of the table. Liverpool had recently beaten Queens Park Rangers and Middlesbrough; a third consecutive win would give their improvement greater substance.

An early injury to Mark Wright brought Jan Mølby into the match. The midfielder had not played since the October draw at Manchester United, and his return gave Liverpool an experienced passer in a game short of decisive finishing. McManaman had opportunities without converting them. Everton also carried a threat, particularly through substitute Stuart Barlow, whose chances on the break kept the result uncertain.

Graeme Souness turned to Rosenthal with the match still goalless. The winning move eventually passed through Mølby, John Barnes and Rush before reaching the substitute. Rosenthal finished, transforming an afternoon of missed opportunities into Liverpool's third successive league victory.

The goal separated teams that had arrived with identical points totals. Liverpool moved to 45 points from 33 games, thirteenth in the immediate table, while Everton remained on 42 from 35 and stood seventeenth. The result did not by itself settle Liverpool's season, but it extended a sequence of wins that was moving them away from their position earlier in March.

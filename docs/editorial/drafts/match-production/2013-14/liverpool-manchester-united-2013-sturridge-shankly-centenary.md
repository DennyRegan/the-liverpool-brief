Daniel Sturridge scored on his 24th birthday as Liverpool beat Manchester United 1–0 at Anfield on 1 September 2013. His early header supplied the only goal against the reigning Premier League champions and kept Liverpool’s opening league record perfect after three matches.

The occasion also carried a tribute to Bill Shankly. Before kick-off the Kop displayed a mosaic marking his approaching 100th birthday. Shankly had been born on 2 September 1913, so the match-day observance came on the eve of the centenary.

The winning move began with a Liverpool corner. Daniel Agger met the delivery and Sturridge applied the header that took the ball past David de Gea. The goal arrived within the opening minutes and gave Liverpool an advantage they would retain for the remainder of the afternoon.

United still had chances to alter the score. Simon Mignolet made saves from Ryan Giggs and Nani, while Robin van Persie sent an effort into the side-netting. Liverpool’s clean sheet survived those attempts, leaving the early goal as the dividing point between the sides.

Raheem Sterling also threatened after coming on, with De Gea saving his late effort. There was no second Liverpool goal, but none was needed: the match ended with Sturridge’s header and another clean sheet.

Three league games had now brought three wins, three goals and none conceded. Liverpool stood top with nine points, giving the birthday winner a wider significance than a single victory over United. The start had been built on three successive 1–0 results.

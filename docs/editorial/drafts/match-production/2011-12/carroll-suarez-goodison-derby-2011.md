Andy Carroll and Luis Suárez scored the second-half goals that gave Liverpool a 2–0 derby victory at Goodison Park on 1 October 2011. Everton had played most of the match with ten men after Jack Rodwell’s dismissal, a decision subsequently overturned on appeal.

The sending-off came in the first half after Rodwell challenged Suárez. It changed the game’s numerical balance, but it did not immediately produce a Liverpool lead. Tim Howard saved Dirk Kuyt’s penalty before half-time, and Charlie Adam struck the crossbar. At the interval, the score remained 0–0.

Liverpool eventually made the breakthrough after Craig Bellamy had come on. Bellamy linked with José Enrique down the left, and the full-back’s delivery found Carroll, who finished to put the visitors ahead. It was Carroll’s first league goal of the campaign.

Suárez added the second after a defensive mix-up in Everton’s penalty area. With two goals and a clean sheet, Liverpool had secured the away derby rather than leaving the outcome dependent on a single late chance. Howard’s penalty save had prolonged Everton’s resistance, but it could not preserve their unbeaten scoreline.

The red card remained an important part of the historical account. On 4 October, the Football Association upheld Everton’s claim of wrongful dismissal and withdrew Rodwell’s three-match suspension. The 2–0 result stood; the appeal outcome changed the disciplinary consequence and must be kept alongside the on-field decision.

For Liverpool, the win followed a home victory over Wolves and preceded the Anfield meeting with Manchester United. Carroll and Suárez had each scored in a meaningful league success, but the score and the later rescission tell different parts of the same derby.

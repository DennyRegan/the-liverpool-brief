Liverpool’s attempt to add the FA Cup to the League Cup ended in a 2–1 defeat by Chelsea at Wembley on 5 May 2012. Ramires and Didier Drogba built Chelsea’s lead before substitute Andy Carroll scored and then threatened an equaliser with a header that was not awarded as a goal.

Ramires opened the scoring after Juan Mata exploited a loss of possession by Jay Spearing and released him on the right. Chelsea held that advantage through the rest of the first half, leaving Liverpool to recover after the interval.

Drogba increased the lead early in the second half. Frank Lampard supplied the pass, and Drogba’s shot went through Martin Skrtel’s legs and into the net. Liverpool were two goals behind, with the final moving towards Chelsea before Carroll’s introduction changed the scoring pattern.

Carroll replaced Spearing and replied after Stewart Downing recovered possession. The striker turned John Terry and finished past Petr Čech, reducing the deficit to one goal. Liverpool had reduced the deficit and could seek the goal needed to bring the final level.

The closest attempt came from another Carroll header. Čech stopped it against the crossbar near the goal line, and Liverpool appealed that the ball had crossed it. No goal was given. The disputed moment left the score at 2–1.

Chelsea retained their lead to win the cup. Liverpool had reached both domestic finals and secured one trophy, but Carroll’s goal could not produce the second recovery needed at Wembley. Only two league fixtures remained after the final: Chelsea at Anfield and Swansea City away.

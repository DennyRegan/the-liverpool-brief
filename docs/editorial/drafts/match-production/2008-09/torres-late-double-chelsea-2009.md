Liverpool and Chelsea began 1 February 2009 level on points, with Chelsea ahead on goal difference. They were still level on the Anfield scoreboard as the match approached its final minute of normal time. Fernando Torres then scored twice, turning a prospective draw into a 2–0 Liverpool victory.

The result was especially valuable after Liverpool’s three January league fixtures had all ended in draws. Manchester United had moved ahead of them, and another shared point would have left a growing gap to recover.

Petr Čech was called into action by Xabi Alonso early in the first half and also stopped efforts from Javier Mascherano and Albert Riera. Liverpool could not convert that pressure before the interval, and Chelsea remained in the match after the restart.

The contest changed on the hour when referee Mike Riley sent off Frank Lampard following a challenge on Alonso. The dismissal was controversial and was subsequently overturned after Chelsea’s successful wrongful-dismissal appeal. On the day, however, Chelsea had to play the remaining half-hour with ten men.

Alonso came close to exploiting the advantage when his shot deflected off Alex and struck the crossbar. The breakthrough still would not come until Torres met Fábio Aurélio’s cross at the near post in the 89th minute, heading past Čech.

In added time Torres was there again. An Ashley Cole error left a loose ball for the striker to convert, securing the points rather than leaving Liverpool to defend a single-goal lead through the closing moments.

Liverpool had completed a league double over Chelsea, having won 1–0 at Stamford Bridge in October. They also moved back within two points of United, although the leaders retained a game in hand. Torres’s late intervention kept Liverpool close enough to continue applying pressure, while leaving Chelsea three points behind them.

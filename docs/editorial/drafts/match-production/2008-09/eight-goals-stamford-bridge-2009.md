Liverpool twice brought a Champions League quarter-final back within reach at Stamford Bridge on 14 April 2009. They led 2–0 before half-time, fell 3–2 behind and then scored twice more, only for Frank Lampard’s second goal to settle an extraordinary 4–4 draw. Chelsea progressed 7–5 on aggregate.

A 3–1 home defeat in the first leg had left Liverpool needing a substantial recovery. Steven Gerrard’s absence added to the task, but Fábio Aurélio supplied an early opening with a free-kick from the right. Rather than lifting the ball towards the players in the penalty area, he sent it directly inside Petr Čech’s near post.

Liverpool soon had a second. Branislav Ivanović was penalised for holding Xabi Alonso, who converted the penalty himself. The aggregate score was level at 3–3, although Chelsea still held the advantage on away goals. One more unanswered Liverpool goal would change that.

Instead, Chelsea scored twice early in the second half. Nicolas Anelka crossed low, Didier Drogba applied a touch and Pepe Reina could not prevent the ball crossing the line. Alex then drove a powerful free-kick into the net, making it 2–2 on the night and restoring Chelsea’s two-goal aggregate margin.

Lampard appeared to have removed the remaining uncertainty when he finished after Drogba’s run with 14 minutes left. Liverpool now needed three more goals to advance.

They found two in quick succession. Lucas Leiva’s shot took a deflection off Michael Essien and beat Čech, before Dirk Kuyt headed in Albert Riera’s cross. Liverpool led 4–3 on the night and trailed only 6–5 across the tie. A further goal would have put them through on away goals.

The final goal, however, went to Lampard. His second brought Chelsea level at 4–4 and closed the route Liverpool had briefly reopened. The visitors had scored four away from home after conceding three at Anfield, yet could not turn that attacking return into qualification.

Chelsea advanced to meet Barcelona.

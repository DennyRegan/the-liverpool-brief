Yossi Benayoun found the space that decided a tight Champions League first leg at the Santiago Bernabéu. With eight minutes remaining on 25 February 2009, he rose to meet Fábio Aurélio’s free-kick and headed Liverpool into a lead they carried back to Anfield.

The 1–0 victory gave Rafael Benítez’s side an away goal as well as the aggregate advantage in their round-of-16 tie against Real Madrid. Liverpool had needed to withstand the home side’s early possession before creating opportunities of their own.

Arjen Robben released Raúl for an early effort that Pepe Reina held. At the other end, Fernando Torres broke beyond Madrid’s defensive line and forced Iker Casillas to turn his shot away. The chance established that Liverpool could threaten even while spending spells without the ball.

Madrid had a Gonzalo Higuaín header ruled out for offside. Robben continued to seek openings, while Xabi Alonso almost produced a goal from extreme range before half-time, spotting Casillas away from his line and attempting a long lob that the goalkeeper tipped over.

For much of the second half the match remained goalless. Torres was eventually replaced by Ryan Babel after taking a knock, and Reina had to turn over another Robben effort. Liverpool remained level without having committed themselves to chasing the game.

The winner came from a set-piece rather than an extended spell of open-play pressure. Aurélio delivered the free-kick into the area and Benayoun, unmarked, directed his header beyond Casillas. Steven Gerrard, who had begun on the bench, appeared late on as Liverpool protected the lead.

The return leg was still to be played on 10 March. Liverpool had not secured a quarter-final place, but they had changed what Madrid needed from Anfield: the Spanish side would arrive behind, after Benayoun had converted the decisive chance of the first meeting.

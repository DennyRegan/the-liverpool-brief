Liverpool brought a one-goal advantage home from Madrid and then extended it with a 4–0 win at Anfield. On 10 March 2009, Real Madrid’s attempt to overturn Yossi Benayoun’s first-leg winner ended in a 5–0 aggregate defeat and Liverpool’s passage to the Champions League quarter-finals.

Steven Gerrard marked his 100th European appearance with two goals, but Liverpool’s threat was evident before the scoring began. Iker Casillas saved from Fernando Torres and turned Javier Mascherano’s shot onto the bar, keeping Madrid level through the opening exchanges.

Torres broke through in the 16th minute. A long ball put Madrid’s defence under pressure, Dirk Kuyt collected possession and squared for Torres to finish from close range. Madrid protested over Torres’s contact with Pepe in the build-up, but the goal stood.

There was further disagreement over Liverpool’s second. Referee Frank De Bleeckere awarded a penalty after judging that Gabriel Heinze had handled while challenging Álvaro Arbeloa. Gerrard converted, putting Liverpool three goals ahead across the two legs before half-time.

The captain scored again soon after the restart. Ryan Babel delivered from the left and Gerrard met the cross with a forceful finish beyond Casillas. The return leg alone now stood at 3–0, with Madrid still unable to score against Liverpool in either meeting.

Casillas continued to prevent a heavier defeat, but substitute Andrea Dossena added a fourth late on. Liverpool had followed the narrow, carefully defended victory in Spain with a match in which their attacks repeatedly created scoring opportunities.

The 5–0 aggregate result sent Liverpool into the last eight and ended Madrid’s European campaign at the first knockout stage. Gerrard’s milestone appearance had produced two of the goals; Torres, Kuyt and Babel had supplied decisive attacking contributions before Dossena completed the scoring.

# Andy Robertson

Andy Robertson’s pursuit of Manchester City’s defenders ended with a foul, rather than a goal or an assist. During Liverpool’s 4–3 victory in January 2018, he chased the ball across the visitors’ half until the referee penalised his challenge on Nicolas Otamendi. The Kop chanted his name. It was a conspicuous expression of the effort that helped make Liverpool’s new left-back a favourite, arriving after months in which he had struggled to secure a place.

The energy was valuable because Robertson learned how to apply it within Jürgen Klopp’s team. His defending improved, his forward runs became an important source of chances and his presence extended into the leadership of the dressing room. By the end of nine Liverpool seasons, he had made 378 competitive appearances and helped win eight major honours, including two league championships. A difficult beginning had become one of the club’s most successful recruitment stories.

## The long way to Anfield

Born in Glasgow on 11 March 1994, Robertson was released by Celtic as a teenager and continued his development with Queen’s Park. In 2012–13, he made 34 league appearances for the amateur club while also working at Hampden Park and in a supermarket. There was no uninterrupted passage from a leading Academy to top-flight football.

Dundee United signed him in June 2013. His single season there brought the PFA Scotland Young Player of the Year award and a move to Hull City in July 2014. Three eventful years in England included two Premier League relegations and promotion from the Championship. Liverpool signed him on 21 July 2017, when he was 23. He arrived with experience of setbacks and advancement, but still had to demonstrate that his game could meet the demands of a team seeking major trophies.

His competitive debut came on 19 August, in a 1–0 win over Crystal Palace at Anfield. It did not immediately establish him in the side. Alberto Moreno held the left-back position, and Robertson made only three appearances during the campaign’s first four months.

Klopp explained in January 2018 that the newcomer had needed to close gaps in his defending. Moreno’s strong form also mattered; Robertson’s wait was not simply a manager refusing to use a ready-made improvement. When Moreno was injured in December, Robertson took his opportunity. Klopp praised both his patience and the way he had used the time to adapt.

In a later account published in 2026, Klopp gave a more specific description of the learning involved: Robertson had needed to get close enough to opponents to complete his defensive challenges. The recollection reinforces the importance of technique and judgement alongside hard work. Running vigorously was part of his game already; becoming effective in Liverpool’s defensive system required more.

## Making the position his own

By February 2018, Robertson was playing in the Champions League. His first appearance in the competition came in the 5–0 away victory over Porto. Klopp praised the complete performance, recognising his contribution in attack and defence. The player who had been outside the side during the autumn was now helping Liverpool progress through European knockout football.

He scored his first Liverpool goal in the final league fixture of that season, completing a 4–0 victory over Brighton at Anfield. He then started the Champions League final against Real Madrid in Kyiv. Liverpool lost, but Robertson’s first season had carried him from a patient reserve role to the starting XI for a European final.

His influence grew further in 2018–19. He made 48 appearances as Liverpool reached another Champions League final and accumulated 97 league points. Alex McLeish appointed him Scotland captain on 3 September 2018, adding international leadership to a rapidly expanding club responsibility. Recognition also came through selection in the PFA Team of the Year in 2019 and again in 2020.

Robertson and Trent Alexander-Arnold gave Klopp’s team a distinctive attacking resource. Robertson supplied 11 Premier League assists in 2018–19, Alexander-Arnold 12; never before had two defenders in the same team both reached double figures. Their freedom depended on cover from midfield and the quality of the attackers’ movement, as Alexander-Arnold explained. Robertson’s delivery became a regular part of Liverpool’s route to goal rather than an occasional supplement.

There were interruptions even on the way to the biggest success. Injury forced Robertson off at half-time against Barcelona in the second leg of the 2019 semi-final. His replacement, Georginio Wijnaldum, scored twice in the 4–0 comeback. Robertson was back in the starting side for the final on 1 June, playing the full match as Liverpool beat Tottenham 2–0 in Madrid.

His character was part of the team’s life too. In later recollections published by the club, Alexander-Arnold described Robertson’s humour as easing the tension before that final. Jordan Henderson recalled being teased by his teammate about lifting the trophy. These were teammates’ memories of the atmosphere around a demanding occasion, illustrating a contribution that appearance and assist totals cannot measure.

## Delivering through winning seasons

Robertson’s forward running could provide a goal as well as a cross. At Aston Villa on 2 November 2019, Liverpool were still behind with three minutes of normal time remaining. He arrived at the back post to head in Sadio Mané’s delivery. Mané then supplied the stoppage-time winner in a 2–1 victory. Robertson’s willingness to enter the penalty area helped turn a threatened defeat into another step towards the title.

He played 36 of the 38 league games in 2019–20 as Liverpool ended their 30-year wait to be champions. UEFA Super Cup and Club World Cup victories in 2019 had already expanded the team’s honours. The championship confirmed how far the side, and its left-back, had progressed since his arrival.

The following season was more difficult for Liverpool, but Robertson’s availability remained substantial. He appeared in all 38 league matches and made 50 appearances across all competitions in 2020–21. The figures describe an enduring part of the side during a campaign that brought no addition to his major honours.

Liverpool returned to trophy contention in 2021–22, with Robertson making 47 appearances. His header from Mohamed Salah’s cross broke the deadlock against Everton on 24 April, setting up a 2–0 derby victory during the league run-in. The goal repeated the value of his movement to the far post, this time in a match where Liverpool had needed patience to find a way through.

They won the League Cup and FA Cup, while finishing runners-up in the league and Champions League. Robertson nearly settled the FA Cup final in normal time when he met James Milner’s cross and struck the post. He was replaced by Kostas Tsimikas during extra time; Tsimikas later scored the winning penalty. Robertson’s part in the success included a near miss as well as a medal, one of the small margins in a season of four trophy pursuits.

## Adapting, recovering and leading

On Boxing Day 2022, Robertson set up Salah’s opener at Aston Villa and became the Premier League’s highest-assisting defender. His total of 54 moved him above Leighton Baines’s 53. Four had come at Hull, with 50 for Liverpool at that point. Alexander-Arnold subsequently overtook him; Robertson’s record was an achievement at a particular stage of his career, rather than a permanent ranking.

Klopp’s tactical changes in spring 2023 required another adjustment. When Alexander-Arnold moved into midfield during possession, Robertson could no longer remain high on the opposite side throughout the build-up. Klopp described the need for him to judge when to advance and when to hold his position. The runs and deliveries remained available, but their timing had to fit the altered balance.

An injury sustained while playing for Scotland in October 2023 required shoulder surgery. Robertson nevertheless returned to start the 2024 League Cup final against Chelsea. His cross produced a Cody Gakpo header against the post, and he was later replaced by Tsimikas. Liverpool won through Van Dijk’s extra-time header, adding a second League Cup to Robertson’s honours.

Arne Slot’s arrival brought a new manager and another league title. Robertson appeared in 33 league matches in 2024–25. This time the celebrations included supporters inside Anfield, a different experience from the championship won and lifted during the pandemic.

The campaign also contained costly mistakes. Against Fulham on 14 December 2024, Harry Wilson seized on Robertson’s attempt to control a ball in behind. The left-back fouled him while trying to recover and was sent off for denying a goalscoring opportunity in the 17th minute. Liverpool fought back to draw 2–2. His status and experience did not make every performance secure.

On 29 August 2025, Slot confirmed Robertson as Liverpool’s vice-captain, succeeding Alexander-Arnold as deputy to Van Dijk. The head coach highlighted his knowledge of the club and influence on its culture. Yet Milos Kerkez’s arrival had changed competition at left-back: Robertson began the season on the bench. He acknowledged the difficulty of adjusting after years as a regular starter, while explaining that he and his family had chosen to remain at Liverpool that summer.

## A completed chapter

Robertson made 36 appearances and scored three goals in his final season. On 9 April 2026, Liverpool announced that he would leave when his contract expired. His last appearance came in the 1–1 draw with Brentford at Anfield on 24 May. Afterwards, he and Mohamed Salah received guards of honour from teammates and staff before the crowd’s farewell.

A move to Tottenham was announced on 5 June. Robertson returned to Anfield with his new club for a League Cup tie in September and received a warm reception when he appeared during the second half. The relationship formed over nine seasons remained visible after his Liverpool playing career had ended.

His final Liverpool totals were 378 appearances, 14 goals and 69 assists in all competitions, including 275 league appearances and 11 league goals. His 60 Premier League assists across Hull and Liverpool comprised 56 for the Reds. The eight major honours were two Premier League titles, the Champions League, UEFA Super Cup, Club World Cup, FA Cup and two League Cups.

The chase against City remains an apt early image, but his career explains why it lasted in the memory. Robertson combined that willingness to work with defensive learning, productive delivery and the ability to adjust to changing teammates, tactics and responsibilities. Liverpool gained a left-back whose importance grew with the team, and whose leadership continued even when his place became less certain.

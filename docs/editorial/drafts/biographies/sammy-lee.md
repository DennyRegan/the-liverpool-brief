# Sammy Lee: Liverpool’s ever-present

Sammy Lee started every match of Liverpool’s 1983–84 season. There were 67 of them, from the Charity Shield to the European Cup final, and Joe Fagan selected him for all 67. In a team that won the league championship, League Cup and European Cup, his place was among the few things that never changed.

That season provides the clearest measure of Lee’s importance to Liverpool. His first-team career at Anfield produced 295 competitive appearances and 19 goals. Its strongest years were concentrated in the early 1980s, when he became a regular under Bob Paisley and remained one under Fagan. Later, he returned to help prepare other Liverpool teams as a coach.

Born in Liverpool on 7 February 1959, Lee signed professional terms in April 1976. His senior debut came against Leicester City at Anfield on 8 April 1978, when he replaced the injured David Johnson after six minutes. Liverpool twice fell behind, but Lee scored the second equaliser before Tommy Smith secured a 3–2 victory.

Scoring on his debut did not bring an immediate place in the side. Lee made only two first-team appearances that season and two more in 1978–79. His opportunities increased towards the end of the following campaign, when an injury to Terry McDermott opened a place. In 1980–81 he finally established himself, taking over from Jimmy Case on the right of midfield.

Lee’s energy was valuable because it came with the discipline to carry out a particular job. Phil Neal, who played behind him, later recalled their effective partnership and singled out Lee’s marking of Paul Breitner in Munich. Paisley had entrusted him with following the Bayern midfielder in the second leg of the 1981 European Cup semi-final. Liverpool drew 1–1 and advanced on away goals. Lee’s contribution lay in restricting an opponent rather than producing the decisive attacking moment.

He started the final against Real Madrid in Paris, where Liverpool won 1–0. Earlier that spring he had also helped the club win its first League Cup, beating West Ham United after a replay. His first full season in the side ended with two major trophies. Three consecutive league championships followed, along with three more League Cups.

International recognition came during that run. On 17 November 1982, Bobby Robson gave Lee his England debut in a European Championship qualifier away to Greece. As he had for Liverpool, Lee scored on his first appearance, completing a 3–0 win. He earned 14 senior caps and scored twice, with every appearance coming while he was a Liverpool player.

Fagan’s first season as manager, 1983–84, was Lee’s most demanding. He started all 42 league fixtures and every cup tie, as well as the Charity Shield. His selection throughout such a long campaign is evidence of how much Fagan relied on him: Liverpool pursued three trophies without resting him for a single match.

There was also a decisive goal. On 11 April 1984, Lee headed in Alan Kennedy’s cross to give Liverpool a 1–0 victory over Dinamo Bucharest in the first leg of their European Cup semi-final. Lee, who stood around 5ft 4in, was an unlikely scorer with his head. Remembering it for Liverpool’s website in 2014, he spoke of how rarely he had scored that way and how much the goal at the Kop end meant to him.

Liverpool won the return leg 2–1. In the final against Roma, Lee played the full 120 minutes as the match finished 1–1, before Liverpool won the penalty shoot-out 4–2. He had now started two victorious European Cup finals, three years apart.

His position became less secure after that peak. He made 25 appearances in 1984–85 and 26 the following season, contributing to the league and FA Cup double without the constant involvement of earlier years. In August 1986 he left for Queens Park Rangers. His Liverpool league record stood at 197 appearances and 13 goals.

After a season at QPR, Lee joined Osasuna in Spain. A loan spell at Southampton and a move to Bolton Wanderers followed before his playing career ended.

His next Liverpool career began on the coaching staff under Graeme Souness. Initially working with the reserves, he remained through Roy Evans’s tenure and became a first-team coach under Gérard Houllier. He was part of the staff when Liverpool won the League Cup, FA Cup and UEFA Cup in 2001: another treble, this time with Lee helping prepare the players.

He combined Liverpool duties with a part-time England coaching role before joining the national set-up full-time in 2004. At Bolton he subsequently assisted Sam Allardyce, then succeeded him as manager in April 2007. The appointment lasted only until October, a brief and unsuccessful spell in charge.

Rafael Benítez brought him back to Liverpool as assistant manager in May 2008. Explaining the appointment, Benítez emphasised Lee’s experience and standing as a coach alongside his knowledge of the club. Lee remained through the managerial changes to Roy Hodgson and Kenny Dalglish, leaving by mutual consent in June 2011.

Across those different jobs, Lee’s Liverpool record rests on substantial work: a regular midfield place in three successive championship teams, starts in two European Cup final victories, and coaching responsibilities under several managers. The 67 starts of 1983–84 remain its outstanding detail. Through an entire season that ended with three trophies, Fagan never selected a starting eleven without him.

# Steve McMahon

Steve McMahon gave Kenny Dalglish’s Liverpool a midfielder who could win possession, use it constructively and score. His tackling was forceful and sometimes reckless, but his contribution extended well beyond the physical contest. Across six years at Anfield he scored 50 goals in 277 competitive appearances and helped Liverpool win three league championships. The passing and finishing mattered as much as the challenges.

Born in Liverpool on 20 August 1961, McMahon grew up supporting Everton. He served as a ballboy at Goodison Park before progressing through the club’s youth system and making his first-team debut in 1980. Over three seasons he made 100 league appearances, scored 11 league goals and captained the side. Liverpool were interested when he left in 1983, but he joined Aston Villa instead. Two years later, Dalglish brought him back to Merseyside.

The £350,000 transfer was completed on 6 September 1985. McMahon became Dalglish’s first signing as Liverpool player-manager, arriving a year after Graeme Souness’s departure. He offered some of the qualities Liverpool had lost: authority in midfield, aggressive tackling and the ability to move the attack forward.

His debut came in a 2–2 draw at Oxford United on 14 September. A week later he scored his first Liverpool goal at Goodison, putting the visitors 3–0 ahead shortly before half-time. Everton recovered to 3–2, making McMahon’s goal the decisive one. Within a fortnight of signing, he had helped beat the club where his career began.

His place was not immediately secure. Kevin MacDonald provided competition in midfield, and an injury interrupted McMahon’s progress. He finished 1985–86 with 36 competitive appearances and ten goals, including 23 appearances and six goals in the league. Liverpool won the championship and FA Cup, but McMahon remained an unused substitute in the 3–1 final victory over Everton. He had contributed to the Double without taking part in its concluding match.

The following season brought a larger role and more goals. McMahon played 50 times and scored 14, his highest total in a Liverpool campaign. Four came against Fulham in a 10–0 League Cup victory at Anfield on 23 September 1986. He also missed a penalty that evening. The four goals were accompanied by assists for Ian Rush and Ronnie Whelan, a particularly clear example of the attacking work he could produce from midfield.

In 1987–88, with John Barnes, Peter Beardsley and John Aldridge in the side, McMahon helped Liverpool turn possession into sustained pressure. He could supply those forwards as well as recover the ball for them. His own nine league goals, across 40 appearances, added another source of scoring to a championship-winning team.

The home match against Arsenal on 16 January 1988 showed his value in a single passage of play. With the ball running towards the touchline, McMahon chased it down and kept it in, his momentum carrying him towards the advertising boards. He recovered and continued the attack. Beardsley’s eventual shot was saved by John Lukic, and Aldridge scored from the rebound. Liverpool won 2–0. McMahon’s persistence had preserved the opportunity; his control and use of the ball helped turn it into a goal.

There were disappointments alongside the championships. McMahon played in the 1988 FA Cup final, when Wimbledon beat Liverpool 1–0 and prevented another Double. A year later he made a direct contribution to winning the trophy. In the final against Everton, played in the aftermath of the Hillsborough disaster, he supplied Aldridge’s opening goal after four minutes. Liverpool won 3–2 after extra time, with Rush scoring twice.

A third league title followed in 1989–90. McMahon made 38 league appearances and scored five times, remaining a regular in Dalglish’s midfield. He also went to the 1990 World Cup with England. His senior international career brought 17 caps, all earned while he was a Liverpool player.

The next season exposed the cost of his more reckless challenges. In January 1991 he was dismissed against Blackburn Rovers after a second booking, following a late tackle on Howard Gayle. In February he was carried off after challenging Everton’s John Ebbrell in an FA Cup tie. The knee injury ended his season. These incidents belong alongside the accounts of his competitive strength: aggression could also leave Liverpool without him.

Dalglish resigned that February, and McMahon’s Liverpool career ended under Souness. He joined Manchester City on 24 December 1991. His final record included 204 league appearances and 29 league goals; the larger totals of 277 and 50 cover competitive matches in all competitions.

After City, he became Swindon Town’s player-manager in 1994. Relegation in his first season was followed by the Division Two title in 1996. He later managed Blackpool and Perth Glory and worked in television.

At Liverpool, McMahon’s strongest claim to distinction is the range of his contribution. He could compete for possession, help build an attack and finish it himself. Dalglish’s first signing became a regular in three championship-winning sides, bringing substantial footballing ability to a role too easily reduced to toughness.

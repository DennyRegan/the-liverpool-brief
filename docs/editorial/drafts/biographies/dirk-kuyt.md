# Dirk Kuyt: the forward Liverpool could count on

Dirk Kuyt scored three times against Manchester United at Anfield on 6 March 2011 without needing a spectacular finish. He followed Luis Suárez’s extraordinary run to put Liverpool ahead, headed in Nani’s misdirected clearance and reached the rebound when Edwin van der Sar failed to hold a free-kick. Liverpool won 3–1. The hat-trick rewarded the instinct that ran through Kuyt’s six seasons on Merseyside: keep moving, anticipate the next opportunity and make sure somebody else’s good work has a finish.

By then he had become much more than the Dutch goalscorer Liverpool had bought in 2006. A striker had become a right-sided attacker, a diligent partner for more flamboyant teammates and a dependable presence in European ties. He would leave with 285 competitive appearances, 71 goals and one major Liverpool honour. The modest trophy return cannot express the range of his contribution, but neither should the affection he earned disguise the disappointments. His career belonged to a team that repeatedly approached major success before finally winning the League Cup in his last season.

Kuyt came to Anfield with a substantial reputation established in the Netherlands. His route had begun at Quick Boys in Katwijk, before senior football with Utrecht and a move to Feyenoord in 2003. At Utrecht he helped win the Dutch Cup, scoring against Feyenoord in the 2003 final. In Rotterdam he became a prolific forward and captain. This was a player accustomed to responsibility, with the stamina to sustain long runs of appearances and the scoring record to make a move abroad plausible.

Liverpool signed him from Feyenoord on 18 August 2006. Rafael Benítez was adding to an attack that also contained Peter Crouch, Craig Bellamy and Robbie Fowler. Kuyt’s arrival therefore involved competition and choices about partnerships, rather than an uncontested place as the sole centre-forward. His record in Dutch football created expectations of goals. The qualities that would make him particularly useful to Benítez included the willingness to pursue a defender, keep an attack alive and continue working after the initial chance had disappeared.

He made his debut eight days later, replacing Crouch in a 2–1 home victory over West Ham United. Years afterwards, Kuyt recalled the applause that greeted his first minutes and the confidence it gave him. The early connection mattered: supporters could recognise his effort before they had seen a large body of Liverpool goals. His first came on 20 September against Newcastle United, when he finished Steve Finnan’s delivery in a 2–0 victory. The opening weeks began to translate a Dutch reputation into something Anfield could judge directly.

The first season produced 14 goals in all competitions. Kuyt was establishing himself as a serious contributor, but the European campaign also showed how a forward could affect results without accumulating goals. Liverpool advanced towards another meeting with Chelsea in the Champions League semi-finals. At Anfield on 1 May 2007, Daniel Agger cancelled the first-leg deficit and the tie stretched into extra time. Kuyt headed against the crossbar and had a follow-up finish disallowed for offside. There was still a decisive task left for him.

Pepe Reina saved two Chelsea penalties in the shootout. Kuyt took Liverpool’s fourth, with the opportunity to settle the contest, and beat Petr Čech. Liverpool were through to Athens. Afterwards he told UEFA that he had felt calm because he knew he needed to assume responsibility and choose his corner. He also shared credit with Reina, the crowd and Benítez’s tactical preparation. His account combined personal composure with an awareness that his final kick had completed work done by the whole team.

The final against AC Milan on 23 May was a different kind of examination. Benítez selected Kuyt as the lone forward, with Steven Gerrard supporting him and Jermaine Pennant and Boudewijn Zenden providing width. Liverpool made a controlled start and found promising moments, yet Milan took the lead when Andrea Pirlo’s free-kick struck Filippo Inzaghi. Inzaghi scored again late in the second half. Kuyt headed in Agger’s flick-on from a corner near the end, but Liverpool lost 2–1. His first season closed with a goal on the greatest club stage and the frustration of a runners-up medal.

Fernando Torres’s arrival in 2007 changed Liverpool’s attacking possibilities. Gerrard increasingly played behind the new striker, and Kuyt began moving towards the right. Looking back in his own club column, he explained that he had always preferred playing centrally but regarded making the team function as a source of satisfaction. He had already experienced a wide role at Utrecht. The Liverpool adjustment demanded different positioning and defensive work, while preserving the forward’s habit of arriving where a chance might break.

It was also a difficult period personally. Kuyt’s father died in 2007, and he later linked the following months with a decline in his form. The career should allow room for that difficulty without reducing every performance to an explanation supplied from outside. In November 2021 he described the October 2007 Merseyside derby as a particularly important memory from that period. He had been struggling, yet Benítez selected him for a game in which Liverpool would need his nerve.

At Goodison Park, Sami Hyypiä’s own goal left Liverpool behind. Kuyt converted the equalising penalty and then another in stoppage time after Phil Neville handled on the line. Liverpool won 2–1. In his later account, Kuyt recalled that Andriy Voronin had been nominated as the first penalty taker but asked him to take the opening kick. He also acknowledged his good fortune in remaining on the pitch after a dangerous lunge at Neville. The two conversions became an enduring derby memory; his own recollection retained the untidiness and risk around them.

Domestic scoring remained uneven in 2007–08, when Kuyt managed three league goals. In Europe he repeatedly provided a breakthrough. Against Internazionale at Anfield, his late, deflected shot opened a last-16 tie that Gerrard’s second goal and Torres’s finish in Milan helped secure. The following round brought Arsenal. Liverpool needed the qualities of an attacker who could support the central pair and still reach the goalmouth himself. Kuyt’s developing role gave Benítez both kinds of service.

At Arsenal on 2 April, Emmanuel Adebayor headed the home side in front. Three minutes later Gerrard drove past opponents down the left and crossed low for Kuyt to equalise. The 1–1 draw gave Liverpool an away goal to take into the second leg. It was a characteristic partnership: Gerrard’s acceleration transformed the attack, and Kuyt supplied the final intervention. His goal helped put Liverpool in position to progress, though the eventual home victory required substantial contributions from others as the tie changed direction again.

Kuyt scored once more in the semi-final’s first leg against Chelsea, reacting to defensive confusion to beat Čech at the Kop end on 22 April. For much of the remaining match his goal appeared to offer Liverpool a narrow advantage. John Arne Riise’s late own goal changed that prospect into a 1–1 draw. Chelsea won the return and eliminated Liverpool. Kuyt had scored in successive knockout rounds, yet those contributions could not reproduce the previous season’s route to the final. A growing reputation for important goals was accompanied by another missed opportunity.

The next European campaign almost ended before the group stage. Standard Liège had held Liverpool in Belgium and were still level late in extra time at Anfield on 27 August 2008. Kuyt reached Ryan Babel’s cross and scored in the 118th minute, on his 100th Liverpool appearance. Benítez afterwards praised his effort and acknowledged Reina’s importance across the tie. The combination was familiar: the goalkeeper helped keep Liverpool alive and the forward continued searching for the moment that would decide the contest.

Liverpool’s 2008–09 league challenge gave Kuyt’s right-sided role its fullest setting. Torres and Gerrard supplied the outstanding central threat, with Kuyt bringing repeated movement, support and goals from another direction. He appeared in all 38 league matches and scored 12 times in the competition, part of a 15-goal total across the season. His availability allowed the manager to rely on the same kind of contribution through changing fixtures. He could help build an attack and still be present when it reached the penalty area.

One of the clearest examples came at Manchester City in October. Liverpool were two goals behind at half-time before Torres scored twice. Kuyt then struck the winner in stoppage time, completing a 3–2 recovery. His job was not exhausted by supplying the more celebrated scorer or covering ground on the flank. When the game offered another opportunity, he was there to finish it. Liverpool’s challenge gained substance from such recoveries, in which persistence turned threatened defeats into points.

The season ended with Liverpool second on 86 points, having scored a division-leading 77 goals. It was the closest Kuyt came to a league championship in England. The strength of the attack and his own contribution deserve recognition without converting a persuasive challenge into a title. The team had come close enough for its missed opportunities to remain painful. For Kuyt, the move wide had helped produce some of Liverpool’s best football during his stay, while the major honour he had sought remained out of reach.

The following campaign brought a marked collective decline. Kuyt remained heavily involved, making 53 appearances in all competitions, but Liverpool could not sustain the previous season’s league challenge. His derby goals offered moments of relief. At Goodison in November 2009 he followed a save by Tim Howard to confirm a 2–0 win. At Anfield in February 2010 he headed in Gerrard’s corner for the only goal after Liverpool had been reduced to ten men. The scorer’s consistency in these fixtures outlasted the team’s best form.

Benítez left in 2010 and Roy Hodgson’s brief spell gave way to Kenny Dalglish’s return in January 2011. Kuyt’s place in Liverpool’s story continued across those changes, though the identity of the attack changed again with Torres leaving and Suárez and Andy Carroll arriving. The Manchester United hat-trick in March showed how quickly Kuyt could establish a useful understanding with a new colleague. Suárez brought the exceptional dribbling and invention; Kuyt repeatedly positioned himself to give that invention an outcome.

His 13 league goals in 2010–11 were his best return in the competition for Liverpool. The hat-trick was its most memorable afternoon, but the season’s contribution extended beyond it. With the club emerging from managerial upheaval, he still supplied a recognisable combination of work and scoring. Dalglish could use the experienced forward alongside newer attacking players. The years of tactical adjustment had left Kuyt able to contribute from more than one position, rather than dependent upon the particular arrangement that first brought him to Anfield.

That flexibility remained valuable when his starting place became less secure in 2011–12. On 28 January 2012 he came off the bench against Manchester United in the FA Cup and scored the 88th-minute winner. Reina’s long kick, Carroll’s flick and Kuyt’s run created a direct route to goal; he finished low past David de Gea to make it 2–1. It was another important intervention during a campaign in which being trusted with a place in the squad no longer guaranteed a place in the opening eleven.

The League Cup final against Cardiff City on 26 February brought the reward he had waited for. Liverpool trailed to Joe Mason before Martin Škrtel equalised, and the match went to extra time. Kuyt replaced Carroll in the 103rd minute. Five minutes later, his first attempt was blocked and he struck the returning ball into the bottom corner. Once more the forward had continued the action after its first apparent conclusion. Liverpool led 2–1 and seemed close to ending their trophy drought.

Cardiff refused to let the afternoon end there. Ben Turner equalised near the end of extra time, forcing a shootout. Liverpool missed their first two penalties before Kuyt converted their third, putting a score on the board when another failure would have increased the pressure sharply. Stewart Downing and Glen Johnson also scored; Anthony Gerrard’s miss left Liverpool winners by 3–2 on penalties. Kuyt’s extra-time goal had not won the match by itself, but his contributions in both phases helped secure the cup.

His final Liverpool appearance came as a substitute at Swansea City on 13 May, a 1–0 defeat that closed the league campaign. On 3 June the club announced that he had agreed a move to Fenerbahçe, with completion expected in the following days. Kuyt explained that, after six years, he wanted a new country and experience. He also described how moved he had been by supporters’ messages. Departure and affection coexisted: the wish to continue his career elsewhere did not erase the attachment built at Anfield.

He subsequently returned to Feyenoord and finished his professional playing career in 2017 with a hat-trick that secured their first league championship since 1999. Liverpool remained part of his football life, through visits, interviews and his continuing interest in the team. His later reflections on playing wide were especially revealing. He understood that his value had involved helping a side take shape around Gerrard and Torres, as well as scoring the goals for which a forward was most easily remembered.

Kuyt’s Liverpool legacy rests on the relationship between those duties. The running mattered because it served a tactical purpose; the persistence mattered because it so often ended in a useful touch. Athens, the derby penalties, the United hat-trick and Wembley made that value visible. Across six seasons, Liverpool had a forward willing to change his role and still assume responsibility when a game demanded a finish. The single trophy arrived late, but the contribution that earned his place in the club’s history had been accumulating throughout.

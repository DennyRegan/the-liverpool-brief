# Ian Rush: Liverpool’s record goalscorer

Ian Rush scored 346 goals in 660 competitive appearances for Liverpool. His record includes four at Goodison Park in one afternoon, two in each of the FA Cup finals against Everton, and 47 during the season Liverpool won the league, League Cup and European Cup. Yet the goals alone leave part of his contribution unexplained. Rush also helped Liverpool win the ball back, applying pressure to defenders before they could build an attack.

His partnership with Kenny Dalglish gave Liverpool a combination of passing, movement and finishing that shaped their attack through much of the 1980s. Later, as captain and strike partner to Robbie Fowler, Rush provided continuity in a team whose dominance had passed.

Born in St Asaph on 20 October 1961, Rush began his senior career with Chester. Bob Paisley brought him to Liverpool in April 1980 for £300,000, when he was 18. His first appearance came at Ipswich that December, but his opening season produced nine games without a goal. The player who would become Liverpool’s leading scorer had to wait for both regular football and his first breakthrough.

That goal arrived against Oulu Palloseura at Anfield on 30 September 1981. Rush came off the bench during a 7–0 European Cup victory and scored within three minutes. By the end of 1981–82, he had 30 goals in 49 appearances, a league championship and a League Cup. His place in Liverpool’s attack was established.

Dalglish’s ability to find him was central to that development. Rush offered runs beyond defenders and the speed to reach passes played into space. In a later recollection published by Liverpool, Sammy Lee described how a midfielder could release him simply by putting the ball beyond the opposition’s back line. That movement gave the passer an option before the chance itself existed.

The work continued when possession changed. Rush closed defenders down and chased opportunities to recover the ball. Liverpool’s account of his playing style identifies this as an essential part of his game. A forward capable of scoring so frequently was also willing to do work that might create a chance for somebody else.

On 6 November 1982, he scored four in Liverpool’s 5–0 win at Everton. It was the outstanding individual performance of his derby career, though hardly an isolated one: he scored 25 times against Everton for Liverpool. The four at Goodison became part of the words to *Poor Scouser Tommy*, preserving the afternoon in the club’s matchday repertoire.

His most productive season followed in 1983–84. Under Joe Fagan, Liverpool won three major trophies and Rush scored 47 goals in all competitions. His 32 league goals earned the European Golden Boot; he also won the PFA and Football Writers’ Association player awards. In Rome, he converted his penalty in the shoot-out victory over Roma that secured the European Cup. His contribution extended from the league campaign to the final act of Liverpool’s season.

Rush played in the following year’s European Cup final at Heysel, where the stadium disaster overshadowed the match. Dalglish subsequently became player-manager, and in 1985–86 Liverpool won their first league and FA Cup double.

Rush scored twice in the Wembley final against Everton. Liverpool trailed to Gary Lineker’s goal before Rush equalised from Jan Mølby’s pass. Craig Johnston put them ahead, then Rush finished Ronnie Whelan’s delivery to complete a 3–1 victory. Both the recovery and the decisive third goal depended on his finishing.

A move to Juventus was agreed in 1986, with Rush remaining at Liverpool on loan for another season. He scored 40 goals in 1986–87 before spending the next campaign in Italy. He struggled to settle and returned to Liverpool in August 1988. Recalling the experience later, Rush argued that Juventus had improved him as a player, despite his difficulties adapting to a changing team.

His return was uneven. John Aldridge had established himself in Liverpool’s attack, while Rush struggled for form and underwent a knee operation. The 1989 FA Cup final nevertheless gave him another decisive role. Played in the aftermath of Hillsborough, it again brought Liverpool and Everton together at Wembley.

Rush replaced Aldridge and scored twice in extra time. After Stuart McCall cancelled out his first, he headed John Barnes’s cross past Neville Southall to win the match 3–2. Three years after the double, Liverpool had again won an FA Cup final through two Rush goals.

He finished 1989–90 with 26 goals in all competitions as Liverpool won his fifth league championship. With Aldridge gone, Rush again had the regular centre-forward's place, and he repeated that 26-goal total in 1990–91. The transition to Souness's Liverpool was less straightforward. Injuries restricted him to 18 league appearances in 1991–92, although he recovered in time to score the second goal in the 2–0 FA Cup final victory over Sunderland. Across Liverpool’s three successful finals of 1986, 1989 and 1992, Rush had contributed five goals.

That October, he overtook Roger Hunt as Liverpool’s record goalscorer. His standing did not guarantee uninterrupted selection: recalled in March 1993, he scored the winner against Queens Park Rangers. Souness was still searching for an effective combination around him, recruiting Nigel Clough that summer as a prospective partner. Fowler's emergence supplied a different answer. Souness made Rush captain in 1993, giving an experienced goalscorer a leading place in a changing side.

Fowler later explained what he learned from training alongside Rush: the timing of runs and how each striker could create space for the other. He remembered a senior player willing to help him, not merely protect his own position. This was continuity expressed through practical attacking work. Rush's value to the young forward came from showing him how a partnership operated, as well as from the example of his scoring record.

Evans continued to use Rush as a regular striker, not just a captain lending his name to a younger team. He played 50 competitive matches and scored 19 goals in 1994–95. At Blackburn in November, his hat-trick took Liverpool into the League Cup quarter-finals on his 600th appearance; Fowler supplied the pass for the opening goal. Rush then lifted the trophy at Wembley in April 1995. His partnership with Fowler was helping Liverpool win again even as the younger man's scoring grew more prominent.

Stan Collymore's arrival changed the balance the following season. Rush initially remained in Evans's attack, but after an operation in November he could not displace Fowler or Collymore. By February 1996 he had announced that he would leave on a free transfer. He wanted to move before the transfer deadline but agreed to remain until the season's end, available if Liverpool needed him. The reduced role was therefore not simply a manager's verdict on a goalscorer: Rush also had to decide how long he could accept waiting behind an established partnership.

His last Liverpool goal came at Manchester City in May 1996. After leaving for Leeds, he also played for Newcastle, Sheffield United on loan, Wrexham and Sydney Olympic. For Wales, he scored 28 goals in 73 appearances; Gareth Bale eventually passed that national record in 2018.

Liverpool received two substantial careers from Rush: 207 goals before his season in Italy and another 139 after it. The first helped sustain a championship-winning side. The second brought further trophies, the captaincy and support for a younger generation. Together, they made him the scorer against whom every Liverpool forward would be measured.

# Peter Beardsley: four seasons at the heart of Liverpool’s attack

Peter Beardsley joined Liverpool for a British record fee in 1987 and left four years later with two league titles, an FA Cup and 59 goals. He helped Kenny Dalglish rebuild the attack after Ian Rush’s departure, playing alongside John Aldridge and John Barnes in a team that won the championship in his first season. His contribution combined invention with a substantial share of the scoring.

Beardsley worked behind the main striker, collecting the ball in deeper positions and using close control, changes of direction and short passes to open a route towards goal. Barnes could attack from the left and Aldridge finish moves in the penalty area; Beardsley gave them another way of reaching one another. He was also capable of carrying an attack himself, as his goals against Watford, Manchester United and Everton would demonstrate.

Born on 18 January 1961, Beardsley reached Liverpool by an indirect route. He played for Carlisle United and Vancouver Whitecaps, and made only one first-team appearance during a brief spell at Manchester United. Newcastle United signed him in 1983. There he played alongside Kevin Keegan and Chris Waddle in the side promoted to the First Division in 1984. By the time Liverpool came for him, he was an established England international.

Dalglish paid Newcastle £1.9 million in July 1987. Barnes arrived that summer too, joining an attack that already contained Aldridge. With Rush moving to Juventus and Dalglish playing less frequently, the newcomers had significant responsibilities immediately. The three forwards combined for Aldridge’s opening goal against Arsenal in the first league match of the season.

Beardsley nevertheless took time to settle. Liverpool’s later accounts acknowledge that his strongest form came during the second half of the campaign. The record fee had bought a player whose value became clearer as his understanding with those around him developed.

At Watford on 13 February 1988, he scored twice in a 4–1 victory. Stuart Jones’s contemporary report in The Times singled out his ability to produce two individual goals on a pitch made difficult by mud, sand and rain. It is useful evidence of the range of his game: the close control remained effective when conditions made accurate football difficult.

Two months later came the 5–0 victory over Nottingham Forest at Anfield. Beardsley scored once and created two goals. In a performance remembered for the fluency of Liverpool’s attack, his contribution was measurable as well as pleasing to watch.

On 23 April, he scored the only goal against Tottenham Hotspur to secure the championship. Liverpool had begun the league season with 29 unbeaten matches; Beardsley finished it with 15 league goals and 18 in all competitions. The FA Cup final defeat by Wimbledon prevented a double, but his first season had established his place in Dalglish’s rebuilt side.

The following campaign brought the FA Cup in the aftermath of Hillsborough. Beardsley started the final against Everton on 20 May 1989 and played throughout the 3–2 victory after extra time. Aldridge scored first, then Rush came off the bench to score twice. Beardsley’s presence across the full match also shows how the attack could accommodate both the original partnership and Rush’s return.

Liverpool lost the league title to Arsenal in the final match, then regained it in 1989–90. Beardsley contributed ten league goals and 16 overall to that second championship season. His own campaign ended early: a knee injury kept him out of the remaining seven matches after the FA Cup semi-final defeat by Crystal Palace.

His fourth season contained some of his most conspicuous performances. In September 1990 he scored a hat-trick in Liverpool’s 4–0 win over Manchester United at Anfield, then twice at Goodison Park six days later. Yet his position became less secure, and by the winter he was experiencing spells outside the starting side.

Recalled for the FA Cup replay at Everton on 20 February 1991, Beardsley scored twice in a 4–4 draw. It proved to be Dalglish’s final match of his first spell as manager. Beardsley later rejected suggestions that he and Dalglish had fallen out, distinguishing his disappointment at being omitted from any personal breach. That recollection matters: selection difficulties are documented, but they do not establish a feud.

Under Graeme Souness, Liverpool signed Dean Saunders and sold Beardsley to Everton for £1 million in the summer of 1991. He was 30. His Liverpool record stood at 175 competitive appearances and 59 goals, including 46 in 131 league matches.

He remained productive after leaving. Two seasons at Everton brought 32 goals in 95 appearances, before he returned to Newcastle in 1993. He later worked in Newcastle’s academy. In September 2019, an independent FA regulatory commission suspended him from football and football-related activity for 32 weeks after three charges concerning racially abusive language towards under-23 players were proved. Beardsley denied the allegations.

At Liverpool, his record rests on four seasons in which he helped win three major trophies and repeatedly contributed decisive goals. The Forest performance illustrates his creative influence; the title-clinching goal against Tottenham and hat-trick against Manchester United demonstrate his finishing. Together they explain why Beardsley was such an important part of Dalglish’s attack.

# Kevin Keegan: From Shankly’s rebuilding to Rome

Kevin Keegan’s six seasons at Liverpool took him from Fourth Division football to the club’s first European Cup triumph. Along the way he scored twice in an FA Cup final, twice in one UEFA Cup final and in both legs of another. His contribution to Liverpool’s rise can be traced through the matches that decided its trophies.

He arrived under Bill Shankly in 1971 and left as a European champion under Bob Paisley in 1977. Across those two managerial eras, his movement, invention and goals helped turn a rebuilt Liverpool side into winners at home and abroad.

Born in Armthorpe on 14 February 1951, Keegan began his senior career with Scunthorpe United. Liverpool signed him on 3 May 1971, when he was 20. Shankly initially considered him for a midfield role, but his partnership with John Toshack gave him a place further forward.

The understanding was evident on his competitive debut. Against Nottingham Forest at Anfield on 14 August 1971, Keegan scored after 12 minutes, with Toshack supplying the assist. Liverpool won 3–1.

Their different strengths suited each other. Toshack provided an aerial target; Keegan had the movement and anticipation to make use of the ball around him. Yet Keegan also created chances through his passing and was an effective header of the ball himself. His size did not prevent him competing in the air, and his willingness to keep moving made him difficult to contain.

That partnership became particularly important in the 1973 UEFA Cup final against Borussia Mönchengladbach. After a waterlogged pitch forced the abandonment of the first leg at Anfield, Shankly brought Toshack into the side for the rearranged match. His height unsettled the German defence and Keegan scored twice in a 3–0 victory, with Larry Lloyd adding the third.

Liverpool lost the return 2–0 but held on to win their first European trophy. Keegan had scored 22 goals in all competitions during a season that also brought the league championship. Two years after leaving Scunthorpe, he was a central figure in a side capable of winning both competitions.

The following season demonstrated his durability as well as his value in decisive games. He appeared in all 61 of Liverpool’s competitive matches in 1973–74 and scored six times during the FA Cup campaign.

Two of those goals came in the final against Newcastle United. After a goalless first half, Keegan opened the scoring with a shot from the edge of the penalty area. Steve Heighway added the second, before Keegan finished Tommy Smith’s low delivery to complete a 3–0 win. He could produce the initial breakthrough and then find the position to finish a passing move. Wembley showed both qualities.

Shankly announced his resignation that July. The beginning of the next season brought a serious setback for Keegan: he and Leeds United’s Billy Bremner were sent off after clashing in the Charity Shield. Both received lengthy bans. Keegan’s suspension disrupted the opening months of a campaign in which Liverpool finished second in the league. His record at Anfield included a costly loss of discipline.

Under Paisley, however, he remained integral to the attack. Liverpool repeated the league and UEFA Cup double in 1975–76, and Keegan was named Football Writers’ Association Footballer of the Year.

Again, his influence was clear in the European final. Club Brugge went two goals ahead at Anfield before Liverpool scored three times in six second-half minutes. Ray Kennedy and Jimmy Case brought them level; Keegan’s penalty made it 3–2. In Belgium, Brugge scored first again. Keegan equalised four minutes later, and the 1–1 draw secured a 4–3 aggregate victory. He had scored the winning goal in the home leg and the goal that preserved Liverpool’s advantage in the return.

By his final season, Liverpool knew he intended to move abroad. He still scored 20 goals in all competitions in 1976–77 as the league title was retained and the team reached both the FA Cup and European Cup finals.

Defeat by Manchester United at Wembley ended the possibility of winning all three. Four days later, on 25 May, Keegan played his last competitive match for Liverpool against Mönchengladbach in Rome.

Phil Neal later recalled Paisley instructing Keegan to keep moving across the pitch because Berti Vogts would mark him closely. That recollection helps explain a performance whose significance went beyond the scoresheet. Keegan did not score, but his running repeatedly tested his marker. With Liverpool leading 2–1 late in the game, Vogts brought him down in the penalty area. Neal converted, completing the 3–1 victory that delivered Liverpool’s first European Cup.

Keegan moved to Hamburg that summer. He won the Ballon d’Or in 1978 and 1979 and the German championship in 1978–79. He subsequently played for Southampton and Newcastle United, and his managerial career included Newcastle, Fulham, England and Manchester City. He died on 20 July 2026, aged 75.

His Liverpool record stands at 100 goals in 323 competitive appearances, including 68 goals in 230 league games. He won three league championships, two UEFA Cups, an FA Cup and a European Cup. The detail behind those honours explains his importance: the goals against Mönchengladbach, Newcastle and Brugge, followed by the running that helped settle the final in Rome. Keegan’s Liverpool career ended at the point when the club reached its greatest achievement to that date. He had helped take it there.

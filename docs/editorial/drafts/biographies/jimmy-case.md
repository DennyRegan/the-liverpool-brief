# Jimmy Case

Jimmy Case scored in a UEFA Cup final, helped Liverpool win their first two European Cups and supplied goals from the right of midfield throughout Bob Paisley’s early years in charge. His tackling and powerful shooting were defining qualities, but his contribution can be measured more precisely: 269 competitive appearances, 46 goals and four league championships before he left Anfield in 1981.

Born in Allerton on 18 May 1954, Case reached Liverpool through local football. He played for Blue Union and then South Liverpool before joining the Anfield club in May 1973. His route into professional football included an electrician’s apprenticeship. As he later explained to LFChistory, he initially declined a full-time contract because he wanted to finish his training. Liverpool took him on as a semi-professional instead.

His first-team debut came on 26 April 1975, in a 3–1 home victory over Queens Park Rangers. Case was still 20. The team sheet placed him alongside Kevin Keegan, John Toshack and Ian Callaghan; Bob Paisley had given another local player his opportunity.

Case established himself the following season. He made 39 appearances in all competitions and scored 12 goals, five of them in the UEFA Cup. Three came against Śląsk Wrocław. Liverpool finished the campaign as league champions and UEFA Cup winners, with Case making a direct contribution to the European final.

On 28 April 1976, Club Brugge led 2–0 at half-time in the first leg at Anfield. Paisley replaced Toshack with Case. Ray Kennedy pulled a goal back before Case equalised, and Keegan’s penalty completed the turnaround. Liverpool had scored three times between the 59th and 64th minutes. The substitute had helped change both the match and the position of the tie.

Case’s work on the right gave Liverpool a midfielder who could compete for possession and threaten the goal. He later described a role built around serving the more creative players and exploiting the space left by defenders concentrating on the forwards. His scoring record shows how often he used it.

The 1977 FA Cup final supplied a particularly clear example of his technique. After Manchester United took the lead, Case controlled the ball, turned and struck the equaliser past Alex Stepney. The movement mattered as much as the power: he made the space for his shot before taking it. United nevertheless won 2–1, ending Liverpool’s chance of adding both cups to the league title they had already secured.

Four days later, Case started the European Cup final against Borussia Mönchengladbach in Rome. Liverpool won 3–1 to become European champions for the first time. It was a victory that also contained a mistake he later acknowledged: his misplaced pass had allowed Allan Simonsen to score the equaliser. Tommy Smith’s header and Phil Neal’s penalty restored Liverpool’s advantage.

Case helped Liverpool reach the final again in 1978. He scored in the 3–0 home victory over Mönchengladbach that overturned a 2–1 first-leg deficit in the semi-final. At Wembley he started against Club Brugge, with Kenny Dalglish scoring the only goal. Case had begun both of Liverpool’s first two European Cup final victories.

He remained a substantial part of the side that followed. In each of the championship seasons of 1978–79 and 1979–80, he made 37 league appearances. Across all competitions those campaigns brought nine goals and then five. His value lay in repeated contributions over a season as well as the more conspicuous goals in cup matches.

His position became less secure in 1980–81 as Sammy Lee established himself. Case still started the League Cup final replay victory over West Ham United, but his third European Cup final brought a different role. Against Real Madrid in Paris on 27 May 1981, he replaced Dalglish in the 86th minute, with Liverpool already leading through Alan Kennedy. Those closing minutes of the 1–0 win were his last competitive appearance for the club.

Case moved to Brighton & Hove Albion that summer, aged 27. Looking back, he acknowledged that his behaviour away from football had counted against him at Liverpool, while also saying he had wanted to stay and compete with Lee. His account adds context to the departure without establishing a single reason for Paisley’s decision.

He returned to Anfield with Brighton in February 1983 and scored the winner in a 2–1 FA Cup fifth-round victory. Another long-range goal followed against Sheffield Wednesday in the semi-final. Brighton reached the final but lost to Manchester United after a replay. Case joined Southampton in March 1985, became captain and remained there until 1991. He later returned to Brighton as a player and subsequently managed the club.

His Liverpool career comprised 186 league appearances and 23 league goals within those overall totals of 269 and 46. The four championships, three European Cups, UEFA Cup, League Cup and European Super Cup record the team’s success. His goals and appearances explain his share in it: a regular place in Paisley’s midfield, a decisive intervention against Brugge and contributions to successive European Cup triumphs.

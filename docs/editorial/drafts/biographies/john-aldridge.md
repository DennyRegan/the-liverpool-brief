# John Aldridge: 63 goals in two and a half years

John Aldridge joined Liverpool to replace Ian Rush and left after Rush had returned. Between those two events, he scored 63 goals in 104 competitive appearances, helped win the league championship and scored in a victorious FA Cup final. His Liverpool career lasted only two and a half years, but his contribution to Kenny Dalglish’s team was substantial.

Born in Garston on 18 September 1958, Aldridge grew up supporting Liverpool. His route to Anfield took him through South Liverpool, Newport County and Oxford United. By the time Dalglish signed him for £750,000 on 27 January 1987, he was 28 and had already established himself in the First Division.

At Oxford, he had scored 30 league goals in 1984–85 and played in the side that beat Queens Park Rangers 3–0 in the 1986 League Cup final. He helped create Trevor Hebberd’s opening goal that afternoon, while Jeremy Charles scored the third after Aldridge’s shot was saved. Liverpool were buying a forward with experience of both regular goalscoring and winning a major final.

Rush was due to join Juventus that summer. Aldridge made his Liverpool debut as a substitute at Aston Villa on 21 February 1987, then scored the only goal against Southampton on his first start a week later. The immediate breakthrough did not bring a settled place: eight of his ten appearances that season came from the bench.

In a 2023 interview with Liverpool’s official podcast, Aldridge recalled that replacing Rush had troubled him less than entering the dressing room. He trusted his scoring record at Oxford and the quality of the players who would supply him. Meeting the Liverpool players, he said, had been the intimidating part. He also explained his preference for the No.8 shirt through his admiration for Roger Hunt.

The arrival of John Barnes and Peter Beardsley in the summer of 1987 gave Liverpool a new attacking combination. Aldridge offered a central target, the ability to bring others into play and sharp movement towards goal. His heading was another strength. Those qualities suited an attack capable of finding him with crosses or passes through the defence.

He scored in each of Liverpool’s first nine league matches of 1987–88. Added to his goal on the final day of the previous season, that made ten consecutive league games with a goal. He finished the championship campaign with 26 league goals and 29 in all competitions. Liverpool went unbeaten through their opening 29 league fixtures.

The 5–0 victory over Nottingham Forest at Anfield on 13 April 1988 showed how well the attack worked together. Beardsley’s through-ball released Aldridge for Liverpool’s second goal; Aldridge also scored the fifth. Four days earlier, he had scored both goals in the FA Cup semi-final victory over the same opponents. His finishing helped Liverpool reach Wembley as well as win the title.

The final against Wimbledon ended the attempt to complete the Double. With Liverpool trailing to Lawrie Sanchez’s header, Dave Beasant saved Aldridge’s second-half penalty. Beasant had already denied him from close range before the interval. Liverpool lost 1–0, and the chance to equalise from the spot became the defining incident of Aldridge’s afternoon. It was a costly failure in a season in which his goals had otherwise been so dependable.

Rush returned from Juventus in 1988, giving Dalglish another centre-forward to accommodate. Aldridge continued scoring: his 1988–89 total reached 31 across all competitions, including 21 in the league and six in the FA Cup. Competition for his place had not reduced his output.

On 15 April 1989, the FA Cup semi-final against Nottingham Forest was abandoned at Hillsborough. Ninety-seven supporters ultimately lost their lives as a result of the disaster. In recollections published by Sky Sports in 2014, Aldridge explained that, had he remained a supporter rather than become a player, he believed he would have been on the Leppings Lane terrace. He described grief that returned every anniversary.

When the semi-final was played at Old Trafford, Aldridge scored twice in a 3–1 win. Both were headers: the first from a rebound, the second from Ray Houghton’s delivery. He had now scored four goals across Liverpool’s two successive FA Cup semi-final victories over Forest.

Against Everton in the final on 20 May, Aldridge opened the scoring after four minutes. Rush later replaced him and scored twice in extra time as Liverpool won 3–2. Aldridge finished the cup run as Liverpool’s leading scorer. Six days later, he was in the side beaten 2–0 by Arsenal at Anfield, a result that cost Liverpool the league title.

His departure followed early in the next season. With Rush restored as Dalglish’s preferred centre-forward, Real Sociedad offered £1.1 million. Aldridge’s final Liverpool appearance came against Crystal Palace on 12 September 1989. Sent on after a penalty had been awarded, he converted it in front of the Kop during the 9–0 win. Afterwards, he threw his shirt and boots into the crowd.

He spent two seasons in Spain before joining Tranmere Rovers in 1991. There he scored 40 goals in his first season and later became manager, taking the club to the 2000 League Cup final. His international career brought 69 appearances and 19 goals for the Republic of Ireland.

Aldridge’s Liverpool record stands at 50 goals in 83 league appearances, and 63 in 104 overall. Sixty of those goals came in his two full seasons. The championship of 1988 and the FA Cup of 1989 both depended in part on that sustained contribution. He arrived with an unusually difficult player to replace and gave Liverpool the goals they needed.

# Alan Kennedy

Alan Kennedy scored the goal that won Liverpool the European Cup in Paris in 1981 and the penalty that settled the final in Rome three years later. Those two finishes gave a left-back an extraordinary place in the club’s history. They also tend to shorten the account of a career that deserves more than a pair of famous moments. Kennedy helped Liverpool win five league championships before leaving in 1985. In the season of their first treble, he played every match.

Born in Sunderland on 31 August 1954, Kennedy established himself at Newcastle United. He was 19 when Liverpool beat Newcastle 3–0 in the 1974 FA Cup final. Four years later, Bob Paisley brought him to Anfield for £330,000.

Liverpool needed a settled answer at left-back. Kennedy offered speed and a willingness to carry his work beyond the halfway line. His overlapping runs gave the player ahead of him another option and allowed Liverpool to attack down the left without relying solely on their midfielders. His later goals in finals were exceptional in their importance, but a defender advancing into attacking positions was part of his normal game.

His debut came against Queens Park Rangers on 19 August 1978, in a 2–1 home victory. He went on to make 37 league appearances that season and another 37 in 1979–80, winning the championship both times. The first title was built on remarkable defensive figures: Liverpool conceded just 16 goals across 42 league matches and finished eight points ahead of Nottingham Forest. Kennedy had joined a defence capable of keeping opponents out while still giving its full-backs room to contribute further forward.

The next championship was closer, with Manchester United finishing two points behind Liverpool. By then, Kennedy had completed two seasons as a regular in the side. His place in the team had been established before the goals for which he would become best known.

In March 1981, he came within minutes of deciding Liverpool’s first League Cup success at Wembley. With the final against West Ham United still goalless late in extra time, Kennedy scored in the 118th minute. Ray Stewart’s penalty forced a replay, which Liverpool won 2–1 at Villa Park through goals from Kenny Dalglish and Alan Hansen. Kennedy had supplied the breakthrough in the first match; it took a second evening to secure the trophy.

His appearance in that season’s European Cup final was less certain. An injury in the first leg of the semi-final against Bayern Munich threatened to keep him out. Recalling it in a 2015 interview with Liverpool, Kennedy said the club doctor had expected an absence of about ten weeks. Richard Money replaced him for the return in Munich, but Paisley selected Kennedy for the final. Kennedy later expressed his gratitude for the manager’s decision.

On 27 May, Liverpool met Real Madrid at the Parc des Princes. With nine minutes remaining and neither side having scored, Ray Kennedy sent a throw-in towards his namesake on the left. Alan controlled it, drove into the penalty area and struck a left-footed shot past Agustín at the near post. Liverpool won 1–0.

It was their third European Cup in five seasons and made Paisley the first manager to win the competition three times. The move also captured Kennedy’s attacking value: a full-back running beyond the midfield, continuing into the box and finishing the chance himself. In an otherwise goalless final, that willingness to advance proved decisive.

He made another major intervention at Wembley in 1983. Manchester United led the League Cup final through Norman Whiteside until Kennedy equalised with a shot from distance, 15 minutes from the end of normal time. Ronnie Whelan then scored the winner in extra time. Liverpool had won the competition for a third successive season, and Paisley lifted the trophy at his final cup final as manager.

Joe Fagan took over for 1983–84. Kennedy appeared in all 67 competitive matches, including all 42 league games, as Liverpool won the championship, League Cup and European Cup. His availability across that programme is one of the strongest measures of his contribution. The left-back who supplied occasional decisive goals was also there throughout the long work of winning three competitions.

The last of those 67 appearances came against Roma at their own Stadio Olimpico on 30 May 1984. Phil Neal put Liverpool ahead and Roberto Pruzzo equalised. After extra time, the score remained 1–1. For the first time, a European Cup final would be decided by a penalty shoot-out.

Steve Nicol missed Liverpool’s opening kick, but misses by Bruno Conti and Francesco Graziani gave Kennedy the chance to finish the contest with Liverpool’s fifth penalty. He beat Franco Tancredi to complete a 4–2 shoot-out victory. Three years after scoring the only goal against Madrid, he had delivered the final kick of Liverpool’s fourth European Cup triumph. The distinction matters: Rome was a winning penalty in the shoot-out, separate from his official goals total.

Kennedy’s England career amounted to two senior appearances, both in 1984. At Liverpool, Jim Beglin’s emergence subsequently challenged his position. Kennedy played the opening eight league matches of 1985–86 before moving to Sunderland that September. His contribution to that campaign came at the beginning; he had left long before Liverpool completed the league and FA Cup double.

He departed with 359 competitive appearances and 20 goals, including 251 appearances and 15 goals in the league. Those figures describe a substantial Liverpool career in their own right. Paris and Rome give it a distinction few defenders can match, but the championship seasons explain why Kennedy was on the pitch when those opportunities arrived. He had earned his place through years of regular service in a demanding, successful team.

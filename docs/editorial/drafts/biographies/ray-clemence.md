# Ray Clemence: the goalkeeper behind Liverpool’s rise

Ray Clemence’s Liverpool career can be measured in five league championships, three European Cups and 323 clean sheets. It can also be understood through two saves against Borussia Mönchengladbach, four years apart. One helped secure Liverpool’s first European trophy. The other came with their first European Cup final level at 1–1. At two of the most consequential moments in the club’s history, Clemence kept the opposition out.

Across 665 competitive appearances, including 470 in the league, he gave Liverpool something less easily recorded: continuity in the position where a single mistake could undo everybody else’s work. Bill Shankly brought him to Anfield. Bob Paisley inherited a goalkeeper around whom he could build.

Born in Skegness on 5 August 1948, Clemence joined Liverpool from Scunthorpe United in June 1967 for £18,000. He was 18. Tommy Lawrence remained the established goalkeeper, and the newcomer’s progress was slow. His debut came in a 2–0 League Cup victory over Swansea Town on 25 September 1968, but it did not immediately bring regular football.

His league debut followed at Nottingham Forest in January 1970. By the end of that season he had displaced Lawrence, becoming part of Shankly’s reconstruction of the side. The long wait was followed by extraordinary durability. From September 1972 to March 1978, Clemence played 336 consecutive first-team matches.

There were disappointments before the trophies. Liverpool lost the 1971 FA Cup final to Arsenal, and Clemence’s first major honours did not arrive until 1973. That season brought the league championship and UEFA Cup, with his contribution to the European final particularly important.

Liverpool beat Mönchengladbach 3–0 in the first leg at Anfield. Clemence saved Jupp Heynckes’s penalty to preserve that margin. In Germany, Heynckes scored twice before half-time, bringing the aggregate lead down to a single goal. Liverpool survived the remainder of the match to win 3–2 overall. The penalty save had denied the Germans an away goal as well as a foothold in the tie.

A year later, Clemence won the FA Cup as Liverpool beat Newcastle United 3–0. His involvement extended beyond keeping a clean sheet: his long clearance started the move for Steve Heighway’s goal. This was the final major trophy of Shankly’s reign. Under Paisley, Clemence remained central as Liverpool won another league and UEFA Cup double in 1976.

His value to the defence is particularly clear in Phil Thompson’s later account of moving from midfield to centre-back. Thompson recalled Clemence constantly talking him through matches, giving instructions and helping him adjust alongside Emlyn Hughes. He described a goalkeeper whose leadership was audible throughout the game. The younger defender had someone behind him who could see danger developing and tell him where it was.

That responsibility required concentration even when Liverpool controlled possession and chances were scarce. A strong defence reduced the goalkeeper’s workload; it did not remove the need to make the next save. Clemence combined that attentiveness with the reactions to intervene when the defence was beaten.

In Rome in 1977, with Mönchengladbach level at 1–1 in the European Cup final, Uli Stielike had an opportunity to put the German side ahead. Clemence stopped him. Liverpool went on to win the trophy for the first time. A year later, Kenny Dalglish’s goal defeated Club Brugge 1–0 at Wembley and Clemence had a second European Cup winner’s medal.

The 1978–79 league season supplied the clearest statistical expression of his consistency. Clemence played all 42 matches as Liverpool conceded only 16 goals on their way to the championship. That was a collective achievement, but the goalkeeper was present for every minute of it. Liverpool retained the title the following season, giving him his fifth league championship.

His final Liverpool campaign brought the League Cup and a third European Cup. On 27 May 1981, Alan Kennedy scored the only goal against Real Madrid in Paris. Clemence’s 665th appearance ended with another clean sheet and another European final won. He joined Tottenham Hotspur that summer.

The move did not end his success. He made 330 appearances for Spurs and won the FA Cup in 1982. He also belonged to their 1984 UEFA Cup-winning squad, although injury kept him out of the final, for which he was an unused substitute. His international career produced 61 England caps between 1972 and 1983. Competition with Peter Shilton limited his opportunities, and although selected for the 1982 World Cup squad, he did not play in the tournament.

After retiring in 1988, Clemence worked in coaching and management, including a spell at Barnet. He joined England’s coaching staff in 1996 and retired from the Football Association in 2013. He died on 15 November 2020, aged 72.

His return to Anfield with Tottenham on 15 May 1982 remains a particularly revealing part of his Liverpool story. The home side needed victory to secure the league title and were losing at half-time. When Clemence approached the Kop for the second half, he received a standing ovation. Liverpool’s immediate need to beat him had not diminished the appreciation of what he had done for them.

The medals establish his place among Liverpool’s most successful players. The saves against Heynckes and Stielike explain why he belongs there. Clemence helped determine the outcome of finals, guided the defenders in front of him and sustained that standard across more than a decade. Liverpool’s rise under Shankly and Paisley depended on him.

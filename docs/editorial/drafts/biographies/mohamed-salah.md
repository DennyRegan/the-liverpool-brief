# Mohamed Salah

*Research cut-off: 27 September 2026*

Mohamed Salah belongs among Liverpool’s greatest players. Across nine seasons, his goals helped turn Jürgen Klopp’s challengers into European and English champions, before he became the decisive attacker in Arne Slot’s championship side. He left in 2026 with 257 goals in 442 competitive appearances, behind only Ian Rush and Roger Hunt in the club’s scoring history. Liverpool also credited him with 120 assists. His importance lay in that combination: a prolific scorer who could make the attack work for others, and sustain his influence through changes around him.

Born in Egypt on 15 June 1992, Salah reached Anfield by a circuitous route. He began at El Mokawloon, moved to Basel and then Chelsea, where his first experience of English football proved unproductive. Fiorentina and Roma gave him the opportunity to establish himself in Italy. Across two Serie A seasons with Roma, he scored 29 goals in 65 appearances. Liverpool were buying an accomplished forward rather than an untested prospect, although the scale of his subsequent achievement would eclipse everything he had done before.

The signing was announced on 22 June 2017 and became official on 1 July. Roma disclosed an initial fee of €42 million, with up to €8 million in additional payments. Salah was 25. Klopp placed him alongside Roberto Firmino and Sadio Mané, forming an attack whose complementary qualities would become fundamental to Liverpool’s revival.

Salah started from the right but attacked the goal like a striker. His acceleration threatened the space behind defenders; when they retreated, he could receive the ball closer to goal and drive inside. He needed little room to move the ball on to his left foot and shoot. Defenders knew the direction he preferred, yet his changes of pace, feints and ability to strike quickly made that knowledge difficult to use. He was powerful as well as fast, capable of carrying an attack through a run rather than waiting for a chance to arrive.

The partnership gave those qualities a structure. Firmino dropped towards the ball, linking play and opening space for Salah and Mané to move infield. Trent Alexander-Arnold’s width and passing on the right offered another route forward, while Salah’s narrow positioning occupied defenders inside him. The three forwards also worked to recover possession high up the pitch. Salah’s scoring was the most visible part of a coordinated attack: his runs, combinations and work without the ball helped create the circumstances in which Liverpool could overwhelm opponents.

His first season was extraordinary even by the standards of Liverpool’s finest forwards. He scored 44 goals in all competitions, including 32 in the Premier League, then a record for a 38-game campaign. Only Rush’s 47 in 1983-84 exceeded Salah’s total for a single Liverpool season. A player nominally stationed on the wing had produced one of the great scoring campaigns in the club’s history.

Against Roma at Anfield in April 2018, he showed why the totals were so formidable. In the first leg of the Champions League semi-final, Firmino supplied both his goals: a curling finish into the top corner and a chip over Alisson. Salah then created goals for Mané and Firmino as Liverpool won 5-2. Within the same match he could be the runner beyond the defence, the individual finisher and the provider from the right.

The final against Real Madrid brought a painful end to that season. A shoulder injury sustained in a challenge with Sergio Ramos forced Salah off during the first half, and Liverpool lost 3-1. His debut campaign had carried the club back to the European final, but he could not complete it.

His response in 2018-19 established that the first year was no isolated peak. With 22 league goals he retained the Golden Boot, sharing it with Mané and Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang. Liverpool accumulated 97 points and still finished behind Manchester City. In Europe, concussion sustained at Newcastle ruled him out of the semi-final second leg against Barcelona. His teammates took Liverpool through without him; in the final against Tottenham Hotspur, he was back to convert a second-minute penalty. Divock Origi’s late goal completed the 2-0 victory in Madrid and Liverpool’s sixth European Cup.

The league championship followed in 2019-20. Salah scored 19 Premier League goals as Liverpool amassed 99 points and ended their 30-year wait to become champions of England again. The UEFA Super Cup and Club World Cup had also been won in 2019. Within three seasons, the forward signed from Roma had helped Liverpool recover the two titles by which their greatest teams had traditionally been measured: the league championship and the European Cup.

He remained productive when the team became less secure. During the difficult title defence of 2020-21, Salah scored 22 league goals as Liverpool eventually recovered to finish third. His contribution mattered in a different way that season: the reigning champions were fighting to retain their place in the Champions League, and their principal scorer continued to deliver.

In October 2021, his performance against Manchester City displayed the breadth of his game. His run and pass released Mané for Liverpool’s opening goal; later, receiving the ball on the right, he dribbled through the defence and finished across Ederson with his right foot. The match ended 2-2. That month also brought a hat-trick in the 5-0 victory over Manchester United at Old Trafford. Salah became the first visiting player to score three there in a Premier League match.

Liverpool pursued four trophies in 2021-22 and finished with the FA Cup and League Cup. Salah’s 23 league goals earned a third Golden Boot, shared with Son Heung-min, while his 13 assists secured the Playmaker award. The margins elsewhere were narrow: Liverpool’s 92 points left them one behind City, and Real Madrid won the European final 1-0. Thibaut Courtois repeatedly denied Salah in Paris.

Even Liverpool’s disappointing 2022-23 campaign contained achievements of lasting significance. Salah scored 30 goals in all competitions, though the side missed Champions League qualification. Coming off the bench against Rangers at Ibrox, he scored three times in six minutes and 12 seconds during a 7-1 victory, the fastest hat-trick in Champions League history. Two goals in the 7-0 defeat of Manchester United in March then carried him beyond Robbie Fowler as Liverpool’s leading Premier League scorer.

His durability did not make him immune to interruption. A muscle injury suffered with Egypt at the Africa Cup of Nations disrupted 2023-24, and he was unavailable for Liverpool’s League Cup final victory over Chelsea. It was the second League Cup of his time at the club, but one won without him on the field at Wembley. Klopp’s departure that summer ended the managerial partnership under which Salah had become a Liverpool great.

Under Slot, he produced another season to stand alongside his first. In 2024-25, now in his thirties, Salah scored 29 league goals and supplied 18 assists as Liverpool won the championship again. Those 47 combined goals and assists set a record for a 38-game Premier League season. The playmaking had long been present, but leading the division in both categories showed how much responsibility he could carry for the whole attack. He won his fourth Golden Boot, matching Thierry Henry, and became the first player to collect it alongside the Playmaker and Premier League Player of the Season awards in one campaign.

Recognition from fellow professionals and football writers reinforced his standing. Salah won the PFA Players’ Player of the Year award in 2018, 2022 and 2025, becoming its first three-time winner. He was also the Football Writers’ Association’s Footballer of the Year in those same years. Just as telling was his consistency between the celebrated peaks: from 2017-18 to 2024-25 he scored at least 20 goals in all competitions in eight consecutive seasons, a Liverpool record. Five of those campaigns reached 30.

His achievements also placed him among Africa’s outstanding footballers. CAF named him its Player of the Year in 2017 and 2018. In his final Liverpool season, he became the first African player to reach 50 Champions League goals, excluding qualifying rounds. He was already Liverpool’s record scorer in European competition. These were accomplishments built largely at Anfield, extending the significance of his Liverpool career beyond the English game.

The ending was less smooth. By his final appearance Salah had publicly criticised Slot’s playing style, and Liverpool finished the 2025-26 league season fifth. His departure had been announced in March. Against Brentford at Anfield on 24 May, he set up Curtis Jones in a 1-1 draw: his 93rd Premier League assist for Liverpool, passing Steven Gerrard’s club record. He joined Trabzonspor in August, continuing his playing career beyond Merseyside.

Salah left as Liverpool’s leading Premier League scorer with 191 goals, and their leading assist provider in the competition. His eight major honours included two league titles, the European Cup and both domestic cups. The records establish his place alongside the club’s greatest; the matches explain it. From dismantling Roma to scoring in Madrid, from the hat-trick at Old Trafford to the championship won under a second manager, he repeatedly supplied the football that made Liverpool a side capable of winning the biggest prizes.

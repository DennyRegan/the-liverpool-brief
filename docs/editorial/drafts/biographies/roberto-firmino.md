# Roberto Firmino: the forward who made Liverpool work

Roberto Firmino helped Liverpool win by making their forwards harder to mark. He would leave the centre of the attack to receive the ball in midfield, draw a defender towards him and find the runner entering the space. With Mohamed Salah and Sadio Mané either side, that movement became central to Jürgen Klopp’s team. Firmino could create the opening, supply the pass or arrive to finish the move himself.

Across eight seasons he made 362 competitive appearances and scored 111 goals. His Liverpool honours included the European Cup and the club’s first league championship for 30 years. Those achievements rested partly on a quality less easily recorded: his ability to make the players around him more effective.

Born in Maceió, Brazil, on 2 October 1991, Firmino established himself at Figueirense before moving to Hoffenheim in 2011. He developed into a creative attacking player in Germany, scoring 16 Bundesliga goals in 2013–14. Liverpool announced an agreement to sign him in June 2015. At the time, he was generally regarded as an attacking midfielder who could also play across the forward line.

His opening weeks in England were unsettled, but Klopp’s arrival that autumn brought a clearer use for his talents. On 21 November, Firmino scored his first Liverpool goal in a 4–1 win at Manchester City. He had already forced the opening own goal with a low cross and supplied Philippe Coutinho for the second. Coutinho returned the pass for Firmino’s finish. Within one half, Liverpool had seen how dangerous he could be as their central attacker.

The role demanded more than occupying the opposition’s centre-backs. Firmino played as a false nine: a centre-forward who regularly drops into midfield to help construct attacks. His movement could pull a defender out of position and open a route towards goal for someone else. An early limitation was that the arrangement worked more naturally against opponents who left space behind them. Against a defence sitting deep, Liverpool still needed runners and penetration around him.

Salah’s arrival in 2017 brought together the partnership that best exploited those qualities. Salah and Mané could attack towards goal while Firmino offered a passing option between midfield and defence. Without the ball, he also helped Liverpool win possession back. In February 2018, Premier League analyst Adrian Clarke noted that he was the squad’s third-most successful tackler. The creative work and the defensive work belonged to the same job.

That season also showed the danger of describing him only through what he did for others. Firmino scored 27 goals in all competitions in 2017–18, his best Liverpool return. Ten came in the Champions League proper, with another in qualifying. Against Roma in the first leg of the semi-final, he supplied both of Salah’s goals and scored twice himself in a 5–2 win. His contribution was measurable as well as tactical.

Liverpool lost the final to Real Madrid, but returned to win the competition a year later. Firmino’s part in the closing stages was restricted by a muscle injury. He missed the return leg against Barcelona and recovered to start the final against Tottenham in Madrid. Liverpool won 2–0, with Salah and Divock Origi scoring.

There were individual displays to remember along the way. In December 2018 he scored a hat-trick in a 5–1 victory over Arsenal at Anfield. His second goal, carrying the ball through challenges before finishing low, demonstrated the close control he could use in crowded areas. The same player who spent so much time finding a teammate could decide a match by taking on the defence himself.

His most direct contribution to a trophy came in Qatar in December 2019. Introduced from the bench against Monterrey, he scored in stoppage time to send Liverpool into the Club World Cup final. Three days later, against Flamengo, he settled the final in extra time. Receiving Mané’s pass inside the penalty area, he waited for the opening and finished. Liverpool had won the competition for the first time, with Firmino scoring the winner in both matches.

He appeared in all 38 league games as Liverpool became champions in 2019–20. Away from Anfield, his goals repeatedly settled difficult fixtures: Crystal Palace, Tottenham and Wolves all conceded winners to him, while he scored twice in the 4–0 victory at Leicester. There was an odd imbalance to his season, however. His first home league goal did not arrive until the final Anfield fixture, against Chelsea. Even during Liverpool’s title campaign, his scoring came unevenly.

His later seasons brought injuries and a changing place in the attack. A serious hamstring injury interrupted his 2021–22 campaign, while Diogo Jota and Luis Díaz gave Klopp different options. Firmino made 20 league appearances that season, compared with 38 two years earlier. He remained part of the squad that won the FA Cup and League Cup, but Liverpool were increasingly finding ways to play without him.

There were still significant goals in his final season. Two against Bournemouth in August 2022 took him to 100 for the club. After his intention to leave became known the following March, he scored the seventh in Liverpool’s 7–0 win over Manchester United. His last Anfield appearance, against Aston Villa on 20 May, brought an 89th-minute equaliser from Salah’s cross.

Eight days later he scored again in the 4–4 draw at Southampton, his final Liverpool match. He left when his contract expired and joined Al-Ahli, then moved to Al Sadd in July 2025. At the research cut-off for this biography, 27 September 2026, he remained an active player with the Qatari club.

Firmino’s Liverpool career amounted to more than an unusual interpretation of centre-forward play. The movement had a purpose, the pressing helped recover possession and the passing served an attack capable of winning the biggest honours. He also supplied 111 goals of his own. Liverpool’s success required all of those things, often from the same player in the same passage of play.

# Phil Neal

Phil Neal played in each of Liverpool’s first four European Cup-winning finals. He scored in two of them. Between the first victory in Rome in 1977 and the return there seven years later, managers and teammates changed, but Liverpool’s right-back remained. His place in that history rests on more than longevity: he helped decide its most important matches.

Liverpool signed him from Fourth Division Northampton Town for £66,000 in October 1974. He left in December 1985 with 650 competitive appearances, 59 goals and a contribution to eight league championship campaigns. Few transfers have served the club so well for so long.

Born in Irchester, Northamptonshire, on 20 February 1951, Neal established himself at Northampton before becoming Bob Paisley’s first signing as Liverpool manager. At 23, he was making the jump from the fourth tier to the First Division. His introduction came at Goodison Park on 16 November 1974, in a goalless Merseyside derby.

Although remembered as a right-back, he initially played on the left. That versatility offered an immediate route into the side. Recalling his arrival in an interview with LFChistory, Neal explained that being able to take Alec Lindsay’s position had helped him settle. He spent around 18 months at left-back before establishing himself on the opposite flank.

Neal’s defensive game depended on judgement. In a 2024 interview with Liverpool’s website, Phil Thompson recalled that his teammate lacked exceptional pace but read play well and supplied passes that Thompson could use immediately. Their understanding mattered: with both operating on the right of the defence, each knew what the other could do. Neal’s reliability was a practical quality, expressed in positioning and the next pass.

The figures show how consistently Liverpool could call on him. Between October 1976 and September 1983, Neal made 417 consecutive competitive appearances, a club record. Across almost seven years, Liverpool went through their league and cup fixtures without once having to leave him out. In 1978–79 he played all 42 league matches as Liverpool conceded only 16 goals on their way to the championship.

His presence was not confined to his own half. Of his 59 Liverpool goals, 38 came from penalties, leaving 21 from open play. That distinction matters when assessing his attacking contribution: taking penalties accounted for most of his goals, but his ability to arrive in a scoring position also brought rewards.

The clearest examples came in Rome. In the 1977 European Cup final, Liverpool led Borussia Mönchengladbach 2–1 when Neal scored an 83rd-minute penalty. Terry McDermott and Tommy Smith had supplied the earlier goals; Neal’s made the result secure. Liverpool were European champions for the first time.

He returned to the winning side against Club Brugge at Wembley in 1978 and Real Madrid in Paris in 1981. By the time Liverpool faced Roma in the 1984 final, Neal was the only player to have appeared in all four of those victorious European Cup finals.

Against Roma, he scored from close range in the 14th minute. Roberto Pruzzo equalised before half-time, and the match remained 1–1 through extra time. Liverpool then began the shoot-out with a miss from Steve Nicol. Neal converted their next kick, and Liverpool eventually won 4–2 on penalties. On a night remembered for Bruce Grobbelaar’s movements on the goal-line and Alan Kennedy’s winning kick, Neal had scored both during the match and in the shoot-out.

European success accompanied sustained domestic achievement. His first full season, 1975–76, brought the league championship and UEFA Cup. He later helped Liverpool win four successive League Cups between 1981 and 1984. The last of those seasons also brought the league title and European Cup under Joe Fagan. Neal’s career connected Paisley’s teams with Fagan’s without a break in his importance to the side.

He succeeded Graeme Souness as captain in the summer of 1984. His only full season in that role ended at Heysel on 29 May 1985, where 39 people, mostly Juventus supporters, died before the European Cup final. The match went ahead and Liverpool lost 1–0. The deaths remain the defining fact of that occasion.

Kenny Dalglish’s appointment as player-manager that summer brought changes. Alan Hansen became captain, and Steve Nicol’s emergence reduced Neal’s opportunities at right-back. Neal made his final Liverpool appearance in November and joined Bolton Wanderers as player-manager the following month.

There is an important qualification to his eight league titles: the last was Liverpool’s 1985–86 championship, secured after he had left. Neal had played 13 league games earlier in that campaign. His contribution belongs in the record, though he was no longer at Anfield when the title was won. The FA Cup was absent from his Liverpool honours; he had departed before the club completed its first league and cup Double.

Neal also won 50 England caps. After Liverpool, his work included managing Bolton, Coventry City and Cardiff City, and assisting England manager Graham Taylor. His Liverpool playing career remains the substantial part of his footballing record.

Availability gave Liverpool continuity. Positional judgement made that continuity useful. And when European finals required a goal, Neal twice supplied one. His 417-match run and his interventions in Rome explain different parts of the same contribution: a full-back Liverpool could depend on, week after week and at the highest level.

# Jordan Henderson: the captain who earned his place

Jordan Henderson captained Liverpool to the European Cup and their first league championship in 30 years. Yet his place in the club’s history rests partly on a decision made long before either success. In 2012, with Liverpool prepared to let him leave for Fulham, he stayed to compete for his position. The midfielder who could have departed after one season eventually spent twelve at Anfield, making 492 competitive appearances.

Born in Sunderland on 17 June 1990, Henderson developed at his hometown club and spent time on loan at Coventry City before establishing himself in Sunderland’s first team. Kenny Dalglish brought him to Liverpool in June 2011, shortly before his twenty-first birthday.

His first season offered plenty of football without establishing his best role. Often used on the right of midfield, he made 48 appearances in all competitions, more than any teammate. He started the League Cup final victory over Cardiff City and the FA Cup final defeat by Chelsea. Those selections reflected Dalglish’s willingness to use him, but Henderson was still learning how to influence matches at Liverpool.

Brendan Rodgers’ arrival brought the possibility of an early exit. Liverpool considered including Henderson in a deal for Fulham’s Clint Dempsey. Henderson rejected the move. Recalling the conversation years later, he explained that he believed he still had much to contribute and wanted to earn a place in Rodgers’ team.

By 2013–14, he had done so. Playing centrally, his running and willingness to support attacks helped a side built around the goals of Luis Suárez and Daniel Sturridge. He started 35 league games. His absence from the other three came through suspension after a late challenge on Samir Nasri in the 3–2 victory over Manchester City on 13 April.

That dismissal removed him from the matches against Norwich City, Chelsea and Crystal Palace during the title run-in. Liverpool finished second. His absence was a significant loss, though it cannot establish that Liverpool would have won the championship had he played. The red card was also his responsibility: after reviewing the challenge, Henderson accepted the decision.

Appointed vice-captain in September 2014, he succeeded Steven Gerrard as captain on 10 July 2015. His first season in the role was interrupted by injury. He was unavailable when Jürgen Klopp arrived that October and remained an unused substitute in the Europa League final at the end of the campaign.

Klopp increasingly used Henderson at the base of midfield. It required him to adapt from the more mobile role he preferred, taking responsibility for play in front of the defence. Liverpool reached the 2018 Champions League final with him as captain, losing to Real Madrid in Kyiv. By the following spring, Fabinho’s emergence offered another way to arrange the midfield.

In April 2019, Henderson explained that he had discussed playing further forward with Klopp after enjoying that position for England. Fabinho could occupy the deeper role, allowing Henderson to make more attacking runs. His late goal as a substitute in the 3–1 win at Southampton demonstrated the value of that change.

There was a more consequential example against Barcelona. In the second leg of the Champions League semi-final, Henderson entered the penalty area and forced Marc-André ter Stegen into the save from which Divock Origi scored Liverpool’s opener. Liverpool overturned a three-goal deficit with a 4–0 victory. His run from midfield had helped start the recovery.

On 1 June, Liverpool beat Tottenham Hotspur 2–0 in Madrid. Henderson lifted the European Cup, four years after taking the captaincy. The UEFA Super Cup and Club World Cup followed before the end of 2019.

The league championship came next. Liverpool won 26 of their opening 27 league matches in 2019–20 and finished with 99 points, ending the wait that had begun after the 1990 title. Henderson received the Football Writers’ Association’s Footballer of the Year award. During the coronavirus pandemic, he had also helped organise fellow Premier League captains behind the Players Together fundraising initiative supporting the NHS.

His value to Klopp extended beyond those public responsibilities. When Henderson left, the manager specifically praised his technique and tactical development, including his ability to play in different midfield positions. During the defensive injury crisis of 2020–21, Henderson also played at centre-back. Klopp cited his understanding of the game as the reason he could manage the change.

Henderson scored 33 times for Liverpool, 29 of those goals coming in the league. Scoring was a relatively small part of his work. His strongest contribution was the combination of energy, positional adaptability and passing that allowed him to serve different versions of the team. The move forward in 2019 showed that this could include creating danger himself, as well as supporting others.

Liverpool won both domestic cups in 2022, although another league title and European Cup escaped them. The Community Shield followed that summer. Henderson’s final Liverpool season brought 43 appearances, taking his total to 492, including 360 in the Premier League. He left for Al-Ettifaq, managed by Gerrard, in July 2023.

The departure brought criticism with a direct connection to his Liverpool captaincy. Henderson had publicly supported LGBT+ inclusion and the Rainbow Laces campaign. Kop Outs, Liverpool’s LGBT+ supporters’ group, condemned the Saudi move and asked him to uphold those commitments. That objection belongs in an account of his time at Liverpool alongside his charitable work and football achievements.

He joined Ajax in January 2024, moved to Brentford in July 2025 and signed for Chelsea in August 2026. His Liverpool record was already complete: seven major trophies, the Community Shield and eight years as club captain. Across three managers, Henderson had progressed from a young midfielder searching for a role to a regular contributor in teams that won the game’s leading honours. The place he chose to fight for in 2012 became one he held through Liverpool’s return to the top.

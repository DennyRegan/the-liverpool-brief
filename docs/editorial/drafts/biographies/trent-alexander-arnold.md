# Trent Alexander-Arnold

Jürgen Klopp did not leave Liverpool’s free-kick taker to chance in Hoffenheim. Having watched Trent Alexander-Arnold strike the ball for the U23s, he instructed the 18-year-old to take it. On 15 August 2017, the teenager curled Liverpool ahead in a Champions League qualifier they won 2–1. His first senior goal was an early demonstration of what made him unusual: a right-back trusted to supply a skill normally associated with the team’s established creators.

That trust grew into a central feature of Liverpool’s football. Alexander-Arnold could create from a wide crossing position, deliver a decisive set-piece or move inside to direct possession. Across nine first-team seasons, the Academy graduate helped Liverpool win eight major honours, including the Champions League and two league titles. His development was intertwined with the team’s rise, although the course of his career contained difficult defensive lessons, injuries and a departure that divided opinion.

## A route through the Academy

Born in West Derby, Liverpool, on 7 October 1998, Alexander-Arnold joined the club in 2004, aged six. His childhood proximity to Melwood and his progress through the Academy gave his first-team arrival a particular local significance. Steven Gerrard was his footballing idol; in a 2017 interview, he recalled watching the captain change games and wanting to follow his example.

Alexander-Arnold was initially a midfielder. Academy coaches, including Alex Inglethorpe, identified right-back as his quickest route towards senior football. He appeared there in a first-team friendly at Swindon under Brendan Rodgers in August 2015, before Klopp’s arrival. The change of position therefore began in the youth system: Klopp subsequently provided the competitive opportunities through which it developed.

His senior debut came on 25 October 2016, in a 2–1 League Cup victory over Tottenham at Anfield. Wearing No.66, he played 68 minutes and finished the season with 12 appearances, understudying Nathaniel Clyne. A first Premier League start followed at Manchester United in January 2017. Klopp entrusted him with the whole of a 1–1 draw at Old Trafford, an early test well beyond the shelter of an Academy fixture.

Clyne’s back injury opened further opportunities in 2017–18. Alexander-Arnold initially shared right-back duties with Joe Gomez, but eventually established himself in the position. The Hoffenheim free-kick showed the value of retaining his midfield skills in a defensive role. It also illustrated how closely Klopp had watched him: the manager’s decision was based on ability already demonstrated at youth level.

## Learning on the biggest stages

The progress was uneven. Manchester United beat Liverpool 2–1 in March 2018, with Marcus Rashford scoring twice. Looking back in a Sky Sports interview in January 2020, Alexander-Arnold described it as his hardest match to that point. He accepted responsibility, especially for the first goal, and said he had used the experience to improve. It was his own assessment of a painful afternoon, rather than a claim that one defender had caused every part of the defeat.

A month later, Liverpool eliminated Manchester City from the Champions League, winning 5–1 on aggregate. Academy coach Neil Critchley highlighted Alexander-Arnold’s defending against Leroy Sané across both legs, particularly his composure under pressure and maturity after being booked. Klopp had kept faith with him for the tie. The teenager’s contribution was a reminder that his claim to the shirt rested on more than the quality of his crossing.

He made 33 appearances that season and started the Champions League final against Real Madrid in Kyiv. Liverpool lost, but Alexander-Arnold had moved from an understudy’s role to the starting side in the club game’s biggest final. The following campaign required him to sustain that advance over a longer challenge.

Liverpool’s full-backs became major sources of attacking service. In the 2018–19 Premier League, Alexander-Arnold supplied 12 assists and Andy Robertson 11: the first occasion on which two defenders from the same team had each reached double figures. Alexander-Arnold later stressed the collective work behind those numbers. Klopp gave them freedom to advance; midfielders covered their positions; forwards converted the chances. Their output depended on the team’s structure as well as two exceptional deliveries.

## The corner and the championship

The most famous of those deliveries was deliberately quicker than Barcelona expected. On 7 May 2019, Liverpool had recovered a three-goal first-leg deficit to level their Champions League semi-final on aggregate. Alexander-Arnold’s low corner found Divock Origi ready to finish while the visitors were unprepared. Origi’s second goal made it 4–0 on the night and took Liverpool to another final.

Alexander-Arnold described the decision as instinctive, recognising both the opening and Origi’s alertness. The episode joined speed of thought to technical execution. It was also the culmination of a collective comeback: Origi and Georginio Wijnaldum each scored twice, and Liverpool’s ability to keep Barcelona out remained essential.

In Madrid on 1 June, Liverpool beat Tottenham 2–0. Alexander-Arnold became the first player under 21 to start consecutive Champions League finals. Within two full seasons as a regular contender for the right-back position, he had gone from the disappointment of Kyiv to a European Cup winner’s medal.

The 2019–20 league campaign made his attacking influence even clearer. At second-placed Leicester on Boxing Day, Liverpool won 4–0 to move 13 points clear. Alexander-Arnold crossed for both Roberto Firmino goals; another of his deliveries was handled to give James Milner a penalty. He then finished Sadio Mané’s lay-off for the fourth. One match displayed the variety of his contribution: crossing from different positions, set-piece pressure and a composed finish of his own.

He played all 38 league games, scoring four times and supplying 13 assists as Liverpool ended their 30-year championship wait. The 13th came through a cross for Firmino in the 5–3 win over Chelsea, surpassing his own previous season’s record for a defender. The Premier League named him its inaugural Young Player of the Season, and he separately won the PFA Young Player of the Year award. UEFA Super Cup and Club World Cup medals also joined his collection during that campaign.

## Responsibility and reinvention

Defending the title in 2020–21 brought a different burden. Liverpool’s changing centre-back combinations increased Alexander-Arnold’s responsibility for organising the defensive line, as Klopp explained that May. His contribution to the pursuit of Champions League qualification remained decisive: a stoppage-time strike beat Aston Villa 2–1 at Anfield in April, and his late corner at West Bromwich Albion provided the delivery for Alisson’s extraordinary winning header.

The end of the club season was followed by personal disappointment. A thigh injury sustained against Austria forced him out of England’s squad for the delayed Euro 2020 tournament. His success at Liverpool had not removed the interruptions or pressures of a developing career.

In 2021–22, he supplied a personal-best 18 assists across all competitions. Liverpool won the League Cup and FA Cup while finishing runners-up in both the league and Champions League. The FA Cup final against Chelsea showed his value at both ends: an outside-of-the-foot pass released Luis Díaz for an early chance, while a sliding challenge denied Christian Pulisic a late shooting opportunity. Liverpool won on penalties after 120 goalless minutes.

A difficult 2022–23 season led to another change of position within games. In April, Klopp began using him inside from right-back when Liverpool had possession. Alexander-Arnold supplied six assists that month. In his explanation of the new role, he described learning the receiving angles, awareness and tempo-setting responsibilities of a central player. The purpose extended beyond finding a better crossing position: he could help shape the way Liverpool built an attack.

The adjustment depended on teammates. Alexander-Arnold recognised the space Ibrahima Konaté had to cover and Robertson’s more restrained positioning. Klopp likewise explained that Liverpool could not send one full-back into central midfield while keeping the other permanently high on the opposite flank. The role gave Alexander-Arnold greater influence, but required a redistribution of the team’s work.

On 31 July 2023, he was appointed vice-captain to Virgil van Dijk. He spoke of wanting to lead through his daily standards and taking responsibility for the side’s performances. Injury nevertheless prevented him from playing in the 2024 League Cup final, another trophy won during Klopp’s last season. Leadership and a medal collection did not guarantee involvement in every defining match.

## A second title and a difficult goodbye

Under Arne Slot, Alexander-Arnold remained an important member of another championship team. He made 33 league appearances in 2024–25. His final Liverpool goal came at Leicester on 20 April: returning from injury as a substitute, he scored the only goal in the 76th minute and brought the title closer.

After the championship had been secured, he announced on 5 May that he intended to leave when his contract expired. In his explanation to Liverpool’s website, he said he wanted a new personal and professional challenge. He also expressed confidence in Slot and the club’s prospects, and said he had delayed the announcement to keep attention on the title race. These were his stated reasons; the decision did not disclose every detail of the preceding negotiations.

Some supporters booed him when he came on against Arsenal on 11 May; others applauded. Robertson defended his friend’s contribution while acknowledging the emotion around the decision. On 25 May, Alexander-Arnold was cheered onto the pitch for his final appearance against Crystal Palace and was visibly moved during the trophy celebrations. The different receptions belong to the story without being turned into a single verdict on behalf of all Liverpool supporters.

Liverpool then accepted an offer from Real Madrid, allowing the transfer to take effect on 1 June, before the planned 30 June contract expiry. The club received a fee. His completed Liverpool record was 354 competitive appearances, 23 goals and 86 assists; within that total were 259 league appearances and 18 league goals.

His eight major honours comprised the Champions League, UEFA Super Cup, Club World Cup, FA Cup, two League Cups and two Premier League titles. The record captures sustained achievement, but the manner of it matters too. Liverpool developed a midfielder into a right-back whose passing could change their attacking possibilities. He repaid the faith of Academy coaches and successive first-team managers with decisive moments, sustained creative service and an influential place in two championship sides.

# Fabinho: the protection behind Liverpool's attacking freedom

Six minutes into Liverpool's meeting with Manchester City on 10 November 2019, Fabinho took possession of Rodri's attempted clearance, set himself and drove a shot beyond Claudio Bravo. Liverpool's holding midfielder had opened the scoring against the reigning champions. A match that would strengthen their hold on the title race began with the player normally stationed behind the attack striking from distance.

Liverpool won 3–1, moving eight points clear at the top and nine ahead of City. The goal was a vivid expression of a capacity Fabinho did not have to use every week. Much of his importance lay in the decisions that prevented the game turning against Liverpool: occupying the right space, recovering possession and giving teammates confidence to advance.

By his departure in July 2023, he had made 219 appearances and scored 11 goals across five seasons. His major honours were the Premier League, Champions League, UEFA Super Cup, FA Cup and League Cup. The record belonged to a midfielder who had first needed time to establish himself, then become a source of stability within one of Liverpool's most successful modern teams.

## From Monaco to a different midfield

Fabinho was born Fabio Henrique Tavares in Campinas on 23 October 1993. His route to Liverpool had already taken him through Brazil, Portugal, Spain and France. After joining Rio Ave in 2012, he spent a season on loan with Real Madrid, principally in their reserve team, before making a senior appearance. Monaco then provided the setting in which he became an established player at the highest level.

He helped Monaco win Ligue 1 and reach the Champions League semi-finals in 2016–17. Liverpool announced an agreement to sign him on 28 May 2018, with the formal move taking effect on 1 July. He was 24 and had made 225 appearances in five seasons at Monaco. He arrived with considerable experience rather than as a prospect requiring an introduction to senior European football.

Yet experience elsewhere did not guarantee an immediate Liverpool place. In early September, Fabinho had still not made a competitive appearance. Jürgen Klopp explained that the team was performing well, the players already selected were more familiar with its demands and the newcomer needed time. Patience was possible because Liverpool were winning; it was still difficult for the player whose opportunities were being delayed.

His debut came as an added-time substitute in the 3–2 Champions League victory over Paris Saint-Germain on 18 September. His first start followed against Chelsea in the League Cup on 26 September. By the latter part of October, that remained his only start. The transition was a process, and the absence of regular football should not be retrospectively erased by the success that followed.

Klopp described the issue as adapting to a different system, comparing the situation with Andy Robertson's earlier wait to establish himself. Against Red Star Belgrade on 24 October, Fabinho helped Liverpool win 4–0 and earned the manager's praise for his aggression and effectiveness. Klopp also noted that the arrangement used two holding midfielders, a system Fabinho particularly liked. Liverpool were finding ways to make his qualities useful while his understanding of their football developed.

## More than a defensive presence

Fabinho's later account of that first season acknowledged how uncomfortable it had been to sit outside the team, even while he tried to remain patient. He also described the help of Alisson Becker, Roberto Firmino and Sadio Mané. He and Mané could speak French, giving the Brazilian another means of settling into a new workplace and country. His integration depended on relationships as well as tactical preparation.

The connection could be seen on the pitch. His lofted pass supplied Mané's opening goal against Manchester United in December 2018. On Boxing Day he scored his own first Liverpool goal, heading in Mohamed Salah's corner in the 4–0 win over Newcastle United. Ball recovery was his principal responsibility, but it did not define the limits of his technique or participation in attack.

Pepijn Lijnders's description of him as Liverpool's 'lighthouse' captured his guiding presence at the base of midfield. The assistant manager valued his ability to recognise danger, interrupt counter-attacks and improve the conditions in which teammates operated. The nickname expressed an important relationship: Fabinho's positioning could help an ambitious team retain direction when its movements became complicated.

Klopp's later farewell explained the protection more concretely. With both full-backs advancing and other midfielders and forwards supporting attacks, Fabinho and the centre-halves could remain ready to deal with a lost ball. That responsibility was central to Liverpool's freedom. Winning possession was one part; anticipating where an opponent might exploit the spaces around the attack was another.

He could also move further back himself. With Virgil van Dijk suspended and Dejan Lovren injured, Fabinho played at centre-back against Bayern Munich at Anfield on 19 February 2019. Liverpool drew 0–0, with Fabinho making an important tackle as Robert Lewandowski tried to turn towards goal in the second half. A player signed for midfield supplied a defensive solution on a major European night.

The first season ended with Liverpool winning the Champions League. Fabinho started with Jordan Henderson and Georginio Wijnaldum in midfield and played the whole of the 2–0 victory over Tottenham Hotspur in Madrid. The league had brought a different outcome: Liverpool's 97 points were insufficient to win the championship. The delayed introduction had nevertheless developed into a place at the centre of a team capable of competing for both prizes.

## Championship football, with an interruption

The City goal at Anfield came during the next season's pursuit of the title. It gave Liverpool an early advantage in a contest whose result increased the distance between them and the defending champions. The midfielder who helped protect their attacking play had made a direct, forceful contribution to its most important domestic objective.

Later that November he suffered ankle ligament damage against Napoli. He was replaced after 18 minutes of the 1–1 draw, and Liverpool announced that he would be unavailable until the New Year. Even as the team's campaign continued to flourish, his own season had been interrupted. His eventual title contribution amounted to 28 league appearances and two goals.

The second goal arrived against Crystal Palace on 24 June 2020, when Liverpool won 4–0 after the league's suspension during the pandemic. Before scoring from distance himself, Fabinho had lofted the ball into Salah's path for the second goal. The match displayed his passing and shooting together. Liverpool moved to the brink of the title, rather than securing it that evening, and ultimately finished the campaign with 99 points.

He had added the UEFA Super Cup to his European Cup medal at the beginning of that season. The league championship supplied the greater sustained domestic achievement. Fabinho's contribution was broader than the two striking finishes: he had helped a team accustomed to pursuing success become one able to maintain its advantage across the campaign.

## The cost of filling a gap

The next season placed different demands on him. Fabinho was already effective at centre-back in the 2–0 victory at Chelsea in September 2020, when Klopp praised his work both in possession and in defending. What had been a useful alternative became a much more frequent requirement as injuries disrupted Liverpool's defence.

Fabinho suffered injury setbacks of his own, including a problem against Midtjylland in October. By early March 2021, he was returning after four matches missed with a muscle complaint. He had spent much of the season at centre-back. Liverpool could draw on his defensive ability, but redeploying him also removed an established player from the midfield position where he had helped others flourish.

Lijnders explained the difficulty as losing part of the midfield through the need to repair the back line. Changing players and recurring problems prevented the team finding consistency. Fabinho's versatility helped Liverpool cope with one shortage while altering the balance elsewhere. It was a valuable attribute being stretched by circumstances, rather than evidence that the change had no cost.

Against RB Leipzig on 10 March, he returned to the No.6 position. Liverpool won the second leg 2–0 in Budapest and progressed 4–0 on aggregate, with Fabinho receiving UEFA's Man of the Match award. He said he had missed midfield, while crediting the pressing and communication of the team around him for the performance.

Ozan Kabak and Nathaniel Phillips played in central defence that night. Klopp explained that restoring Fabinho also allowed Wijnaldum to play further forward and helped Thiago Alcântara. He resisted treating one player's return as a complete cure for Liverpool's problems, but the benefits of a more familiar arrangement were evident. The league season eventually ended in third place, with Champions League qualification recovered after a difficult title defence.

## More goals, two cups and another near miss

In 2021–22, Fabinho contributed eight goals in 48 appearances, his highest scoring total for Liverpool in a season. An increased presence in the penalty area at set-pieces helped. In February, he explained that he was usually stationed further back to provide protection, but now had more opportunities to seek out loose balls near goal. His contribution changed without displacing the underlying defensive role.

The League Cup final supplied another memorable finish. Fabinho was Liverpool's second taker in the shoot-out against Chelsea and converted with a Panenka, chipping his penalty as the side went on to win 11–10. Afterwards he recalled practising the attempt with Luis Díaz the day before. A midfielder whose normal work required careful management of risk had chosen a conspicuous one on a major occasion and executed it successfully.

He also scored the goal that began Liverpool's recovery in the Champions League semi-final return at Villarreal on 3 May. The hosts had wiped out the first-leg advantage by leading 2–0 at half-time. Salah found Fabinho on the right side of the penalty area, and his low shot went through Gerónimo Rulli to restore Liverpool's aggregate lead. Díaz and Mané added further goals: a 3–2 win on the night secured progress by 5–2 overall.

An injury at Aston Villa then kept Fabinho out of the FA Cup final. Liverpool won that competition, adding it to the League Cup, but his part in the cup-winning season did not include playing in both finals. He returned to start against Real Madrid in the Champions League final on 28 May. Liverpool lost 1–0 in Paris and were also league runners-up. The pursuit of four trophies ended with two, a remarkable campaign containing real disappointments.

## A difficult final season

The following season revealed a less secure Liverpool and a less effective Fabinho. Opta's comparison of his 2022–23 performance with the championship season recorded fewer tackles, passes, dribbles and aerial duels per 90 minutes. Those measures do not explain every change in a team, but they provide evidence that his contribution had diminished from the level reached during the title campaign.

There was improvement late in the season, including after Alexander-Arnold began moving into midfield alongside him. It helped Liverpool finish more strongly, although they remained fifth and missed Champions League qualification. In May, Fabinho acknowledged the uneven campaign while looking towards returning for the next pre-season. At that point, his public account was of preparing to improve with Liverpool.

Instead, a permanent transfer to Al-Ittihad was completed on 31 July 2023. He left at 29, after five seasons in which his position had been central to the team's identity. The move ended a Liverpool career containing both exceptional achievement and a final campaign that had fallen short of the standards he and the side had established.

Klopp's farewell recognised the midfielder's willingness to provide the protection that let others play expansively. That is the strongest account of Fabinho's legacy: his influence extended to the options available to teammates. The long-range goals and chipped penalty remain clear memories, but his greatest Liverpool teams also depended on the presence behind those moments, ready to regain the ball and keep the whole side moving.

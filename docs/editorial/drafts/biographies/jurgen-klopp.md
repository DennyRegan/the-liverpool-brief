# Jürgen Klopp: rebuilding Liverpool as champions

Liverpool's return to the league championship in 2020 came after a season in which they had taken 97 points and still finished second. That sequence captures something essential about Jürgen Klopp's management. His team had reached an extraordinary standard without winning the title; the response was to improve again. A year later, Liverpool were champions with seven matches still to play.

The league title ended a wait stretching back to 1990. It followed the European Cup won in Madrid in 2019 and formed the centre of a career at Anfield that brought seven major trophies. Klopp also won the Community Shield. His Liverpool teams reached three Champions League finals, twice won the League Cup and added an FA Cup, a UEFA Super Cup and the club's first FIFA Club World Cup.

Those honours came through several distinct stages: an uneven beginning, the construction of a more complete side, two seasons at the summit, an injury-disrupted title defence, another challenge on every front and a final rebuilding. Klopp's Liverpool career was long enough to contain each of them. He arrived in October 2015 and managed his last match in May 2024.

## From Mainz and Dortmund to Anfield

Born in Stuttgart in 1967, Klopp had already spent many years building teams before he came to England. His professional playing career centred on Mainz, where he moved into management in 2001 after 11 years as a player. He took the club into the Bundesliga for the first time. Mainz subsequently returned to the second tier, but his work there established the basis of a managerial career that continued at Borussia Dortmund in 2008.

Dortmund won successive Bundesliga championships under him and completed a league and German Cup double in 2011–12. In 2013 they reached the Champions League final, losing to Bayern Munich. The pressing and attacking football of those teams gave Liverpool a substantial body of work to assess when they appointed him on 8 October 2015.

He was succeeding Brendan Rodgers with Liverpool tenth in the Premier League. There was no immediate transformation of their league position: they finished eighth that season. Nor did the two cup finals they reached produce a trophy. Manchester City beat them on penalties after a 1–1 draw in the League Cup final; Sevilla won the Europa League final 3–1 in Basel.

The route to Basel nevertheless supplied an early demonstration of what Klopp's side could do. Against his former club Dortmund in April 2016, Liverpool fell 2–0 and then 3–1 behind at Anfield. Philippe Coutinho and Mamadou Sakho brought them level before Dejan Lovren headed the late winner. A 4–3 victory on the night took Liverpool through 5–4 on aggregate.

That game should not obscure the limitations of the first season. Liverpool's recovery was remarkable, but Dortmund had punished errors to establish their lead. In Basel, Liverpool led at half-time and then lost control. Klopp's own assessment after the final concentrated on his side's response to Sevilla's equaliser and his responsibility to help them react better. The promise and the work still required were both visible.

His response also set out a practical consequence of the defeat: Liverpool would have no European competition in 2016–17, leaving more time to train. He spoke about using that time and the experience of losing. The subsequent improvement was not simply an emotional reaction to a final; it required sustained work and changes to the squad.

Sadio Mané, Joël Matip and Georginio Wijnaldum joined in the summer of 2016. Liverpool finished fourth in Klopp's first full league campaign and returned to the Champions League. It was a significant step, although the season had again exposed their inconsistency. They had been second at the turn of the year; fourth place brought progress without concealing how much remained between them and a championship.

## Making the team more complete

Mohamed Salah's arrival in 2017 helped establish the attacking combination most closely associated with Klopp's Liverpool. Salah and Mané played alongside Roberto Firmino in a front three that could attack rapidly and press opponents before they had time to settle. The addition of pace and direct running changed what Liverpool could do when possession turned over.

Firmino's importance lay partly in how he connected the other forwards. Liverpool's attack was not arranged around a centre-forward waiting for service while everyone else worked. Its effectiveness depended on movement, combination and the willingness of the front line to begin the team's defensive work. Mané and Salah could move towards goal from wider starting positions while Firmino helped link the play.

The shape was supported by changes behind them. Andy Robertson arrived in 2017, and Trent Alexander-Arnold developed from the Academy into a first-team regular. Their ability to supply the attack from full-back became central to Liverpool's football. Width did not have to come entirely from the forwards; the full-backs could advance and deliver while the attackers occupied more central positions.

Klopp's methods developed with the players available. Pressing remained fundamental, but his best side could also keep opponents pinned back, change the direction of an attack with a long pass and create chances from carefully delivered crosses. The work of assistants including Peter Krawietz and Pepijn Lijnders was part of that development. By the championship season, Liverpool's game offered more routes to goal than the rapid breaks that had initially attracted so much attention.

The improvement at the other end was equally important. Virgil van Dijk joined in January 2018; Alisson Becker and Fabinho followed that summer. Liverpool conceded 22 Premier League goals in 2018–19. A team whose attacking quality had previously coexisted with defensive uncertainty now had a stronger goalkeeper, a commanding central defender and a specialist presence in front of the defence.

These developments crossed two European campaigns. Liverpool reached the 2018 Champions League final in Kyiv, where Real Madrid won 3–1. Salah went off with a shoulder injury in the first half. Mané equalised after Karim Benzema put Madrid ahead, but substitute Gareth Bale scored twice. Klopp had taken Liverpool to the final of Europe's leading competition, yet the first trophy of his tenure remained out of reach.

The following league season brought another kind of disappointment. Liverpool lost only once and accumulated 97 points. Manchester City finished with 98. Liverpool's sole defeat came at City, who eventually won their final 14 league matches to retain the title. The contest established the standard Klopp's team had reached and the narrow margin separating the two sides.

Alongside that title race, Liverpool were pursuing the European Cup. Their progress in Europe offered another opportunity to turn an exceptional season into a major honour.

## European champions, then English champions

Barcelona appeared to have put that opportunity beyond them by winning the first leg of the Champions League semi-final 3–0. At Anfield on 7 May 2019, Liverpool overturned the deficit with a 4–0 victory. Divock Origi scored first. Wijnaldum, introduced at half-time for the injured Robertson, scored twice in quick succession. Alexander-Arnold's rapidly taken corner then found Origi for the decisive fourth.

The match illustrated the contribution of players beyond the most celebrated attacking trio. Origi supplied two goals, and a midfielder coming from the bench supplied two more. Alisson made important saves before the interval. Liverpool's recovery depended on those interventions as well as the collective pressure they generated. The aggregate score was 4–3, and Klopp had reached a second successive Champions League final with the club.

Tottenham Hotspur were the opponents in Madrid on 1 June. Salah scored an early penalty, and Origi came on to make it 2–0 late in the match. Between the goals, Liverpool had to protect their advantage rather than reproduce the attacking fluency of the semi-final. Alisson denied Tottenham as they threatened an equaliser. The team's capacity to win this different kind of match secured Liverpool's sixth European Cup.

For Klopp, it was the first trophy of his Liverpool tenure, following defeats in his previous three finals with the club. The distinction mattered: a team repeatedly reaching decisive matches had now won the most important one in European club football. The years of improvement had produced an honour to stand alongside the performances.

Two further trophies followed in 2019. Liverpool beat Chelsea on penalties in the UEFA Super Cup in Istanbul. In December they defeated Flamengo 1–0 after extra time in Doha to become FIFA Club World Cup winners for the first time. Firmino scored the final's only goal, completing a year in which Liverpool became European and world champions.

The league campaign running alongside those victories was even more consistent than the previous one. Liverpool won 26 of their first 27 Premier League matches and drew the other. What had been a contest decided by one point in 2019 became a substantial lead in 2020. The team was sustaining its level from one fixture to the next while also adding international honours.

Then the COVID-19 pandemic interrupted the season. The competition resumed behind closed doors, and Liverpool's title was confirmed on 25 June when Chelsea beat Manchester City 2–1. Liverpool had ended their 30-year wait and secured the championship earlier in terms of matches remaining than any previous English top-flight winner.

They finished with 99 points, 18 ahead of City. The championship belonged to a team that had developed attacking speed, defensive security and several ways of creating chances. Klopp's Liverpool could be forceful without depending on an open match, and could win tightly contested games as well as overwhelming opponents. The league table measured the cumulative result of that development.

The circumstances also separated this championship from the celebrations supporters had anticipated. The decisive weeks took place without the normal Anfield crowd. A title pursued for three decades arrived during a public-health emergency, and its place in the club's history includes that interruption as well as the dominance of the team.

## Recovery and another challenge

Liverpool could not sustain those results in 2020–21. Long-term injuries to Van Dijk and Joe Gomez were followed by the loss of Matip for the remainder of the season. Klopp had to manage a defence without the established centre-backs who had been so important to the preceding success.

The season nevertheless ended with Champions League qualification. Liverpool went unbeaten through their final ten league matches and beat Crystal Palace 2–0 on the last day to finish third. Mané scored both goals in front of 10,000 supporters at Anfield. The result was a recovery within a difficult campaign, preserving their place in Europe's leading competition even though the title defence had fallen short.

In 2021–22 they competed for the major honours again. Liverpool played 63 competitive matches, reaching the final of every cup competition they entered. They scored 147 goals across the season. The schedule stretched to the maximum available fixtures, with the league championship still being contested on its final afternoon.

Chelsea were beaten in both domestic cup finals at Wembley. The League Cup was won 11–10 on penalties after a goalless match, and the FA Cup followed through a 6–5 shoot-out victory after another 0–0 draw. Kostas Tsimikas converted the winning FA Cup penalty. Klopp had added the two principal domestic cups to his Liverpool honours.

The other competitions ended just short of success. A 3–1 victory over Wolverhampton Wanderers on the final league day could not overcome Manchester City's one-point advantage. In the Champions League final, Real Madrid won 1–0 through Vinícius Júnior's goal. Thibaut Courtois repeatedly denied Liverpool, whose third Champions League final under Klopp ended in defeat.

The final in Paris also involved distressing events for supporters outside the stadium. In February 2023, after publication of the independent review, UEFA apologised both for their experiences and for messages that had unjustly blamed Liverpool supporters for the delayed kick-off. Those events form an essential part of the occasion's history, separate from the analysis of the match.

Liverpool's two cups remained substantial achievements in a season of sustained contention. The difference between that outcome and further honours had been small, but it was real. Klopp's record includes the finals and title races lost as well as those won; their repeated presence in those contests demonstrates the level Liverpool maintained.

## The final rebuilding

The Community Shield came in July 2022, when Liverpool beat Manchester City 3–1 at Leicester's King Power Stadium. The league season that followed was much less successful. Liverpool finished fifth and missed Champions League qualification for the first time in Klopp's seven completed full seasons at the club.

The forward line was changing too. Diogo Jota, Luis Díaz and Darwin Núñez had added different attacking options during the later stages of Klopp's tenure. Firmino left in 2023 after eight seasons at Anfield. The combination that had helped establish Liverpool's success could not remain the basis of every subsequent team. Klopp's final phase involved developing a new attack as well as restoring the level of performances behind it.

The midfield underwent extensive change in the summer of 2023. Jordan Henderson and Fabinho departed, and Alexis Mac Allister, Dominik Szoboszlai, Wataru Endo and Ryan Gravenberch arrived. This was a substantial revision of the team rather than the replacement of one missing player. Klopp had to establish new relationships in the area connecting his defence and attack while continuing to compete.

On 26 January 2024, he announced that he would leave at the end of the season. He had told the club in November. His explanation centred on declining energy and his inability to keep doing the job indefinitely. The decision was presented while the season still had months to run, with time for Liverpool to prepare a managerial transition.

There was one more trophy. On 25 February, Liverpool beat Chelsea 1–0 after extra time in the League Cup final. Van Dijk headed the winner in the 118th minute. Klopp's team finished with young substitutes Bobby Clark, James McConnell, Jayden Danns and Jarell Quansah on the pitch alongside senior players whose contributions remained decisive.

The composition of that side gave the victory a particular place in his last season. Caoimhín Kelleher's goalkeeping helped Liverpool remain in the match, and Van Dijk supplied its winning moment, but inexperienced players also carried responsibility through extra time. The final connected the established team with the developing squad Klopp would leave behind.

Liverpool finished third in the Premier League. His last match, on 19 May 2024, was a 2–0 home victory over Wolves, with Mac Allister and Quansah scoring. It was his 491st game in charge. The occasion brought an extended farewell from the Anfield crowd, ending almost nine years in which the manager's relationship with supporters had become a conspicuous part of the club's life.

That connection had received formal recognition before his departure. In 2022 Klopp was awarded the Freedom of the City of Liverpool. The council's account recognised his charitable and community work as well as the team's achievements. Football supplied the setting for his public role, but the honour recorded a contribution beyond match results.

Klopp left a record of seven major trophies across six different competitions, with the 2022 Community Shield counted separately. Liverpool had scored 1,035 goals during his 491 matches. More revealing than any single total was the sequence of teams and responses behind it: learning after the early final defeats, adding defensive strength to an exciting attack, improving after 97 points proved insufficient, recovering from injuries and renewing the midfield.

His Liverpool career ended with Champions League football secured for the next season and another cup added to the club's record. The European Cup of 2019 and the championship of 2020 remain its defining achievements. They were the outcome of a rebuilding sustained long enough to turn a promising side into a winning one, and then to demand that it win again.

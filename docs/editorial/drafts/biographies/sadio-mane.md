# Sadio Mané

Sadio Mané helped change what Liverpool could expect from themselves. Signed in 2016, he was central to their return to the Champions League, their victory in that competition and the league title that followed. Across six seasons, his contribution extended from goals in European knockout ties to the defensive work that made Jürgen Klopp’s attack function. He left with 120 goals in 269 competitive appearances and six major honours.

Born in Senegal on 10 April 1992, Mané reached European football with Metz before moving to Red Bull Salzburg, where he won the Austrian league and cup double in 2013–14. Southampton brought him to England in 2014. On 16 May 2015, he scored three times against Aston Villa in two minutes and 56 seconds, setting a Premier League hat-trick record. Liverpool had their own experience of his threat: his two goals helped Southampton overturn a two-goal deficit to win 3–2 in March 2016.

He joined Liverpool on 28 June that year, ahead of Klopp’s first full season as manager. His competitive debut brought a goal in a 4–3 victory at Arsenal. In December, his stoppage-time winner at Goodison Park settled the Merseyside derby. Although injury ended his campaign early, he scored 13 league goals and won Liverpool’s Player of the Season award as the club finished fourth.

Mané initially played on the right. Mohamed Salah’s arrival in 2017 moved him to the left, with Roberto Firmino completing the front three. The change suited a forward comfortable in several positions. In a November 2017 interview, Mané explained that his responsibilities remained the same wherever he played: offering a passing option, attacking directly, defending and helping Liverpool move the ball quickly.

That defensive contribution mattered. Assistant coach Peter Krawietz later highlighted Mané’s positioning, willingness to learn and ability to combine speed with strength. He could beat a defender, finish with either foot and compete in the air. His work also helped Liverpool recover possession, allowing the attack to begin again before opponents could settle.

There were setbacks. A red card at Manchester City brought a three-match suspension early in 2017–18. But his first Champions League campaign with Liverpool produced ten goals, including a hat-trick in the 5–0 away victory over Porto. He also scored Liverpool’s goal in the 3–1 final defeat by Real Madrid in Kyiv.

The following season brought his most productive league campaign. His 22 goals earned a share of the Premier League Golden Boot alongside Salah and Arsenal’s Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang. None of Mané’s league goals came from penalties.

His performance in Munich in March 2019 demonstrated the range of his finishing. Taking down Virgil van Dijk’s long pass, he wrong-footed Manuel Neuer and clipped Liverpool ahead. Later, he headed in Salah’s cross to complete a 3–1 victory over Bayern. UEFA named him man of the match as Liverpool reached the quarter-finals.

His contribution could be just as substantial without a goal. Recalling the 4–0 semi-final victory over Barcelona in a 2021 interview, Krawietz singled out Mané’s defending and dribbling as exceptional. It was a coach’s assessment, grounded in the work around the goals scored by others. In the final against Tottenham, Mané’s attempted pass struck Moussa Sissoko’s arm, bringing the early penalty from which Salah scored. This time Liverpool finished as European champions.

Mané remained decisive during the 2019–20 title campaign. He scored both Liverpool goals in the UEFA Super Cup draw with Chelsea, which Liverpool won on penalties, and his stoppage-time header secured a 2–1 comeback victory at Aston Villa in November. He finished the league season with 18 goals as Liverpool ended their 30-year wait for the championship. The Club World Cup completed a season in which they won three trophies.

The next year was harder. His league total fell to 11, and Liverpool struggled through a title defence disrupted by injuries. Mané’s own output no longer matched the preceding two seasons. Yet he scored both goals in the final-day victory over Crystal Palace, helping secure third place and another Champions League campaign. Even in a diminished season, he made an important contribution to its recovery.

In 2021–22, a move into the centre of the attack gave his final Liverpool season a different shape. The FA Cup semi-final against Manchester City showed how effective he remained. He closed down Zack Steffen and slid in to turn the goalkeeper’s delayed clearance into a goal, then volleyed past him before half-time. Those two finishes helped Liverpool win 3–2: one came from pressure without the ball, the other from the quality of his striking.

He finished the campaign with 23 goals in all competitions and added the League Cup and FA Cup to his honours. Liverpool also reached another Champions League final and narrowly missed the league title. His last appearance came against Real Madrid in the European final on 28 May 2022; his transfer to Bayern Munich was completed on 22 June.

That final Liverpool season also brought a defining achievement for Senegal. In February 2022, Mané recovered from having a penalty saved during the Africa Cup of Nations final against Egypt to score the winning kick in the shootout. Senegal were African champions for the first time.

His subsequent year at Bayern yielded 12 goals in 38 competitive appearances, a Bundesliga title and the German Super Cup. Injury interrupted the season and ruled him out of the 2022 World Cup. He joined Al Nassr in August 2023 and remained with the Saudi club at this biography’s research cut-off in September 2026.

At Liverpool, his record was 90 goals in 196 league appearances, within that overall total of 120 in 269. But the defining feature of those six years was how often he supplied what the team needed: direct running from either flank, goals through the middle and the effort to win possession back. His changing position never reduced the responsibility he carried in Klopp’s side.

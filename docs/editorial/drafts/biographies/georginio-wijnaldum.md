# Georginio Wijnaldum: the midfielder who made Liverpool's progress possible

With the first half almost over against Middlesbrough on the last day of the 2016–17 season, Georginio Wijnaldum found the opening Liverpool needed. Nathaniel Clyne passed to Roberto Firmino, Firmino released the midfielder and Wijnaldum's touch carried him through before he drove the ball inside the near post. Liverpool had struggled to break through. Now their route back towards the Champions League was clearing.

The eventual 3–0 victory secured fourth place and a place in the following season's qualifiers. Two seasons later Liverpool would be European champions. Wijnaldum would provide far more spectacular evidence of his importance along that journey, but this first-season goal captured something central to his career: a player doing the work in midfield could still recognise the moment to arrive beyond it.

He stayed for five seasons, making 237 appearances and scoring 22 goals. The Champions League, Premier League, UEFA Super Cup and FIFA Club World Cup all followed. Those achievements depended on the exceptional attackers and defenders around him, and on the less conspicuous judgement with which he helped connect them. His Liverpool story was one of learning what a team required and retaining the ability to do something decisive himself.

## An experienced arrival with room to grow

Born in Rotterdam on 11 November 1990, Wijnaldum had reached Feyenoord's first team at 16. He moved to PSV Eindhoven in 2011, became captain and led the side to the Dutch league title in 2014–15, winning the Dutch Footballer of the Year award. His arrival in England with Newcastle United brought 11 goals in 38 Premier League appearances, but also relegation. Personal productivity had not been enough to secure the team's survival.

Liverpool completed his signing on 22 July 2016, when he was 25. He joined a summer intake that included Sadio Mané and Joël Matip as Jürgen Klopp prepared for his first full season at the club. Klopp's interest was in what Wijnaldum might still become. At the announcement, the manager stressed his tactical flexibility, experience and willingness to improve rather than presenting him as a finished solution.

Wijnaldum's versatility had sometimes complicated his own sense of where he belonged. In a later Liverpool interview, he remembered coaches encouraging him to concentrate on playing on the wing while he remained convinced he was a midfielder. Those assignments gave him valuable first-team football, but central midfield was the position he had wanted to reach. At Liverpool he would establish himself there through responsibilities different from the attacking role his Newcastle goal total might have suggested.

His competitive debut came in the 4–3 win at Arsenal on 14 August 2016. He started and supplied the pass for Adam Lallana's goal early in the second half. His own first goal arrived as a substitute in the 6–1 home victory over Watford on 6 November. The opening months already offered different kinds of contribution: making an attack possible, supporting the collective work and eventually finishing one.

## Goals that advanced the team

On New Year's Eve, Wijnaldum headed Liverpool to a 1–0 victory over Manchester City at Anfield. Lallana carried the ball down the left and crossed; Wijnaldum arrived in the area to beat Claudio Bravo after eight minutes. Liverpool then had to defend their advantage against Pep Guardiola's side. The result kept them second in the table and showed that an early attacking intervention could be sustained by the work that followed it.

The Middlesbrough goal in May had a different significance. Liverpool needed to secure their return to Europe's leading competition, and the breakthrough came before the interval could prolong an increasingly uncomfortable wait. The fourth-place finish was a staging post rather than a trophy. Wijnaldum had helped give Klopp's developing team the opportunity to test itself at a higher level.

He made another important intervention in the next season's Champions League semi-final. Starting the return against Roma in place of the injured Alex Oxlade-Chamberlain, he headed Liverpool's second goal at the Stadio Olimpico on 2 May 2018. Liverpool lost 4–2 on the night but progressed 7–6 on aggregate. His goal mattered within a difficult match, in which the lead had to be protected as well as extended.

The final brought defeat. Wijnaldum started against Real Madrid in Kyiv as Liverpool lost 3–1. The European campaign had taken the team further than the previous season's immediate objective, without delivering the prize. His place in that progress was already substantial before the more famous semi-final contribution still to come.

## Learning when to stay

The midfielder who could time a run into the penalty area also had to learn when to resist making it. By August 2018, Klopp was using Wijnaldum at the base of midfield. In the opening league victories over West Ham United and Crystal Palace, James Milner and Naby Keïta played ahead of him, with greater freedom to advance.

Wijnaldum explained that Klopp wanted him more involved in matches and able to fulfil different midfield positions. He had been thinking principally as a No.8 while the manager was also developing him as a No.6. The adjustment required attention to the players around him: if both midfield partners went forward, he could not simply follow them. His own attacking instinct had to be balanced against the space left behind.

That account helps explain his value beyond goals. Retaining the ball under pressure could give teammates time to move; staying in position could let another player take a risk. His control and strength in possession were useful because they served a wider arrangement. Liverpool did not need every midfielder to attempt the same pass or make the same run at once.

Nor did versatility mean every assignment was immediately comfortable. With Firmino only fit enough for the bench at Barcelona in May 2019, Klopp used Wijnaldum in an advanced role alongside Mané and Mohamed Salah. The manager wanted attacking involvement combined with defensive work and the ability to hold possession between the lines. Wijnaldum subsequently spoke of a new appreciation for the demands Firmino met across a season.

Liverpool lost that first leg 3–0. Wijnaldum could recognise encouraging elements in the performance without confusing them with the result. In his interview before the return, he acknowledged both the team's development and the scale of the task still facing them. Progress did not cancel the goals conceded or provide the away goal they had failed to score.

## Two minutes that changed a semi-final

On 7 May 2019, Wijnaldum began the return against Barcelona on the bench. Liverpool were without Salah and Firmino, but Divock Origi's early goal gave them a foothold. Andy Robertson's injury forced a change at half-time: Milner moved to left-back and Wijnaldum entered midfield.

He scored twice in 122 seconds. First, Trent Alexander-Arnold recovered possession on the right and delivered a low cross which Wijnaldum met with a shot that got through Marc-André ter Stegen. Then Xherdan Shaqiri crossed from the other flank and Wijnaldum directed a powerful header into the top corner. The three-goal first-leg deficit had disappeared.

Origi's second goal, from Alexander-Arnold's quickly taken corner, completed a 4–0 win and sent Liverpool to another final. Wijnaldum's part was extraordinary in its concentration: a half-time substitute had changed the entire tie before the hour. The finishes also belonged to abilities demonstrated earlier, from the well-timed arrival against City to the European header in Rome. The occasion magnified qualities already present.

In Madrid, Wijnaldum started with Fabinho and Jordan Henderson in midfield as Liverpool beat Tottenham Hotspur 2–0. Milner replaced him around the hour, and the team completed the victory. A career that had begun at Anfield with the effort to reach the Champions League now included winning it. The season also contained the frustration of a 97-point league campaign that still left Liverpool behind Manchester City.

## A champion through sustained work

Liverpool's next league campaign demanded a different form of excellence: maintaining results week after week. Wijnaldum appeared in 37 of their 38 league matches in 2019–20, starting 35 and playing 2,948 minutes. He played more league minutes than any other Liverpool midfielder. Availability was part of his contribution, giving Klopp a familiar option as the team pursued the championship.

His four league goals included the winner at Sheffield United, the final goal in a 5–2 derby victory over Everton and the opener against West Ham United. He also scored against Chelsea on the night Liverpool received the trophy. These were occasional interventions within a season defined by much more frequent work without the final touch. Liverpool finished with 99 points and ended their 30-year wait for the league title; the UEFA Super Cup and Club World Cup had been won along the way.

Klopp's assessment during the campaign made the breadth of Wijnaldum's role clear. In October 2019, he praised the midfielder's contribution in both directions, his ability in tight spaces and larger ones, his challenges and his aerial timing. It was a manager's judgement of a player whose usefulness could survive changes in the match around him. Wijnaldum did not have to dominate the highlights to remain central to selection.

## An unsettled ending, and a lasting contribution

The final season was harder. Liverpool's title defence unfolded largely without supporters in the grounds, amid long-term injuries and severe disruption. Wijnaldum played in every league match, starting 34. The team could not repeat its championship, but a late recovery brought third place and Champions League qualification. His reliability remained useful when so much else about Liverpool's season had become uncertain.

He captained the side in the final-day 2–0 win over Crystal Palace on 23 May 2021. With supporters back at Anfield, a sequence of one-touch passes involving him and Thiago Alcântara drew appreciation early in the second half. Later came the standing ovation as he was replaced, followed by a guard of honour from teammates and staff. It was a farewell to five seasons of work, beyond the spectacular moments everyone could readily recall.

Wijnaldum's contract expired at the end of June and his move to Paris Saint-Germain was announced on 10 June. Klopp's tribute praised both his football intelligence and his place within the group. Wijnaldum's own final-day interview emphasised relationships with teammates, staff and the club, as well as the trophies. He regretted leaving without another prize but valued helping Liverpool finish in a position to return to the Champions League.

After leaving, Wijnaldum distinguished the stadium's support from online criticism, which he said had hurt him. His account contained affection and disappointment together.

The football record is clear. Across five seasons he helped Liverpool advance from fourth-place qualification to successive European finals and then an English championship. His goals supplied important turns in that story, but the story also required a midfielder who could keep possession, accept different responsibilities and decide when another teammate should be the one to move forward. Wijnaldum's place in Liverpool's successful team was built through those decisions, then illuminated by the occasions when he stepped beyond them to score.

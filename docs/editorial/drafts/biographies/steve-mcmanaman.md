# Steve McManaman

Steve McManaman had a direct hand in both trophies Liverpool won during his first-team career. In the 1992 FA Cup final, he created the opening goal against Sunderland. Three years later, he scored twice against Bolton Wanderers to win the League Cup. For a player whose time at Anfield is often discussed through the success that escaped his team, those contributions deserve to come first.

Across 364 competitive appearances, McManaman scored 66 goals. His greater value lay in how he advanced an attack: receiving the ball, beating an opponent and carrying it into space that had not been available a moment earlier. He could change direction without surrendering control, and his stamina allowed him to keep making those runs. Liverpool’s football under Roy Evans depended heavily on that ability.

Born in Liverpool on 11 February 1972, McManaman grew up supporting Everton before entering Liverpool’s youth system. Kenny Dalglish gave him his senior debut on 15 December 1990, sending him on for Peter Beardsley in a 2–0 victory over Sheffield United at Anfield. He was eighteen.

His breakthrough came under Graeme Souness the following season. McManaman made 51 appearances and scored eleven goals in 1991–92, establishing himself while still a teenager. His first senior goal arrived at Manchester City on 21 August 1991, although Liverpool lost 2–1. By the end of that campaign, he was playing a central part in winning a major final.

A cartilage injury had kept him out for a month before the FA Cup final on 9 May 1992. John Barnes was unavailable, and McManaman returned against Sunderland. After a goalless first half, his move to the right helped Liverpool take control. Two minutes after the restart, he supplied Michael Thomas for the opening goal. Ian Rush added the second, and McManaman was named man of the match.

The performance mattered beyond the medal. At twenty, and returning from injury, he had helped decide the most important game of Liverpool’s season. The promise of his first full campaign already had a substantial achievement attached to it.

Progress was less straightforward afterwards. His scoring fell to just two goals in 1993–94, both in the same match against Swindon Town. For all his ability to beat defenders, the final return could be uneven. Evans’s arrival in 1994 brought greater freedom to move away from the wing, allowing McManaman to find the ball across the pitch and attack from different positions.

The clearest reward came at Wembley on 2 April 1995. Against Bolton in the League Cup final, McManaman scored both Liverpool goals in a 2–1 victory. For the first, he took a pass from Barnes and worked his way past defenders before finishing. For the second, he moved in from the left, carried the ball across challenges and placed a low shot into the far corner. Alan Thompson replied for Bolton, but Liverpool held on.

Both goals showed why the freedom suited him. McManaman could begin an attack away from goal, draw opponents towards him and still supply the finish himself. In this final, his dribbling produced the two goals that won the cup.

Alongside Robbie Fowler, he became one of the principal attacking players in Evans’s side. Liverpool’s own retrospective credits him with ten goals and twenty assists in 1995–96. Yet that season also brought a different Wembley experience. He played in the FA Cup final defeat by Manchester United, settled by Eric Cantona’s late goal. Liverpool’s attacking talent had not secured another trophy, and McManaman never won the league at Anfield.

His individual contribution continued. At Celtic Park on 16 September 1997, Liverpool were trailing 2–1 late in a UEFA Cup tie when he carried the ball from his own half and finished left-footed from the edge of the penalty area. The equaliser secured a 2–2 draw. Contemporary reporting also recorded his earlier hesitation when presented with a good scoring opportunity: the same match contained both the spectacular finish and the less decisive moment. He ended 1997–98 with twelve goals in all competitions, his highest Liverpool total for a season.

His departure followed in 1999. In January, McManaman confirmed that he would join Real Madrid when his contract expired that summer. Liverpool would receive no transfer fee. In his statement at the time, he explained that the move offered the chance to test himself in another leading European league. The free transfer frustrated supporters, but his stated ambition was clear.

Madrid brought the honours that had been scarce at Liverpool. During four seasons in Spain, he won two Champions Leagues and two league titles, scoring with a volley against Valencia in the 2000 European final. He returned to England with Manchester City in 2003, making 44 appearances over two seasons.

McManaman’s Liverpool career ended with two major trophies and no championship. That limits any account of the team’s achievement, but his part in those cup wins was considerable. He created the breakthrough in one final and scored both goals in the other. Across nine seasons of first-team football, his running with the ball gave Liverpool a way through defences; at Wembley, it helped put silverware in their hands.

# Ian Callaghan: Liverpool’s constant through an age of change

Ian Callaghan made his Liverpool debut in the Second Division and was still in the side when the club won its first European Cup seventeen years later. Across 857 competitive appearances, he helped Bill Shankly build a championship team, adapted when that team was rebuilt and remained important under Bob Paisley. His record captures both the length of his service and his ability to keep earning a place as Liverpool’s standards rose.

Born in Toxteth on 10 April 1942, Callaghan joined Liverpool in 1957 and turned professional in March 1960. On 16 April, six days after his eighteenth birthday, he made his first-team debut in a 4–0 home victory over Bristol Rovers. He had been given the right-wing position associated with Billy Liddell, the player he had admired as a boy.

A regular place took longer. Kevin Lewis’s arrival restricted his opportunities, and Callaghan became established during the promotion season of 1961–62. He made 23 league appearances as Liverpool won the Second Division title. Two seasons later, he played all 42 league matches as Shankly’s side became champions of England. He was ever-present again when they regained the title in 1965–66.

Callaghan gave that first Shankly team speed and direct running on the right. His crossing supplied Roger Hunt and Ian St John, while his stamina allowed him to sustain his involvement throughout matches. Peter Thompson provided the width on the opposite flank. Callaghan’s contribution was often the delivery that allowed a forward to finish, and his most significant cross came at Wembley.

On 1 May 1965, Liverpool and Leeds United were level at 1–1 in extra time of the FA Cup final. Hunt’s opening goal had been answered by Billy Bremner. Callaghan crossed from the right and St John headed the winner in the 111th minute. Liverpool had won the competition for the first time. In a career containing five league championships and European success, Callaghan later identified that afternoon as his most unforgettable footballing memory.

His international career was much less substantial. He won four England caps, the first against Finland in June 1966. His second came in the 2–0 World Cup group victory over France. He did not play in the knockout rounds, although he remained part of the victorious squad. More than eleven years passed before his next appearance, against Switzerland in September 1977. A final cap against Luxembourg followed the next month.

At Liverpool, the greater threat to his career came in 1970–71, when a knee operation interrupted his season. Brian Hall took his place on the right. With Shankly changing the personnel of his side, Callaghan faced the loss of a position he had occupied for most of the previous decade.

Shankly brought him back in central midfield. The move gave Liverpool another use for his energy and reading of the game, and extended his first-team career well into his thirties. He no longer depended on beating a full-back and delivering from the touchline. His work now belonged in the centre of a team being built around a different generation of players.

The scale of his involvement is clear in the appearance records. In 1972–73, when Liverpool won the league and UEFA Cup, Callaghan played 66 competitive matches, including every league fixture. He followed that with 61 appearances in 1973–74, again without missing a league game. These were among the busiest seasons of his career, after the injury and change of position.

In 1974 he became the first Liverpool player to receive the Football Writers’ Association Footballer of the Year award. That May brought another FA Cup, secured with a 3–0 victory over Newcastle United. Nine years after providing the winning cross against Leeds, he was still part of Shankly’s cup-winning side, now performing a different job.

Paisley’s succession brought further league titles in 1976 and 1977, and another UEFA Cup in 1976. There were disappointments too. Callaghan appeared in four FA Cup finals for Liverpool and lost two: against Arsenal in 1971 and Manchester United in 1977. In the latter he came off the bench for David Johnson as Liverpool lost 2–1.

Four days later, on 25 May 1977, he started the European Cup final against Borussia Mönchengladbach in Rome. Liverpool won 3–1. At 35, Callaghan played the full match alongside team-mates including Ray Kennedy, Jimmy Case and Terry McDermott. He was the only member of that winning side who had also played in Liverpool’s first European fixture, against KR Reykjavik in 1964.

His place eventually passed to another generation. Graeme Souness displaced him during his final season, and Callaghan’s last Liverpool appearance came against Mönchengladbach on 29 March 1978. He was an unused substitute when Liverpool retained the European Cup against Club Brugge at Wembley. His second European Cup therefore belonged to a campaign in which he had contributed, but a final in which he did not play.

After a loan spell with Fort Lauderdale Strikers, he joined John Toshack’s Swansea City in September 1978 and helped them win promotion from the Third Division. In a 1979 interview, Callaghan recalled that Paisley had offered him an improved contract to stay at Liverpool, but he wanted regular football. He later played for Cork United and Crewe Alexandra before retiring in 1982.

Callaghan finished with 640 league appearances and 49 league goals for Liverpool; his totals across competitive football were 857 and 68. The appearance record remains his, but the changing work behind it matters as much. He supplied the cross for Liverpool’s first FA Cup, rebuilt his career in midfield and was still trusted to start their first European Cup final. His longevity was founded on continuing usefulness to two demanding managers.

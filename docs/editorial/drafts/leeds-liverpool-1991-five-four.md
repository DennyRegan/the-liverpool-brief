# Barnes’s fifth proves decisive as Liverpool survive Leeds fightback

Liverpool scored four times in the opening 28 minutes at Elland Road on 13 April 1991, then needed a fifth goal to secure a 5–4 victory over Leeds United. John Barnes scored twice for Ronnie Moran’s side, while Lee Chapman’s second-half hat-trick brought Leeds within one goal of a remarkable recovery.

Ray Houghton opened the scoring in the 11th minute. Jan Mølby converted a penalty five minutes later after John Lukic brought down Ian Rush, and David Speedie added Liverpool’s third. Houghton and Speedie had both found space at the far post as Liverpool repeatedly broke through Leeds’ defence.

Barnes made it 4–0 after exchanging passes with Rush. The fourth goal arrived before the half-hour, and Liverpool carried that lead into the interval. Four different scorers had contributed to an opening spell that left Howard Wilkinson’s team facing a substantial deficit.

Leeds did not score until Chapman struck in the 68th minute. Carl Shutt then reduced the gap to two with 13 minutes remaining. What had seemed a settled contest had become a test of Liverpool’s ability to protect their lead.

Barnes intervened again in the 79th minute, restoring a three-goal cushion at 5–2. That goal became decisive. Chapman headed in twice more, completing his hat-trick and taking Leeds to 4–5 before time ran out.

Liverpool had conceded four second-half goals but kept all three points. Barnes’s second was their only goal after the interval, separating the teams at the end of a match in which both had produced four-goal spells.

The victory ended a three-match league sequence without a win, following defeats by Queens Park Rangers and Southampton and a draw with Coventry City. Liverpool remained second in the First Division, with Leeds fourth, after an afternoon whose final score scarcely resembled the position at half-time.

Fernando Torres spent three and a half seasons at Liverpool, scoring 81 goals in 142 competitive appearances. His partnership with Steven Gerrard helped Rafael Benítez’s side reach a Champions League semi-final and challenge for the Premier League title. Injuries interrupted that progress, and his transfer to Chelsea in January 2011 brought an abrupt end to his Liverpool career.

Torres arrived from Atlético Madrid in July 2007, aged 23, for a club-record fee of approximately £20 million. He had joined Atlético’s academy at 11 and made his first-team debut in May 2001; by 19 he was captain. Liverpool were acquiring an established senior player, although English football was new to him. He took the number nine shirt previously worn by Robbie Fowler, then made his competitive debut at Aston Villa on 11 August.

His first Liverpool goal followed eight days later against Chelsea at Anfield. Gerrard supplied the pass, Torres supplied the acceleration, and a 1–1 draw provided an early example of their effectiveness together. By March 2008, Gerrard was playing just behind him, with Xabi Alonso and Javier Mascherano deeper in midfield. Torres’s movement gave the captain a target beyond the defence; Gerrard could release him or advance into scoring positions himself. Against Newcastle that month, in a 3–0 victory, each created a goal for the other.

There was more to Torres than speed into open space. At San Siro on 11 March, he controlled Fábio Aurélio’s delivery near the edge of the penalty area, turned and beat Júlio César. It was the only goal of the second leg against Inter and completed a 3–0 aggregate victory in the Champions League’s last 16. The sequence depended on a clean first touch and a quick finish. Liverpool had a centre-forward capable of making a chance count in several different ways.

His first season produced 33 goals in all competitions, including 24 in the Premier League. Successive home league appearances against Middlesbrough and West Ham brought hat-tricks. He also scored against Arsenal and Chelsea in the Champions League knockout rounds, although Liverpool’s European run ended in the semi-finals. That summer brought a trophy with Spain: Torres scored the only goal against Germany in the Euro 2008 final. His first year as a Liverpool player had established him in England while delivering a decisive contribution on the international stage.

The following season offered Liverpool their strongest league challenge of his time at the club, but hamstring injuries limited his involvement. He finished with 17 goals in 38 appearances across all competitions, 14 of them in the league. Torres remained an important scorer, but Liverpool had fewer opportunities to use the attacking partnership that had developed so quickly in his first season.

March 2009 demonstrated what that attack could achieve. Torres opened the scoring against Real Madrid at Anfield on 10 March, finishing from Dirk Kuyt’s pass. Gerrard scored twice and Andrea Dossena added the fourth as Liverpool won 4–0, completing a 5–0 aggregate victory. Four days later, Torres equalised at Old Trafford after Nemanja Vidić’s error, and Liverpool went on to beat Manchester United 4–1. Liverpool nevertheless finished the league season second, four points behind United on 86 points.

The contrast between his scoring and his availability became sharper in 2009–10. Torres managed 18 league goals in 22 appearances, but underwent two operations on his right knee. The second, in April, ended his club season and prevented him facing Atlético in the Europa League semi-finals. He had scored twice against Benfica in the previous round. Liverpool finished seventh, and Benítez left that summer. Torres was still producing goals at a high rate when available; sustaining his presence in the side had become a different problem.

He returned from Spain’s 2010 World Cup triumph to a Liverpool team managed by Roy Hodgson. Kenny Dalglish replaced Hodgson in January, but Torres’s time under him was brief. On 28 January 2011, Torres submitted a written transfer request after Chelsea had made a bid. Three days later he joined them for £50 million, then a British transfer record. His final Liverpool return was 65 goals in 102 league appearances, within that overall total of 81 in 142.

At Chelsea, Torres won the Champions League, Europa League and FA Cup, scoring in the Europa League final against Benfica. A spell at AC Milan preceded his return to Atlético, where he was part of the 2018 Europa League-winning side. He finished his playing career with Sagan Tosu in Japan in August 2019. Alongside the 2008 European Championship and 2010 World Cup, his international honours included Euro 2012.

Liverpool remained a significant reference point when Torres looked back. In 2019 he named Gerrard as the best player he had played alongside; later, he expressed regret about the manner of his departure from Anfield. He also returned to Anfield for charity matches, playing again at the ground where his partnership with Gerrard had developed.

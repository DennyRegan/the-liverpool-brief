Everton won the first league Merseyside derby on Saturday 13 October 1894. At Goodison Park, before a recorded crowd of 44,000, Tom McInnes, Alex Latta and Jack Bell scored in a 3–0 victory over Liverpool.

The clubs had emerged from the same dispute only two years earlier. Everton had played at Anfield and won the First Division championship there in 1891. A split with John Houlding, involving disagreements over the ground's rent, led to Everton's departure in 1892. They moved to the site that became Goodison Park. Houlding and his associates formed Liverpool to play at Anfield.

Liverpool's arrival in the First Division brought the neighbours together in the league. They had completed the 1893–94 Second Division programme unbeaten, with 22 wins and six draws. A 2–0 victory over Newton Heath in the subsequent Test match, followed by election to the top division, secured their promotion.

By the time of the derby, the teams' results could hardly have been more different. Everton had won their opening seven league matches. Liverpool were still waiting for their first victory. The Goodison result extended those records: Everton had eight wins from eight, while Liverpool had four draws and five defeats from nine games.

The interest went well beyond those contrasting starts. An account published in the North Wales Chronicle a week afterwards described crowded approaches to Goodison and spectators arriving in trams, cabs and horse-drawn vehicles. Its writer had watched from the press box and reported loud welcomes for both teams. The occasion had attracted a substantial audience before the clubs had established any history of league meetings.

McInnes put Everton ahead early in the match. They led 1–0 at half-time, then added goals through Latta and Bell after the interval. Liverpool could not reply.

The Chronicle's observer thought Liverpool's finishing had failed them. His account described attacking passages involving Jimmy Ross, Neil Kerr and Harry Bradshaw, but judged that promising positions had repeatedly come to nothing.

The result left Everton top of the First Division and Liverpool fourteenth. For the home side, victory over their new league opponents was also another win in an unbroken start to the season. For Liverpool, the wait for a first top-flight success continued.

The return fixture followed five weeks later, at Anfield on 17 November. Liverpool earned a 2–2 draw, with David Hannah and Jimmy Ross scoring. Ross's equaliser came from a late penalty.

Liverpool's UEFA Cup campaign ended in Marseille after an advantage established by Emile Heskey disappeared either side of half-time. Igor Bišćan's dismissal and Didier Drogba's penalty brought the French side back into the tie before Abdoulaye Méïté's header secured a 2–1 victory, 3–2 on aggregate.

The first leg at Anfield had finished 1–1. Liverpool therefore began the return needing to score, and Gérard Houllier started Heskey alongside Michael Owen, with Harry Kewell supporting the attack.

That selection produced an opening in the 15th minute. Kewell won possession from Laurent Batlles, Steven Gerrard supplied the pass and Heskey drove a low shot past Fabien Barthez. Liverpool now led on aggregate and had matched Marseille's away goal.

For a spell they looked capable of extending their advantage. Danny Murphy curled an effort over, while Owen's movement troubled the home defence. Then Batlles sent Steve Marlet through and Bišćan pulled him back. Although Marlet's shot went wide, referee Arturo Daudén Ibáñez awarded a penalty and dismissed the Liverpool defender. Drogba converted before the interval.

Liverpool reorganised with ten men, but Marseille increasingly controlled the second half. There was still a chance for Sami Hyypiä, whose header from Murphy's corner was cleared off the line by Manuel Dos Santos. The next decisive header came at the opposite end: Méïté met Demetrius Ferreira's corner in the 58th minute to beat Dudek.

Another Liverpool goal would have made the aggregate score 3–3 and taken them through on away goals. Heskey found Owen with a low cross, only for Barthez to intervene, and Owen soon departed with a hamstring problem. Milan Baroš replaced him; later, Bruno Cheyrou shot over as Liverpool searched for an equaliser.

Marseille held their lead. Liverpool had already gone out of both domestic cups, so this defeat ended their remaining route to a trophy that season. The immediate task was now their league pursuit of a Champions League qualifying place.

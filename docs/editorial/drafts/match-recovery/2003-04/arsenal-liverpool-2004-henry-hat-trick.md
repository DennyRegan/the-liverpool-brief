Liverpool led Arsenal twice at Highbury and reached half-time 2–1 ahead. Within five minutes of the restart, they were behind. Thierry Henry's hat-trick turned a promising afternoon for Gérard Houllier's side into a 4–2 defeat and restored Arsenal's momentum after their exits from the FA Cup and Champions League.

The visitors were contesting fourth place while Arsenal remained unbeaten in the league. Liverpool began with an early breakthrough from a corner: Steven Gerrard headed Harry Kewell's delivery back across goal and Sami Hyypiä nodded past Jens Lehmann in the fifth minute.

Arsenal equalised after half an hour. Robert Pires released Henry, whose shot went under Jerzy Dudek. Liverpool recovered their advantage before the break through another Gerrard contribution, this time a pass through the defence which Michael Owen converted in the 42nd minute.

Both Liverpool goals had joined a precise final ball to a decisive finish. The second half gave their opponents room to do the same at a much higher tempo. Pires levelled in the 49th minute following a move involving Ashley Cole, Henry and Freddie Ljungberg, escaping Jamie Carragher to finish left-footed.

Henry then carried the ball through Liverpool's defence, passing Dietmar Hamann and Bišćan before working his way beyond Carragher and beating Dudek. Arsenal had transformed the score from 1–2 to 3–2 almost immediately after the interval.

Houllier replaced Emile Heskey with Milan Baroš, but the change did not restore Liverpool's lead or bring an equaliser. Henry completed his hat-trick in the 78th minute, settling a contest in which Liverpool had made their first-half openings count but could not withstand Arsenal's response.

The defeat left Liverpool fourth, on 49 points from 32 matches, with Newcastle United a point behind and holding a game in hand. Arsenal were seven points clear at the top. Liverpool's first half had threatened to deepen the leaders' difficult week; the second left their own Champions League qualification far from secure.

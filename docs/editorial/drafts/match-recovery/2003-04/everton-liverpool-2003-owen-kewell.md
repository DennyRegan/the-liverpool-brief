Liverpool's first league victory of 2003–04 came at the ground where a fourth successive derby win offered Gérard Houllier some relief. Michael Owen scored twice at Goodison Park before Harry Kewell completed a 3–0 victory with his first goal for the club.

The opening three fixtures had brought two points and one goal: a defeat by Chelsea followed by goalless draws with Aston Villa and Tottenham Hotspur. Houllier nevertheless retained the adventurous side used against Spurs. Steven Gerrard provided the midfield base for Kewell, El Hadji Diouf and Vladimír Šmicer, with Owen and Milan Baroš ahead of them.

Everton had opportunities to punish that approach. Wayne Rooney and Tomasz Radzinski found space early on, while Igor Bišćan needed a well-timed tackle to halt Radzinski inside the penalty area. Jerzy Dudek saved from Gary Naysmith after Liverpool gave possession away.

Liverpool's finishing made the difference. Seven minutes before half-time, Kewell sent Owen through the centre and the striker clipped his shot in off a post. Everton continued to threaten after the interval, but Dudek kept out efforts from Mark Pembridge and Rooney before Liverpool scored again.

Kewell was involved once more, sending Baroš away down the left. The Czech forward resisted Joseph Yobo and pulled the ball back for Owen to drive home. Liverpool now had a two-goal advantage, earned through the pace and accuracy of their attacking combinations rather than an absence of danger at the other end.

Rooney missed further chances and substitute Duncan Ferguson struck the bar with a free-kick. With eleven minutes remaining, however, goalkeeper Steve Simonsen came out of his area without securing the ball. Owen played it across for Kewell to finish.

Liverpool moved up to ninth with five points from four matches. The result did not erase the slow start, but the contributions of Owen and Kewell gave Houllier a substantial answer to it: three goals from open play, a clean sheet and another victory at Goodison.

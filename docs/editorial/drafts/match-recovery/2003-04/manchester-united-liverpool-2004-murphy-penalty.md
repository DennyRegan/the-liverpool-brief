Danny Murphy again supplied Liverpool's winning goal at Old Trafford, converting a penalty after Gary Neville brought down Steven Gerrard. The 1–0 victory kept Liverpool fourth and ended Manchester United's remaining mathematical chance of retaining the Premier League title.

Liverpool had taken only one point from their previous three matches, losing at Arsenal and at home to Charlton Athletic before drawing with Fulham. Their pursuit of a Champions League qualifying place needed a result, with Aston Villa and Newcastle United close behind.

The first half offered openings without a breakthrough. Cristiano Ronaldo crossed for Louis Saha to shoot over, while Ryan Giggs failed to test Jerzy Dudek from a promising position. At the other end Michael Owen, often isolated in attack, sent a shot across goal from a narrow angle.

Liverpool became more assertive after the interval. Harry Kewell moved forward in support of Owen, and Vladimír Šmicer replaced Steve Finnan shortly before the decisive incident. Gerrard advanced down the left into the area, where Neville's challenge brought him down. Referee Mike Riley pointed to the spot.

Murphy beat Tim Howard in the 61st minute. It was the third time he had scored the winner in a Liverpool victory at Old Trafford under Houllier, and the first successful Premier League penalty there by a visiting player for a decade.

United sent on Ole Gunnar Solskjær and later David Bellion, while Liverpool used Bruno Cheyrou and Emile Heskey in the closing stages. The score remained unchanged. A match which had been goalless for an hour had been settled by Gerrard's run and Murphy's finish.

Liverpool's 53 points from 35 matches left them one ahead of Aston Villa. Newcastle had 50 points with two games in hand, so qualification was still to be secured. United, eleven points behind Arsenal with three matches remaining, could no longer catch the leaders. For Liverpool, the immediate value lay in three points towards fourth place after a damaging run of results.

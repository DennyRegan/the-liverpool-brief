A short corner and an accurate cross settled Liverpool’s FA Cup fifth-round meeting with Manchester United. Peter Crouch supplied the header, Steve Finnan the delivery, and Liverpool protected a 1–0 lead to reach the quarter-finals on 18 February 2006.

The breakthrough followed a save from Edwin van der Sar, who had turned Harry Kewell’s header behind. Gerrard played the corner short to Finnan. Crouch found room in the penalty area and met the resulting cross; Van der Sar got a touch, but the ball went in off the inside of the post.

Liverpool had started with Crouch and Fernando Morientes in attack. With Xabi Alonso absent from the starting side, Dietmar Hamann played alongside the younger energy of Mohamed Sissoko, while Gerrard and Kewell gave Benítez options beyond the middle. United fielded Nemanja Vidić and Wes Brown in central defence, without Rio Ferdinand.

The visitors changed their attack at half-time, sending on Louis Saha for Mikaël Silvestre. Liverpool also made adjustments, with Luis García replacing Morientes and Jan Kromkamp later taking over from Kewell. None altered the score. United could not force the opening that would undo Crouch’s contribution.

A severe injury to Alan Smith overshadowed the closing stages. The substitute broke his leg and dislocated his ankle while trying to block a John Arne Riise shot; there was no contact between the players. He had to leave the match after only a brief appearance.

For Liverpool, the result ended an exceptional gap in this particular rivalry: they had last beaten United in the FA Cup in 1921. The winning margin was narrow, but the next round belonged to Benítez’s side. Crouch’s header had become another decisive step in the campaign which would finish with the trophy in Cardiff.

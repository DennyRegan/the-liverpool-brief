Steven Gerrard kept Liverpool in the 2006 FA Cup final; Pepe Reina completed the rescue. Against West Ham United at the Millennium Stadium, Liverpool recovered from two goals down, fell behind again and needed Gerrard’s stoppage-time strike to reach extra time. Reina then saved three penalties as a 3–3 draw became a 3–1 shoot-out victory.

The match had initially gone badly for Liverpool. Alan Pardew’s West Ham took the lead when Dean Ashton released Lionel Scaloni down the right and Jamie Carragher turned the full-back’s low cross into his own goal. Seven minutes later Reina failed to hold Matthew Etherington’s shot and Ashton followed it in. Liverpool were 2–0 down before half an hour had been played.

Their recovery began with a piece of accurate, direct football. Gerrard lifted the ball into the penalty area and Djibril Cissé met it on the volley, beating Shaka Hislop. The goal restored a manageable deficit before the interval and ensured West Ham could not settle into protecting a two-goal advantage.

The final remained open. Reina had to make important saves early in the second half before Liverpool drew level in the 54th minute. Xabi Alonso crossed, Peter Crouch headed the ball down and Gerrard struck powerfully into the net. Liverpool had erased West Ham’s lead without yet bringing the game under control.

Paul Konchesky restored it ten minutes later. His cross from the left sailed over Reina and dropped inside the far side of the goal. After all Liverpool’s work to recover, West Ham were again in front, this time with little more than a quarter of the match remaining.

Benítez had begun with Cissé and Crouch in attack, supported by Gerrard and Harry Kewell, with Alonso and Mohamed Sissoko in midfield. As the final wore on, Kewell, Alonso and Crouch gave way to Fernando Morientes, Jan Kromkamp and Dietmar Hamann. The personnel changed; the need for another goal did not.

Gerrard had been struggling with cramp as normal time ran out. Then West Ham only partially cleared Riise’s delivery. The ball dropped outside the area, about thirty yards from goal, and the Liverpool captain struck it first time past Hislop. The shot was the final’s defining moment because it arrived when defeat appeared so close. It earned another thirty minutes, not the trophy itself.

Extra time supplied West Ham with one more serious opportunity. Reina turned Nigel Reo-Coker’s looping header on to the post, and the injured Marlon Harewood could not convert the rebound. Liverpool survived to reach penalties.

Hamann converted their first kick, while Reina saved from Bobby Zamora. Hislop then stopped Sami Hyypiä’s attempt and Teddy Sheringham scored for West Ham. Gerrard and Riise both converted for Liverpool; between those kicks Reina denied Konchesky. When the goalkeeper also stopped Anton Ferdinand, Liverpool had won the shoot-out 3–1.

It was Liverpool’s seventh FA Cup and the last final played in Cardiff before the competition returned to Wembley. Gerrard’s two goals and his pass for Cissé had made the result possible. Reina, whose mistake had helped West Ham build their early lead, supplied the saves which finally decided it.

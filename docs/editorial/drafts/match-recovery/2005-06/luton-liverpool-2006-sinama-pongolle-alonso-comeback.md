The goal from inside his own half supplied the finish, but Liverpool’s 5–3 victory at Luton depended on more than Xabi Alonso’s last extraordinary intervention. They had been two goals behind in the second half of their FA Cup third-round tie, with a missed penalty adding to the danger, before Florent Sinama-Pongolle changed the attack.

Steven Gerrard had given Liverpool the lead after fifteen minutes, finishing a move involving Peter Crouch and Djibril Cissé. Luton’s response was forceful. Steve Howard equalised after Paul Underwood’s pass put him through, then Steve Robinson turned in the penalty area and beat Scott Carson before half-time.

The interval brought no immediate recovery. Marlon Beresford saved Cissé’s penalty, awarded after Gerrard was brought down. At the other end, Rowan Vine went through following a Liverpool defensive mistake and collided with Carson. The award was disputed, but Kevin Nicholls converted the penalty and Luton led 3–1.

Benítez replaced Mohamed Sissoko with Sinama-Pongolle. Within minutes the substitute ran on to Gerrard’s pass and drove Liverpool back into contention. Alonso then equalised with a dipping shot from distance. The two-goal deficit had disappeared; soon Sinama-Pongolle headed in Steve Finnan’s delivery to put Liverpool ahead.

Luton still had reason to attack. Beresford went forward for a late corner, leaving his goal exposed when Liverpool cleared. Alonso collected inside his own half, evaded the goalkeeper and sent a left-footed shot the long distance into the empty net. It made the score 5–3 and finally removed the possibility of another reversal.

Liverpool’s route to Cardiff had begun with a serious examination by Championship opposition. The eventual winners had required four unanswered goals to escape Kenilworth Road, and their two rescuers shared those goals equally.

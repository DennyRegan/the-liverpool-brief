Liverpool’s eleven consecutive clean sheets ended in the Club World Championship final, and one goal was enough to decide it. Mineiro scored midway through the first half in Yokohama; São Paulo protected that advantage through an increasingly one-sided Liverpool pursuit to win 1–0.

Benítez began with Fernando Morientes up front and Peter Crouch among the substitutes. Harry Kewell and Luis García offered support, while Gerrard, Xabi Alonso and Mohamed Sissoko formed the midfield. São Paulo had Rogério Ceni in goal and the attacking partnership of Amoroso and Aloísio ahead of them.

The decisive move rewarded a run from midfield. Aloísio supplied the pass and Mineiro went through to finish past Reina. Liverpool’s defensive sequence was over, but the greater problem became finding an equaliser against Ceni and a back line which repeatedly caught them offside.

García headed against the crossbar as Liverpool responded. His threat continued after the interval, when a finish was disallowed for offside. Sami Hyypiä also put the ball in the net, only for the preceding corner to be judged to have gone out of play. These were distinct decisions, although their practical effect was the same: Liverpool remained behind.

Benítez eventually introduced Sinama-Pongolle and Riise, then Crouch for Morientes. Sinama-Pongolle became the third Liverpool player to have a goal ruled out. São Paulo survived, with Ceni’s goalkeeping a central part of a victory which brought the Brazilian club another world crown.

Liverpool had spent much of the final attacking and came home with a result that did not reflect the volume of their chances. It did reflect the decisive difference between the teams: São Paulo converted their breakthrough and defended the lead to the finish. The European champions’ run of clean sheets and their attempt to add the world title both ended on the same evening.

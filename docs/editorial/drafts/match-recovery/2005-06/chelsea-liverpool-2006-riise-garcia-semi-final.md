Liverpool reached the FA Cup final by beating Chelsea 2–1 at Old Trafford, building a lead through John Arne Riise and Luis García before resisting a late recovery. Another cup meeting between Rafael Benítez and José Mourinho turned on Liverpool’s ability to punish mistakes and protect what they had earned.

Chelsea began with Didier Drogba and Hernán Crespo in attack but no place in the starting eleven for Arjen Robben, Joe Cole or Damien Duff. Drogba had chances before Liverpool scored; one clear opening ended with his shot wide.

Riise’s goal followed a free-kick awarded for John Terry’s challenge on García. Riise moved the ball to Gerrard, who stopped it, and the Norwegian drove through the wall. Liverpool went in at half-time a goal ahead, with Harry Kewell also troubling Chelsea from wide positions.

Mourinho introduced Robben at the interval. Terry then headed in, but the referee ruled that he had fouled Riise. Instead of an equaliser, Chelsea conceded again. Defensive headers following a Liverpool throw-in left García with a clear opportunity, and his left-footed finish made it 2–0 early in the second half.

Cole and Duff joined the match as Chelsea searched for a way back. Their greater width helped turn the pressure towards Liverpool, and Drogba reduced the deficit with about twenty minutes remaining, reacting to Riise’s attempted headed clearance.

Benítez used Cissé, Djimi Traoré and Morientes from the bench as Liverpool tried to see out the lead. Chelsea’s best late opening fell to Cole, who sent his effort over. Liverpool held on.

The victory placed them in the Cardiff final on 13 May. Chelsea could still win the league, but Liverpool had removed the possibility of a league and FA Cup double. Riise’s set-piece and García’s finish had supplied a sufficient advantage, even though the closing stages were considerably less comfortable than the first hour.

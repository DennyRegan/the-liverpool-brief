Roy Evans's last match in charge of Liverpool ended with a home defeat, elimination from the League Cup and an injury to the player who scored their only goal. Tottenham won 3–1 at Anfield on 10 November 1998. Two days later, Evans departed, leaving Gérard Houllier in sole charge.

The joint management introduced that summer had lasted only a few months. Before the Tottenham match, Liverpool were already under scrutiny over the arrangement. The performance against George Graham's side increased the pressure: two goals conceded in the opening twenty minutes left Liverpool trying to recover a tie they had scarcely begun.

There had been an opportunity to make a very different start. Jason McAteer headed against the crossbar inside the first minute. Almost immediately, Spurs scored at the other end, Steffen Iversen looping a header past Brad Friedel.

Friedel was back in goal with David James unwell. His evening deteriorated when John Scales, a former Liverpool defender, scored the second from close range after the goalkeeper failed to gather the ball. At 2–0 down, Liverpool had given Tottenham a substantial advantage without forcing Espen Baardsen into comparable difficulty.

The attendance was 20,772. Those inside Anfield saw Liverpool attempt to change the game after half-time, with Karl-Heinz Riedle replacing Øyvind Leonhardsen. Riedle offered a greater aerial threat and helped create an opportunity for Paul Ince, while Sol Campbell denied Michael Owen with a recovery tackle.

The next goal nevertheless belonged to Tottenham. Scales and Iversen helped release Allan Nielsen, who finished past Friedel in the 62nd minute. Liverpool now needed three goals simply to take the tie beyond normal time.

They managed one, through a mistake at the other end. Baardsen's attempted clearance struck Owen, allowing the striker to score in the 81st minute. Even that small recovery brought a further problem: Owen was injured in the incident and had to be replaced by Danny Murphy. Tottenham's progress to the quarter-finals was never overturned.

Evans had led Liverpool to the League Cup in 1995, his first full season as manager. He had also brought through and supported attacking players capable of producing much better football than this. His departure ended an association which extended through his years as player and coach, long before he succeeded Graeme Souness in 1994.

The defeat by Tottenham was the final match of that association. Liverpool's immediate loss was a cup tie; the consequence around it was the end of shared control. Houllier inherited the task of rebuilding the side under one manager.

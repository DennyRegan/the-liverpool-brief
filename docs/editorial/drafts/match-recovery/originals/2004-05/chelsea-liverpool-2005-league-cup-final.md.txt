Liverpool led the League Cup final from its opening minute until the 79th, but Rafael Benítez's first final with the club ended in a 3–2 extra-time defeat. Steven Gerrard's own goal cancelled out John Arne Riise's early volley before Didier Drogba and Mateja Kežman put Chelsea in control.

The start at Cardiff's Millennium Stadium could scarcely have been better for Liverpool. Fernando Morientes found space on the right and crossed towards Riise, arriving unmarked on the opposite side. The Norwegian met the ball with a left-foot volley past Petr Čech, scoring the fastest goal in a League Cup final.

Chelsea gradually took control of possession. Jerzy Dudek saved from Frank Lampard and Drogba, while Luis García made a clearance off the line. After half-time, when José Mourinho introduced Eidur Gudjohnsen, Dudek blocked the substitute's header and then William Gallas's follow-up.

Liverpool made their own changes, bringing on Antonio Núñez, Igor Bišćan and Milan Baroš. Their lead survived until Paulo Ferreira delivered a free-kick into the penalty area. Gerrard's attempted defensive header sent the ball beyond Dudek for an own goal.

Mourinho was dismissed from the dugout after his reaction to the equaliser. His team nevertheless carried the game into extra time and scored twice after the additional period's interval. Drogba put Chelsea ahead in the 107th minute after Liverpool failed to deal with Glen Johnson's long throw. Kežman added a third from close range five minutes later.

Núñez quickly headed Liverpool back to within one goal, giving them a chance of forcing penalties. It was his first goal for the club, but it came too late to change the destination of the trophy. Chelsea held on to win Mourinho's first silverware as their manager.

For Liverpool, the final combined an excellent opening, prolonged defensive work and a late reversal. They had prevented Chelsea scoring for most of normal time, helped by Dudek's saves, but could not regain their advantage after the equaliser. The European meetings still to come would offer another test against the same opponents; the first trophy between the two new managers belonged to Mourinho.

Liverpool won the European Cup for the fifth time after recovering from a 3–0 half-time deficit against Milan at the Atatürk Olympic Stadium on 25 May 2005. The final finished 3–3 after extra time; Jerzy Dudek's save from Andriy Shevchenko settled the penalty shoot-out 3–2 in Liverpool's favour.

Rafael Benítez had taken Liverpool to the final in his first season at the club. Their route had included a late escape against Olympiacos and knockout victories over Bayer Leverkusen, Juventus and Chelsea.

## Three down before the interval

Milan scored inside the first minute. Andrea Pirlo's free-kick was met by Paolo Maldini, whose volley beat Dudek. Liverpool were already chasing the match when Harry Kewell went off injured in the 23rd minute, replaced by Vladimír Šmicer.

Kaká was central to Milan's attacking play, supplying passes for Shevchenko and Hernán Crespo. In the 39th minute, after Liverpool's appeal for a handball against Alessandro Nesta had been rejected, Milan broke forward. Kaká found Shevchenko on the right and his square pass left Crespo to finish.

Five minutes later, Kaká sent another pass through Liverpool's defence. Crespo reached it and chipped Dudek to make the score 3–0. Liverpool had conceded twice shortly before the interval, after losing the opening goal almost as soon as the final began.

## Six minutes

Liverpool made a substitution at half-time — Dietmar Hamann on for Steve Finnan — and within nine minutes of the restart they had begun their recovery.

In the 54th minute, captain Steven Gerrard headed in a John Arne Riise cross. Two minutes later, Šmicer drove a low shot past Dida from outside the penalty area. A three-goal deficit had become a single goal, with more than half an hour still to play.

Gerrard then ran into the area and was brought down by Gennaro Gattuso. Xabi Alonso took the penalty. Dida saved it, but Alonso reached the rebound and scored. By the hour Liverpool were level at 3–3.

## Extra time, and Dudek's double save

The recovery did not end Milan's threat. Djimi Traoré cleared off the line from Shevchenko, while Liverpool had to defend through the remainder of normal time. Benítez introduced Djibril Cissé for Milan Baroš; Milan brought on Jon Dahl Tomasson and Serginho for Crespo and Clarence Seedorf.

Late in extra time, Jerzy Dudek produced a double save from point-blank range to deny Andriy Shevchenko. He stopped the striker's header and then his immediate follow-up, keeping the score at 3–3. There was no further goal before the shoot-out.

## The shoot-out

Milan shot first. Serginho put his effort over the bar and Dudek saved from Pirlo. Hamann and Cissé converted Liverpool's opening two kicks, giving them a 2–0 advantage in the shoot-out.

Tomasson scored for Milan before Dida saved Riise's penalty. Kaká then brought Milan level at 2–2, but Šmicer converted Liverpool's fourth kick. Shevchenko had to score to keep the final alive. Dudek saved, and Liverpool had won.

The result brought Liverpool their first European Cup since 1984. It also gave them the trophy permanently under the rules for five-time winners. Three goals between the 54th minute and the hour had recovered the match; the defending and goalkeeping that followed had carried that recovery through to a fifth title.

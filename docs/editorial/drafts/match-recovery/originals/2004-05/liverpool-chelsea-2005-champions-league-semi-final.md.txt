Liverpool reached their first European Cup final for twenty years by protecting a lead gained in the fourth minute against Chelsea. Luis García's goal at Anfield was the only one of the two-leg semi-final, enough to send Rafael Benítez's team to Istanbul.

Chelsea arrived three days after securing the Premier League title. Liverpool had lost all three domestic meetings with them that season, including the League Cup final, but the first European leg at Stamford Bridge had finished goalless. Xabi Alonso's suspension forced one change to Liverpool's starting side, with Dietmar Hamann replacing him.

The decisive move began with John Arne Riise finding Steven Gerrard. Gerrard lifted a pass into Milan Baroš's path and the forward reached it ahead of Petr Čech. As Baroš and the goalkeeper collided, García moved on to the loose ball and sent it towards goal. William Gallas cleared, but the assistant referee signalled that the ball had crossed the line and the goal was awarded.

Whether the whole ball had crossed remained disputed. The decision stood, and the task for both sides was clear: Liverpool needed to preserve their advantage; one Chelsea goal, without a further Liverpool reply, would send the visitors through on away goals.

Jamie Carragher stopped Didier Drogba after Joe Cole's pass, setting the pattern for the defensive work ahead. Chelsea gained more of the ball, while Liverpool found it difficult to retain possession and bring support up to their lone striker. Benítez introduced Djibril Cissé for Baroš in the second half.

Jerzy Dudek made an important save from Frank Lampard's free-kick, turning it away to his right. Chelsea brought on Arjen Robben and Mateja Kežman, then sent defender Robert Huth forward as another attacking option. Liverpool's back four and midfield continued to protect the penalty area.

Čech held a Cissé header, but the final threat came from Chelsea. Deep into added time, Eidur Gudjohnsen sent a shot across the face of Liverpool's goal and wide. There was no equaliser.

García's early intervention had decided the score; the work of Carragher, Hyypiä, Finnan, Traoré and those in front of them had made it sufficient. Liverpool had kept Chelsea scoreless over both legs and earned a sixth appearance in the European Cup final.

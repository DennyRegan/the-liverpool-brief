Gérard Houllier returned to Anfield on 19 March 2002 to see Liverpool reach the Champions League quarter-finals. Jari Litmanen's early penalty and Emile Heskey's second-half header secured the 2–0 victory over Roma which carried Liverpool through the second group stage.

Houllier had been absent from Anfield matches for five months while recovering from heart surgery. He gave the players their pre-match talk, and Phil Thompson afterwards credited his presence with helping the team. The occasion had a personal importance beyond the qualification contest, but Liverpool still had to overcome a Roma side containing Francesco Totti, Gabriel Batistuta and Emerson.

Michael Owen was unavailable, leaving Litmanen and Heskey to lead the attack. Liverpool began quickly. Vladimír Šmicer tested Francesco Antonioli, and the pressure produced a penalty when Marcos Assunção fouled Danny Murphy. Litmanen sent the goalkeeper the wrong way to score in the seventh minute.

Roma gradually found openings. Stéphane Henchoz intercepted an Emerson cross intended for Batistuta, preserving Liverpool's lead. Fabio Capello changed his attack at half-time, introducing Vincenzo Montella and Marco Delvecchio, and Totti threatened after the restart.

Heskey remained a difficult opponent for the visiting defence. His strength in the air offered Liverpool a target, while his running also forced Roma to concede ground and free-kicks. Midway through the second half, Murphy delivered from the left and Heskey rose to head past Antonioli. Liverpool had their second goal.

Emerson subsequently missed a chance for Roma, but the visitors could not score. Houllier and Thompson sent on Igor Bišćan and Gary McAllister late in the game, and Liverpool finished with the clean sheet intact.

The result put Liverpool second in the group and eliminated Roma. Litmanen had provided the composed first finish; Heskey supplied the header that completed the required score. On the night their manager returned, Liverpool's progress rested on those two forwards and the defence which protected their work.

Liverpool were three goals ahead of the European champions less than a minute into the second half. By the end they were protecting a one-goal advantage, but Bayern Munich could not complete their recovery. The 3–2 victory at Monaco's Stade Louis II on 24 August 2001 brought Liverpool the Super Cup for the first time since 1977.

Gérard Houllier's side had qualified by winning the UEFA Cup. Bayern had beaten Valencia in the Champions League final. Their meeting gave Liverpool's attack a test against Oliver Kahn and a defence accustomed to the strongest opposition in Europe.

Michael Owen supplied the opening goal midway through the first half. His low cross reached John Arne Riise at the far post, where the Norwegian slid in to score his first Liverpool goal. Riise's arrival had given Houllier another runner on the left; here he finished an attack made on the other side of the pitch.

Emile Heskey doubled the lead just before half-time, going past Thomas Linke and Robert Kovač before beating Kahn. Owen then scored immediately after the restart, getting behind Bayern's defence to make it 3–0.

Bayern's response came through the air. Hasan Salihamidžić headed in an Owen Hargreaves corner, and substitute Carsten Jancker later looped a header over Sander Westerveld. The second goal left Liverpool facing a finish very different from the one their earlier control had promised.

Houllier used Igor Bišćan, Danny Murphy and Robbie Fowler from the bench as Liverpool saw the game out. Bayern had reduced the margin, but the goals scored before and just after the interval remained enough. Liverpool's second Super Cup was secured by the speed and finishing that had established the lead, followed by the successful defence of what remained of it.

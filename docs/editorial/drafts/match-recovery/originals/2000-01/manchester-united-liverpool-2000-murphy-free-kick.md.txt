Danny Murphy's free-kick gave Liverpool the lead at Old Trafford. Their midfield and defence made it last. Manchester United had not lost a home league match for almost two years, but Gérard Houllier's side denied them an equaliser and left with a 1–0 victory.

Steven Gerrard and Igor Bišćan played an important part in disrupting United's possession. Behind them, Sami Hyypiä and Stéphane Henchoz dealt with an attack missing several established forwards. United started Ole Gunnar Solskjær with Paul Scholes supporting him, while Liverpool had Michael Owen and Emile Heskey available to lead their breaks.

For much of the first half, that organisation produced few clear chances at either end. The match changed just before the interval. Scholes cleared a Heskey header off the line; moments later, Gary Neville handled outside the penalty area. Nick Barmby ran over the resulting free-kick, and Murphy curled it beyond Fabien Barthez in the forty-third minute.

Liverpool could have increased their advantage around the break. Owen missed one opportunity and then hit the crossbar early in the second half. At the other end, Sander Westerveld turned away a Solskjær effort. United's response did not develop into the sustained pressure required to overturn the lead.

The dismissal of substitute Luke Chadwick further reduced their prospects. He pulled down Vladimír Šmicer as Liverpool's substitute advanced towards the penalty area, leaving United to finish with ten men.

Liverpool climbed to fifth. They remained ten points behind the leaders, so the result did not erase the distance between the teams. It did demonstrate that Houllier's side could impose its organisation at the champions' ground. Murphy supplied the goal; Liverpool's collective control of the remaining match gave it its value.

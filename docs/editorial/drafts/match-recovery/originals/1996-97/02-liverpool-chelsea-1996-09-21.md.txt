# Berger’s first start brings five goals against Chelsea

**Liverpool 5–1 Chelsea | 21 September 1996**  
Premier League · Anfield · Season 1996-97

Patrik Berger had scored twice as a substitute at Leicester. Given his first Liverpool start six days later, he scored twice again.

Chelsea were beaten 5–1, and Liverpool remained unbeaten at the top of the Premier League. Berger’s introduction had given Roy Evans another way to threaten teams: a runner who could break beyond the defence and finish the chances he reached.

Stan Collymore began on the bench. Berger started alongside Robbie Fowler, with Steve McManaman also carrying the ball forward from midfield. Chelsea, managed by Ruud Gullit, arrived without a league defeat themselves.

Fowler put Liverpool ahead after 15 minutes, heading in Stig Inge Bjørnebye’s cross. The decisive damage came just before half-time. Dominic Matteo won possession and advanced before releasing Berger, who went round Kevin Hitchcock to score. Then Andy Myers tried to head another Bjørnebye delivery back to his goalkeeper and sent it into his own net.

Liverpool went in 3–0 ahead.

Berger scored again early in the second half, finishing another opportunity behind Chelsea’s defence. John Barnes made it five with a shot from outside the area that took a deflection.

Chelsea’s difficulties were partly of their own making, particularly for the own goal, but Liverpool’s movement repeatedly gave them decisions they could not handle. Matteo’s advance for Berger’s first was as important as the finish. Bjørnebye’s delivery had helped create the opening goal and the third.

Berger left the field to a standing ovation when Jamie Redknapp replaced him. Frank Leboeuf’s late penalty reduced the margin, but by then the contest had long been settled.

Liverpool had five league wins and two draws from seven matches. Their new signing had four goals from the last two of them.

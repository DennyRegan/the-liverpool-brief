# The penalty Fowler tried to refuse at Highbury

**Arsenal 1–2 Liverpool | 24 March 1997**  
Premier League · Highbury · Season 1996-97

Robbie Fowler tried to tell the referee that Liverpool should not have a penalty. Gerald Ashby awarded it anyway.

What followed decided an important match in the title race. David Seaman saved Fowler’s kick, but Jason McAteer reached the rebound and scored. Liverpool left Highbury with a 2–1 victory and remained three points behind Manchester United.

The match had been goalless at half-time. Arsenal had made Liverpool work for space, although Stan Collymore had threatened early and Dennis Bergkamp missed an opportunity at the other end.

Collymore broke the deadlock in the 51st minute. Stig Inge Bjørnebye struck from distance, Seaman failed to hold the shot, and the Liverpool forward finished the rebound.

The disputed penalty came midway through the half. Fowler went down as he tried to get past Seaman and immediately indicated that the goalkeeper had not fouled him. His intervention did not change Ashby’s decision.

Seaman then stopped the penalty. McAteer, following in, converted the loose ball to give Liverpool a two-goal advantage.

Fowler later said he had tried to score; the saved kick was not a deliberate act of restitution.

Arsenal still made Liverpool defend the result. Ian Wright met Bergkamp’s header to score with 12 minutes remaining, reducing the margin and giving the home side a route back into the game. Liverpool held out.

The result left Evans’s team with seven league matches remaining, the same number as United. Highbury had provided three points at a stage when Liverpool could not afford to let the leaders pull away.

Fowler’s intervention remained the defining incident. He had challenged a decision that benefited his own side, in a match with consequences at the top of the table. The referee’s refusal to reverse it meant his honesty and Liverpool’s winning goal became part of the same sequence.

Titi Camara sank to his knees at the Anfield Road end after scoring against West Ham. Earlier that day he had learned that his father had died. His tears gave the celebration a meaning far beyond the lead he had just secured for Liverpool.

The goal came two minutes before half-time in a match Liverpool had threatened to make unnecessarily difficult. Michael Owen and Robbie Fowler were both absent, leaving Camara and Erik Meijer to lead the attack. Behind them, David Thompson was among the most persistent threats, forcing Shaka Hislop to push one effort over the bar.

Liverpool had possession and opportunities without a finish. Meijer and Camara both found room to volley, while Jamie Redknapp also tried his luck. West Ham, without Paolo Di Canio, still carried danger through Paulo Wanchope and Trevor Sinclair; Joe Cole helped them escape Liverpool's pressure with his skill on the ball.

Rigobert Song supplied the cross that finally broke the game open. Rio Ferdinand failed to clear it, and Camara took the opportunity to beat Hislop. Liverpool had their lead; Camara's response revealed the personal burden he had carried into the match.

There was no second goal to make the remaining half comfortable. Thompson struck the side-netting, Song had defensive work to do, and Houllier eventually replaced Camara with Vladimír Šmicer. The score stayed 1–0.

The win moved Liverpool to ninth in the Premier League. It was a useful result for a side still establishing itself under Houllier, secured without either of its best-known forwards. For Camara, whose Liverpool career would be brief, it became the goal most closely associated with his time at Anfield: a decisive contribution on an evening of bereavement.

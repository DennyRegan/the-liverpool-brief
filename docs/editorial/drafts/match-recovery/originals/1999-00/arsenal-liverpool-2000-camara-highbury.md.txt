Liverpool arrived at Highbury without Michael Owen, Robbie Fowler or Jamie Redknapp and left above Arsenal in the table. Titi Camara's eighteenth-minute goal was enough, though the work required to protect it lasted considerably longer than the move that created it.

Gérard Houllier used Erik Meijer as the main attacking reference, with Camara dropping towards the left when Arsenal had possession. Liverpool defended together, then sought the space behind the home side. The winning goal followed that pattern: after possession was won from Freddie Ljungberg, Steven Gerrard played a first-time pass through the defence and Camara finished past David Seaman.

Gerrard's contribution was cut short by injury. Vegard Heggem replaced him after thirty-three minutes, with Jamie Carragher moving into midfield. Liverpool had to reorganise again when Meijer went off for Danny Murphy in the second half.

The changes did not remove their threat. Meijer had missed an opportunity before the interval, and Murphy later advanced on Seaman without finding the second goal. Those chances mattered because Arsenal kept testing a lead that remained only one goal wide.

Sander Westerveld saved from Thierry Henry. Dominic Matteo intervened when Oleg Luzhny threatened at the far post, and Patrick Vieira hit the underside of the crossbar in the closing stages. Liverpool's defending included anxious moments as well as discipline, but Arsenal could not turn either into an equaliser.

The result placed Liverpool third on 47 points, three clear of Arsenal after twenty-five matches each. Houllier's side had won with a makeshift attack and absorbed the loss of the midfielder who had supplied the goal. Camara provided the finish; the organisation around him made it sufficient.

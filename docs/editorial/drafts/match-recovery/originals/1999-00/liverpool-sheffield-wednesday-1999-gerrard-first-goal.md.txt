Steven Gerrard's first Liverpool goal arrived when the game still needed winning. Sheffield Wednesday had been behind since before half-time, but Niclas Alexandersson had twice threatened an equaliser. Then the nineteen-year-old midfielder took the ball forward, beat the challenges in his path and finished beyond Kevin Pressman. Liverpool's narrow lead became 3–1.

It was the decisive change in an afternoon that had begun badly for Gérard Houllier's team. Wednesday were bottom of the Premier League, yet scored first when Wim Jonk rolled a free-kick towards Alexandersson. The winger struck past Sander Westerveld before Liverpool could close him down.

Sami Hyypiä quickly replied with a header, and Liverpool pressed for more. Jonk cleared an Owen effort off the line, while David Thompson tested a defence increasingly occupied by Liverpool's movement.

Thompson also helped create the second goal. His volley was saved by Pressman, but Danny Murphy converted the rebound before the interval. The home side had recovered its position without securing the result.

Wednesday made that distinction clear after the restart. Westerveld had to save from Alexandersson, who later struck a post. Liverpool's response came through Gerrard. He carried the ball into the area, passed Des Walker and placed his shot beyond the goalkeeper, turning a precarious contest into a much more manageable one.

Thompson completed the scoring with a left-footed finish after working room against Lee Briscoe. Four different players had scored for Liverpool; neither Owen nor the substitute Robbie Fowler was among them.

The 4–1 win kept Liverpool fifth. Gerrard's goal would acquire a particular place in the club's history as the first of his senior career, but its immediate value was straightforward: it stopped Wednesday's recovery and gave a lively, uneven Liverpool performance the security it had been missing.

Liverpool needed extra time to finish their League Cup semi-final against Sheffield United on 21 January 2003. El Hadji Diouf's early goal cancelled the visitors' first-leg advantage, and Michael Owen's finish in the 107th minute completed a 2–0 Anfield win and a 3–2 aggregate success.

The defeat at Bramall Lane had left Gérard Houllier's side a goal behind. Chris Kirkland kept goal for Liverpool in the return. Sheffield United, managed by Neil Warnock, were seeking a place in the final from the First Division.

Diouf gave Liverpool the early score they wanted. Heskey laid the ball back and the Senegal forward drove it home from outside the area. Liverpool had erased the deficit, but the tie did not become straightforward. No further goal came during normal time.

The away-goals provision applied only after extra time. Liverpool's goal at Bramall Lane therefore offered an advantage if the score stayed 1–0 for 120 minutes, without settling the contest after 90. Sheffield United still had an opportunity to change it.

Kirkland was booked for handling outside his area late in normal time, and Tommy Mooney's resulting free-kick was deflected narrowly wide. Liverpool reached the extra period with their lead intact. Houllier then introduced Bruno Cheyrou for Šmicer, while Warnock later sent on Paul Peschisolido for Wayne Allison.

Owen finally increased the margin after Steven Gerrard put him through. His finish made Liverpool's position less precarious: a Sheffield United goal would now level the aggregate score and the away-goal count, rather than immediately put the visitors ahead overall. Liverpool allowed neither that goal nor a penalty shoot-out.

The final whistle confirmed a place at Cardiff's Millennium Stadium on 2 March. Liverpool would meet the winners of the other semi-final, between Manchester United and Blackburn Rovers. After going behind over the first leg and being taken beyond 90 minutes in the second, they had kept the chance of a seventh League Cup alive.

Arsenal arrived at Anfield as champions and left with their title entirely intact, despite a 4–0 defeat. For Liverpool, the purpose of the evening was more immediate. Victory on 6 May 1998 secured third place and gave their home league programme an emphatic finish.

Paul Ince scored twice in two minutes, Michael Owen added the third before half-time and Øyvind Leonhardsen completed the scoring late on. Liverpool also saw Owen's penalty saved. The margin was considerable, although the circumstances mattered: Arsène Wenger had made extensive changes after clinching the championship against Everton three days earlier, with the FA Cup final still ahead.

Liverpool acknowledged the new champions with a guard of honour. Then they set about beating the side Wenger had selected. Owen and Karl-Heinz Riedle started in attack, with Steve McManaman available again after an ankle problem. Arsenal's defence included Matthew Upson and Gilles Grimandi, while Alex Manninger took the place in goal.

The breakthrough came in the 28th minute. McManaman's advance led to a Jason McAteer cross, and after Owen contested the ball Ince drove it into the bottom corner. The second followed almost immediately from another attack which Arsenal failed to clear. Owen turned the ball towards goal and Ince finished from close range.

For a midfielder with two goals already, a hat-trick was suddenly a possibility. Ince headed just over, but his next decisive contribution created Owen's goal. A McManaman corner was headed on by the captain, and Owen volleyed in at the far post to make it 3–0. It was his eighteenth league goal of the season.

Manninger then saved Owen's penalty after Upson brought down McManaman. Liverpool had established a commanding lead without taking every opportunity offered to them. Arsenal reached the interval three behind and could at least prevent the game continuing at the same scoring rate after it.

There were uncomfortable moments in a largely settled contest. Ian Wright left the field following an Ince challenge, while Brad Friedel had work to do in Liverpool's goal. Leonhardsen eventually made it four in the 87th minute after Steve Bould's attempted clearance.

The result completed Liverpool's league double over Arsenal and fixed their own finishing position. Those were worthwhile achievements, even with the champions resting players. They also defined the limits of the night: Liverpool could beat Arsenal twice, and beat this version of them heavily, but Arsenal's sustained run had already settled the championship. Anfield's final home match supplied a convincing victory without altering that distinction.

Steve McManaman began his run inside Liverpool's half with the UEFA Cup tie moving away from them. Celtic had overturned Michael Owen's early goal and led 2–1 as the first leg approached its end. Liverpool had already discovered how completely a match could change at Celtic Park. McManaman changed it once more.

He carried the ball towards the retreating defence, kept going as the space narrowed and curled a left-footed shot beyond Jonathan Gould. The 89th-minute equaliser gave Liverpool a 2–2 draw on 16 September 1997 and two away goals to take back to Anfield. A night which had threatened to leave Roy Evans's side repairing considerable damage ended with the tie level.

The opening had promised something more comfortable. Karl-Heinz Riedle released Owen through the middle after six minutes, and the young striker escaped Alan Stubbs before finishing past Gould. His speed made Liverpool dangerous whenever Celtic left room behind their defence. McManaman offered another route forward, carrying possession over distance and forcing opponents to turn towards their own goal.

Liverpool reached half-time ahead. Their midfield had helped them control the early stages, but the advantage concealed a warning: Celtic were finding more opportunities as the interval approached. The contest after it was altogether different.

Jackie McNamara equalised in the 53rd minute, exchanging passes with Craig Burley before striking into the top corner. Simon Donnelly then hit the underside of the crossbar. Liverpool, who had looked capable of extending their lead, were being made to defend it and then to recover it.

The second Celtic goal came from the penalty spot. David James brought down Henrik Larsson, and Donnelly converted with 16 minutes remaining. Wim Jansen's side had turned a half-time deficit into a position from which they could take an advantage to Merseyside.

McManaman's response mattered beyond the quality of the finish. His run took Liverpool from defence into a scoring position without allowing Celtic to settle behind the ball. Having helped create the first-half danger, he supplied the escape when the visitors were at their most vulnerable.

The score also changed what Liverpool would need in the return. Two away goals offered protection which a 2–1 defeat would not have provided. When the second leg finished goalless at Anfield a fortnight later, Liverpool progressed on away goals. The dribble at Celtic Park had lasted only a small part of the evening; its value survived the whole of the next match.

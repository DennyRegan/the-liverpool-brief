Within a few first-half minutes, Michael Owen gave Liverpool a way back into the match and then left them a much harder task. His equaliser at Old Trafford on Good Friday was followed by a second yellow card. Liverpool faced Manchester United with ten men from the 40th minute and held on for a 1–1 draw.

The result on 10 April 1998 reached beyond the rivalry. United remained top, but their seven-point advantage over Arsenal came with four extra matches played. A home game in which they led, and then enjoyed a numerical advantage for more than half the contest, had brought them only a point.

Liverpool's opening offered little suggestion of the resistance to come. Brad Friedel stopped Ryan Giggs, and Phil Babb cleared Paul Scholes's follow-up from the line. A succession of United corners then exposed another gap. David Beckham's delivery found an unmarked Ronny Johnsen, whose header gave the home side the lead after 12 minutes.

Danny Murphy, partnering Owen in attack, helped Liverpool answer. His passing and Owen's movement began to create difficulties for Gary Pallister and the United defence. Steve McManaman had opportunities to equalise before Owen did so himself, exploiting hesitation between Pallister and Peter Schmeichel to reach Murphy's forward ball and score.

Owen had already been booked for a challenge on Schmeichel. His late tackle on Johnsen brought the second caution from Graham Poll and ended his afternoon. Liverpool had recovered the score without securing any comfort: Murphy was now left to work up front as the team reorganised behind him.

United controlled more of the ball after the dismissal, but possession did not become a succession of clear chances. Babb and Dominic Matteo helped protect Friedel, and Liverpool retained enough pace to threaten on the break. Øyvind Leonhardsen forced Schmeichel into a save, a reminder that the visitors could still do damage if United overcommitted.

Alex Ferguson introduced Teddy Sheringham in pursuit of the winner. Liverpool were pressed increasingly deep, yet the decisive chances arrived only near the end. When Friedel failed to reach a cross, Rob Jones cleared Andy Cole's effort off the line. Scholes also shot wide as United's opportunities ran out.

Liverpool had begun by conceding another avoidable headed goal and had complicated their recovery with Owen's dismissal. Their response over the remaining minutes was far more secure. The teenager supplied the equaliser; the players who stayed on the field made it worth a point, and made United's route to another championship less certain.

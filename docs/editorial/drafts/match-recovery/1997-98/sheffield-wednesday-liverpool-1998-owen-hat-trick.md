Michael Owen scored three times at Hillsborough and still needed Kevin Pressman to make one fewer save. Liverpool's 3–3 draw with Sheffield Wednesday on 14 February 1998 was a landmark in the teenager's emergence, yet it carried the frustration of a victory left unclaimed.

At 18 years and 62 days, Owen became the youngest player to score a Premier League hat-trick. He had made his senior England debut that week. At club level, the responsibility was already substantial: Liverpool were relying on his finishing to keep them in touch with Manchester United.

They needed it early. Benito Carbone put Wednesday ahead with a chip from outside the penalty area, before Steve McManaman's pass sent Owen through for the equaliser. Owen beat Pressman to make it 1–1, the score at half-time. Liverpool had chances to improve on that position, but Wednesday's goalkeeper repeatedly intervened.

The second half made the distinction between Liverpool's attacking promise and their defending painfully clear. Paolo Di Canio headed the home side back in front, and Andy Hinchcliffe increased the lead after David James could not hold Mark Pembridge's shot. With 69 minutes gone, Liverpool were 3–1 down.

Roy Evans responded by sending on Patrik Berger and Karl-Heinz Riedle, withdrawing two defenders. There was little room left for caution. Robbie Fowler struck a post and Owen drove in the rebound, cutting the deficit in the 73rd minute. Five minutes later, Paul Ince supplied the pass for another composed finish. Owen had brought Liverpool level twice in the same match, the second time completing his first league treble.

The recovery did not exhaust their chances. Berger also hit the woodwork, and Pressman kept out attempts from Owen and Fowler late on. Liverpool could leave knowing they had escaped defeat; they could also identify enough opportunities to have won.

Contemporary reaction reflected both feelings. Owen's performance drew applause from both sets of supporters, while the league position offered less uncomplicated encouragement. Liverpool moved into second place on 46 points, four behind United, having played a game more. The dramatic rescue had still produced only one point.

That was the burden behind Owen's achievement. Evans had a striker whose finishing could repair a match which appeared lost, but Liverpool's difficulties demanded those repairs too often. At Hillsborough, a record belonged to Owen and a share of the points belonged to Liverpool. His hat-trick was the reason the second prize remained available at all.

Liverpool led 2–0, 3–1 and 4–3 in Dortmund. Each time Alavés came back. The UEFA Cup final finally ended late in extra time when Gary McAllister's free-kick glanced off Delfí Geli's head and into his own net. Under the golden-goal rule, that touch settled the match immediately. Liverpool had won 5–4 and completed their cup treble.

Four days after recovering to beat Arsenal in Cardiff, Gérard Houllier's side had begun this final as though they might avoid another ordeal. Markus Babbel headed in McAllister's free-kick after four minutes. Michael Owen then released Steven Gerrard, who drove through to score the second.

Alavés coach Mané reacted early, replacing defender Dan Eggen with striker Iván Alonso. The change brought an almost immediate return: Alonso headed in Cosmin Contra's cross to reduce the deficit. Liverpool restored their two-goal lead before half-time when Martín Herrera brought down Owen and McAllister converted the penalty.

At 3–1, Liverpool had a position from which to control the remaining match. Within six minutes of the restart, it had gone. Contra crossed for Javi Moreno to head home, and Moreno then drove a free-kick beneath Liverpool's jumping wall. The score was 3–3, and Alavés had recovered without needing to wait for the closing stages.

Houllier changed his side. Vladimír Šmicer replaced Stéphane Henchoz, and Robbie Fowler came on for Emile Heskey. Fowler restored Liverpool's lead in the seventy-third minute, receiving McAllister's pass and working room for a right-footed shot inside the post.

Again the advantage did not last. With normal time almost over, Jordi Cruyff met a corner and headed past Sander Westerveld. Liverpool, who had kept Barcelona out across both semi-final legs, had conceded four in the final. Extra time would decide whether the third cup followed the first two.

Alavés lost Magno to a second booking, then captain Antonio Karmona was also dismissed after fouling Šmicer late in the additional half-hour. The Basque side were down to nine men when McAllister prepared the resulting free-kick from the left.

Geli reached the delivery, but his attempted clearance became the winner for Liverpool. There would be no kick-off and no further Alavés recovery. The golden goal was an own goal.

Liverpool had won their third UEFA Cup and added it to the League Cup and FA Cup secured earlier in the season. This was the sixty-second match of the campaign, and one more remained at Charlton to settle Champions League qualification. The treble itself was complete: achieved through nine goals, repeated reversals and a final touch from an opponent.

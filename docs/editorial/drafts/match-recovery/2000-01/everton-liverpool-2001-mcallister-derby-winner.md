Gary McAllister stood over a free-kick well outside the penalty area with the derby deep into stoppage time. Liverpool had lost two leads and Igor Bišćan had been sent off. Paul Gerrard anticipated a delivery into the box. McAllister shot instead, finding the corner and giving Liverpool a 3–2 victory at Goodison Park.

It was an extraordinary finish to a match in which Liverpool had repeatedly appeared to gain control, then surrendered it. Emile Heskey scored after five minutes, collecting Dietmar Hamann's pass and holding off Steve Watson before finishing. Duncan Ferguson equalised shortly before half-time after Liverpool failed to clear the danger inside their area.

The visitors went ahead again through another break. Robbie Fowler carried the ball forward and tried to find Vladimír Šmicer. A deflection diverted the pass towards Markus Babbel, who arrived to score.

Fowler then had the chance to make it 3–1 from the penalty spot. His kick struck a post. The missed opportunity became more costly when Bišćan received his second booking and Everton were awarded a penalty after Sami Hyypiä's challenge on Ferguson. David Unsworth converted to make it 2–2.

Liverpool had seven minutes of normal time, plus stoppages, to survive with ten men or seek another lead. McAllister's free-kick settled both questions. Its distance helped disguise his intention; the precision of the strike made Gerrard's position decisive.

The win kept Liverpool fifth and ended a run without victory at Goodison stretching back to 1990. With the race for third place still open, these were points they could scarcely afford to lose. Three days later they would face Barcelona in the UEFA Cup semi-final at Anfield. McAllister's intervention ensured they approached that match after a derby victory won at the last possible stage, rather than another lead allowed to disappear.

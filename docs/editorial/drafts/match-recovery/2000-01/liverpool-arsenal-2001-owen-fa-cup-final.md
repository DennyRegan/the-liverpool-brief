For most of the first FA Cup final played outside England, Liverpool's task was to remain in it. Arsenal had the possession, the clearer openings and eventually the lead. Then Michael Owen scored twice in five minutes, turning a game Liverpool had struggled to influence into their sixth FA Cup victory.

At the Millennium Stadium, Robert Pires and Patrick Vieira helped Arsenal control midfield while Thierry Henry repeatedly threatened the space behind Liverpool's defence. Gérard Houllier had started Owen with Emile Heskey, but opportunities to use their pace and strength were limited.

Liverpool also benefited from a decision that should not disappear beneath the story of the comeback. In the first half, Henry rounded Sander Westerveld and his goalbound effort struck Stéphane Henchoz's arm. Arsenal appealed, but no penalty was awarded. The interval arrived with the score still 0–0.

The pressure continued after the restart. Westerveld made saves, and Sami Hyypiä's interventions on the line helped prevent Arsenal converting their superiority. Liverpool had an opportunity through a Heskey header, but it did not change the general direction of the match.

Gary McAllister replaced Dietmar Hamann around the hour. Arsenal nevertheless made the breakthrough with eighteen minutes remaining, when Pires released Freddie Ljungberg. The Swede rounded Westerveld and scored. Liverpool now needed a goal as well as relief from the pressure.

Houllier added Robbie Fowler and Patrik Berger, replacing Vladimír Šmicer and Danny Murphy. The substitutions put more attacking options around Owen, while McAllister supplied the set-piece from which the final changed.

In the eighty-third minute his free-kick reached the penalty area. Arsenal failed to clear, Markus Babbel helped the ball towards Owen, and the striker finished low past David Seaman. Liverpool had equalised without ever having established the sustained control Arsenal had enjoyed.

Five minutes later, Berger sent a long pass into the space ahead of Owen. The forward accelerated beyond Lee Dixon, resisted Tony Adams's attempt to recover and struck left-footed across Seaman into the far corner. There was scarcely time for Arsenal to respond.

Liverpool won 2–1. Their goalkeeper and defenders had kept the score within reach, the substitutes had provided the means to attack it, and Owen had supplied two finishes of sharply different kinds. The League Cup already stood behind them; the UEFA Cup final lay four days ahead. A treble remained possible because a final that had belonged to Arsenal for so long had been decided by Liverpool's centre-forward at its end.

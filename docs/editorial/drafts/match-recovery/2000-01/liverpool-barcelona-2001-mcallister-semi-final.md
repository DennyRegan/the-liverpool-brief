Three days after winning the derby at Goodison Park, Gary McAllister scored the goal that took Liverpool to a European final. This time the opportunity came from the penalty spot. Patrick Kluivert handled a corner just before half-time, and McAllister beat Pepe Reina to establish the only lead either side would hold across the UEFA Cup semi-final.

The 0–0 first leg in Barcelona had left the tie finely balanced. At Anfield, Michael Owen and Steven Gerrard returned to Liverpool's starting side, but the visitors supplied early reminders of their threat. Sander Westerveld tipped over Rivaldo's long-range effort, and Luis Enrique narrowly missed the target later in the half.

Liverpool also found openings. Reina intervened at Owen's feet, while Sami Hyypiä could not direct a header from a McAllister corner towards goal. A Liverpool penalty appeal following Gerrard's run into the area was rejected before Kluivert's handball brought the award that mattered.

McAllister sent Reina the wrong way. Liverpool had reached half-time with an advantage, but still needed to prevent the away goal that would put Barcelona through.

Luis Enrique did find the net after the restart, only for the goal to be ruled out for offside. Liverpool had an anxious moment when Westerveld mis-kicked while trying to deal with a through-ball, but Kluivert could not capitalise before it ran out of play.

Emile Heskey tested Reina and Gerrard shot narrowly wide as Liverpool looked for a second. It did not come. Houllier used Patrik Berger, Danny Murphy and Robbie Fowler from the bench, and the home side protected the 1–0 lead through the closing stages.

Liverpool had kept Barcelona scoreless over both legs. Their first European final since 1985 would be against Alavés in Dortmund. With the League Cup already won and an FA Cup final also ahead, the chance of three trophies had survived one of the campaign's most demanding ties.

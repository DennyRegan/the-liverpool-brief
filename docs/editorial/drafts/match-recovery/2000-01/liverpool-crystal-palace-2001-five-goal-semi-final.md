Liverpool began the second leg a goal behind Crystal Palace. By the eighteenth minute they were 3–0 ahead on the night, with the League Cup semi-final transformed. A concentrated spell of finishing and invention removed the danger of the 2–1 defeat at Selhurst Park and carried Gérard Houllier towards his first Liverpool final.

Robbie Fowler captained the side and led the attack, with Jari Litmanen among those working behind him. Their combinations helped Liverpool establish the pace. Danny Murphy was denied an early goal by Aleksandrs Kolinko, but Palace could not keep closing the openings.

Vladimír Šmicer scored first, running onto Fowler's pass and finishing despite Wayne Carlisle's challenge. Murphy then volleyed in Litmanen's delivery to put Liverpool ahead on aggregate. Fowler supplied the next goal with a backheel for Igor Bišćan, whose finish was his first for the club.

Three goals in six minutes had changed the task completely. Palace now needed a substantial response against a Liverpool side still creating chances. They reached half-time without conceding again, but could not begin a recovery.

Murphy scored his second six minutes after the restart. Fowler, who had helped make the opening burst possible, eventually added a goal of his own. Palace were by then down to ten men after Kolinko's dismissal for a foul on the Liverpool captain; replacement goalkeeper Matt Gregg was beaten by Fowler's late finish from Murphy's pass.

The 5–0 victory made the aggregate score 6–2. Liverpool had reached their first major final since 1996, and the quality of the response mattered as much as the margin. A tie made awkward away from home had been settled by attacking partnerships that Palace could neither contain early nor disrupt later. Fowler scored once, but his influence ran through the whole evening.

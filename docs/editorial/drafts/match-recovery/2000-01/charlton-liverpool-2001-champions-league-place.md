Three trophies had been won, but Liverpool still needed a league result. Their sixty-third and final competitive match of the season offered the place in the Champions League qualifying round they had missed a year earlier. For forty-five minutes at Charlton, that last objective remained uncertain.

The home side created the better openings before the interval. Sander Westerveld kept Liverpool level, and Mathias Svensson headed against a post. Robbie Fowler started alongside Michael Owen, but Liverpool's forwards had little sustained service as Charlton's midfield controlled much of the play.

The response after half-time was decisive. Danny Murphy replaced Nick Barmby, and almost immediately Fowler put Liverpool ahead. A Gary McAllister corner was touched on by Markus Babbel and only partly cleared by goalkeeper Saša Ilić. With his back to goal, Fowler sent an overhead kick into the net.

Murphy added the second six minutes later from Owen's pass. The lead altered the demands of the afternoon: Liverpool could now attack a Charlton team being forced to open up, rather than search for a breakthrough while the possibility of losing third place remained close.

Owen created Fowler's second, then scored himself after a Mark Fish error. Four goals between the fifty-fifth and eightieth minutes turned a difficult first half into an emphatic 4–0 victory.

Liverpool finished on 69 points, one ahead of Leeds and one behind Arsenal. Third place secured entry to the qualifying stage of the Champions League; passage to the group stage would still have to be earned.

The distinction did not diminish the achievement at The Valley. The previous season had ended with defeat at Bradford and an opportunity lost. This time Liverpool followed the FA Cup final, the UEFA Cup final and the exhaustion of extra time in Dortmund with one more winning performance. Fowler's overhead kick began the final surge, and the season ended with every remaining task completed.

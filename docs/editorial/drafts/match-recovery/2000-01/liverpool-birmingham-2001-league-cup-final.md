Sander Westerveld's save from Andy Johnson ended Liverpool's wait for a trophy. Jamie Carragher had just scored their sixth penalty, putting them 5–4 ahead in the shootout. When Westerveld stopped Birmingham's reply, a final Liverpool had nearly won in normal time was finally theirs.

It was Gérard Houllier's first trophy as Liverpool manager and the club's first since the League Cup of 1995. Cardiff's Millennium Stadium, hosting the final while Wembley was rebuilt, provided a new setting for a familiar competition. Liverpool were favourites against First Division Birmingham, but Trevor Francis's side made them earn the result across two hours and penalties.

Robbie Fowler supplied the outstanding goal of the match on the half-hour. Emile Heskey's aerial touch brought the ball towards him outside the area, and Fowler struck it first time with his left foot. The shot carried over Ian Bennett and into the net.

The lead could have been extended. Vladimír Šmicer missed a chance before half-time, and Liverpool continued to find opportunities after the interval without producing the second goal. Birmingham kept themselves close enough for one late incident to change everything.

That incident arrived in stoppage time. Stéphane Henchoz brought down Martin O'Connor inside the area, and Darren Purse converted the penalty. A match that had seemed almost over was level at 1–1.

Extra time brought chances and controversy. Birmingham appealed for another penalty when Henchoz challenged Johnson, but David Elleray allowed play to continue. At the other end, Bennett kept Liverpool out and Dietmar Hamann struck a post from distance. Neither side found the goal that would avoid a shootout.

Liverpool began it well. Gary McAllister, Nick Barmby and Christian Ziege scored, while Westerveld saved Martin Grainger's kick. Hamann's failure to beat Bennett then brought Birmingham back into contention. Fowler converted Liverpool's fifth attempt, but the teams remained level after five kicks each.

Carragher stepped forward in sudden death and scored. Westerveld completed the job against Johnson, giving Liverpool the shootout 5–4 after the 1–1 draw.

The manner of victory contained much of what would recur in the months ahead: a fine attacking moment, chances left unused, a setback that threatened to undo the work, and enough resolve to recover. The FA Cup and UEFA Cup were still to be won. At Cardiff, Houllier's side had secured the first tangible reward of its rebuilding.

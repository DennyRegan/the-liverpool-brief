Liverpool reached the Champions League semi-finals by holding Juventus to a goalless draw in Turin. The 2–1 lead established at Anfield survived a return leg in which Rafael Benítez's side restricted their opponents' opportunities and had chances of their own to settle the tie.

Steven Gerrard missed the match with a groin injury. Xabi Alonso, making his first start since breaking an ankle in January, returned alongside Igor Bišćan in midfield. Jerzy Dudek replaced Scott Carson in goal, while Antonio Núñez came into the side for Anthony Le Tallec.

Liverpool's defensive organisation was central to the result. Djimi Traoré could move inside alongside Sami Hyypiä and Jamie Carragher, with John Arne Riise dropping back on the left. Ahead of them, Alonso and Bišćan worked to deny Pavel Nedvěd and Alessandro Del Piero space between midfield and defence.

A 1–0 Juventus victory would have eliminated Liverpool on away goals. The home side had an early opening when Gianluca Zambrotta crossed for Zlatan Ibrahimović, but the striker volleyed over. Juventus held possession for long periods without converting it into a sustained series of chances.

After the break, Milan Baroš had an opportunity to increase Liverpool's aggregate lead. Alonso's pass released him beyond Paolo Montero, but he shot wide under pressure from Lilian Thuram. Another Baroš effort was blocked by Fabio Cannavaro.

Juventus's greater danger came from set-pieces. Dudek saved Emerson's header, and Cannavaro struck a post with another header from Del Piero's free-kick in the closing stages. Liverpool survived that scare and kept the score at 0–0.

Djibril Cissé replaced Baroš for his first appearance since breaking his leg the previous October. His return added a personal milestone to a collective achievement: Liverpool had protected a narrow advantage without their captain and with Alonso only just back in the side.

The result secured a 2–1 aggregate victory and an all-English semi-final against Chelsea. Liverpool's goals in the first leg had put them ahead; their organisation and defensive concentration in Turin ensured that the advantage remained enough.

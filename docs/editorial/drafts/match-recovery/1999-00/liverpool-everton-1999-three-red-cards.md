Steve Staunton finished the Merseyside derby wearing the goalkeeper's gloves. Sander Westerveld had been sent off, Liverpool had used all three substitutes, and there was no way to bring on Jørgen Nielsen. Before the evening was over, Steven Gerrard would also be dismissed. Everton, however, had taken control of the result long before Liverpool lost control of their discipline.

Kevin Campbell scored after four minutes. Nick Barmby found Francis Jeffers, whose pass released his strike partner to beat Westerveld. It was a quick, direct move that left Liverpool pursuing the match almost from its beginning.

Gérard Houllier had started Robbie Fowler alongside Michael Owen, with Dietmar Hamann returning to midfield. The attacking names did not translate into sustained pressure. Everton remained organised and had opportunities to increase their advantage: Westerveld saved from a Jeffers header before the break, and the young forward missed another chance early in the second half.

Liverpool's changes brought on Erik Meijer and Gerrard for Hamann and Fowler, followed by Titi Camara for Vladimír Šmicer. All three replacements were therefore already involved when Westerveld and Jeffers clashed inside the penalty area. Mike Riley dismissed both, leaving Staunton to take over in goal for the closing stages.

That improvised arrangement did not cost another goal. Nor did it help Liverpool find an equaliser. Gerrard's high challenge on Campbell in stoppage time brought the evening's third red card and reduced the home side to nine.

Everton left Anfield with a 1–0 victory and sixth place. Liverpool remained twelfth, with four defeats from eight league matches. In Houllier's first full season as sole manager, the derby exposed how much rebuilding remained: an early opening conceded, too little created in response, and a finish in which the team's frustration became its most conspicuous feature.

The opportunity remained available elsewhere. Leeds drew at West Ham, so a Liverpool victory at Bradford would have secured third place and entry to the Champions League qualifying round. At Valley Parade, however, Liverpool never recovered from David Wetherall's early header. The season ended with Bradford celebrating survival and Liverpool confronting a chance they had let pass.

Gunnar Halle supplied the decisive free-kick after being fouled on the right. Wetherall met it in the penalty area and headed beyond Sander Westerveld. Liverpool had almost the whole afternoon to respond, but their possession rarely became a clear opening.

Their best first-half chance showed what Michael Owen and Emile Heskey could offer. Heskey flicked on for Owen, who got beyond the defence and goalkeeper Matt Clarke. Halle recovered to clear off the line. Having created Bradford's goal, the full-back had prevented Liverpool's answer.

Robbie Fowler was absent from the matchday squad. Houllier sought a change with Titi Camara and Vladimír Šmicer just after the hour, then sent on Erik Meijer late in the match. None of those adjustments produced a goal.

Bradford remained dangerous themselves. Substitute Isaiah Rankin missed an opening, and Dean Windass attempted a long lob that Westerveld tipped over. News of Southampton's goals against Wimbledon increased the home side's prospect of staying up; Liverpool could not turn that pressure into an advantage.

The 1–0 defeat left Liverpool fourth, two points behind Leeds. They had taken only two points from their last five matches and failed to score in any of them. A campaign that had put them second in April ended outside the Champions League places.

Houllier's first full season had brought improvement, but its final afternoon exposed the limits of that progress. The route back to Europe's leading club competition had been open. Liverpool could not find the goal, or the performance, to take it.

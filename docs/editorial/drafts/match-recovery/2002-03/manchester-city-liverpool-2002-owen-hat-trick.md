Michael Owen arrived at Maine Road without a league goal from open play that season. He left with a hat-trick. Liverpool's 3–0 victory over Manchester City on 28 September 2002 kept them second, two points behind Arsenal, and gave their leading striker an emphatic answer to questions about his form.

Gérard Houllier started Owen with Milan Baroš and Emile Heskey also in the side. Dietmar Hamann returned in midfield, while Djimi Traoré partnered Sami Hyypiä in central defence. Kevin Keegan's City included Nicolas Anelka, who had spent the second half of the previous season on loan at Liverpool.

Owen scored after four minutes. City failed to clear Danny Murphy's corner, and the loose ball reached him close to goal. The finish ended his wait quickly, but the first goal did not establish the easy afternoon suggested by the eventual score.

City found openings of their own. Darren Huckerby failed to take two early chances, and Anelka threatened the Liverpool goal. Peter Schmeichel also had to deny Owen as the game developed into a contest with opportunities at both ends.

Liverpool remained only one ahead until the 64th minute. Steven Gerrard then released Owen with a pass through City's defence. Owen ran clear, held off Sun Jihai and beat the advancing Schmeichel. It was the decisive expansion of Liverpool's lead, made by Gerrard's speed of thought and Owen's run and finish.

City continued to create chances, including a Marc-Vivien Foé header which went over and another Anelka effort saved by Jerzy Dudek. Liverpool preserved their clean sheet, however, and Owen completed his hat-trick late in the game, moving away from Sun before firing past Schmeichel.

The result left Liverpool unbeaten after eight league matches, with five wins, three draws and 18 points. All three goals at Maine Road belonged to Owen: an early finish in a crowded area, a break through the middle and a final strike to complete the afternoon. Liverpool had taken their chances through the player whose finishing had come under scrutiny.

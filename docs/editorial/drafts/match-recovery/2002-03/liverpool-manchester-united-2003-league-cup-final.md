Steven Gerrard put Liverpool ahead and Michael Owen finished the match, but Jerzy Dudek's goalkeeping held the victory together. Liverpool beat Manchester United 2–0 at Cardiff's Millennium Stadium on 2 March 2003 to win the League Cup for the seventh time.

The route to the final had included a penalty shoot-out against Ipswich Town, a 4–3 win at Aston Villa and extra time in the semi-final against Sheffield United. In Cardiff, Gérard Houllier selected Owen and Emile Heskey up front, with El Hadji Diouf also starting and Sami Hyypiä captaining the side.

United created a first-half opening when Paul Scholes released Ryan Giggs, whose cross Ruud van Nistelrooy turned wide. Liverpool then threatened through Owen before Gerrard scored six minutes before the interval. His shot from distance took a deflection off David Beckham and looped beyond Fabien Barthez.

The lead almost disappeared before half-time. Dudek blocked Juan Sebastián Verón's effort, and Stéphane Henchoz intervened close to the goal line to turn Scholes's follow-up over the bar. Liverpool reached the break 1–0 ahead.

United pressed harder in the second half. Dudek denied Van Nistelrooy and Scholes as Liverpool defended their narrow advantage. At the other end, Barthez saved from Gerrard after substitute Milan Baroš helped create a chance on the break. Liverpool remained capable of scoring again, even while their goalkeeper was required to keep United out.

Four minutes from time, Dietmar Hamann supplied the pass which settled the final. Owen ran through to beat Barthez for 2–0. Houllier made two late changes, introducing Igor Bišćan and Vladimír Šmicer, and Liverpool completed the clean sheet.

Dudek was named man of the match. His performance came after a costly mistake against United at Anfield earlier in the season, but the significance in Cardiff was immediate: Liverpool had protected Gerrard's goal until Owen could add the second. The trophy belonged to a side that had found its finishes and a goalkeeper who repeatedly prevented the reply.

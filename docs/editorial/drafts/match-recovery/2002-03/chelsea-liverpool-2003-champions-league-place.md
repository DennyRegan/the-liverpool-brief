For a few minutes at Stamford Bridge, Liverpool had the lead they needed. Sami Hyypiä headed them ahead in the final league match of the season, only for Chelsea to reply through Marcel Desailly and Jesper Grønkjær. The 2–1 defeat on 11 May 2003 left Liverpool fifth and gave Chelsea the last English place in the following season's Champions League qualifying rounds.

Liverpool had to win to overtake their opponents. A draw would be enough for Chelsea, making the first goal particularly valuable to Gérard Houllier's side. Danny Murphy delivered a free-kick and Hyypiä escaped his marker to head past Carlo Cudicini.

Chelsea's answer came quickly. Grønkjær crossed for Desailly to score with a header, restoring the position in which the home side would qualify. Then, in the 27th minute, Grønkjær scored himself with a curling shot from the edge of the area. Liverpool went into half-time behind, needing to score twice without conceding again.

Houllier had started Michael Owen and Milan Baroš, with El Hadji Diouf also in the team and Salif Diao in midfield. He replaced Diao with Emile Heskey at the interval, then introduced Patrik Berger for Diouf and Bruno Cheyrou for John Arne Riise as Liverpool sought a way back.

Baroš did get the ball over the line after Cudicini failed to hold a high ball, but the effort was disallowed for handball. Chelsea's lead survived, and Liverpool's afternoon ended with a further setback when Steven Gerrard was sent off for a late challenge on Graeme Le Saux.

The final table placed Chelsea fourth with 67 points and Liverpool fifth with 64. A Liverpool win would have reversed their positions, but the opening goal had been followed by two concessions and no valid reply.

Liverpool had won the League Cup in March. They would return to European competition through the UEFA Cup, with the chance of a third consecutive Champions League campaign lost on the last afternoon.

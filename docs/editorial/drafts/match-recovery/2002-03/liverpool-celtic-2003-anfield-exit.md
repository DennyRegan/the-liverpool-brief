A free-kick just before half-time and a long-range finish late in the second half ended Liverpool's UEFA Cup campaign on 20 March 2003. Alan Thompson and John Hartson scored as Celtic won 2–0 at Anfield and advanced from the quarter-final 3–1 on aggregate.

Liverpool had brought a 1–1 draw back from Glasgow. The away goal gave them a useful position, but Celtic's first goal at Anfield removed it. By the end, Liverpool had been unable to score the goals needed to recover the tie.

The opening half offered chances for both sides. Michael Owen's running helped Liverpool threaten, while Dietmar Hamann and Steven Gerrard tried from distance. Jerzy Dudek had to save from Henrik Larsson, and Celtic's defence also survived Liverpool pressure. The game remained level until the closing moments of the half.

Thompson then struck his free-kick low as Liverpool's wall jumped. The ball passed underneath and into the net, leaving the home side behind at the interval. Liverpool now had to find a way past Robert Douglas.

Their clearest route back came early in the second half. Owen found Gerrard running forward, but Douglas came out to block. Gerrard's movement had opened the defence; the goalkeeper prevented Liverpool turning that opening into an equaliser.

Gérard Houllier replaced Vladimír Šmicer with Milan Baroš after 55 minutes. Liverpool pressed, but Celtic retained a threat at the other end. Dudek kept out a Larsson effort before Hartson settled the contest in the closing stages, exchanging passes with Larsson and striking from outside the area.

That made the score 2–0 and left Liverpool needing three goals to win the tie. None came. Celtic's two finishes had decided a match in which Liverpool's attempts, including Gerrard's opportunity against Douglas, had brought no return.

Less than three weeks after winning the League Cup in Cardiff, Liverpool were out of Europe. The UEFA Cup run which had followed elimination from the Champions League ended at home, with Celtic taking the semi-final place.

# Liverpool 2–1 Everton: Rush and Fowler win the standing Kop’s last derby

The standing Kop’s final Merseyside derby ended with Liverpool coming from behind to win. Ian Rush answered Everton’s opening goal almost immediately, and Robbie Fowler settled the match just before half-time.

This was Roy Evans’s first derby as Liverpool manager and Fowler’s first as a senior player. The 18-year-old was returning from injury, and his winning goal gave Liverpool a 2–1 victory in the last meeting of the clubs before the Kop’s summer reconstruction.

Everton went ahead when Dave Watson headed in Preki’s free-kick. Their advantage barely survived the restart. Julian Dicks sent a long ball through the visiting defence, Rush let it bounce and finished past Neville Southall. It was his 25th goal in Merseyside derbies.

Liverpool’s attacking play improved after the equaliser. Steve McManaman threatened Southall’s goal, but it was Fowler who put them ahead in the 44th minute. John Barnes supplied the through-ball; Fowler timed his run beyond the defence and scored from the left of the penalty area as Southall advanced.

The angle was narrowing, but Fowler chose the shot and found the far side of the goal. The supporter who had grown up following Everton had scored the winner against them on his derby debut.

Southall kept Everton within reach in the second half, saving from Rush and Barnes. As Liverpool tired, the visitors applied more pressure. David James preserved the lead near the end with a save from Peter Beagrie’s volley.

Both Liverpool goals had been scored towards the Kop. For its final derby as a standing terrace, the familiar scorer and the new one had each supplied a goal, while James’s late intervention ensured they were enough.

Liverpool’s first league defeat of 2006–07 came at Goodison Park and exposed weaknesses at both ends of the pitch. Everton won 3–0, with Tim Cahill opening the scoring and Andy Johnson adding two goals, in their biggest victory over Liverpool since 1964.

Benítez started with Peter Crouch and Robbie Fowler up front. Jamie Carragher returned alongside Sami Hyypiä in central defence, while Fábio Aurélio played at left-back. Everton’s attacking reference point was Johnson, supported by Cahill, whose movement from midfield brought the first goal.

In the 24th minute Mikel Arteta crossed, Lee Carsley helped the ball on and Cahill finished from close range. Liverpool appealed over Leon Osman’s involvement against Steve Finnan in the build-up, but the goal stood. Their immediate response included a Luis García shot saved by Tim Howard and a Gerrard follow-up against the post.

Instead of an equaliser, the next goal went to Everton. Liverpool failed to clear and Johnson took advantage of the confusion around Hyypiä and Carragher, finishing beyond Reina. Everton carried a 2–0 lead into half-time.

Dirk Kuyt replaced Crouch and Riise came on for Fowler as Liverpool sought a different attack. Gerrard hit the post again, and a follow-up from Kuyt struck Tony Hibbert’s arm without producing a penalty. The chances and appeals offered Liverpool no change to the score.

Their problems increased after the last substitution. Riise injured his ankle and had to be carried off, leaving Liverpool with ten men. In stoppage time Reina failed to deal with Carsley’s long-range shot and Johnson headed the loose ball into the net.

Everton had made the most of Liverpool’s defensive mistakes and survived the moments when the visitors threatened. The result was an early warning for Benítez: possession and occasional chances offered little protection when his side conceded so readily. The three-goal margin made a difficult afternoon a particularly severe derby defeat.

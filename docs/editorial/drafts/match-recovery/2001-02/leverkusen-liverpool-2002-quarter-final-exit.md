Jari Litmanen's goal had put Liverpool back in a qualifying position. With the score 3–2 to Bayer Leverkusen on the night, the Champions League quarter-final was level on aggregate and Liverpool held the advantage on away goals. Then Lúcio came forward to score, completing a 4–2 home win and a 4–3 aggregate victory for Leverkusen on 9 April 2002.

Liverpool had arrived at the BayArena with a 1–0 lead from the first leg. Emile Heskey had an early opportunity to extend it, but his first touch let him down.

Michael Ballack cancelled the first-leg advantage with a powerful shot after 16 minutes. Leverkusen attacked with pace, but Liverpool retained a route through on the break. Michael Owen missed an opportunity before Abel Xavier headed in Danny Murphy's corner shortly before half-time. Liverpool were level on the night and ahead again in the tie.

Litmanen had replaced Heskey before the interval. Leverkusen also changed their attack, bringing on Oliver Neuville and Dimitar Berbatov for the second half. Owen struck a post as Liverpool sought another away goal, but the decisive spell initially belonged to the hosts.

Ballack headed his second, then Berbatov scored to make it 3–1. Liverpool had lost the aggregate lead. Houllier had introduced Vladimír Šmicer for Dietmar Hamann and later replaced Xavier with Patrik Berger as the tie changed around them.

Litmanen appeared to have rescued it. Taking Murphy's pass inside the area, he moved away from the defenders and finished low past Hans-Jörg Butt. At 3–3 overall, Liverpool's two away goals put them in position to advance.

That position lasted only a few minutes. Yıldıray Baştürk found Lúcio arriving in the area, and the defender beat Jerzy Dudek from a tight angle. There was no further Liverpool reply. Two away goals, including Litmanen's late intervention, had been insufficient to survive Leverkusen's four.

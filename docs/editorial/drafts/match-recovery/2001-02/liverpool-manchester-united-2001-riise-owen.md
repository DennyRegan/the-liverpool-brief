John Arne Riise supplied the spectacular goal, but Michael Owen's second was the one that stopped Manchester United's recovery. Barely had David Beckham reduced Liverpool's lead when Owen headed in at the other end. Liverpool won 3–1 at Anfield on 4 November 2001 and moved into second place in the Premiership.

The Kop displayed a mosaic for Gérard Houllier before kick-off. Phil Thompson directed the side, with Owen and Emile Heskey together in attack.

Owen opened the scoring after half an hour. Šmicer's forward ball put United's defence under pressure, Heskey challenged Wes Brown, and Owen reached the opening to finish past Fabien Barthez. Heskey's involvement mattered again later: both of Owen's goals followed his work in the penalty area.

Before half-time Brown fouled Owen within shooting range. Dietmar Hamann moved the free-kick sideways for Riise, whose left-footed drive struck the underside of the bar and went in. Liverpool took a 2–0 lead into the interval.

United's chance to alter the game came early in the second half. Riise failed to deal with Denis Irwin's cross and Beckham scored. The response was immediate. Riise sent a long throw into the area, Heskey helped it on and Owen headed into the exposed goal. United had scarcely begun to benefit from their first goal before the two-goal margin was restored.

Owen and Šmicer later made way for Robbie Fowler and Patrik Berger. United introduced Dwight Yorke, Paul Scholes and John O'Shea, but there were no further goals. Liverpool finished with 22 points from ten league games, one behind Leeds United with a game in hand. Riise's strike gave the afternoon its lasting image; the partnership of Heskey and Owen had supplied the other two goals that made the result secure.

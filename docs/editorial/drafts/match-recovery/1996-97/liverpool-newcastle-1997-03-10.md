# Fowler wins another 4–3 after Liverpool lose a three-goal lead

**Liverpool 4–3 Newcastle United | 10 March 1997**  
Premier League · Anfield · Season 1996-97

Liverpool were 3–0 ahead at half-time. With stoppage time approaching, the score was 3–3.

Then Stig Inge Bjørnebye crossed from the left and Robbie Fowler headed past Shaka Hislop. Eleven months after Stan Collymore had settled the same fixture with a late winner, Liverpool had beaten Newcastle 4–3 again.

The score was familiar. The route to it was different.

Kenny Dalglish now occupied Newcastle’s dugout. His side began without Alan Shearer, while Les Ferdinand was only fit enough for the bench. For much of the evening, Liverpool made the visitors look incapable of contributing to another seven-goal match.

Steve McManaman opened the scoring with a controlled finish from the edge of the area. Almost immediately, Fowler struck a post and Patrik Berger put away the rebound.

Fowler added the third before half-time. Liverpool had scored through three different players and were moving the ball with a confidence Newcastle could not match.

Dalglish sent on Ferdinand after the break, but the striker soon had to leave the field again. Liverpool’s advantage remained intact until Keith Gillespie scored in the 71st minute.

Even then, Newcastle needed two more. They found both in the closing stages.

Faustino Asprilla punished a mistake by Jamie Redknapp to make it 3–2. Warren Barton then forced in the equaliser after the ball broke loose in the Liverpool penalty area. A match that had appeared settled was level.

Liverpool still had time for one more attack. Bjørnebye sent his cross into the box, and Fowler angled his header across Hislop to win it in stoppage time.

The victory left Liverpool one point behind Manchester United. It also left Evans with a performance that resisted any straightforward verdict. His side had played well enough to establish a three-goal lead, defended poorly enough to lose it, then found the quality to win after all.

Fowler’s second goal rescued two points. Without it, the first-half football would have been remembered chiefly for what Liverpool threw away.

# Michael Owen scores on debut as Liverpool lose at Wimbledon

**Wimbledon 2–1 Liverpool | 6 May 1997**  
Premier League · Selhurst Park · Season 1996-97

Michael Owen needed 16 minutes to score his first Liverpool goal. His debut offered the clearest encouragement on an evening when defeat at Wimbledon finally ended Liverpool’s mathematical chance of winning the league.

He was 17 years and 144 days old. By scoring, he became the youngest player to have found the net for Liverpool’s first team at that point in the club’s history.

The circumstances of his introduction were unpromising. Jason Euell had headed Wimbledon in front shortly before half-time, meeting Alan Kimble’s free-kick. Dean Holdsworth added a second after the interval from Neal Ardley’s cross.

Liverpool had again been punished by deliveries into their penalty area. Their attacking play had produced too little to compensate.

Roy Evans sent Owen on for Patrik Berger in the 58th minute. With Liverpool 2–0 down, the teenager was being asked to make an immediate difference in a match the senior players had not brought under control.

In the 74th minute, he did. Stig Inge Bjørnebye’s pass found him and Owen finished past Neil Sullivan.

Liverpool’s best spell followed, but they could not find an equaliser. The match finished 2–1, leaving them on 67 points with one league game remaining. Manchester United already had 71.

Owen’s goal therefore changed neither the result nor the title arithmetic. Its significance was personal and immediate: on his first appearance, a young forward had entered a struggling team and scored.

Liverpool still had the pursuit of second place to consider. For the longer term, they had been given a first senior glimpse of a player who would become central to their attack. A defeat that confirmed one disappointment also contained Owen’s beginning.

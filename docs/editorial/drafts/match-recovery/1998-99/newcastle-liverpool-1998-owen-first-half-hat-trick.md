Michael Owen had completed his hat-trick by the 32nd minute. By half-time, Patrik Berger had added a fourth and Liverpool had done all the scoring they would need to beat Newcastle United 4–1 at St James' Park on 30 August 1998.

It was an early demonstration of what Roy Evans and Gérard Houllier's joint management could produce. Liverpool remained unbeaten and went top of the Premier League, their passing and movement giving an eighteen-year-old striker repeated chances to run at an unsettled defence.

Newcastle were beginning another managerial chapter. Ruud Gullit had replaced Kenny Dalglish and watched the first half from the directors' box before moving to the touchline after the interval. The anticipation surrounding his arrival had barely had time to settle when Liverpool took the game away from his new side.

Owen's first two goals came in consecutive minutes. Paul Ince's long-range shot forced Shay Given to push the ball away, but Owen reached the rebound and scored from a narrow angle. Then Steve McManaman released him through the middle. Owen drew Given and finished beneath him to make it 2–0.

The chances were different, but both depended on Owen reaching a position before the defence could recover. The first required an immediate response to a loose ball; the second rewarded a run into the space McManaman had identified. Newcastle were having to turn and chase a forward whose finishing gave them little allowance for delay.

They briefly reduced the damage. Rob Lee took possession from Phil Babb and supplied Stéphane Guivarc'h, who scored on his Newcastle debut. At 2–1, a contest which seemed to be escaping the home side was available again.

Owen restored the two-goal advantage within minutes. Karl-Heinz Riedle helped win the ball, Owen accelerated forward and kept his balance through a challenge from Philippe Albert before finishing his third. Liverpool had answered Newcastle's attempted revival with the same qualities that had established their lead.

Berger completed the first-half scoring. He dispossessed Laurent Charvet, went past Steve Watson and drove his shot across Given. Four goals had exposed Newcastle's difficulty both in protecting the space behind their defence and in keeping possession close to it.

Gullit's changes helped stem the flow after half-time, but they did not produce a recovery. The score remained 4–1. Liverpool could leave with three points, first place and a performance in which Owen's finishing had given their attacking play its full reward. All three of his goals had arrived before Newcastle could reach the interval and reorganise.

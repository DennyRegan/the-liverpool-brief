Liverpool led Manchester United from the third minute until the 88th. By the final whistle, they had lost both the lead and their place in the FA Cup. Dwight Yorke's equaliser and Ole Gunnar Solskjær's stoppage-time winner turned a fourth-round tie at Old Trafford into a 2–1 defeat on 24 January 1999.

Michael Owen had given Liverpool the start they wanted. Vegard Heggem delivered from the right and Owen found room between United's defenders to head past Peter Schmeichel. It was a precise reward for an early attack, and for most of the afternoon it appeared capable of deciding the match.

Gérard Houllier's side defended with Jamie Carragher, Dominic Matteo and Steve Harkness at their centre. United pushed them back, but Liverpool repeatedly kept bodies between the ball and David James's goal. Their route forward was more occasional: an opportunity to release Owen, or to find Robbie Fowler before the home defence could recover.

There were narrow escapes. Roy Keane hit a post with a header, with Paul Ince on the line as the ball rebounded. Keane struck the woodwork again in the second half. At the other end, Fowler had chances to extend Liverpool's advantage and make United's pursuit considerably harder.

The score remained 1–0 through the interval and deep into the second half. Ince was replaced by Jason McAteer, while Alex Ferguson brought on Paul Scholes and later added Ronny Johnsen and Solskjær. United were still looking for one goal; Liverpool were running out the minutes needed to preserve the one they already had.

The equaliser began with a free-kick awarded against Jamie Redknapp. Beckham delivered, Andy Cole headed the ball back across goal and Yorke finished. Houllier disputed the decision which had created the opening, but Liverpool now faced the prospect of a replay rather than a completed victory.

Even that did not survive. In added time, the ball reached Solskjær inside the penalty area and the substitute beat James. The goalkeeper was wrong-footed by a finish directed towards the near side. A tie Liverpool had led for almost its entire length was over in United's favour.

Liverpool had not beaten United in the FA Cup since 1921. That history made the lost opportunity particularly sharp, but the immediate defeat needed no historical embellishment. Their early goal and sustained defending had taken them close to the fifth round. Within the closing minutes, two United forwards had reduced all of it to elimination.

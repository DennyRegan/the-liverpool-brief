Paul Ince arrived in the penalty area in the 89th minute and gave Liverpool the equaliser they had spent much of the evening appearing unlikely to score. Manchester United had led 2–0 at Anfield. They left on 5 May 1999 with a 2–2 draw and two points lost in their pursuit of Arsenal.

The scorer added an unavoidable personal history to the moment. Ince was Liverpool's captain and a former United midfielder, subjected to the visiting supporters' attention throughout the match. His late intervention turned a difficult night into a recovery Liverpool could celebrate.

Gérard Houllier had neither Michael Owen nor Robbie Fowler available. Karl-Heinz Riedle led the attack, with Steve McManaman supporting him and Rigobert Song and Dominic Matteo providing width. Liverpool began energetically, but United converted their own opening into something more decisive.

David Beckham exchanged passes with Roy Keane after a free-kick on the right, then crossed for Dwight Yorke to head past Brad Friedel in the 23rd minute. Liverpool had been active without obtaining an equivalent reward. United reached half-time a goal ahead.

The advantage doubled early in the second half. Jamie Carragher's challenge on Jesper Blomqvist brought a penalty, and Denis Irwin converted. Liverpool now needed two goals against a side whose counter-attacking threat had already punished them.

Patrik Berger replaced Song, and Liverpool eventually found a way back from the penalty spot themselves. Blomqvist challenged Øyvind Leonhardsen as a cross reached the far side of the area; David Elleray awarded the second penalty of the match. Jamie Redknapp scored in the 69th minute to halve the deficit.

United's position became more difficult when Irwin received a second yellow card for kicking the ball away. Ferguson introduced Nicky Butt and Phil Neville, while Liverpool kept pressing for the goal which would rescue their evening. Riedle remained the central attacking reference, but Ince was now joining him in the search for a finish.

The equaliser followed a Leonhardsen break and Riedle's involvement in the area. Ince slid in to score, completing the recovery just before normal time expired. Liverpool had taken one point from a match which had seemed to be yielding none.

The table preserved the wider consequence. Arsenal had 75 points from 36 matches; United had 72 from 35. Liverpool were seventh, well outside that title contest, but their intervention had kept it tighter. The significance at Anfield was immediate enough: a two-goal deficit against United had been erased, and Ince had supplied the final touch.

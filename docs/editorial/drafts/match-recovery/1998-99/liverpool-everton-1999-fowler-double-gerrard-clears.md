Liverpool's first derby victory in five years depended on contributions at opposite ends of the pitch. Robbie Fowler scored twice to overturn Everton's early lead, Patrik Berger made it 3–1, and Steven Gerrard helped ensure the visitors' late recovery stopped one goal short. The 3–2 win at Anfield on 3 April 1999 ended a run of nine meetings without a Liverpool victory.

Before kick-off, both sets of supporters observed a minute's silence ahead of the tenth anniversary of the Hillsborough disaster. The match which followed began at once. Olivier Dacourt struck inside the opening minute, his shot taking a deflection past David James to put Everton ahead.

Walter Smith's side needed points near the foot of the table. For Liverpool, the local importance of the fixture was accompanied by a need to end a difficult league run. They had not won in the competition for two months.

Fowler changed the score twice before the first half was halfway through. Marco Materazzi's challenge on Paul Ince brought a penalty, which Fowler converted in the 15th minute. Soon afterwards, Steve McManaman flicked on Berger's corner and Fowler headed in at close range. The opening deficit had become a 2–1 lead.

His celebration of the penalty became a separate and lasting controversy. In front of Everton supporters, Fowler knelt at the white line and mimicked cocaine use. He had been subjected to unfounded allegations about drug-taking and apologised the following day. The gesture drew attention away from two finishes which had put Liverpool in control of the score.

Everton continued to threaten. Materazzi hit a post with a free-kick before the interval, while Michael Owen forced Thomas Myhre into a save at the other end. Liverpool reached half-time ahead, but the match offered little reason to assume that one goal would be sufficient.

Gerrard replaced Vegard Heggem in the 71st minute for his first Merseyside derby appearance. Eleven minutes later, Berger struck a low volley after a corner was only partly cleared. At 3–1, Liverpool appeared to have secured the distance they needed.

Francis Jeffers immediately reduced it, scoring in the 84th minute. Everton pressed again, and Gerrard cleared Danny Cadamarteri's shot off the line with James out of position. The substitute's defensive work preserved the advantage Fowler and Berger had created.

Liverpool finished the afternoon eighth; Everton remained seventeenth. The league table was not transformed, but a long sequence of derby frustration had ended. The winning margin was narrow enough to give Gerrard's intervention a place alongside the goals: on his first appearance in the fixture, he helped protect a victory Liverpool had waited years to obtain.

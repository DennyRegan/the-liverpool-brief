# Liverpool survive Everton’s recovery to make it six wins from six

Liverpool’s sixth successive league victory of 1990–91 demanded a late defensive effort after a three-goal lead had almost disappeared. A 3–2 win at Goodison Park on 22 September preserved their perfect start, with Peter Beardsley scoring twice six days after his hat-trick against Manchester United.

Everton put Liverpool under pressure before the visitors struck twice in quick succession. Beardsley opened the scoring with a direct free-kick after a foul on John Barnes. Barnes’s movement across the goal added to Neville Southall’s difficulties as the ball found the net.

Soon afterwards, Beardsley sent Ian Rush through and Ray Atteveld brought the striker down. Barnes converted the penalty to give Liverpool a 2–0 lead that they carried into half-time. The two goals had transformed the score in a match in which Everton had repeatedly forced the champions back.

Beardsley added his second midway through the second half, taking Liverpool three clear. It was his fifth goal across the two fixtures against United and Everton, but the remaining minutes brought a very different contest from the previous weekend’s comfortable finish at Anfield.

Andy Hinchcliffe scored after Everton took a free-kick quickly. Later, Stuart McCall’s backward header struck the crossbar and rebounded into the net off Glenn Hysén. Liverpool’s advantage was down to one, with Everton still searching for an equaliser.

Bruce Grobbelaar made important late saves and Ronnie Whelan cleared off the line as Liverpool held on. Beardsley and Barnes had supplied the goals; the final stages required their team-mates to protect them.

Liverpool finished the afternoon on 18 points, four ahead of Arsenal. The opening league sequence would extend to eight wins before Norwich City finally took a point from them in October.

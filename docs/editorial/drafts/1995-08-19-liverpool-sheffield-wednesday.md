Stan Collymore began his Liverpool career with the goal that won their opening league match of 1995–96. His 61st-minute strike gave Roy Evans's side a 1–0 victory over Sheffield Wednesday at Anfield on 19 August.

Liverpool had paid Nottingham Forest £8.5 million for the striker that summer. On his competitive debut, Evans partnered him with Ian Rush, leaving Robbie Fowler among the substitutes. John Barnes, Jamie Redknapp and Steve McManaman also started, but Wednesday reached half-time without conceding.

David Pleat's visitors had chances of their own. David James saved from Dan Petrescu, while Chris Waddle sent a volley narrowly wide before the interval. At the other end, Redknapp struck the crossbar from distance. Wednesday goalkeeper Kevin Pressman then denied both Redknapp and Steve Harkness after the restart.

Collymore settled the match with another attempt from outside the penalty area. He made space as the defenders retreated and curled a left-footed shot beyond Pressman. The goalkeeper could not reach it, and Liverpool finally had their lead.

Pressman prevented McManaman from adding a second after Barnes had played him through. Collymore also shot over before Evans made his late changes. Fowler replaced Rush, and Michael Thomas subsequently came on for the goalscorer.

The crowd of 40,535 had seen Liverpool secure three points and a clean sheet. Collymore's first appearance had supplied something more memorable than a routine opening-day win: a debut goal struck from distance, with his left foot, that proved enough to decide the match.

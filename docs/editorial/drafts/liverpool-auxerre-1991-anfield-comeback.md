Mark Walters scored seven minutes from time to complete Liverpool’s recovery from a two-goal first-leg defeat against Auxerre. The 3–0 win at Anfield on 6 November 1991 sent Graeme Souness’s side into the UEFA Cup third round, 3–2 ahead on aggregate.

Liverpool had erased the deficit before half-time. Jan Mølby converted a fourth-minute penalty after Stéphane Mahé brought down Steve McManaman, giving the home side the early breakthrough they needed.

Mike Marsh supplied the second. Selected at right-back, an unfamiliar position for the midfielder, he advanced to meet Ray Houghton’s cross at the far post and headed in his first senior Liverpool goal. Less than half an hour had passed, and the tie was level.

Auxerre still threatened to undo that work. Bruce Grobbelaar had already made an important early save and denied Alain Roche after the interval. Liverpool needed a third goal while keeping out opponents whose first-leg lead had given them control of the tie.

The French side were reduced to ten men with 15 minutes remaining when Frédéric Darras received a second booking for fouling Walters. Eight minutes later, Mølby sent Walters through. He reached the ball ahead of goalkeeper Bruno Martini and finished inside the far post in front of the Kop.

The crowd of 23,094 had seen Liverpool overturn a two-goal first-leg deficit in Europe for the first time. Marsh’s first goal had restored parity; Walters’s late finish ensured there would be no need for extra time.

Liverpool dismissed Brendan Rodgers on 4 October 2015, hours after a 1–1 draw with Everton at Goodison Park. His contract was terminated with immediate effect, ending a tenure that had begun in June 2012.

The final match offered a lead but not a victory. Danny Ings headed Liverpool in front from a corner in the 41st minute; Romelu Lukaku equalised before half-time. The draw left Liverpool tenth in the Premier League, with 12 points from eight matches.

In their statement that evening, Liverpool's owners thanked Rodgers for his contribution while arguing that a change gave the club a better prospect of success. They confirmed that the search for a new manager was underway. This was a dismissal, not a resignation or the announcement of an agreed successor.

Rodgers's time at Liverpool had contained a substantial achievement of its own. After finishing seventh in his first season, his team rose to second in 2013–14, scoring 101 league goals. Liverpool ended that campaign with 84 points, two behind Manchester City. Champions League qualification was restored, and the title remained in contention until the final day.

The following season did not sustain that progress. Liverpool finished sixth, went out of the Champions League at the group stage and lost in the semi-finals of both domestic cups. The league campaign ended with a 6–1 defeat at Stoke City. Rodgers remained in charge for the start of 2015–16, but the derby was his last match.

The club's departure statement acknowledged the title challenge as well as Rodgers's work with young players. Its decision to end his contract did not erase those contributions; equally, the height of 2013–14 had not been maintained. The contrast between that second-place finish and Liverpool's subsequent results was central to the record he left behind.

On 4 October, the immediate outcome was the end of Rodgers's three years and four months in charge. Liverpool's statement promised a prompt search for the next manager, but the day's confirmed decision concerned the manager who was leaving.

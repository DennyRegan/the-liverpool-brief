New England Sports Ventures completed its purchase of Liverpool Football Club on Friday 15 October 2010. The transaction valued the club at £300 million and ended the ownership of Tom Hicks and George Gillett, who had bought Liverpool in February 2007.

The new ownership group, led by John Henry and Tom Werner, also owned the Boston Red Sox. Its arrival followed a sale process overseen by Liverpool chairman Martin Broughton and a dispute that had carried the club's future through courts in England and the United States.

Liverpool's board had agreed to sell to NESV on 6 October. Agreement was only one stage of the transaction. Hicks and Gillett opposed the sale, having attempted to remove managing director Christian Purslow and commercial director Ian Ayre from the board. Broughton maintained that the attempted changes breached written undertakings governing the sale process.

On 13 October, the High Court ruled in favour of Royal Bank of Scotland's application for orders concerning the board. Broughton said the board would be reconstituted that evening and continue with the sale. The ruling cleared a substantial obstacle, but ownership had not yet passed to NESV.

A restraining order obtained in Texas created a further barrier. Its withdrawal allowed the sale to proceed on the Friday. Henry then appeared with Broughton at the London offices of solicitors Slaughter and May, where the change of ownership was announced.

The financial distinction in that announcement was specific. NESV said the purchase removed the acquisition debt placed on Liverpool by the previous owners. It put the resulting annual debt-servicing costs at £2 million to £3 million, compared with £25 million to £30 million beforehand. These were the incoming owners' stated figures. They described a reduction in the burden of debt, rather than an assertion that Liverpool would have no debt or financing costs at all.

The sale also resolved the immediate threat surrounding repayment to Royal Bank of Scotland. Contemporary reporting put the amount owed to the bank at £237 million and described administration as a danger that the completed transaction had avoided.

At his first appearance after the sale, Henry did not announce a transfer budget or commit to a new stadium. He emphasised winning and being judged by the owners' actions. In the accompanying statement, Werner said the group would begin by listening to the manager, players, staff, supporters and local community. Those were commitments made on taking over, with the practical decisions still ahead.

Nine days after the board accepted its offer, NESV was confirmed as Liverpool's owner.

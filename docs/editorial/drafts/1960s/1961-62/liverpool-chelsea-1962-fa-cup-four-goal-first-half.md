Liverpool scored four times before half-time against Chelsea at Anfield on 6 January 1962, then had to protect a shrinking lead to reach the FA Cup’s fourth round. A 4–1 interval advantage became a 4–3 victory after Bobby Tambling and Barry Bridges brought the visitors back into the tie.

Bill Shankly’s Second Division leaders had lost their last two league games, both 1–0, at Leeds United and Rotherham United. Chelsea supplied First Division opposition for the start of the cup campaign, and an early exchange of goals gave neither side time to settle into a lead.

Ian St John scored in the 16th minute, with Roger Hunt involved in the opening. Tambling equalised two minutes later. Liverpool’s response was more substantial: Hunt restored the lead before the half-hour, following Ian Callaghan’s contribution, and two further goals arrived shortly before the break.

St John scored his second, with Alan A’Court supplying him, before the pair exchanged roles for Liverpool’s fourth. A’Court’s goal left Chelsea three behind at half-time. Liverpool had turned an evenly scored opening into a position from which a home passage looked increasingly likely.

Chelsea made it difficult. Tambling scored again midway through the second half, and Bridges reduced the deficit to one with roughly a quarter of an hour remaining. Liverpool could no longer treat their first-half work as a comfortable margin, but they conceded no further goal.

The victory sent them on to Oldham Athletic in the fourth round. It also began an extraordinary January week at Anfield: seven days later, Liverpool would win another match by a single goal, this time 5–4 against Norwich City.

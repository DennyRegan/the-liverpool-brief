Terry Allcock scored a second-half hat-trick at Anfield on 13 January 1962 and still left with a defeat. Liverpool beat Norwich City 5–4, with two goals each from Roger Hunt and Jimmy Melia, in a match which repeatedly threatened to undo the home side’s lead.

A week after Liverpool’s 4–3 FA Cup win over Chelsea, another visiting attack made them work for victory. Melia put Liverpool ahead after 25 minutes, but Jimmy Conway equalised three minutes later. Hunt converted Alan A’Court’s cross before the interval to restore the advantage.

Allcock’s first goal followed a poor defensive header by John Molyneux early in the second half. At 2–2, Norwich were level for the second time. Hunt answered almost immediately, collecting Melia’s mishit attempt and scoring past Geoff Barnsley. Liverpool then went 4–2 ahead when A’Court’s delivery produced another goal around the hour.

That two-goal cushion lasted only briefly. Allcock headed in off the underside of the bar to bring Norwich back to 4–3. With ten minutes left, Melia volleyed Liverpool’s fifth, putting the gap back to two; once again it was insufficient to settle the contest entirely.

Allcock completed his hat-trick in the closing minutes. Liverpool held their 5–4 lead to the finish, having scored nine and conceded seven across two consecutive home victories. The attack had supplied enough on both occasions, but each match had required a lead to be defended until the end.

Liverpool ultimately completed their entire home league programme unbeaten, winning 18 matches and drawing three. Norwich’s four goals were an unusually severe test within that record: across all 21 league fixtures at Anfield, Liverpool conceded 19.

Kevin Lewis scored twice as Liverpool beat Southampton 2–0 at Anfield on 21 April 1962, securing promotion and the Second Division championship with five league matches to spare. Eight seasons outside the top flight were over.

Lewis took the place of the suspended Ian St John. Liverpool needed a draw to make promotion certain and a victory to settle the title; the replacement centre-forward supplied both goals before half-time.

Alan A’Court’s cross struck a post during Liverpool’s early pressure. The first goal followed in the 19th minute, from a Ronnie Moran free-kick. Roger Hunt helped the ball forward, it came off Tony Knapp to Lewis, and the striker’s first effort was blocked. His next went in off the post.

Ten minutes later, Liverpool turned defence into attack. Ian Callaghan received the ball on the right and crossed; Hunt headed it back across goal, Tony Godfrey pushed it away, and Lewis headed the rebound into the net. The player brought in for St John had given Liverpool a decisive advantage.

The muddy conditions and two-goal lead made the later stages less fluent. Liverpool had further opportunities but needed no third goal. At the finish, the crowd called the players back out, and supporters spilled onto the pitch during the celebrations.

Lewis’s double completed the central task of Bill Shankly’s second full season. The campaign would end with Liverpool on 62 points, eight ahead of Leyton Orient, with 99 league goals scored. Hunt supplied 41 of those and St John 18, but the goals which confirmed the return to the First Division belonged to Lewis.

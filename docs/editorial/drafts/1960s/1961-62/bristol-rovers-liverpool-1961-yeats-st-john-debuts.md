Liverpool began the 1961–62 league season with a 2–0 victory at Bristol Rovers on 19 August. Ron Yeats and Ian St John made their league debuts, but Kevin Lewis supplied the opening goal before a Johnny Hills own goal put the result beyond Rovers.

Eastville had been an unhappy destination four months earlier. Lewis had scored three times there in April and still lost 4–3, during a promotion challenge which ended with Liverpool third. This return brought a clean sheet and the first two points of a fresh attempt to leave the Second Division.

Lewis scored after seven minutes. Liverpool took that advantage into the interval, and Hills’s own goal in the second half gave them a second. The two newcomers could begin their league careers for Bill Shankly with a win, without either having to score it.

St John had arrived from Motherwell in May, Yeats from Dundee United in July. The additions changed the centre of the side, although Dick White remained captain at Eastville. Yeats lined up between Gordon Milne and Tommy Leishman in the half-back line, with St John at centre-forward and Roger Hunt at inside-right.

Horace Yates’s assessment in the Liverpool Football Echo offered a measured first impression of Yeats. He praised the new defender’s aerial reach and positioning, while observing that his headers were not always directed to a teammate. The value already visible was his ability to interrupt Rovers’ attacks; refinement could follow as he settled.

Liverpool went on to beat Sunderland 3–0 and Leeds United 5–0 at Anfield. Three victories without conceding made this a markedly different start from the previous campaign, when the first five league games had yielded one win.

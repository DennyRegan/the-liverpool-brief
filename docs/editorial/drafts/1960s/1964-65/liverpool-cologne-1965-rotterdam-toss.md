Liverpool reached the European Cup semi-finals after a 2–2 draw with Cologne in Rotterdam on 24 March 1965, but the result on the field did not decide who advanced. After three matches and five hours of football, a toss settled a tie that neither side had been able to win.

The first meeting in Germany had finished 0–0 on 10 February. The Anfield return, eventually played on 17 March after a postponement, produced the same score. A third match at Stadion Feyenoord was therefore required to separate the English and German champions.

For a while, Liverpool appeared to have found the answer without recourse to chance. Roger Hunt reached the byline and supplied Ian St John for a close-range opener after twenty-two minutes. Hunt then scored himself, his looping header going in off the crossbar. With half-time approaching, Liverpool led 2–0.

Cologne’s response began before the break. Karl-Heinz Thielen headed in to reduce the deficit, and Hannes Löhr equalised shortly after the restart. Liverpool’s two-goal advantage had disappeared either side of the interval.

The German side also had to contend with an injury to Wolfgang Weber. Thielen later recalled his teammate playing on despite a broken fibula, with no substitute available. Cologne’s account records a subsequent goal being disallowed, but neither side added to the four goals that stood.

Extra time left the score unchanged. The two earlier ninety-minute games and this additional two hours had produced no winner, and the deciding procedure was a toss rather than a penalty shootout.

Even that required a second attempt. Cologne’s account describes a wooden disc, red on one side and white on the other, which first lodged upright in the turf. Ron Yeats’s later recollection described a coin landing on its edge. On the repeat toss Liverpool won, and their first European campaign continued.

Inter awaited in the semi-final. Before that came another immediate test: Liverpool were due to face Chelsea in the FA Cup semi-final at Villa Park only three days later.

Liverpool’s first European campaign ended at San Siro on 12 May 1965. Inter overturned the 3–1 defeat they had suffered at Anfield, winning the return 3–0 and the semi-final 4–3 on aggregate. Two goals in the opening ten minutes erased Liverpool’s advantage; Giacinto Facchetti’s second-half strike completed the reversal.

Bill Shankly selected the same eleven who had won the first leg. Ronnie Moran remained at left-back in place of the injured Gerry Byrne, and Geoff Strong continued in the side with Gordon Milne absent. Liverpool arrived with a lead to protect against the holders, whose own stadium would also stage the final.

Mario Corso scored from a free-kick after eight minutes. Almost immediately, Joaquín Peiró added the second. Tommy Lawrence was bouncing the ball when the forward intervened, taking it away and finishing into the unguarded goal. Liverpool’s players protested, but the goal stood and the aggregate scores were level.

Both early goals became central to Liverpool’s account of the defeat. In his autobiography, Shankly maintained that Corso had scored directly from an indirect free-kick and that Peiró had kicked the ball out of Lawrence’s hand. His objection to the second goal was not shared in every Liverpool recollection. Peter Thompson later told LFChistory that, from his position on the wing, he saw Peiró take the ball as it bounced, rather than kick it out of the goalkeeper’s hands.

Inter’s later account likewise describes Peiró intercepting the bounce. Those contrasting memories explain the lasting dispute, while the match itself continued with Inter leading 2–0. At half-time, Liverpool’s two-goal cushion from Anfield had gone, but the tie was still level overall.

Facchetti broke that equality around the hour. Advancing from defence, he received the ball near the edge of Liverpool’s penalty area and drove in a right-footed shot. Inter now led on aggregate for the first time.

Liverpool did not score in reply. The 3–0 result sent Inter into a final against Benfica, which they subsequently won, and left Shankly’s side one round short in their European debut season.

The defeat closed a campaign that had brought Liverpool’s first FA Cup and a shared Charity Shield, but seventh place in the league. Their European journey had begun with five goals in Iceland and reached a two-goal semi-final lead at Anfield. In Milan that lead was lost, amid disagreements that would remain part of the tie’s history.

Gordon Wallace needed only three minutes to score Liverpool’s first European goal. By the end of their visit to KR Reykjavík on 17 August 1964, he and Roger Hunt had scored twice apiece and Phil Chisnall had added his first for the club in a 5–0 victory.

The English champions were beginning a new competition only two days after sharing the Charity Shield with West Ham. With Ian St John recovering from appendicitis and Alf Arrowsmith injured in that match, Wallace and Chisnall started together at Laugardalsvöllur. Chisnall had made his debut as Arrowsmith’s substitute at Anfield; this was his first start.

Wallace’s early goal gave Liverpool the lead, but KR reached half-time only one behind. The score changed quickly after the interval. Hunt made it 2–0 almost immediately, beginning a second half in which Liverpool scored four times.

The Icelandic side nevertheless had a chance to reduce the deficit. Ellert Schram’s header beat Tommy Lawrence, only for Ronnie Moran to clear from the line. Instead of the lead being cut, Liverpool extended it: Chisnall scored after Callaghan’s work on the right, then Wallace headed in another Callaghan cross.

Hunt completed the scoring near the end with a shot from outside the penalty area. The five-goal advantage made the first European journey an emphatic success and left Liverpool firmly in control before the return at Anfield.

That second meeting, played on 14 September, brought a further 6–1 win and an 11–1 aggregate passage into the next round. Liverpool’s European history had begun away from home, with Wallace providing its opening goal.

Liverpool began the new season with a share of the Charity Shield after West Ham United twice came from behind to draw 2–2 at Anfield on 15 August 1964. Gordon Wallace and Gerry Byrne supplied the champions’ goals; Geoff Hurst’s late reply prevented an outright victory.

The meeting of the league champions and FA Cup holders also brought an unfamiliar sight. Alf Arrowsmith was injured and Phil Chisnall replaced him after fifteen minutes. It was Chisnall’s Liverpool debut and the club’s first recorded substitution, permitted in this fixture before substitutes became part of League football.

Wallace put Liverpool ahead just before the half-hour. He had an early opportunity in the side while Ian St John recovered from appendicitis, and took it with the first of what would become five goals in Liverpool’s opening three matches of the season.

West Ham recovered before the interval through Johnny Byrne. Liverpool’s own Byrne restored the lead soon after the restart, leaving the visitors with another deficit to retrieve. They did so with only six minutes remaining, Hurst making it 2–2.

The Shield was shared, and Liverpool’s attention turned immediately to a different competition. Two days later they were due in Reykjavík for the first European Cup match in the club’s history, with Arrowsmith’s injury having already forced a change to Shankly’s attack.

For more than seventy minutes, Liverpool’s visit to Burnley on 5 December 1964 offered no certainty that their wait for an away league win was ending. The match was level at 1–1. Then four Liverpool goals transformed it into a 5–1 victory, with Roger Hunt completing a hat-trick and Geoff Strong and Chris Lawler opening their scoring accounts for the club.

Liverpool had taken only three points from their previous eight away league matches, drawing at Birmingham, Fulham and Stoke and losing the other five. Their European form had been more encouraging, including the recent 3–0 home win over Anderlecht, but the league champions still needed a first domestic victory on the road.

Hunt gave them a half-time lead at Turf Moor, scoring after the half-hour with Strong involved. Johnny Price equalised early in the second half, leaving Liverpool with another away game to recover.

Strong’s first Liverpool goal restored the lead with roughly a quarter of an hour remaining. Hunt then scored twice more in the closing stages. Lawler added the fifth just before the end, joining Strong as a first-time scorer on an afternoon that had become emphatic only late on.

The result extended Liverpool’s unbeaten league run to five matches. It also gave substance to their recovery after the difficult opening months: a first away win, a hat-trick for their leading scorer and goals from two players establishing themselves in Shankly’s side.

Three days after winning the FA Cup, Liverpool beat the champions of Europe. Goals from Roger Hunt, Ian Callaghan and Ian St John secured a 3–1 victory over Inter at Anfield on 4 May 1965 and a two-goal lead in the European Cup semi-final.

The newly won trophy was part of the occasion before the match began. Gerry Byrne and Gordon Milne, both injured, paraded it around the ground. Byrne’s broken collarbone meant Ronnie Moran took his place in the side; Geoff Strong continued in place of Milne. The other ten players who had started the final were retained.

Liverpool scored within the opening five minutes. Callaghan crossed from the right and Hunt met the ball first time, beating Giuliano Sarti. Against the holders, the home side had immediately carried the Wembley success into a new contest.

Inter’s answer showed why the tie was a different test. Joaquín Peiró got through after a missed challenge by Ron Yeats and supplied Sandro Mazzola, who scored the equaliser. Liverpool’s early advantage had lasted only a few minutes.

They regained it through a carefully worked free-kick. Callaghan ran beyond the defensive wall; Stevenson took the kick and Hunt helped the ball into the winger’s path. Callaghan finished past Sarti to put Liverpool 2–1 ahead before half-time.

Chris Lawler then had an effort disallowed, while Yeats hit the bar with a header before the interval. The contemporary report described Liverpool continuing to threaten despite the disappointment of the ruled-out goal. Inter nevertheless remained only one behind, with another ninety minutes to come in Milan.

The third goal arrived in the later stages of the second half. Sarti saved Hunt’s shot but could not keep the rebound away from St John, who followed in and scored. Liverpool had established the two-goal cushion they would take to San Siro.

Their first European campaign had now brought a victory over the holders, following wins against Reykjavík and Anderlecht and the deciding toss against Cologne. The result left a place in the final within reach, but the return on 12 May still had to be played. At Anfield, the new FA Cup winners had given themselves a substantial advantage.

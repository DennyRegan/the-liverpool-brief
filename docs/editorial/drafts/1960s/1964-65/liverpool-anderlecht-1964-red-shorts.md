Liverpool wore red shorts with their red shirts and gave Anderlecht a three-goal deficit to take back to Belgium on 25 November 1964. Ian St John, Roger Hunt and Ron Yeats scored in a 3–0 Anfield victory that put Bill Shankly’s side in command of their European Cup tie.

The change of strip has become inseparable from the occasion, though Liverpool still wore white socks with red tops. The fully red combination came in the return match. Shankly’s later account explained the intention behind the change: he believed the stronger appearance would make his players look more imposing.

Liverpool had reached this stage by beating KR Reykjavík 11–1 on aggregate. Against Anderlecht, St John supplied another early European breakthrough, scoring after ten minutes. Hunt added the second shortly before half-time, with St John involved in creating it.

The captain completed the scoring early in the second half. Yeats’s goal, following Stevenson’s contribution, made the advantage 3–0. Liverpool’s side included Tommy Smith, wearing the number ten shirt, alongside the more established figures of the championship team.

No reply followed from Anderlecht. Three weeks later, Liverpool completed the job in Brussels with a 1–0 win through Hunt, advancing 4–0 on aggregate. That was also the night the socks became red: the Anfield victory had introduced the shorts and established the lead.

## Season as a whole

Bill Shankly’s defending champions finished seventh: 42 league matches, 17 wins, ten draws and 15 defeats; 67 goals scored, 73 conceded and 44 points. Home results were 12 wins, five draws and four defeats; away results five wins, five draws and eleven defeats. Manchester United won the title on goal average from Leeds, both with 61 points. These figures were freshly read in LFChistory and independently checked in RSSSF. The campaign comprised 60 national/international matches: 42 league, eight FA Cup, nine European Cup and one Charity Shield fixture. No League Cup campaign appears in the complete record.

The opening Shield was shared with West Ham after a 2–2 draw. Liverpool then entered Europe for the first time in Reykjavík, winning 5–0 before a 6–1 Anfield return. That return already has a completed public report and retained draft and is explicitly reused unchanged. Anderlecht were defeated 4–0 on aggregate. Three matches against Cologne ended level, with Liverpool advancing after a toss in Rotterdam. Inter won the semi-final 4–3 on aggregate after Liverpool had taken a 3–1 first-leg lead.

The FA Cup route ran through West Bromwich Albion (2–1 away), Stockport County (1–1 home, 2–0 away replay), Bolton Wanderers (1–0 away), Leicester City (0–0 away, 1–0 home replay), Chelsea (2–0 at Villa Park) and Leeds United (2–1 after extra time at Wembley). The first FA Cup win followed previous final defeats in 1914 and 1950. Stockport finished bottom of the Fourth Division; Bolton were third in the Second Division. The league and Europe therefore provide essential context to the domestic cup success, not a continuous story of dominance.

Liverpool won the first league match 3–2 against Arsenal but were twenty-first after the 4–0 home derby defeat on 19 September. Defeat by Manchester United on 31 October left them nineteenth. A run of fourteen unbeaten league matches followed, from Fulham on 7 November through Birmingham on 24 February, ending at West Ham on 27 February. The 5–1 win at Burnley on 5 December was the first away league victory, after eight away league fixtures without one. Four late goals followed Burnley’s equaliser; it supplies a useful recovery story amid the European ties. Four consecutive league defeats in April, and an eventual seventh-place finish, prevent treating the first FA Cup campaign as a successful title defence.

Roger Hunt led scoring with 37 in all competitions: 25 league, five FA Cup and seven European Cup. Ian St John scored eleven (four league, two FA Cup, five Europe). Callaghan scored eight; Milne and Thompson six apiece. Tommy Lawrence appeared in 59 of the 60 matches, Hunt in 58 and Stevenson in 57. Twenty-three players appeared. Geoff Strong joined from Arsenal on 7 November; Alan A’Court left for Tranmere on 30 October. The change in personnel is relevant to the new European campaign and Strong’s place in the final, but transfer fees are unnecessary for the selected stories.

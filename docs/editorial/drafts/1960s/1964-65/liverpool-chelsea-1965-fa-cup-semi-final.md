Liverpool returned from 120 minutes against Cologne and, three days later, beat Chelsea 2–0 to reach the FA Cup final. Peter Thompson and Willie Stevenson scored in the second half at Villa Park on 27 March 1965, taking the club back to Wembley for the first time since 1950.

Shankly used the same eleven who had played the European Cup decider in Rotterdam. The demanding interval between the two ties made this a further test of a side already pursuing two cup competitions, with Liverpool still waiting for their first FA Cup.

Chelsea had a John Mortimore header disallowed before Liverpool found the goal that counted. Thompson broke the deadlock after the hour with an individual run and finish. The winger who had knocked Liverpool out of the competition as a Preston player in 1962 had now put them ahead in a semi-final.

A penalty gave Liverpool the opportunity to make the lead secure. Stevenson took it and beat Peter Bonetti with around ten minutes remaining. His later recollection was that the shot had not gone where he intended: a slip sent it higher than planned, but still into the net.

Chelsea could not recover. Liverpool had followed their drawn European marathon with a domestic victory, and supporters came onto the pitch at the final whistle to celebrate with the players.

The other semi-final still required a replay before Leeds United emerged as Liverpool’s Wembley opponents. For Shankly’s team, the immediate achievement was already substantial: the difficult week had ended with places in a European semi-final and the FA Cup final.

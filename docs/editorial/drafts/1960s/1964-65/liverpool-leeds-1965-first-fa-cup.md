Ian St John’s diving header won Liverpool the FA Cup for the first time on 1 May 1965. After ninety goalless minutes at Wembley, Roger Hunt had put them ahead and Billy Bremner had brought Leeds United level. St John’s winner in the second period of extra time settled the final 2–1 and ended a wait that had lasted through the club’s first seventy-three years.

The victory required Liverpool to overcome an injury almost from the start. Gerry Byrne broke his collarbone in an early collision with Bobby Collins. With no substitutions permitted, the left-back stayed on and completed the match, including the additional half-hour. He would also help create the opening goal.

Liverpool had already been forced to replace Gordon Milne, whose knee injury kept him out of the final. Geoff Strong played in his place. Leeds arrived having finished second in the league, ahead of Liverpool in seventh, but neither club had won the FA Cup. One would leave Wembley with a new honour.

The first half brought no goal. Gary Sprake’s work in the Leeds goal helped keep Liverpool out, while the match’s early interruptions and developing rain made this a difficult occasion to settle. Liverpool had the greater attacking threat, but could not turn it into a lead.

They continued to press after half-time. Hunt headed a Callaghan centre over, Jack Charlton blocked a St John effort and Thompson threatened from the left. Leeds’s defence, with Sprake behind it, survived. At the end of ninety minutes the final was still 0–0, requiring extra time for the first time since 1947.

The breakthrough came early in the additional period, through the injured Byrne. Stevenson carried the ball forward and released him on the left. Byrne reached the byline and crossed; Hunt stooped to head past Sprake. Liverpool finally had a goal to reward the long effort to break Leeds down.

It did not settle the match. Charlton headed the ball down in Liverpool’s penalty area and Bremner met it on the volley, driving the equaliser past Tommy Lawrence. The first period of extra time ended with the sides level again.

Liverpool had to find another opening. During the second period Callaghan advanced on the right and crossed for St John, who bent into a low, diving header beyond Sprake. The centre-forward’s finish restored the lead and gave Leeds less time to answer than they had after Hunt’s goal.

This time Liverpool held it. Ron Yeats collected the trophy, following the club’s defeats in the finals of 1914 and 1950. Byrne had played through the injury; Hunt and St John had provided the goals that made Liverpool cup winners at last.

There was scarcely time for the team to pause. Inter were due at Anfield three days later for the European Cup semi-final, but Liverpool would meet them with the FA Cup already won.

Gordon Wallace scored twice as Liverpool opened their league title defence with a 3–2 win over Arsenal at Anfield on 22 August 1964. His late winner rescued an afternoon in which a two-goal advantage had disappeared within minutes.

The match also opened a new chapter on television. Its highlights formed the first edition of the BBC’s Match of the Day, with Kenneth Wolstenholme introducing the programme from Anfield. The cameras had a match whose result remained uncertain until the closing stages.

Roger Hunt scored first, meeting Ian Callaghan’s cross with a hooked effort over former Liverpool goalkeeper Jim Furnell. The champions carried that lead into half-time, then doubled it early in the second half through Wallace.

Arsenal answered with two goals in rapid succession. Geoff Strong, who would join Liverpool later that season, scored first; Joe Baker followed to make it 2–2. Liverpool had led for much of the match and now had to find a winner against a side that had recovered from two down.

It came through Wallace near the end. Callaghan was again involved in creating the goal, completing an afternoon in which the match record credits him with an assist for all three Liverpool scores.

Wallace’s two goals followed one against West Ham in the Charity Shield and two in Reykjavík. Five goals in eight days had made the young forward central to Liverpool’s opening week, while the Arsenal victory gave the defending champions their first league points.

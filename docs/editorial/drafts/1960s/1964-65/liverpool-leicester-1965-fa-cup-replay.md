Roger Hunt finally beat Gordon Banks, and Liverpool finally removed Leicester City from their FA Cup path. His goal at Anfield on 10 March 1965 settled a quarter-final replay 1–0 and put Bill Shankly’s side into the last four.

There was ample reason for Liverpool to regard this as a difficult draw. Leicester had ended their 1963 campaign in the semi-final, when Banks’s goalkeeping helped preserve a one-goal victory. They had also won both league meetings this season, 2–0 at Filbert Street and 1–0 at Anfield. The first cup meeting, four days before this replay, had produced another ninety minutes without a Liverpool goal.

The replay remained goalless at half-time. The breakthrough came well into the second half, from a free-kick taken by Chris Lawler. Ron Yeats headed the ball down and Hunt struck it into the Leicester net.

Hunt later remembered the finish as a left-foot volley and regarded it among his best goals. The ball had come down from his captain at the right moment; after another long wait against Leicester, Liverpool had the lead they needed.

There was no equaliser. The win completed a route through West Bromwich Albion, Stockport County and Bolton Wanderers before this two-match quarter-final. Two years after losing to Leicester one round short of Wembley, Liverpool had reached that stage again.

The next opponent would be Chelsea at Villa Park. First, Liverpool still had European business to settle with Cologne: the rearranged second leg at Anfield was only a week away.

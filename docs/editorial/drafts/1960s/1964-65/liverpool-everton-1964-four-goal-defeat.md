Liverpool’s difficult start to their title defence became a home derby defeat of striking proportions on 19 September 1964. Everton won 4–0 at Anfield, scoring three times before half-time and leaving the champions twenty-first in the First Division.

Derek Temple gave Everton the lead in the first minute. John Morrissey’s centre was not cleared by Ron Yeats, and Temple finished the loose ball. Liverpool responded quickly: Roger Hunt headed against the bar, and Andy Rankin subsequently saved from Gordon Wallace and Ian St John.

Those chances mattered to the shape of the afternoon. Liverpool were creating openings while Everton were taking theirs. Fred Pickering doubled the lead after beating Willie Stevenson and Yeats, then Colin Harvey controlled the ball and lifted his finish beyond Tommy Lawrence before the interval. The score was 3–0.

Liverpool still could not turn their attacks into a goal. Jimmy Gabriel headed away a Peter Thompson effort from the line. Morrissey then completed Everton’s scoring, driving in from distance against his former club midway through the second half.

The result came only five days after Liverpool had beaten KR Reykjavík 6–1 at Anfield. European progress had offered little indication of the domestic difficulties: this was already their fifth defeat in eight league games. The title won in April was being defended from the bottom end of the table.

Liverpool recovered to beat Aston Villa 5–1 in their next fixture, but the derby remained a severe early measure of the work required. On this afternoon Everton’s finishing, and Rankin’s resistance at the other end, made the difference unmistakable.

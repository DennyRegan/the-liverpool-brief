Liverpool’s first Football League substitution helped save a point. Geoff Strong replaced Chris Lawler at half-time against West Ham on 15 September 1965 and scored the equaliser in a 1–1 draw at Anfield.

Nine days earlier Liverpool had won 5–1 at Upton Park. The return brought a different contest. Geoff Hurst put West Ham ahead before the interval, leaving Bill Shankly’s side a goal down when Strong entered the game.

The change had a significance beyond the score. Liverpool had already used a substitute in the Charity Shield, when Phil Chisnall replaced Alf Arrowsmith in August 1964. Strong’s appearance was the club’s first such change in a league match. He took over from a defender, but his contribution was to the attack.

With about a quarter of an hour left, Strong equalised, Willie Stevenson helping create the goal. West Ham goalkeeper Alan Dickie later remembered it as a header and recalled the pressure Liverpool’s forwards had put on his side. In a contemporary assessment published before the derby, the Echo’s Michael Charters also singled out Strong’s attacking impact after coming on for the second half.

The point left Liverpool sixth, with ten from seven league games. Strong had converted a place on the bench into a goal when the team needed one. The club’s first league substitute had also become its first scorer from the bench.

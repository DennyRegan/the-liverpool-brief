Liverpool’s defence of the FA Cup lasted one match. Chelsea recovered from Roger Hunt’s early goal to win 2–1 at Anfield on 22 January 1966, Bobby Tambling heading the decisive goal midway through the second half.

The draw had brought back the opponents Liverpool beat in the previous season’s semi-final. Then Peter Thompson and Willie Stevenson had taken Shankly’s side to Wembley; now the holders began their new campaign at home, leading the First Division and seeking another Cup run.

Hunt put them ahead inside two minutes, with Ian St John involved in the opening. Chelsea’s response was almost immediate. Peter Osgood stooped to head the equaliser within the first ten minutes, and the sides reached half-time level at 1–1.

Osgood gave Liverpool more to handle than his goal. Chelsea’s retrospective account recalls a run from inside his own half to the edge of Liverpool’s penalty area, a conspicuous example of the young forward’s confidence in a match that continued to offer openings.

Tambling’s winner came after the hour. In his autobiography, he remembered moving in from the left to receive Terry Venables’s pass, then finding George Graham on the right and continuing his run. Graham’s cross met him in the middle. Tambling described a looping header that passed over a defender and dropped beneath the bar, rather than a powerful close-range finish.

Liverpool still had time to force a replay, but Peter Bonetti’s saves helped Chelsea preserve the lead. The score remained 2–1, reversing the outcome of the previous spring’s semi-final and ending Liverpool’s attempt to retain the trophy they had waited so long to win.

Their league campaign and Cup Winners’ Cup run continued. At Anfield that afternoon, however, the first of the season’s three principal competitions had been lost.

Liverpool were four goals ahead by half-time at Upton Park on 6 September 1965. Roger Hunt then completed his hat-trick early in the second half, turning a commanding evening into a 5–1 victory over West Ham United.

The result came only two days after a 3–2 win at Blackpool. Liverpool’s league opening had been uneven: victory at Leicester was followed by defeat and a goalless draw against Sheffield United. Two successive away wins now gave their attack a much more convincing return.

Gordon Milne opened the scoring after twelve minutes, with Ian St John involved in the move. Ian Callaghan doubled the lead midway through the half. Milne’s contribution extended beyond his own goal: the match record credits him in the creation of Callaghan’s finish and Hunt’s first.

Hunt’s two goals immediately before the interval changed the scale of the match. West Ham had been two down; they went into the dressing room trailing 4–0. Five minutes after the restart, Hunt scored again to complete his treble. All three had come in a short spell straddling half-time.

Martin Peters replied for West Ham soon afterwards, but there were no further goals. Liverpool finished with seven points from five league matches, third in the table. The European Cup Winners’ Cup holders had conceded five at home, and Liverpool had scored eight in two away games inside three days.

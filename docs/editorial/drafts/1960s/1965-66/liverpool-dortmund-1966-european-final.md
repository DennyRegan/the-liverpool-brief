Liverpool’s first European final ended with a desperate attempt to clear and a 2–1 defeat. Early in the second period of extra time at Hampden Park on 5 May 1966, Reinhard Libuda sent the ball towards the exposed goal; it struck the woodwork and went in off Ron Yeats as the captain tried to keep it out.

Borussia Dortmund had beaten the newly crowned English champions. Liverpool had won the league against Chelsea five days earlier and were seeking a second major trophy, having eliminated Juventus, Standard Liège, Honvéd and Celtic on the way to Glasgow. Dortmund had removed the holders, West Ham, in the other semi-final.

The first half finished goalless. Liverpool had an early effort cleared from the line by Theo Redder, while Tommy Lawrence was required to stop Dortmund’s attacks, including a shot from Held. Hans Tilkowski’s goal remained intact at the other end.

After the interval Liverpool continued to seek the breakthrough, but Dortmund scored first. Held combined with Lothar Emmerich, continued his run and met the return delivery with a powerful finish past Lawrence. Just over an hour had gone, and Liverpool were behind.

Roger Hunt equalised within minutes. Peter Thompson reached the byline and delivered the ball for Hunt to control and finish with his left foot. Dortmund protested that Thompson had taken the ball out of play before crossing. The referee allowed the goal, and the match was held up while celebrating Liverpool supporters were cleared from the pitch.

Liverpool had an opportunity to win before extra time. Near the end of the ninety minutes, Hunt got through but took the ball on rather than shooting immediately, and Tilkowski saved. Hunt later recalled that he had expected a defender to block a first-time effort and had carried the ball too far.

The decisive attack came after the teams changed ends in extra time. Lawrence came out to meet Held and stopped his effort, but the loose ball reached Libuda. With the goalkeeper stranded, Libuda lifted his shot towards goal. Yeats raced back; the ball struck the woodwork and deflected into the net from him. Dortmund’s official history celebrates Libuda’s winner while also describing the captain’s final touch.

Liverpool could not find another equaliser. Dortmund became the first German club to win a European trophy, and Liverpool’s second continental campaign finished one match further on than their first, but again without the cup. There was still a league fixture at Nottingham Forest to play; the chance to add a European trophy to the championship had ended at Hampden.

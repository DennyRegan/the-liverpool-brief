Roger Hunt scored both goals as Liverpool beat Chelsea 2–1 at Anfield on 30 April 1966 and regained the First Division championship. Chelsea’s equaliser delayed the celebration, but Hunt’s second settled the match and put the title beyond Liverpool’s remaining challengers.

Chelsea had ended Liverpool’s FA Cup defence at the same ground in January. They offered resistance again, reaching half-time at 0–0. Hunt had struck the inside of a post, the rebound falling into goalkeeper John Dunn’s arms, while Peter Osgood helped create openings at the other end.

Three minutes after the restart, Hunt put Liverpool ahead following a ball through from Gerry Byrne. The breakthrough brought the home side close to the victory they needed, but Chelsea answered after the hour. Bobby Tambling sent the ball down the right and Bert Murray got beyond Ron Yeats to score as Tommy Lawrence came out.

At 1–1, the championship was still waiting to be secured. Seven minutes later Hunt restored Liverpool’s lead. He worked room to shoot with his left foot, driving low; Dunn got something on the ball but could not keep it from crossing the line.

Liverpool held on to win. The two points took them to sixty with one league match left. Burnley could reach no more than 57 and Leeds no more than 58: neither could catch Shankly’s side.

The Echo’s report described Shankly joining the players on the pitch before their lap of honour. Geoff Strong, still limping after his contribution against Celtic, came out to join them when the crowd called for him. The European final was only five days away, but the league trophy had been won at Anfield.

Liverpool would finish with 61 points, six ahead of Leeds and Burnley, having used only fourteen players in the league. Hunt’s double against Chelsea had delivered the decisive victory in Shankly’s second First Division title, two years after the first.

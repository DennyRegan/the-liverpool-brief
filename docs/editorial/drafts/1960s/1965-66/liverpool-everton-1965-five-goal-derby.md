A year after Everton had won 4–0 at Anfield, Liverpool answered with five. The 5–0 victory on 25 September 1965 came through sustained pressure, a narrow half-time lead and four more goals after the interval.

Tommy Smith, playing at right-back, scored the opener. Peter Thompson’s free-kick brought him into the goalmouth for a low header, finally beating Gordon West after Liverpool had repeatedly threatened. Ron Yeats had already hit an upright, and Everton’s defence had been kept busy. Yet the interval arrived with only one goal between the sides.

Both teams then made enforced changes. Alf Arrowsmith replaced Gordon Milne, who had taken a knock, while Everton lost Brian Labone and introduced Gerry Glover. Liverpool’s alteration quickly contributed to the next goal: Arrowsmith supplied Roger Hunt, whose angled shot found the far corner.

Willie Stevenson made it three soon afterwards, lifting the ball over West after Thompson’s delivery had come off Tommy Wright. Liverpool had turned a 1–0 lead into 3–0 within the opening minutes of the second half.

Hunt scored his second with a little over a quarter of an hour left. At 4–0, Liverpool had matched Everton’s winning margin from the corresponding match of the previous season. Ian St John then added the fifth in the final minute, heading Callaghan’s cross beyond West.

The goals were spread across four players, with Hunt’s double at the centre of the second-half scoring. Liverpool had also kept Everton out throughout, completing the reversal at both ends of the pitch. It was their first league derby victory since September 1963, after three successive defeats in the fixture.

The result put Liverpool sixth in the First Division, on twelve points from nine matches. Their league position still left work to do; the derby itself had been settled emphatically.

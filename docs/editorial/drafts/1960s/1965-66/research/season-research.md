## Whole-season evidence

The entire 53-match fixture list was retrieved and read before selection, alongside the full final table, appearance register, goals register and transfer register. RSSSF independently corroborates the final league table and every European tie. Existing season JSON was read as context and checked against this evidence.

Liverpool played 42 league games, nine European Cup Winners’ Cup games, one FA Cup tie and the Charity Shield. They were champions with 61 points: 26 wins, nine draws, seven defeats, 79 goals for and 34 against. Leeds and Burnley both finished on 55 points, Leeds second on goal average. Liverpool’s home record was 17 wins, two draws and two defeats, 52–15; away it was nine wins, seven draws and five defeats, 27–19. Bill Shankly remained manager.

The squad was exceptionally concentrated: fourteen players appeared in the league, fifteen across all recorded competitions because Phil Chisnall appeared only in Europe. Gerry Byrne, Ian Callaghan, Tommy Lawrence, Tommy Smith and Ron Yeats played all 53 matches. Ian St John and Willie Stevenson played 52; Chris Lawler and Peter Thompson 50; Roger Hunt 46; Gordon Milne 36; Geoff Strong 27; Alf Arrowsmith six; Chisnall and Bobby Graham one apiece. Hunt scored 29 league goals and 32 in all competitions, not the older 30/33 totals: the Tottenham home winner on 12 March is now assigned to Ted Clayton as an own goal. St John scored 12 overall; Lawler nine, including four in Europe. John Ogston arrived from Aberdeen in September, with no first-team appearance this season; exact signing date and fee need independent reconciliation before use.

## Season movement and full cup routes

The Shield was shared after 2–2 at Old Trafford. Liverpool won their league opener 3–1 at Leicester, lost the first home league game to Sheffield United and were still inconsistent through October. The 5–1 away win at West Ham included a Hunt hat-trick; the home return nine days later was a separate 1–1 game in which Strong replaced Lawler and equalised. The 5–0 derby followed the previous season’s 0–4 defeat at Anfield.

Four successive league wins from 30 October to 17 November — Forest 4–0, Sheffield Wednesday 2–0, Northampton 5–0 and Blackburn 5–2 — took Liverpool to the top. The complete fixture-position sequence then keeps them there. Their unbeaten league run from 30 October through 18 December comprised nine games, ending with a 0–1 home defeat to Leeds on 27 December. Liverpool won the return at Elland Road the very next day. Eight league games unbeaten from 28 December through 19 February included wins over Manchester United and Arsenal; this was not an all-competition unbeaten run because Chelsea eliminated the FA Cup holders on 22 January.

The entire FA Cup defence was that 1–2 Anfield third-round defeat. European route: Juventus away 0–1/home 2–0 (2–1 aggregate); Standard Liège home 3–1/away 2–1 (5–2); Honvéd away 0–0/home 2–0 (2–0); Celtic away 0–1/home 2–0 (2–1); Borussia Dortmund at neutral Hampden Park, 1–2 after extra time. The competition is the Cup Winners’ Cup, not the European Cup. The Celtic home win produced Liverpool’s first European final.

The title was secured against Chelsea on 30 April, Hunt scoring twice in a 2–1 win. There was one league match left. The European final on 5 May was therefore not the season’s last match: the final league fixture was 1–1 at Nottingham Forest on 10 May.

- Assist ledgers alone do not establish the mechanics of a pass, cross, rebound or shot. Do not invent those details.

Ron Yeats brought Liverpool level with four minutes left at Old Trafford, securing a share of the Charity Shield after Manchester United had twice taken the lead. The 2–2 draw on 14 August 1965 joined the league champions and FA Cup holders as joint holders of the season’s opening trophy.

Liverpool had won the Cup at Wembley in May and now began against Matt Busby’s champions. Geoff Strong started, with Peter Thompson on the bench. United lost Denis Law after eighteen minutes, Willie Anderson replacing him, but George Best put them ahead before the half-hour. Bobby Charlton’s through ball sent Best beyond the defence to finish past Tommy Lawrence.

Willie Stevenson equalised before the interval with an individual goal. The contemporary Daily Post report described him working his way through a succession of defenders before beating Pat Dunne with his left foot. Liverpool reached half-time at 1–1 and continued to press, without finding the goal that would put them ahead.

Instead, United struck late. Best supplied David Herd, who turned and shot past Lawrence. With less than ten minutes remaining, Liverpool were behind again.

The captain’s answer came from open play. Tommy Smith brought Chris Lawler into the move; Lawler advanced down the right and crossed from near the byline. Yeats met the delivery with a forceful header to make it 2–2. His arrival in the penalty area had supplied the finish Liverpool’s forwards had been unable to find in the second half.

Each club would hold the Shield for six months. Liverpool had shared it with West Ham the previous year after twice losing a lead; this time, two equalising goals ensured they left Old Trafford with the same entitlement.

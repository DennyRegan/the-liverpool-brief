Chris Lawler’s fourth goal of Liverpool’s European campaign helped take them into the Cup Winners’ Cup semi-finals. He opened the scoring against Honvéd at Anfield on 8 March 1966, and Ian St John added the second just after half-time to complete a 2–0 aggregate win.

The first leg in Budapest had finished goalless a week earlier. Liverpool therefore began the return with the tie level, knowing that an Anfield victory would bring a second successive European semi-final appearance after the previous season’s run in the European Cup.

Lawler made the breakthrough after twenty-eight minutes. Peter Thompson shot from distance and hit an upright; Lawler, arriving with the forwards, headed in the rebound. The right-back had already scored against Juventus and twice against Standard Liège. Now he had found the net in a third successive home European tie.

Honvéd reached half-time only a goal behind, but Liverpool scored again within two minutes of the restart. Thompson supplied the cross. Ron Yeats and Lawler were unable to make contact, and St John threw himself forward to head the ball into the net.

Both goals had begun with Thompson, first shooting and then crossing. Lawler’s advance had supplied the first finish; the centre-forward’s header supplied the second. The tie had moved decisively in Liverpool’s favour either side of the interval.

Bob Russell’s contemporary report described Honvéd offering little threat in the closing quarter of an hour. Liverpool kept their second clean sheet of the tie and went through 2–0, with Lawler again among the scorers in a competition increasingly shaped by his contributions at the other end of the pitch.

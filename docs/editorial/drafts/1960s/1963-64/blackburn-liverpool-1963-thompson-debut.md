Peter Thompson’s Liverpool debut ended in a comeback victory at Ewood Park on 24 August 1963. Blackburn led at half-time, but Ronnie Moran followed up his saved penalty to equalise and Ian Callaghan scored the winner in a 2–1 opening-day success.

Thompson had arrived from Preston North End ten days earlier. The new left-winger immediately gave Liverpool an attacking outlet: Horace Yates’s contemporary Liverpool Echo report described him combining with Willie Stevenson and Ian St John, repeatedly testing Blackburn’s defence. After taking the ball from Bray, he sent Stevenson through for a shot which goalkeeper Fred Else turned away.

Blackburn nevertheless reached the interval ahead. A corner was deflected to Mike Harrison, who put the ball through the crowded goalmouth shortly before half-time. Liverpool then had a Roger Hunt effort disallowed early in the second half for a foul.

The equaliser came midway through the half after Hunt was brought down inside the penalty area. Else stopped Moran’s spot-kick but could only push the ball back into play. Moran followed it in and scored from the rebound.

With about a quarter of an hour remaining, Callaghan gave Liverpool the lead. St John was involved in the opening, and Blackburn had no further reply.

Thompson had begun a season in which he would play every league and FA Cup match. The win itself did not produce an immediate surge: Liverpool lost their next two games, both at Anfield. At Ewood Park, however, the new winger’s contribution and a recovery from behind had given Shankly’s side a successful start.

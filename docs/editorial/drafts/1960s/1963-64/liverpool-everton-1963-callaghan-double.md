Ian Callaghan scored on either side of half-time as Liverpool beat reigning champions Everton 2–1 at Anfield on 28 September 1963. Two very different finishes gave Liverpool a lead they had to defend through a late Everton revival.

The early exchanges offered little room for either attack. St John struck the crossbar shortly before the interval, then Callaghan received from Jimmy Melia and drove a right-foot shot from distance beyond Gordon West. Liverpool went in one goal ahead.

Soon after the restart, Melia supplied Callaghan again, this time with a low ball from the left after St John had brought him into the move. Callaghan’s left-foot effort was poorly struck, but West missed his attempted clearance and the ball rolled into the net.

Liverpool threatened to extend the lead before Roy Vernon brought Everton back into the game. Alex Scott crossed, Derek Temple headed down and Vernon finished from close range with about a quarter of an hour remaining.

Everton then pressed for an equaliser. Liverpool pulled players back to protect their advantage, and Tommy Lawrence intercepted another Temple header before it could reach Vernon. The Everton captain also put a late chance narrowly wide. Callaghan’s double remained decisive.

Liverpool had lost 3–0 at Sheffield United the previous weekend. This victory began a different sequence: Aston Villa, Sheffield Wednesday, West Bromwich Albion and Ipswich Town were beaten in the next four league games. Callaghan’s two goals against the champions had started a run which moved Liverpool towards the top of the table.

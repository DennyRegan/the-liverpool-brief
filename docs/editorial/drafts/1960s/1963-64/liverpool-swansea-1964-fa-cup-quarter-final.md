Swansea Town ended Liverpool’s FA Cup campaign with a 2–1 victory at Anfield on 29 February 1964. Two goals shortly before half-time gave the Second Division visitors an advantage which Peter Thompson’s reply and a late Liverpool penalty could not erase.

Liverpool had reached the quarter-final by beating Derby County, Port Vale after a replay, and Arsenal. Swansea’s own route had taken them past Barrow, Sheffield United and Stoke City. League standing favoured the hosts, who were challenging for the First Division title while Swansea were struggling near the bottom of the division below.

Jimmy McLaughlin put Swansea ahead late in the first half. Eddie Thomas added a second almost immediately, leaving Liverpool two down at the interval. The visitors had given themselves a lead to defend against an attack which had already produced several large home victories that season.

Thompson reduced the deficit just after the hour, with Gerry Byrne involved in the opening. Swansea goalkeeper Noel Dwyer and his defence resisted the pressure that followed. Their chance of reaching the semi-final then faced another test when Liverpool were awarded a penalty with roughly ten minutes left.

Ronnie Moran failed to convert. Liverpool still needed another goal and did not find it. Swansea held on for a victory which carried them into a semi-final against Preston North End.

For Liverpool, the cup ended one round earlier than the previous season. The league remained open, and their eventual championship would follow a seven-match winning run in March and April. On this afternoon, however, a lower-division opponent had survived their recovery and taken the place in the last four.

Roger Hunt scored four second-half goals as Liverpool beat Stoke City 6–1 at Anfield on Boxing Day, 1963. A match level at the interval changed rapidly after the restart, with three Liverpool goals in the opening eight minutes of the half.

Ian St John had put Liverpool ahead early, but John Ritchie equalised for Stoke before half-time. Liverpool therefore began the second half with the work of securing the points still to do.

The contemporary Times report attributed the change to a greater concentration of Liverpool attacks down the left. It identified Stoke right-back Bill Asprey as the defender Shankly’s side increasingly tested, with Peter Thompson providing the threat on that flank.

Hunt restored the lead two minutes after the restart and scored again two minutes later, with Thompson involved in both goals. Alf Arrowsmith then made it 4–1. The quick succession of goals had turned a close score into a commanding Liverpool advantage.

Hunt completed his hat-trick midway through the half and added his fourth in the closing stages. Gordon Milne was involved in both of those later goals. All four of Hunt’s goals had arrived after the break, while Stoke were unable to add to Ritchie’s first-half equaliser.

Liverpool finished the day third, with 32 points from 23 matches. They remained behind Blackburn and Tottenham, but their attack had produced another emphatic home win. Hunt would finish the league campaign with 31 goals, the largest individual share of the 92 Liverpool scored in winning the championship.

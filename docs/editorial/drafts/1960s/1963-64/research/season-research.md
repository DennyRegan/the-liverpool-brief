## Whole campaign

Bill Shankly’s Liverpool won the First Division in their second season back, ending a seventeen-year championship wait. Their 42 league matches produced 26 wins, five draws and eleven defeats, 92 goals for and 45 against, and 57 points. Manchester United finished second with 53 and Everton third with 52. Home record: 16 wins, no draws, five defeats, 60–18 goals; away: ten wins, five draws, six defeats, 32–27. RSSSF independently agrees with LFChistory’s final table.

The complete national competitive programme was 42 league matches and five FA Cup ties; no League Cup, European or Charity Shield game belongs to this season. Cup route: Derby County 5–0 at Anfield; Port Vale 0–0 at home and 2–1 away in the replay; Arsenal 1–0 away; Swansea Town 1–2 at home in the quarter-final. Swansea were a Second Division club and ultimately finished nineteenth, narrowly above relegation. Port Vale were in the Third Division.

The August opening victory at Blackburn included new signing Peter Thompson, but Forest and Blackpool won at Anfield. A goalless Forest return meant three points from four games. Away wins at Chelsea and Wolves were followed by a third home defeat, to West Ham. The 6–0 Wolves win ended that home sequence, then a 3–0 defeat at Sheffield United showed the recovery was not immediate. The 28 September derby victory began five successive league wins, ended by Leicester’s November win at Anfield. Wins at Bolton, against Fulham and at Old Trafford lifted Liverpool top in November. They were not permanently top: a December Blackburn defeat and later results changed the lead repeatedly.

Boxing Day’s 6–1 Stoke win left Liverpool third. Large home wins continued against Sheffield United and Ipswich, but defeats at West Ham, Everton and Fulham and the Swansea cup exit interrupt any simple narrative of dominance. Fulham away on 14 March was the last league defeat before the title was secured. Seven consecutive league victories followed: Bolton, Tottenham away, Leicester away, Tottenham home, Manchester United, Burnley away and Arsenal. The Easter three wins were on 27, 28 and 30 March, not three successive days. Arsenal’s 5–0 defeat on 18 April secured the title with three matches remaining; the final three away games then yielded one draw and two defeats. The Arsenal match was the final home fixture, not the final match of the season.

Hunt scored 31 league and two cup goals (33 total); St John 21+1=22; Arrowsmith 15+4=19; Thompson 6+2=8; Callaghan 8 league goals. Seventeen players appeared in national first-team competition. Callaghan, Milne and Thompson played all 47 matches; Hunt 46, Lawrence/StJohn 45, Stevenson 43, Yeats 41, Moran 39, Byrne 37, Melia 25, Arrowsmith 24, Ferns 19, Lawler 6, Thomson 3, Furnell 2, Wallace 1. Tommy Smith did not play this season. The official Thompson profile independently confirms his ever-present role; avoid mistaking its six league goals for his eight total.

Transfer register: Thompson from Preston 14 August, John Sealey from Warrington 11 September, Chisnall from Manchester United 15 April. Departures: Allan Jones to Brentford 17 August, Lewis to Huddersfield 26 August, Furnell to Arsenal 21 November, Melia to Wolves 9 March. Fees are not needed and are omitted. The official season retrospective links Melia’s injury, Arrowsmith’s emergence and St John’s deeper role; any match-specific tactical claim needs separate evidence.

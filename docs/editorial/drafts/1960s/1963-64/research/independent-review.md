## Baseline checked

Liverpool won the First Division with 57 points: 26 wins, five draws, 11 defeats, goals 92–45. Manchester United finished second on 53, Everton third on 52 and Tottenham fourth on 51. Liverpool's home record was 16 wins, no draws and five defeats, goals 60–18; away ten wins, five draws and six defeats, goals 32–27. The two-point arithmetic is consistent.

The national competitive programme comprised 42 league and five FA Cup games, with no League Cup or European fixtures. Cup route: Derby 5–0 at Anfield; Port Vale 0–0 at Anfield, then 2–1 away after extra time; Arsenal 1–0 at Highbury; Swansea Town 1–2 at Anfield in the sixth round. Contemporary opponent name Swansea Town is distinguished from the modern Swansea City provider.

League results start with an Ewood Park win, followed by two Anfield defeats and a goalless away draw. The first home league win was Wolves 6–0 on 16 September after three home defeats. Five consecutive league victories followed the Sheffield United defeat, beginning with Everton on 28 September, before Leicester won at Anfield. The late seven-match winning run began with Bolton on 20 March and ended with Arsenal on 18 April. The Easter wins over Spurs away, Leicester away and Spurs home were on 27, 28 and 30 March. Three away fixtures remained after the title was secured; Liverpool lost at Birmingham, drew at West Brom and lost at Stoke. There was no final-day clinching game.

Callaghan, Milne and Peter Thompson played all 47 national competitive fixtures. Hunt played 46; Lawrence and St John 45; Stevenson 43; Yeats 41; Moran 39; Byrne 37; Melia 25; Arrowsmith 24; Ferns 19; Lawler six; Bobby Thomson three; Furnell two; Wallace one. Tommy Smith made no appearance in this season's national competitive register. Thompson's first appearance was the 24 August opener. Sealey and Chisnall were signings but not national competitive debutants in this season.

The scoring register gives Hunt 31 league plus two cup goals (33), St John 21+1 (22), Arrowsmith 15+4 (19), Callaghan eight league, Peter Thompson 6+2 (eight), Melia four, Milne two, Moran two, Stevenson one, Yeats one and one opposition own goal. Older or retrospective Arrowsmith figures require scope checking rather than substitution for these totals.

## Conflicts and safeguards identified before drafting

- Swansea's older quarter-final retrospective names Herbie Williams as first scorer; its newer classic-cup account and LFChistory agree on Jimmy McLaughlin, followed by Eddie Thomas. Writer notified. Moran's miss is not evidence of a Dwyer penalty save.
- LFChistory's Old Trafford note offers only speculation about JFK's assassination affecting attendance. It cannot support a causal claim. Writer notified.
- Title-game McGhee transcription puts Thompson's first goal at 53 minutes; LFChistory gives 52. McGhee credits Thompson with the pass for Hunt, while LFChistory credits St John with the assist. Writer notified to omit unnecessary precision or resolve before asserting either account.
- LFChistory's title-game table gives Liverpool 56 points from 39 matches. United had 51 from 41, Everton 50 from 41 and Spurs 49 from 40: none could reach 56 under two points for a win. The title consequence is therefore checked independently of retrospective wording.
- An official heroes retrospective encountered in search uses 18 goals in 23 games for Arrowsmith's breakthrough period. The actual season register reads 19 in 24, including four cup goals. Do not silently equate a selected spell with season totals.

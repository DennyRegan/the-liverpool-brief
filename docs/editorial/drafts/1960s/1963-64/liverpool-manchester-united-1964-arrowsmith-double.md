Alf Arrowsmith scored twice as Liverpool beat Manchester United 3–0 at Anfield on 4 April 1964. Ian Callaghan’s early opener and Arrowsmith’s goals on either side of half-time gave Liverpool a fifth consecutive league victory and strengthened their lead in the championship race.

The Easter programme had already brought wins at Tottenham and Leicester, followed by another victory over Spurs at Anfield. United were another direct rival, and Liverpool took the lead after only six minutes, with Roger Hunt involved in the opening for Callaghan.

Arrowsmith made it 2–0 before half-time, with Callaghan contributing to the chance. Early in the second half he scored again after St John’s involvement. Liverpool had a three-goal advantage with much of the half still to play, and United produced no reply.

The afternoon showed the value of Arrowsmith’s emergence. He would score fifteen goals in twenty league appearances during the campaign, adding another substantial source of goals alongside Hunt and St John. Against United, two of those goals had helped turn an early lead into a clear victory.

Liverpool now had 52 points from 37 matches. Everton were three points behind with two more games played; United were five behind having played one more. The championship was not yet settled, but the result had widened Liverpool’s advantage over the side that would eventually finish second.

Two further wins completed the decisive run. Liverpool won 3–0 at Burnley, then beat Arsenal 5–0 in their final home match to become champions. United’s defeat had removed another difficult fixture from the run-in without costing Liverpool a point.

Liverpool ended a run of three home defeats with a 6–0 victory over Wolverhampton Wanderers at Anfield on 16 September 1963. Alf Arrowsmith scored inside the opening minute, Peter Thompson added his first Liverpool goal, and four more followed after half-time.

Nottingham Forest, Blackpool and West Ham United had each won 2–1 at Anfield during the opening weeks. Liverpool had already beaten Wolves away a week before this return, but their home form had offered little support for a championship challenge.

Arrowsmith changed the pattern almost immediately, opening his Liverpool scoring account after only 45 seconds. Thompson made it two before the interval. With Ian St John absent from the eleven, Arrowsmith was leading the attack, while Jim Furnell was in goal.

Ian Callaghan added the third after the restart. Roger Hunt then scored with Arrowsmith involved in the move, putting Liverpool four ahead before Wolves lost their goalkeeper. Malcolm Finlayson hurt his hand saving from Thompson and had to leave the field with roughly twenty minutes remaining.

Forward Jimmy Murray took over in goal. Gordon Milne and Hunt scored past him to complete the six-goal margin, Hunt finishing with two and Liverpool with five different scorers. Finlayson’s painful departure would prove to be his final first-team appearance for Wolves.

The result supplied Liverpool’s first home points of the season. It did not remove every difficulty: they lost 3–0 at Sheffield United in the next match. But Anfield’s sequence of defeats had ended, and Liverpool would win sixteen of their twenty-one home league games on the way to the title.

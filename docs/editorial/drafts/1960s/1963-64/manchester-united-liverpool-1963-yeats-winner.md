Ron Yeats’s first Liverpool goal earned a 1–0 victory at Old Trafford on 23 November 1963 and put Bill Shankly’s side top of the First Division. The captain’s late header settled a match changed by a serious injury to Manchester United goalkeeper Harry Gregg.

Liverpool had responded to an early-November defeat by Leicester City with wins over Bolton Wanderers and Fulham. They arrived in Manchester with a chance to extend that recovery, but the first half ended without a goal.

Before the interval, Gregg collided with Yeats as they contested a corner and was carried off. United forward David Herd took over in goal. Gregg had broken his collarbone; he returned only for the closing minutes, with an arm strapped against his chest.

Liverpool eventually broke through with about a quarter of an hour left. Yeats headed the winner, with Ian Callaghan involved in creating the chance. United could not answer, and the visitors left with both points.

The result gave Liverpool 25 points from eighteen matches. Blackburn Rovers, Tottenham Hotspur and Arsenal also had 25, but Liverpool stood first on goal average. It was the first time they had led the division during this campaign.

Yeats would score no other league goal that season. His contribution at Old Trafford nevertheless formed part of eight wins in nine league matches, lifting Liverpool from their uneven opening into the championship contest. When the teams met again at Anfield in April, Liverpool won 3–0; they eventually finished four points ahead of United as champions.

Liverpool became league champions for the first time in seventeen years by beating Arsenal 5–0 at Anfield on 18 April 1964. Peter Thompson scored twice, Tommy Lawrence saved a penalty when the lead was still only one goal, and Bill Shankly’s first championship was secured with three matches remaining.

The afternoon did not begin without danger. Lawrence had to intervene after an early Ronnie Moran back-pass put Liverpool under pressure. Then Hunt and Arrowsmith combined to create the opening for St John, who put Liverpool ahead inside the first ten minutes.

Arsenal had their opportunity to draw level when Ron Yeats handled in the penalty area. George Eastham took the kick, but Lawrence turned his shot away. Before half-time, Thompson crossed, St John headed across goal and Arrowsmith supplied the finishing header. Liverpool led 2–0.

Thompson then made the championship certain with two goals early in the second half. For the first he came in from the wing and drove the ball past former Liverpool goalkeeper Jim Furnell. His second followed within minutes. Hunt added the fifth on the hour.

Liverpool also won a penalty. Callaghan, the only Liverpool forward yet to score, took it, but Furnell saved. The missed chance made no difference to the result or to the destination of the title.

The celebration extended beyond the final whistle. Contemporary reports described the singing on the Kop and the players’ return to the field for a lap of honour. Liverpool had reached 56 points; Manchester United, their nearest challengers, had only one match left and could no longer catch them.

It was Liverpool’s final home game of the season and the seventh consecutive league victory in the run that had secured the championship. Two years after promotion, Shankly’s team had earned a place in the European Cup.

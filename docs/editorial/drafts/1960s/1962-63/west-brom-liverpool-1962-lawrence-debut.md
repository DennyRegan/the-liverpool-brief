Tommy Lawrence began his Liverpool first-team career with a 1–0 defeat at West Bromwich Albion on 27 October 1962. Derek Kevan scored the only goal, and Jimmy Melia’s missed penalty denied Liverpool an early opportunity to equalise.

Lawrence had waited five years for his chance after joining the club in 1957. Jim Furnell had played the opening 13 league matches of Liverpool’s return to the First Division, but injury opened the position. Gordon Wallace also made his debut at The Hawthorns, taking a place in the forward line.

Kevan put Albion ahead in the 17th minute. Two minutes later, Melia stepped up to take Liverpool’s penalty, only for goalkeeper Tony Millington to save. The opportunity had come quickly after the opening goal; its loss left the visitors still chasing the match.

Albion reached half-time in front and kept that advantage through the second half. Liverpool failed to score for a second consecutive league match, following a 3–0 defeat at Leicester City a fortnight earlier.

The afternoon contained another change less obvious than the two debuts. Tommy Leishman was making his final first-team appearance for Liverpool. Willie Stevenson had joined from Rangers earlier that month and would soon enter the side, while Leishman moved to Hibernian in January.

Lawrence’s chance lasted much longer than one match. He kept the place for the rest of the campaign, playing 29 league games and all six FA Cup ties. The defeat at The Hawthorns was the beginning of his long run in Shankly’s team, although it brought no immediate improvement to a difficult autumn.

Liverpool scored five times without reply in the second half to beat Tottenham Hotspur 5–2 at Anfield on Good Friday, 12 April 1963. Two goals down at the interval, they were level within nine minutes of the restart and eventually turned the match into a convincing victory.

Tottenham were pursuing the First Division title. Terry Dyson and Cliff Jones put them in control of the score before half-time, leaving Liverpool with a substantial deficit against one of the division’s strongest attacks.

Willie Stevenson started the recovery seven minutes after the break. Jimmy Melia equalised two minutes later, erasing the advantage Tottenham had built over the first half. With more than half an hour still to play, the visitors had lost both their cushion and their lead.

Ian St John put Liverpool ahead in the 72nd minute. Kevin Lewis added a fourth late in the game, with Melia involved, and Melia then scored his second after St John’s contribution. Five goals had come from four Liverpool players; Roger Hunt, their leading scorer for the season, was not among them.

The change was stark enough for captain Ron Yeats later to recall the difficulty of believing a team could perform so differently within the same match. The score alone explains the contrast: Liverpool had lost the first half 2–0 and won the second 5–0.

Easter had two further league games in store. Liverpool beat Manchester United 1–0 at Anfield the next day, then travelled to Tottenham on Monday. There, in another extraordinary reversal, they lost 7–2. The Good Friday recovery remained a substantial victory, but the return would expose how uneven their first season back could still be.

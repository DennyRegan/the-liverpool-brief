Liverpool’s first league match back in the First Division ended in a 2–1 defeat by Blackpool at Anfield on 18 August 1962. After a goalless first half, Ray Charnley and Des Horne scored in quick succession late in the game. Kevin Lewis pulled one back, but Liverpool could not recover a point.

Promotion had ended eight seasons in the Second Division. Bill Shankly began the new campaign with an eleven drawn entirely from the previous season’s side: Jim Furnell in goal, Ron Yeats as captain, and Roger Hunt and Ian St John in attack. Lewis, whose two goals against Southampton had secured promotion in April, was again among the forwards.

The match remained level well beyond the interval. Its decisive passage arrived with about a quarter of an hour left, when Charnley put Blackpool ahead. Horne added a second almost immediately, transforming a goalless contest into a two-goal deficit for the home side.

Lewis’s reply gave Liverpool a chance to retrieve something from the opening day, but it remained their only goal. More than 51,000 spectators had seen the return to top-flight football; the first points would have to wait.

The result also ended the run of home league matches without defeat that had characterised the promotion campaign. Liverpool had won 18 and drawn three at Anfield in 1961–62. Their next two fixtures, both away, brought a draw at Manchester City and defeat at Blackburn before a 4–1 victory over City at Anfield gave them their first league win of the season.

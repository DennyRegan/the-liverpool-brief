An early Mike Stringfellow goal and a determined Leicester City defence ended Liverpool’s FA Cup run at Hillsborough on 27 April 1963. Gordon Banks helped protect the 1–0 lead, including a late save from Ian St John, as Bill Shankly’s side fell one match short of the final.

Liverpool had reached the semi-final by beating Wrexham, Burnley after a replay, Arsenal and West Ham United. Leicester were familiar and difficult opponents: they had already won both league meetings, 3–0 at Filbert Street and 2–0 at Anfield. Jimmy Melia missed the semi-final through injury, with Chris Lawler in Liverpool’s eleven.

Stringfellow scored in the 18th minute. Howard Riley delivered a free-kick from the right and the winger headed it into the corner.

Liverpool’s response forced Leicester into prolonged defence. Graham Cross worked deep, Riley came back to help cover the flank, and the space for a clear attempt at goal was difficult to find. Leicester’s later recollections and the contemporary reports reproduced by the club describe sustained Liverpool pressure after the goal.

The danger remained until the end. In the closing minutes Banks moved across goal to turn away St John’s effort with one hand. Liverpool had no equaliser, and Leicester held on to reach Wembley.

For Shankly, the cup run ended alongside a league campaign that would finish in eighth place. Leicester had beaten Liverpool three times without conceding across the season. At Hillsborough, Liverpool had made the last of those victories hard to secure, but they could not change the outcome.

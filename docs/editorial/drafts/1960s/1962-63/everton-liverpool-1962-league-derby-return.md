Roger Hunt’s late equaliser rescued Liverpool a 2–2 draw at Goodison Park on 22 September 1962, in the clubs’ first league meeting for eleven years. Twice Everton led and twice Liverpool recovered, with Kevin Lewis scoring the first equaliser and helping to create the second.

The derby’s return followed Liverpool’s promotion. Johnny Morrissey had crossed the park to Everton the previous month, while Ian St John’s absence gave Lewis a place at centre-forward. Michael Charters’s contemporary Liverpool Echo report recorded that St John had failed a fitness test that morning.

Everton took the lead through Roy Vernon’s penalty after a handball decision against Gerry Byrne. Liverpool answered before half-time: Alan A’Court’s cross reached Ian Callaghan, whose return into the middle allowed Lewis to finish from close range. The interval arrived with the match level.

Morrissey restored Everton’s advantage after the break. Vernon’s attempt was blocked by Yeats and the ball reached the former Liverpool winger, who struck it back towards goal. Liverpool tried to clear from the line, but referee Kevin Howley awarded the goal.

Liverpool still trailed as the last minute approached. A’Court delivered another cross, Lewis headed it down and Hunt drove the ball past Gordon West. The striker had turned another Lewis contribution into the point which his side had been pursuing.

It was the end of Everton’s winning start at Goodison that season, though not of their unbeaten home record. They would finish the campaign without a league defeat on their own ground and win the championship. Liverpool’s return visit to the league derby had ensured that one of those home results was a draw.

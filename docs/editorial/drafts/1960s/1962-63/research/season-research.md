## Campaign surveyed before selection

Liverpool returned to the First Division under Bill Shankly and finished eighth with 44 points: 17 wins, ten draws, 15 defeats, 71 goals scored and 59 conceded. Home record 13–3–5 (45–22 goals), away 4–7–10 (26–37). Everton won the title with 61 points; Tottenham finished second with 55. RSSSF independently confirms the LFChistory table. Liverpool's complete national competitive programme comprised 42 league matches and six FA Cup matches, no League Cup or European matches.

The opening three league games produced one point, before a 4–1 home win over Manchester City. September brought the revived league derby at Goodison, 2–2. A difficult autumn included defeats at Leicester, West Bromwich Albion and at home to Burnley. Liverpool stood twentieth after the Burnley match. A 3–3 draw at Manchester United preceded seven successive league victories from Arsenal on 14 November through Blackburn on 22 December, lifting them to fifth. The next league fixture was not until 13 February: the severe winter interrupted the schedule, although Liverpool played two January FA Cup ties. Wins over Villa and Wolves extended the league run to nine; Leicester ended it at Anfield on 2 March.

The FA Cup route was Wrexham 3–0 away, Burnley 1–1 away and 2–1 in the replay, Arsenal 2–1 away, West Ham 1–0 at home, then Leicester 0–1 at neutral Hillsborough on 27 April. The Easter sequence is particularly revealing: Tottenham beaten 5–2 on Good Friday after Liverpool trailed 0–2 at half-time, Manchester United beaten 1–0 the following day, then a 2–7 defeat at Tottenham on Easter Monday. These were not consecutive fixtures against Spurs. Six league games after the semi-final yielded one win, one draw and four defeats, with final match a 0–2 loss at Villa on 18 May.

Hunt led scoring with 24 league goals and two in the FA Cup (26); St John scored 19+1 (20), Lewis 10+2 (12). Hunt alone played all 48 national competitive matches. Milne played 47; St John 46; Byrne, Yeats and Melia 44 each; Callaghan 43. Lawrence's 29 league and six cup appearances began after Furnell's opening 13 league games. The official Lawrence profile corroborates his opportunity after Furnell's injury and retention of the place. Gordon Wallace also debuted at West Brom on 27 October. Stevenson, signed from Rangers on 19 October after time in Australia, did NOT debut alongside Lawrence: the WBA line-up still has Leishman. His own first appearance was the subsequent Burnley match. He made 34 competitive appearances in the season. Bobby Thomson arrived from Partick Thistle in December; White, Slater, Molyneux, Morrissey and Leishman departed across the preparation/campaign. Fees are unnecessary to selected reports and omitted.

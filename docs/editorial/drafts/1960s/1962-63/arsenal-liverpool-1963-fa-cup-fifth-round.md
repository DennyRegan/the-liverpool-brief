Liverpool returned to Highbury on 16 March 1963 and this time left with a victory. Jimmy Melia’s first-half goal and Ronnie Moran’s penalty gave them a 2–1 win over Arsenal in the FA Cup fifth round, despite a late reply from John McLeod.

Seven days earlier, the same Liverpool eleven had drawn 2–2 on the ground in the league. On that occasion they led twice and were caught twice. The cup meeting again gave them an advantage to protect, but they carried it through to the final whistle.

Melia put Liverpool ahead in the 33rd minute, with Roger Hunt involved in the opening. The visitors took that lead into half-time. After the restart, Moran had the opportunity to extend it from the penalty spot and converted in the 65th minute.

The two-goal margin changed the task for Arsenal. One reply would no longer be enough to force a replay. McLeod supplied it with five minutes remaining, but Liverpool conceded no second goal and secured their place in the quarter-finals.

The victory continued a cup campaign already stretched across the severe winter. Liverpool had beaten Wrexham away in January, then drawn at Burnley before winning the fourth-round replay at Anfield in February. Highbury supplied another away success, this time against a side that would finish immediately above them in the league.

West Ham United were next, at Anfield. Liverpool won that tie 1–0 to reach the semi-finals, where Leicester City would end the run at Hillsborough. At Highbury, the decisive difference from the previous week was their ability to keep the lead.

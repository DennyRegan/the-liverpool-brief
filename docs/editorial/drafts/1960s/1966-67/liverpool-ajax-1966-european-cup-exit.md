Roger Hunt twice brought Liverpool level on the night, but Ajax’s four-goal advantage from Amsterdam was never reduced. The 2–2 draw at Anfield on 14 December 1966 sent the Dutch champions into the European Cup quarter-finals, 7–3 ahead on aggregate.

Liverpool needed early goals to give their attempt at recovery a chance. Peter Thompson struck the crossbar, but Ajax’s defence held. Goalkeeper Gert Bals denied further Liverpool efforts, and the interval arrived with the game still goalless. Half the remaining match had passed without any reduction in the deficit.

The evening was also marked by a serious crush on the Kop. Spectators came out on to the track and around the pitch, and injured supporters were carried away. Contemporary reports described ambulance crews and stretcher-bearers treating casualties while the match continued.

Soon after the restart, Ajax increased their aggregate lead. Piet Keizer and Sjaak Swart combined to give Johan Cruyff the opening, and he beat Tommy Lawrence. Liverpool answered within minutes: Ron Yeats advanced, Ian St John supplied Hunt, and the striker finished to make it 1–1.

Ajax’s defence continued to resist Liverpool’s pressure, while their breaks remained dangerous. Cruyff scored again around the seventy-minute mark, restoring the visitors’ lead on the night. Hunt equalised near the end after Thompson and St John helped create the opening, but neither of his goals had brought Liverpool closer across the two legs.

The return had removed any possibility of explaining the tie solely through the fog in Amsterdam. Liverpool had hit the woodwork, forced saves and kept attacking, yet Ajax had protected their advantage and scored twice away from home. Hunt’s second goal preserved a draw at Anfield; it could not preserve Liverpool’s place in the competition.

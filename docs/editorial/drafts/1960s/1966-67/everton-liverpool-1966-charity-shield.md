Roger Hunt scored the only goal as Liverpool won the Charity Shield at Goodison Park on 13 August 1966. Two weeks after helping England win the World Cup, he decided another meeting of champions, this time between Liverpool’s league winners and Everton’s FA Cup holders.

Before the match, the teams had shared a lap of honour. Ron Yeats and Brian Labone led their clubs with the domestic trophies; Hunt and Ray Wilson carried the Jules Rimet trophy. The display brought together the achievements of a summer in which club and international football had repeatedly put Merseyside players at the centre of events.

Once the game began, Hunt made the decisive contribution in the ninth minute. The Daily Post’s report described a move involving Hunt, Ian Callaghan and Peter Thompson before the ball came back to the striker. From the left, Hunt moved it on to his left foot and drove into the top corner beyond Gordon West.

Liverpool held that lead through the remaining first half and all of the second. The contemporary Times account judged them firmly in control, with Everton unable to make their challenge count. There was no second Liverpool goal, but neither was there an equaliser to force the sharing of the trophy.

The previous two Charity Shields had ended in draws, against West Ham and Manchester United. This time Liverpool took possession outright, with Hunt’s finish separating the clubs after their joint celebration.

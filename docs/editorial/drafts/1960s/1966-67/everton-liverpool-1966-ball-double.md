Liverpool returned to Goodison Park a fortnight after winning the Charity Shield and left with a 3–1 league defeat. Alan Ball scored twice inside the opening twenty minutes on 27 August 1966, giving Everton a lead that Tommy Smith’s reply could not overturn.

Ball had arrived from Blackpool after the Shield match and scored on his Everton debut at Fulham. Liverpool were therefore facing a significant addition to the side they had beaten 1–0. James Corbett’s Everton history describes the first goal coming when Johnny Morrissey’s shot was deflected into Ball’s path; the newcomer supplied the finish.

The second followed a misunderstanding between Ron Yeats and Gordon Milne. Ball seized the opening and drove into the roof of the net. With less than twenty minutes played, Liverpool were two down to the England player who had joined their neighbours only days earlier.

Smith reduced the deficit shortly before half-time, bringing Liverpool back to 2–1. The interval arrived with a recovery still possible, but the equaliser did not come. Everton replaced Fred Pickering with Sandy Brown during the second half, and Brown scored their third with seven minutes left.

For Liverpool, it was a second successive league defeat after losing 2–1 at Manchester City in midweek. They had begun with a 3–2 win over Leicester, but now had only two points from three matches and stood sixteenth. The Shield victory had offered a confident opening; the league derby exposed how quickly that advantage could disappear.

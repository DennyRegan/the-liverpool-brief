## Chronology and complete cup routes

The opening Shield at Goodison was won 1–0, Hunt scoring. The champions then beat Leicester 3–2 but lost at Manchester City and Everton, leaving them sixteenth after three league games. Their early league record remained uneven. Four successive league wins in November — Forest 4–0, Burnley 2–0, Newcastle 2–0 and Leeds 5–0 — produced a 13–0 aggregate and second place, a point behind Chelsea. This is a league sequence, not an invented stylistic explanation.

European route: Petrolul home 2–0, away 1–3, then a neutral Brussels play-off won 2–0. Do not apply a modern away-goals rule to this tied aggregate. Ajax won the first leg 5–1 in Amsterdam, then drew 2–2 at Anfield to go through 7–3. The two Ajax reports warrant separate selection because the first is the catastrophic deficit and the second demonstrates that Liverpool could not overturn it at home.

Liverpool beat Chelsea 2–1 at Stamford Bridge on Christmas Eve and by the same score at Anfield on Boxing Day. The latter took them first, level on thirty points with Manchester United but ahead on goal average. They returned to first after beating West Ham on 7 January and were still contenders into March. Their final return to first in the recorded sequence was the 2–1 Stoke win on 4 March, Hughes’s debut: 43 points from 31 matches, Manchester United 42 from 30.

The FA Cup route was Watford away 0–0 and home 3–1, Aston Villa home 1–0, then Everton away 0–1. The Everton fifth-round match had a 7pm kick-off and a closed-circuit screening at Anfield. After the Stoke win Liverpool won only two of their remaining eleven league matches, against Newcastle and Sheffield United. Their final eleven returned two wins, four draws and five defeats: eight points. The final home defeat by bottom-placed Blackpool completed a fifth-place finish. This is the observable decline; fatigue, dressing-room tension or tactical stagnation are not established by these results.

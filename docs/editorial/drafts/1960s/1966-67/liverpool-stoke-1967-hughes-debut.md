Emlyn Hughes began his Liverpool career in a winning side on 4 March 1967. Chris Lawler and Roger Hunt scored in a 2–1 victory over Stoke City at Anfield, returning Bill Shankly’s team to first place in the league.

Hughes had arrived from Blackpool in February for £65,000. Shankly put the new signing straight into the starting eleven for a match with an immediate bearing on the championship race. Liverpool had drawn 2–2 at Fulham the previous weekend and needed another home win to keep pace with Manchester United.

Lawler supplied the opening goal after fourteen minutes. Liverpool carried that 1–0 lead into the interval, then doubled it through Hunt just after the hour. Stoke answered quickly: Peter Dobing scored four minutes later, leaving more than twenty minutes in which a further goal could change the result.

Liverpool conceded no equaliser. The two points took them to 43 from 31 matches, one ahead of United, who had played thirty. It was a lead with a qualification, but Liverpool still had their title defence in contention as Hughes completed his first appearance.

He would play ten league matches before the season ended. The team’s position proved less durable: this was Liverpool’s final return to the top in 1966–67, and only two victories followed in their remaining eleven league games. Hughes had joined at a moment when a new Liverpool career was beginning and the existing side’s championship challenge was approaching its decisive weeks.

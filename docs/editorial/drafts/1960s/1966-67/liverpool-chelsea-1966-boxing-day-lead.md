Willie Stevenson’s long-range strike broke the deadlock as Liverpool beat Chelsea 2–1 at Anfield on Boxing Day 1966 and went top of the First Division. Roger Hunt added a second before Bobby Tambling’s late penalty, completing a second victory over Chelsea in three days.

Liverpool had won by the same score at Stamford Bridge on Christmas Eve. Now, after their European Cup elimination by Ajax, four league points against another contender put the domestic challenge back at the centre of the season.

Neither side scored before half-time. Before Liverpool eventually broke through, Charlie Cooke had struck the crossbar for Chelsea, Peter Bonetti had repeatedly denied Liverpool and a penalty appeal after Hunt went down had been unsuccessful.

The breakthrough came midway through the second half. A Callaghan delivery was headed on by Geoff Strong and headed out by Marvin Hinton. Stevenson met the clearance from about thirty yards and drove it past Bonetti.

Hunt made it 2–0 with fifteen minutes left. Hinton misjudged a long clearance, allowing the ball over his head; Hunt drew Bonetti to the left before finishing from an angle. Chelsea recovered one goal near the end when Yeats handled a high ball and Tambling converted the penalty, but Liverpool held on.

The result put Liverpool and Manchester United together on thirty points from 22 matches. Liverpool’s superior goal average placed them first, while Chelsea were two points behind having played a game more. The Christmas double had taken Shankly’s side to the head of the table for the first time that season.

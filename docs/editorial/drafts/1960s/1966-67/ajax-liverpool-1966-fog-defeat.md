Ajax scored four goals before half-time as Liverpool lost 5–1 in Amsterdam on 7 December 1966. Chris Lawler’s late reply reduced the damage, but the first leg of the European Cup second round left Bill Shankly’s side needing to recover a four-goal deficit at Anfield.

Liverpool had survived a three-match opening tie with Petrolul Ploiești. Ajax, the Dutch champions, had beaten Beşiktaş 4–1 on aggregate. At the Olympic Stadium, their attack quickly made the difference in conditions that grew increasingly difficult to follow from the stands.

Cees de Wolf headed Ajax in front after only three minutes. Johan Cruyff added the second when Tommy Lawrence could not hold Klaas Nuninga’s shot. Nuninga then scored twice before the interval: first after a free-kick was blocked and the ball came back to him, then from a move across the Liverpool goal involving Sjaak Swart and Cruyff.

The half-time score was 4–0. Fog obscured much of the second half from the press box, and contemporary correspondents acknowledged that they could not see every incident. What remained clear was the scale of Liverpool’s task. Henk Groot added Ajax’s fifth, and Liverpool did not score until the closing moments, when Lawler supplied their solitary reply.

The English champions had conceded five in one European match. The weather became an immediate subject of dispute, but the contemporary reports also described the speed and accuracy of Ajax’s forwards and the openings in Liverpool’s defence. The first-half damage had already established a commanding lead before visibility deteriorated further.

Lawler’s goal meant Liverpool would return to Anfield four behind rather than five. They still had a second leg to play, but a side that had reached a European semi-final and final in the previous two seasons now faced a much earlier exit.

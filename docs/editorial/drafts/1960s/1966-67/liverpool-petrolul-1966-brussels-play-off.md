Liverpool settled their European Cup tie with Petrolul Ploiești at the third attempt, winning 2–0 at the Heysel Stadium in Brussels on 19 October 1966. Ian St John and Peter Thompson scored before half-time to send the English champions through to meet Ajax.

The play-off had become necessary after two contrasting legs. Liverpool won 2–0 at Anfield, but lost 3–1 in Romania, leaving the aggregate score level. A neutral match was required to separate the clubs, taking Liverpool back to the ground where they had beaten Anderlecht two years earlier.

They threatened almost immediately. Thompson put Roger Hunt through, but his attempt was cleared from the line; goalkeeper Mihai Ionescu then saved from Chris Lawler at the resulting corner. Liverpool’s early pressure became a lead when Hunt supplied St John, who finished low from the right through the gap between Ionescu and his post.

Thompson scored the second shortly before the interval, driving a low shot through after the ball came back out of Petrolul’s defence. Liverpool had created further openings for Hunt, but two goals were enough to put the tie firmly in their hands.

The contemporary reports agreed on Liverpool’s control after half-time, with Milne linking their play and Petrolul offering only a late challenge. One free-kick produced a contentious moment when Yeats used his hand to keep out Mircea Dridea’s effort. The referee ordered the kick to be retaken rather than awarding a penalty; Liverpool’s lead survived.

The score remained 2–0. After losing their first-leg advantage in Romania, Liverpool had established another two-goal lead and this time kept it intact. Ajax, who had beaten Beşiktaş, would be their next opponents.

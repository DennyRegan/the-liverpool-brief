Barry Lyons scored both goals as Nottingham Forest beat Liverpool 2–0 at Anfield on 15 February 1969. The defeat cost Liverpool first place and proved their only home league loss of the campaign.

Liverpool had maintained their lead with a 1–0 victory against Sheffield Wednesday in the previous league fixture. That win left them with 45 points, one ahead of Leeds, although Leeds had played a match fewer. Forest’s visit therefore came with little margin available at the top.

Lyons scored after five minutes to put the visitors ahead. Forest retained that advantage through a goalless remainder of the first half, reaching the interval 1–0 in front. Lyons scored again in the second half, and Liverpool were unable to answer either goal.

Forest finished the day nineteenth in the table, while Liverpool slipped to second. Leeds had 48 points from thirty games, three more than Liverpool from the same number of matches. A side near the bottom had inflicted a setback with an immediate effect on the championship positions.

Liverpool did not lose another league match before the April meeting with Leeds, but draws limited their recovery. At Anfield on 28 April, Leeds took the point that secured the title. Liverpool ultimately finished second; the Forest result remained the sole defeat in a home league record otherwise comprising sixteen wins and four draws.

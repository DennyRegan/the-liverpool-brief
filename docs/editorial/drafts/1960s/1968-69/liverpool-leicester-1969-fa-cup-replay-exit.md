Andy Lochhead’s first-half goal gave Leicester City a 1–0 victory over Liverpool at Anfield on 3 March 1969 and ended Liverpool’s FA Cup campaign. Peter Shilton saved a Tommy Smith penalty soon afterwards to preserve the lead in the fifth-round replay.

The teams had drawn 0–0 at Filbert Street two days earlier. Liverpool had reached the fifth round by beating Doncaster Rovers 2–0 and Burnley 2–1, both at Anfield. The replay offered another home opportunity, but Leicester supplied the decisive goal.

Lochhead scored after 34 minutes. Leicester’s detailed historical account describes him heading in a Len Glover cross from the left. Six minutes later Liverpool had a penalty and a chance to equalise before the interval. Smith took it, but Shilton saved, leaving Leicester 1–0 ahead at half-time.

The goalkeeper’s contribution extended beyond that penalty. The same club account records saves from Ian St John, Peter Thompson and Emlyn Hughes during a match in which Liverpool failed to score. Lochhead’s header remained the only goal, and Leicester advanced to a cup run that eventually carried them to the final.

Liverpool were now out of all three cup competitions. Their Fairs Cup campaign had ended against Athletic Bilbao and their League Cup run at Arsenal. The championship was the remaining possibility, but the recent home defeat by Nottingham Forest had already put Leeds ahead of them.

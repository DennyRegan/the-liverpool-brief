Alun Evans marked his Liverpool debut with a goal in a 4–0 victory over Leicester City at Anfield on 21 September 1968. All four goals arrived in the first twelve minutes, giving the teenager an immediate part in Liverpool’s improving league run.

Evans had joined from Wolverhampton Wanderers for £100,000 earlier that month. Liverpool had won their previous two league matches, against Queens Park Rangers and Ipswich Town, without conceding; Leicester’s visit extended that sequence in unusually decisive fashion.

Ron Yeats scored after two minutes, and Tommy Smith quickly added a penalty. In Steve Horton’s retrospective account, Yeats’s opener followed a corner and the penalty came after Roger Hunt was fouled. Before Leicester had recovered from those two goals, Evans scored the third after ten minutes.

Hunt supplied the pass for the new forward’s goal. Ian Callaghan then added the fourth after twelve minutes, with Ian St John providing the pass. The score remained 4–0 at half-time and at the final whistle. Horton also records an Evans effort disallowed for offside after the interval.

The debut was followed a week later by another striking result. Liverpool won 6–0 at Molineux, with Evans scoring twice against his former club. The two matches gave a new signing three league goals while Liverpool’s winning sequence continued towards first place.

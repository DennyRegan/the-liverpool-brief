John Radford’s late goal gave Arsenal a 2–1 victory over Liverpool at Highbury on 15 October 1968 and ended Liverpool’s League Cup campaign in the fourth round. Chris Lawler had equalised with fifteen minutes remaining, but Liverpool could not take the tie to a replay.

Their route to this stage had begun with a 4–0 home win over Sheffield United. A 2–0 victory against Swansea Town followed, with Lawler scoring the opener in that third-round match. The move to Highbury brought a first away tie in Liverpool’s cup run.

Peter Simpson put Arsenal ahead after 33 minutes, and the home side carried that advantage into half-time. Liverpool remained a goal behind until the 75th minute, when Lawler levelled. It was his second goal in successive League Cup rounds.

The equaliser kept the contest open until Radford scored after 87 minutes. Arsenal regained the lead with only three minutes of normal time remaining and advanced on a 2–1 scoreline. They would reach the final, where Swindon Town beat them.

For Liverpool, this was a second competition lost in less than a fortnight. Athletic Bilbao had eliminated them from the Fairs Cup on 2 October. Their recent league results still sustained a championship challenge, but the League Cup was now another route to silverware closed.

Roger Hunt scored the only goal as Liverpool beat Sheffield Wednesday 1–0 at Anfield on 1 February 1969. It was his 234th league goal for the club, taking him beyond Gordon Hodgson’s total of 233, and it kept Liverpool at the top of the First Division.

The first half ended goalless. Hunt broke the deadlock after 65 minutes, with Peter Thompson credited with the assist. Liverpool held the advantage to the final whistle, adding two points to a title challenge that had carried them through the winter in first place.

The scoring milestone needs a distinction between two club records. Hunt had already passed Hodgson’s overall Liverpool total in November 1967. Against Wednesday he surpassed the league total, a separate achievement accumulated across Liverpool’s Second Division years and their subsequent return to the top flight.

There is also a correction in the historical tally. At the time, Hunt was believed to have broken the league record against Chelsea two weeks earlier. An error in the accumulated total placed the milestone too soon; the corrected record identifies this Wednesday match, as the club’s later account confirms.

Liverpool stood on 45 points from 29 games. Leeds had 44 from 28 and Everton 42 from 29, so Hunt’s decisive goal preserved a narrow lead rather than opening a commanding gap. The next league match, against Nottingham Forest, would bring a damaging home defeat.

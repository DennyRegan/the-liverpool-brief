Leeds United secured their first league championship with a 0–0 draw against Liverpool at Anfield on 28 April 1969. Liverpool could no longer catch the visitors, even with two matches still to play.

The arithmetic had made the meeting decisive. Leeds arrived five points ahead, with Liverpool holding a game in hand. After the draw Leeds had 65 points from 41 matches and Liverpool sixty from forty. Four points were the most Liverpool could still earn, leaving the new champions beyond reach.

Liverpool’s starting attack included Bobby Graham and Alun Evans. The game produced few clear chances. The retrospective account reproduced by LFChistory records efforts wide from Ian Callaghan and Evans before the interval, and a Gary Sprake save from Callaghan in the second half. Evans also missed a later opportunity after an Ian St John pass.

Leeds held the goalless scoreline. Their official historical account recalls the defensive work of Jack Charlton and Norman Hunter in a match that delivered the point required. The wider retrospective also records the Kop applauding the champions, a sporting response to the end of Liverpool’s own challenge.

Liverpool’s season continued with defeat at Manchester City and a draw at Newcastle. They finished second on 61 points, six behind Leeds. Anfield’s final league match had decided the title in the visitors’ favour, leaving Liverpool as runners-up after a long pursuit.

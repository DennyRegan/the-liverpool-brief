Tommy Smith’s direct free kick earned Liverpool a 1–1 draw with Everton at Anfield on 8 October 1968. Alan Ball had put Everton ahead ten minutes earlier, ending Liverpool’s sequence of league clean sheets before Smith recovered a point.

Liverpool entered the derby after five successive league victories, scoring eighteen goals without conceding. The latest, a 4–0 win at Burnley three days before, had taken them to the top of the First Division. Everton therefore faced a side whose recent results had combined substantial scoring with an unbroken defensive record.

The first half was goalless. Ball broke through after 65 minutes to give Everton the lead and score the first league goal Liverpool had conceded since August. Liverpool’s response came after 75 minutes, when Smith equalised directly from a free kick.

There were no further goals. The result ended the five-match winning run but kept Liverpool first, with nineteen points from thirteen games. Leeds and Arsenal each had eighteen, while Everton had seventeen. Leeds had played two fewer matches than Liverpool, so the position still left room for the chasing sides.

The season’s earlier derby, at Goodison Park on 27 August, had finished 0–0. Smith’s equaliser ensured the return also ended level: neither neighbour won a league derby in a campaign in which both would finish among the top three.

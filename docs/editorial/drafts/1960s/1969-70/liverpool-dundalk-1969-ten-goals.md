Liverpool scored five goals in each half to beat Dundalk 10–0 at Anfield on 16 September 1969. Seven different players contributed to the Inter-Cities Fairs Cup first-leg victory, while Alec Lindsay made a scoring competitive debut.

Alun Evans opened the scoring after just 59 seconds. Chris Lawler added the second after ten minutes, and Tommy Smith scored from a direct free kick after 24. Bobby Graham’s goal after 36 minutes and Evans’s second two minutes later left Liverpool 5–0 ahead at half-time.

The interval did not interrupt the scoring. Lindsay, playing in the forward line, added the sixth after 56 minutes. Smith then scored his second, followed by goals from Peter Thompson and Ian Callaghan. Graham completed the ten after 82 minutes, joining Evans and Smith in finishing with two goals apiece.

Ray Clemence was in goal because Tommy Lawrence was suffering from gastritis. The night therefore brought Lindsay’s first competitive appearance alongside another opportunity for the younger goalkeeper. Liverpool kept a clean sheet while recording their largest victory of the season.

The match also acquired a connection with a future manager. In Liverpool’s later account, Gérard Houllier recalled attending the Kop for the first time with Patrice Bergues, who would later work with him at the club. That recollection places their visit on a night when Liverpool scored ten without conceding.

A 4–0 victory in the return leg completed a 14–0 aggregate success. The first-leg margin had already given Liverpool a substantial advantage; the second match confirmed their progress to the next round.

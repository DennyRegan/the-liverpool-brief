Roger Hunt scored in injury time to earn Liverpool a 2–2 draw at The Hawthorns on 27 September 1969. The equaliser came on Larry Lloyd’s competitive debut for the club, placing a new arrival alongside one of the established forwards in the same match.

Lloyd had signed from Bristol Rovers for £50,000 in April. His first competitive appearance followed five months later, with Tommy Lawrence in goal and Tommy Smith captaining the side. Ron Yeats was absent from the starting eleven.

West Bromwich Albion went ahead through Jeff Astle after five minutes. Bobby Graham equalised after 25, and the teams reached half-time at 1–1. The second half brought another Albion lead when Danny Hegan scored after 53 minutes.

Alun Evans replaced Phil Boersma after 70 minutes. Liverpool remained behind until Hunt’s late goal, which turned a prospective defeat into a draw. Graham and Hunt had each cancelled an Albion advantage, leaving Liverpool with a point from a match in which they had twice trailed.

The draw followed Liverpool’s League Cup elimination at Manchester City three days earlier. It also came after the four consecutive league wins with which they had opened the season. At The Hawthorns, the immediate outcome was a rescued point; the longer record identifies Lloyd’s first competitive appearance as another step in the changing composition of Shankly’s side.

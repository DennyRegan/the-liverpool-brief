Second Division Watford knocked Liverpool out of the FA Cup with a 1–0 quarter-final victory at Vicarage Road on 21 February 1970. Barry Endean scored after 63 minutes, providing the only goal of the match.

Liverpool had reached the quarter-final by beating Leicester City 2–0 in a fifth-round replay. At Watford, the teams went into half-time at 0–0. Tommy Lawrence was Liverpool’s goalkeeper, with Ron Yeats and Ian St John also in the starting eleven.

Endean’s second-half goal settled the tie and ended Liverpool’s remaining cup campaign. Manchester City had eliminated them from the League Cup in September, and Vitória Setúbal had gone through on away goals in the Fairs Cup in November. The defeat at Vicarage Road left the league programme to complete.

The match subsequently became part of the account of Shankly’s rebuilding. In an official club article reproducing his autobiographical recollection, Shankly identified the Watford defeat as the point at which he decided to break up the established side. A later club account also records Ian Callaghan remembering changes in training after the game.

That retrospective significance sits alongside changes that had already begun. Roger Hunt had left in December; Larry Lloyd had joined the previous April; Ray Clemence had made his league debut in January. Watford was therefore a landmark in the later telling of the transition, within a process already visible in the playing record.

Everton beat Liverpool 2–0 at Anfield on 21 March 1970, reversing the result of the December derby at Goodison Park. The league leaders scored once in each half, with the first goal credited to Joe Royle and the second scored by Alan Whittle.

Royle was credited with the early opener, leaving Everton 1–0 ahead at half-time. Whittle added the second after 48 minutes. Liverpool sent on Alun Evans for Peter Thompson after 71 minutes but did not score.

The contemporary Liverpool Echo reports, preserved by LFChistory, describe Alan Ball’s influence in midfield and early saves by Ray Clemence. One report records Clemence keeping out a misdirected Ron Yeats header and a header from John Hurst before Everton took the lead.

The first goal’s actual contact was disputed afterwards. The reproduced Echo accounts include differing claims from Royle and Yeats, so the official credit to Royle is clearer than any definitive description of the touch that sent the ball in.

Liverpool were fifth in the table after the match, while Everton remained first. The visitors went on to win the championship with 66 points; Liverpool finished fifth with 51. The two league derbies had produced one win each, both with the away side keeping a clean sheet.

Geoff Strong’s appearance proved his last for Liverpool. He finished with 201 competitive games and 33 goals for the club. The March derby thus carried a final entry in one established player’s record alongside Everton’s progress towards the title.

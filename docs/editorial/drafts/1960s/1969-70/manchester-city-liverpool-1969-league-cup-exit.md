Manchester City ended Liverpool’s League Cup campaign with a 3–2 third-round victory at Maine Road on 24 September 1969. Liverpool had already beaten City twice in the league, but the cup meeting reversed those results.

Mike Doyle gave City an early lead before Alun Evans equalised after 21 minutes. Liverpool’s later account of the clubs’ League Cup meetings describes an attempted clearance striking Evans and sending the ball into the net. The sides reached half-time level at 1–1.

City regained the lead through Neil Young after 51 minutes. Phil Boersma then made his competitive Liverpool debut, replacing Evans after 65 minutes. A minute later Ian Bowyer scored City’s third, leaving Liverpool two goals behind.

Bobby Graham answered after 67 minutes, reducing the deficit to one almost immediately after Bowyer’s goal. That was the final goal of the match. The club’s retrospective records further Liverpool pressure, but no equaliser followed and City advanced.

The result stood in contrast to the August league meetings. Liverpool had won 3–2 at Anfield on 12 August and 2–0 at Maine Road eight days later. Those victories had formed part of their four-win opening to the league season, but provided no protection in a knockout tie.

Liverpool’s League Cup run consisted of a 2–1 second-round win at Watford followed by this defeat. City went on to win the competition, beating West Bromwich Albion 2–1 in the final. For Liverpool, the Fairs Cup and FA Cup remained the other cup routes after their first elimination of the season.

Liverpool’s FA Cup campaign ended at neutral Maine Road on 18 April 1968 when West Bromwich Albion won their quarter-final second replay 2–1. Tony Hateley had cancelled out Jeff Astle’s early goal, but Clive Clark scored the second-half winner that sent Albion into the semi-finals.

The tie had already occupied two matches. A goalless draw at The Hawthorns on 30 March was followed by a 1–1 replay at Anfield on 8 April. In that replay Hateley scored before Astle equalised, and extra time failed to separate the teams. The third meeting moved to Manchester City’s ground.

This time Astle scored first, putting West Brom ahead in the seventh minute. Hateley brought Liverpool level six minutes before half-time, and the interval again arrived with the sides tied at 1–1. Across three matches neither had yet established a lasting advantage.

Clark supplied it on the hour. His goal restored Albion’s lead, and Liverpool could not equalise again. A tie that had begun nearly three weeks earlier finally had a winner.

For Bill Shankly’s side the defeat concluded a nine-match cup run. Bournemouth and Boscombe Athletic, Walsall and Tottenham Hotspur had each required a replay before Liverpool progressed. West Brom needed a second replay, and Liverpool’s third meeting with them ended without a place in the last four.

Hateley finished with eight goals in Liverpool’s nine-match FA Cup run, including one in each replay against Albion. His equaliser at Maine Road was Liverpool’s final goal in the competition that season. West Brom subsequently beat Birmingham City in the semi-final and Everton in the final, turning the victory over Liverpool into part of a successful route to the trophy.

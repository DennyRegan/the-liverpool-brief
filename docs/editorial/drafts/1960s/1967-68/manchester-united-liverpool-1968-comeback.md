Liverpool recovered from an early George Best goal to beat Manchester United 2–1 at Old Trafford on 6 April 1968. Ron Yeats equalised and Roger Hunt scored the winner, with all three goals arriving in the opening seventeen minutes.

Best had scored twice in United’s 2–1 win at Anfield the previous November. In the return fixture he put United ahead after only three minutes, but Liverpool’s response came quickly. Yeats brought them level in the ninth minute, and Hunt scored eight minutes later to put Bill Shankly’s side in front.

The score was already 2–1 to Liverpool before twenty minutes had been played. It remained that way at half-time and throughout the second half. Tony Hateley started alongside Hunt and was replaced by Alf Arrowsmith late in the match; Ian Ross and Geoff Strong were also in Liverpool’s starting eleven.

The victory kept Liverpool close to the leaders. They stood fourth with 45 points from 33 matches. Leeds United and Manchester United had 47, having played 34 and 35 games respectively, while Manchester City were third on 45 from 34. Liverpool had nine league fixtures left and games in hand on all three clubs above them.

They had also won a third successive league match, following victories over Burnley and Sheffield Wednesday. The Old Trafford result recovered the points lost to United at Anfield and kept the championship contest open as April’s fixtures gathered pace.

Liverpool’s next match came only two days later, an FA Cup replay against West Bromwich Albion at Anfield. Their league challenge and cup campaign were proceeding together, and this away win had preserved the possibility of advancing both.

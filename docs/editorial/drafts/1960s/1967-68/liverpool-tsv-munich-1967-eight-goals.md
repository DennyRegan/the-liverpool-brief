Liverpool recorded their biggest European victory to that point when they beat TSV 1860 Munich 8–0 at Anfield on 7 November 1967. Roger Hunt and Ian Callaghan scored twice each, with four other players contributing to a Fairs Cup first leg that left the German side facing an eight-goal deficit.

Bill Shankly’s team had reached the second round by beating Malmö 4–1 on aggregate. Against Munich they established an advantage almost immediately. Ian St John opened the scoring in the sixth minute, and Tony Hateley added the second shortly afterwards. The following day’s Daily Express described St John’s opener as a shot from distance and Hateley’s goal as a header.

Tommy Smith converted a penalty near half-time to make it 3–0. Liverpool already had a substantial lead, but the margin grew much more quickly after the interval. Hunt scored the fourth, Peter Thompson the fifth and Hunt the sixth in a short burst early in the second half.

The contemporary report described Thompson scoring from outside the penalty area. It also singled out Hunt’s second, recounting how he turned close to goal, eluded defenders and the goalkeeper and finished with his left foot. These goals took Liverpool from a three-goal advantage to six before the second half was far advanced.

Callaghan then supplied the last two. The Daily Express recorded a left-footed shot for his first and an individual move for his second, completing an evening on which six Liverpool players scored against Petar Radenkovic. The goalkeeper, also known for his recording career, had conceded eight while Liverpool kept a clean sheet.

There was still a return leg to play. Liverpool lost 2–1 in Munich a week later but advanced 9–2 on aggregate. The scale of the Anfield result made that defeat immaterial to qualification; it did not guarantee the same outcome in the next round, where Ferencváros would end Liverpool’s European campaign.

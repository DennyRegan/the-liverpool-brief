Liverpool reached the FA Cup quarter-finals by beating holders Tottenham Hotspur 2–1 at Anfield on 12 March 1968. Roger Hunt gave them the lead, Tommy Smith scored a retaken penalty and a late goal from substitute Cliff Jones left Spurs one short of an equaliser.

The teams had drawn 1–1 at White Hart Lane three days earlier. For Liverpool it was a third successive cup tie requiring a replay: Bournemouth and Boscombe Athletic and Walsall had both been held away before being beaten at Anfield. Tottenham, who had won the previous year’s final against Chelsea, now faced the same second-match task.

Hunt put Liverpool ahead in the 23rd minute. The home side retained that 1–0 advantage at half-time and through most of the second half. Spurs then brought on Jones for Dave Mackay shortly before Liverpool’s second goal.

Smith’s penalty involved a reprieve. LFChistory records that Pat Jennings saved the first kick, but Tottenham had twelve players on the field while making their substitution. The referee ordered a retake, and Smith scored at the second attempt. Liverpool led 2–0 with twelve minutes remaining.

Jones scored for Tottenham in the 88th minute. His goal reduced the margin to one with little time left, and Spurs could not force another equaliser. Liverpool had eliminated the holders after winning a third replay at home.

Six matches had taken Bill Shankly’s side through three rounds. The sequence had begun with the January draw at Bournemouth and continued through Walsall in February to Tottenham in March. Liverpool’s quarter-final opponents were West Bromwich Albion, and that tie would require still more matches before either side could claim a semi-final place.

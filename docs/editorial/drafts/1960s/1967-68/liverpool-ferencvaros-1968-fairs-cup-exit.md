Ferencváros ended Liverpool’s Fairs Cup campaign with a 1–0 victory at Anfield on 9 January 1968. László Branikovits scored the only goal, completing a 2–0 aggregate win for the Hungarian side after their identical first-leg result in Budapest.

Liverpool had begun the competition with victories in both games against Malmö and had then scored eight at home to TSV 1860 Munich. The third-round tie presented a much narrower task. Sándor Katona’s goal in Budapest on 28 November had left Bill Shankly’s team one behind before the return.

At Anfield, Branikovits increased that deficit inside the opening twenty minutes. Ferencváros led 1–0 on the night and by two goals across the tie. Liverpool reached half-time still behind, and the second half produced no change to the score.

Geoff Strong started alongside the familiar attacking names of Ian Callaghan, Roger Hunt, Ian St John and Peter Thompson. Tony Hateley was among the listed substitutes. Liverpool nevertheless finished the tie without a goal in either game; the thirteen they had scored in the first two rounds supplied no protection against elimination here.

Ferencváros’s achievement was made without Flórián Albert. In a later recollection published by the Puskás Institute, Gyula Rákosi explained that the injured Albert had missed both Liverpool matches and that Branikovits had taken his opportunity in the return. Rákosi also recalled the difficulty of winning at Anfield.

The visitors went on to reach the final, losing to Leeds United over two legs. Liverpool’s route ended in the third round, leaving domestic football as their remaining competitive programme. The Anfield defeat had completed a tie decided by a single Ferencváros goal in each match.

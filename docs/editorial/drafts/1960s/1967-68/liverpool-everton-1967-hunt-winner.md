Roger Hunt scored the only goal of the Merseyside derby at Anfield on 23 September 1967, giving Liverpool a 1–0 win over Everton. His breakthrough in the 78th minute kept Bill Shankly’s side at the head of the First Division table after nine matches.

Liverpool had lost their previous league game 1–0 at Southampton. They had since opened their Fairs Cup campaign with a 2–0 win at Malmö, but the derby offered the opportunity to recover league points against their neighbours. The home record was still perfect: Arsenal, Newcastle and Chelsea had all been beaten at Anfield.

Everton arrived with Harry Catterick in charge and a side that included Alan Ball, Howard Kendall and Colin Harvey. Liverpool retained the attacking names that had begun the campaign together: Ian Callaghan, Hunt, Tony Hateley, Ian St John and Peter Thompson. Neither team scored in the first half, and the match remained level well into the second.

Hunt finally separated them with twelve minutes remaining. LFChistory credits Hateley with the assist, another contribution from the partnership that had shared five goals against Newcastle in August. Everton could not find an equaliser, and Liverpool completed a fourth successive home league victory.

The two points took Liverpool to thirteen, level with Arsenal and Sheffield Wednesday but first on goal average. Everton stood fifteenth with seven. Liverpool had answered the Southampton defeat with a result that preserved both their unbeaten home record and their place above the other early leaders.

Liverpool’s League Cup campaign ended at Burnden Park on 27 September 1967 when Bolton Wanderers won their second-round replay 3–2. Tommy Smith had brought Liverpool level before half-time, but two Bolton goals in the second half left Ian Callaghan’s late reply insufficient.

The teams had drawn 1–1 at Anfield a fortnight earlier. Liverpool therefore travelled to Bolton still seeking their first win in the competition that season. Four days before the replay they had beaten Everton in the league, but the same starting eleven could not secure another victory.

Gordon Taylor gave Bolton the lead after twenty minutes. Liverpool answered through Smith, who converted a penalty in the 35th minute, and the sides reached the interval at 1–1. The tie remained unresolved after a match and a half, with Liverpool again having recovered from a deficit.

Bolton established the decisive advantage during a five-minute spell late in the second half. Freddie Hill made it 2–1 in the 70th minute, and Taylor scored his second five minutes later. Liverpool now needed two goals simply to draw level.

Callaghan reduced the arrears in the 80th minute, leaving ten minutes in which to seek another equaliser. None followed. Bolton progressed, and Liverpool’s two League Cup appearances had produced a draw and a defeat against the same opponents.

The replay closed one route to a trophy while the league and European campaigns were still in their early stages. Liverpool returned to league football three days later with a 2–1 home win over Stoke City, but their involvement in this cup was already over before September ended.

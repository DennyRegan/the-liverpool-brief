Billy Liddell’s final competitive appearance for Liverpool ended in a 1–0 defeat by Southampton at Anfield on 31 August 1960. On the same evening Gordon Milne made his debut. Two careers crossed in a side still some distance from the team Bill Shankly would build.

Liverpool had already lost 4–1 at Southampton a week earlier. Between the two meetings came a draw at Middlesbrough, leaving the opening victory over Leeds United as their only league win. Liddell returned at centre-forward in place of Dave Hickson; Milne, newly arrived from Preston North End, took his place among the half-backs.

The start offered Liverpool opportunities to alter that sequence. Tommy Leishman struck a post within the first five minutes, during which Liverpool also forced four corners. Southampton goalkeeper Ron Reynolds made two saves singled out in the following morning’s Daily Express report. The score at half-time was 0–0.

That account was much harsher about Liddell. Its correspondent described a player whose desire remained but whose attempts to control the ball, turn and shoot were no longer coming easily. It was an uncomfortable assessment of a footballer who had carried so much of Liverpool’s attack through the previous decade.

Southampton found the decisive combination around midway through the second half. Tommy Mulgrew supplied George O’Brien, who scored the only goal. Liverpool could not recover. The defeat left them with three points from their first four league matches.

For Liddell, it was appearance number 534, then the club record. He had helped Liverpool win the 1946–47 championship, played in the 1950 FA Cup final and remained through relegation and the years in the Second Division. His competitive playing career ended with 228 goals. The testimonial that followed in September gave supporters a separate occasion to honour him.

Milne’s Liverpool story was just beginning. He would contribute to promotion and two First Division titles, but those achievements lay ahead. On his first night, the immediate problem was another defeat. Shankly’s first full season had opened with a change of personnel and a reminder of the work still required.

Jimmy Harrower answered both of Sheffield United’s goals in a 4–2 Liverpool victory at Anfield on 26 November 1960. His hat-trick first helped overturn the leaders’ advantage, then checked their attempt to recover. Liverpool’s autumn improvement had produced a win against the side they were chasing.

Shankly’s team had arrived with three successive league victories behind them, over Plymouth Argyle, Norwich City and Charlton Athletic. The poor opening weeks were giving way to a sustained promotion challenge. Sheffield United provided a more direct test of it.

Billy Russell put the visitors ahead after 23 minutes. Harrower equalised on the half-hour, and the teams went in level. Liverpool had recovered the score; the decisive separation came after the interval.

Dave Hickson made it 2–1 ten minutes into the second half. Harrower added his second in the 63rd minute, giving Liverpool a two-goal advantage against the division’s leading side. Three consecutive Liverpool goals had transformed the match.

Russell then reopened it. His second goal, with a quarter of an hour remaining, reduced United’s deficit to one. Harrower’s response took only two minutes. His third restored the margin to 4–2, leaving the visitors to find two further goals instead of one. They found neither.

Liverpool remained second, with 26 points from 19 matches. United still led on 31, having played a game more. This was a significant result within the chase, rather than a change at the top: the distance had narrowed, but there was more to do.

Harrower would finish the campaign with eight league goals and one in the FA Cup. Before the season ended, he had left for Newcastle United. His part in the developing Shankly team was shorter than those of several players around him, but this afternoon was a substantial contribution to its promotion attempt.

The final table eventually favoured United, who went up with Ipswich Town while Liverpool finished third. At Anfield in November, however, Liverpool had come from behind, survived a renewed threat and beaten the leaders.

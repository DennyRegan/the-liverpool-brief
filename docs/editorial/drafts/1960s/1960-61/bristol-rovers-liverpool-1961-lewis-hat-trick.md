Kevin Lewis scored a hat-trick at Eastville on 4 April 1961 and still finished on the losing side. Liverpool twice recovered from behind against Bristol Rovers, only to concede two late goals and lose 4–3. A demanding Easter sequence ended with another setback in the promotion race.

The same opponents had been beaten 3–0 at Anfield on Good Friday. Liverpool then drew 1–1 at Sheffield United the following day before travelling to Bristol for the Tuesday return. Three matches in five days offered a substantial chance to shape the closing weeks of the season.

Rovers led through Bobby Jones in the first half. Lewis brought Liverpool level, and the interval arrived at 1–1. The pattern repeated after the restart: Jones scored again, leaving Liverpool to work back from a second deficit.

Lewis’s second equaliser came with a quarter of an hour left. At 2–2, Liverpool were level again, but the closing minutes took the match away from them. Harold Jarman restored Rovers’ lead and Peter Hooper added a fourth. Lewis then completed his hat-trick near the end, reducing the margin without rescuing the result. His three goals included a penalty.

The defeat left Liverpool third on 47 points, with five league games remaining. Sheffield United had 50 and Ipswich Town 52; all three had played 37 matches. Promotion was still available to pursue, but Liverpool had failed to build on their home victory over Rovers and had taken three points from the six available across those Easter fixtures.

Lewis’s scoring was one of the strengths of his first Liverpool season. Signed from Sheffield United the previous June, he would finish with 19 league goals and 22 in all national competitions, the club’s leading total. At Eastville that contribution could scarcely have been greater, yet Liverpool left without a point.

They eventually finished with 52, six behind promoted Sheffield United. Across the league campaign, nine away defeats contrasted with just two at Anfield.

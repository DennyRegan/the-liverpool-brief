Tommy Leishman scored Liverpool’s first League Cup goal, but their first evening in the competition ended with a replay to arrange. Dave Brogan equalised late for Luton Town at Anfield on 19 October 1960, turning a lead Liverpool had held since the first half into a 1–1 draw.

This was the inaugural season of the competition, and Liverpool entered at the second round. The occasion was modestly attended: 10,502 watched. The club issued a four-page programme costing twopence, compared with the usual 16-page first-team edition at threepence. A new national cup had arrived without the scale of an ordinary league afternoon at Anfield.

The opposition was already familiar. Liverpool and Luton had met twice in September, drawing 2–2 at Anfield before Luton won 2–1 at Kenilworth Road. Bill Shankly’s side had still to beat them that season.

Leishman gave Liverpool that opportunity with the opening goal after 25 minutes. The half-back supplied the breakthrough in a team containing Roger Hunt, Dave Hickson and Kevin Lewis, and Liverpool carried their advantage into the interval.

There was no second Liverpool goal. Brogan, making his Luton debut, supplied the equaliser at the end of the match. The scoring record places it in the final minute: enough to keep the visitors in the competition and require another meeting five days later.

At Kenilworth Road, Liverpool finally turned the pairing in their favour. Trailing at half-time, they scored five times after the break to win 5–2, with two goals apiece from Lewis and Hunt and one from Hickson. Their introduction to the League Cup nevertheless remained brief. Southampton won 2–1 at Anfield in the next round.

Leishman’s goal therefore began a cup history that initially consisted of three matches and one tie won. Liverpool would not enter again until 1967–68.

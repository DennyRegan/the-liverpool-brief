Sunderland settled the scoring early and resisted Liverpool for the rest of the afternoon. Harry Hooper’s third-minute goal and Ian Lawther’s second after 14 minutes carried the visitors to a 2–0 FA Cup fourth-round victory at Anfield on 28 January 1961.

Liverpool had beaten Coventry City 3–2 in the previous round. Sunderland arrived having eliminated Arsenal, and their early goals immediately made another cup success possible. Before a quarter of an hour had passed, Liverpool needed two simply to take the tie further.

The deficit remained intact at half-time. Dave Hickson, Kevin Lewis, Jimmy Melia, Alan A’Court and Johnny Morrissey were all in Liverpool’s starting side, but the goals that had taken the team past Coventry were absent. Sunderland went into the second half with their lead preserved.

Charlie Hurley later recalled defending the Kop end as Liverpool increasingly sent high balls towards the visitors’ defence. In Mark Metcalf’s biography of the centre-half, Hurley described that approach as one he welcomed. His recollection explains the frustration of the second half from the defender’s perspective: Liverpool were seeking a way back, but their delivery was giving him the sort of contest he wanted.

He also remembered applause from the Kop at the end. Sunderland had preserved their early advantage through the rest of the match. Liverpool failed to reduce the deficit, and the visitors advanced without conceding.

For Shankly, the FA Cup was the second national cup route to close that season, after Southampton had ended Liverpool’s League Cup involvement in November. Promotion remained the principal unfinished task. Four consecutive league victories followed in February, but Liverpool ultimately finished third in the Second Division.

Sunderland’s cup run continued through Norwich before ending against Tottenham in a quarter-final replay. At Anfield they had won the tie through an early lead and a second half in which Liverpool could not find the goal needed to change its direction.

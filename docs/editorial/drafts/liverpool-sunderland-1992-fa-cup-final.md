Michael Thomas and Ian Rush scored after half-time as Liverpool beat Sunderland 2–0 at Wembley on 9 May 1992. The victory brought the club its fifth FA Cup, with Steve McManaman creating the opening goal and Rush setting a record for scoring in finals.

Second Division Sunderland reached the interval on level terms. Thomas had wasted an early chance for Liverpool, shooting over after Ray Houghton sent him through, while John Byrne miscued a promising opportunity at the other end.

McManaman, who began on the left, moved to the right before half-time. Two minutes after the restart he worked his way past Sunderland’s defenders and lifted the ball into Thomas’s path. Thomas met it with a forceful right-footed shot that beat Tony Norman and put Liverpool ahead.

Norman kept out a long-range Mølby effort, and Dean Saunders headed against the crossbar as Liverpool sought a second. It came in the 67th minute. Saunders found Thomas, and the ball ran through to Rush, who finished low into the far corner.

Rush had now scored five goals across three FA Cup finals, adding to his doubles against Everton in 1986 and 1989. Liverpool had a two-goal lead to protect, and Sunderland could find no way back.

Graeme Souness was on the bench after heart surgery, while Ronnie Moran, who had deputised during his absence, led the team out. Captain Mark Wright lifted the trophy at the end of a final decided by Liverpool’s improvement after the interval.

Liverpool reached the FA Cup final without leading Portsmouth during four hours of their semi-final tie. After a second 120-minute contest finished goalless at Villa Park on 13 April 1992, three successful penalties carried them through the replay, 3–1 in the shoot-out.

Ronnie Moran was deputising for Graeme Souness, who was recovering from heart surgery. His side had been rescued by Ronnie Whelan’s late equaliser at Highbury eight days earlier, but Portsmouth again threatened to take the Wembley place.

Darren Anderton lobbed wide when Bruce Grobbelaar came out early in the game. Liverpool created their own opening through Michael Thomas and Dean Saunders, with Alan Knight making the save before Kit Symons blocked the follow-up.

Portsmouth came particularly close late in normal time, Alan McLoughlin hitting the crossbar after John Beresford’s delivery. Extra time brought no breakthrough, leaving the tie to be settled from the penalty spot.

Martin Kuhl put Portsmouth’s first kick wide. John Barnes scored for Liverpool, and although Symons levelled the shoot-out, Ian Rush converted to put Liverpool back ahead. Grobbelaar then saved Warren Neill’s penalty.

Saunders made it three successful Liverpool kicks from three. Beresford had to score to keep Portsmouth in contention, but his effort went wide and the contest was over.

Liverpool could now prepare for Sunderland at Wembley on 9 May. Portsmouth had conceded just once across four hours of football, but the penalty shoot-out had cost them their Wembley place.

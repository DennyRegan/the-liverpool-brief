## 1. Saint-Étienne: the first leg

**Saint-Étienne 1 Liverpool 0. European Cup quarter-final, first leg. Wednesday 2 March 1977, Stade Geoffroy-Guichard. Attendance 28,000.**

Liverpool went to France without Kevin Keegan. He travelled with the squad but failed a fitness test, and LFChistory records that he did not even make the bench. Horace Yates of the Liverpool Daily Post thought John Toshack missed him most, and wrote that Toshack without Keegan was like coffee without cream.

Saint-Étienne were French champions and had reached the final the previous May. Dominique Rocheteau, their young winger, was back after half a season out injured. Yates judged that he was nowhere near his best, and far more manageable than Liverpool had expected.

For most of the night the plan worked. Phil Thompson, playing in place of Tommy Smith, had the ball in the net and was ruled offside, and later headed wide from in front of goal. Around the 70th minute Steve Heighway beat Gérard Farison, ran into the penalty area with the goalkeeper coming out, and hit the post. The few Liverpool supporters in the ground were singing "When the Reds Go Marching In".

Then, with 12 minutes left, the one lapse. Ray Clemence turned a cross over his own bar for a corner. Gérard Janvion crossed, and Dominique Bathenay stabbed the ball in from six yards. Paisley took off Toshack and sent on David Johnson, but there was no way back that night.

Yates still called it one of Liverpool's great European performances, spoiled only by that moment. The Daily Mirror's reporter noted that the Liverpool players left the field laughing, confident that the second leg would put it right. A fortnight later, at Anfield, they needed two goals after Saint-Étienne had scored, and found the second six minutes from time.

## 2. FC Zürich: the road to Rome

**FC Zürich 1 Liverpool 3. European Cup semi-final, first leg. Wednesday 6 April 1977, Letzigrund. Attendance 30,000.**
**Liverpool 3 FC Zürich 0. Second leg. Wednesday 20 April 1977, Anfield. Attendance 50,611. Liverpool won 6–1 on aggregate.**

After Saint-Étienne, the semi-final draw gave Liverpool the Swiss champions. It was only the second time Liverpool had reached the last four of the European Cup. The first had been in 1965, when Inter Milan beat them. In the other semi-final, Borussia Mönchengladbach played Dynamo Kyiv.

The first leg in Zürich began badly. With Phil Thompson injured, Tommy Smith was back in the side, and inside the first six minutes his tackle on Alfred Scheiwiler sent the ball over the byline. Frank McGhee of the Daily Mirror thought that in England it would have been worth no more than a corner. The Turkish referee, Doğan Babaçan, gave a penalty. Clemence got a hand to Peter Risi's kick but could not keep it out. A few minutes later Smith was booked for a foul on René Botteron, whom McGhee rated Zürich's most dangerous midfielder.

Liverpool were level in the 14th minute. Hilmar Zigerlig fouled Keegan, Ray Kennedy floated the free kick across, and Phil Neal arrived to score. McGhee gave some of the credit to Terry McDermott, whose decoy run had pulled the defence away. From there, by McGhee's account, Liverpool did what they always tried to do away from home in Europe: slow the game down and quieten the crowd.

The tie turned at the start of the second half. Heighway forced his way between three defenders to collect a pass and score. Midway through the half he was brought down in full flight, Babaçan gave Liverpool a penalty, and Neal scored his second. It finished 3–1.

Emlyn Hughes called the result "bloody marvellous", according to the agency report. Paisley praised Botteron but said that, as a team, Zürich did not compare with Saint-Étienne. Zürich's coach, Timo Konietzka, blamed the injuries that had plagued his side.

\* \* \*

The second leg was a formality, and it was played like one. The Daily Mirror noted that Zürich played with only two men forward. Jimmy Case scored just after the half-hour, and Yates wrote that Liverpool could easily have had four by then.

The only worry came 20 minutes from the end, when Heighway went off with a knock on the knee and Alan Waddle came on. Then Liverpool scored twice more in the last 15 minutes. Case scored his second with a direct free kick, and Waddle made the third for Keegan. Yates thought the Swiss had never looked like causing an upset.

He also noted that Smith was the only Liverpool player on the pitch who had played in the 1965 semi-final against Inter. The gate receipts were the biggest in the club's history. Liverpool were the third English club to reach a European Cup final, after Manchester United and Leeds United. Gladbach beat Dynamo Kyiv the same night, so the final would be against the West German champions, in Rome on 25 May.

There was no time to enjoy it. Three days later Liverpool were at Maine Road for an FA Cup semi-final against Everton.

## 3. Everton: two afternoons at Maine Road

**Liverpool 2 Everton 2. FA Cup semi-final. Saturday 23 April 1977, Maine Road. Attendance 52,637.**
**Liverpool 3 Everton 0. Replay. Wednesday 27 April 1977, Maine Road. Attendance 52,579.**

Everton came to the semi-final with something to play for beyond the derby. They had already reached the League Cup final that season, and Yates pointed out that one more win would make them the first club to reach two Wembley finals in the same season. Gordon Lee had not been in charge long, and Yates credited him with giving Evertonians back their pride. Liverpool were heavy favourites.

It rained and blew all afternoon, and the pitch played its part from the first minute. David Fairclough got clear and struck at the ball in front of goal, but a puddle had stopped it dead and he swung at nothing. Yates had little doubt it would have been a goal.

Liverpool led after ten minutes anyway. David Lawson, the Everton goalkeeper, was well off his line, and McDermott chipped the ball over him. By LFChistory's account Hughes said afterwards that the team had talked about Lawson's habit of coming a long way out. The Liverpool end started singing about running round Wembley with the Cup.

That was premature. After 33 minutes Hughes tried to beat Jim Pearson on the touchline instead of putting the ball out, slipped on the wet turf, and Pearson ran through. Martin Dobson's shot rebounded and Duncan McKenzie stroked it in. Yates noted that Liverpool did not win a single corner in the first half.

Fairclough's first-minute effort had brought back a back strain, and he went off after 72 minutes. A minute later Liverpool were ahead again. Terry Darracott fouled Heighway, Kennedy's free kick dropped into the goalmouth, and Lawson, challenged by Keegan, punched it straight to Case, who took his time and headed into the corner.

Everton came back again. Eight minutes from the end, Bruce Rioch ran in to score his first goal for the club.

Then came the moment the game is remembered for. In the last minute Ronnie Goodlass crossed, McKenzie headed on, and Bryan Hamilton, on as a substitute, put the ball past Clemence. The referee, Clive Thomas, disallowed it. Yates was sure Hamilton was inches offside. The papers could not agree on what television showed: the Daily Mirror's reporter did not think the pictures supported the decision, while the agency report said the camera proved Thomas right. LFChistory's account today says the goal was wrongly ruled out.

Yates was candid. Everton had been the better side for much of the game, and he wrote that the Liverpool players graciously admitted Everton had surprised them.

\* \* \*

Thomas refereed the replay four days later, and he was in the middle of it again. On the half-hour, Hughes floated a free kick into the box, and Thomas judged that Mike Pejic had pushed David Johnson in the back as they went up for it. The Everton players protested furiously. Charles Burgess of the Daily Post thought the push hardly noticeable. McGhee in the Mirror saw it differently: as he saw it, Johnson was clearly shoved in the back a yard inside the area. Neal put the penalty past Lawson's dive.

The derby edge never went away. After Kennedy fouled Rioch, Thomas told both dug-outs to calm down, and Lee, who had come off the bench, went straight back to it. Everton had their chances, and until the closing minutes a goal for them would have reopened the tie.

Then Liverpool finished it in two minutes. With three minutes left Case sidestepped a defender and drove the ball past Lawson from the edge of the box. Two minutes later Kennedy scored from close range at the second attempt, after Keegan's free kick from the right had beaten the defence. As the final whistle went, the Liverpool supporters were singing "We Shall Not Be Moved".

Tommy Smith had a score to settle. By LFChistory's account, Joe Fagan reminded him after the first game that McKenzie had nutmegged him: "He nutmegged you, didn't he?" Smith said an early tackle in the replay made sure it did not happen again. Afterwards, according to the agency report, he said Liverpool had been over-confident on the Saturday and Everton had been the over-confident ones in the replay. Hughes would not talk about the treble yet, not until Liverpool had won two of the three.

It was Liverpool's third FA Cup final of the decade. Their opponents at Wembley on 21 May would be Manchester United.

## 4. Manchester United: the end of the treble

**Liverpool 1 Manchester United 2. FA Cup final. Saturday 21 May 1977, Wembley. Attendance 100,000.**

Liverpool came to Wembley as league champions, with the European Cup final four days later. No English club had won the league, the FA Cup and the European Cup in the same season.

Liverpool had most of the first half and should have scored before the break. Three minutes before half-time Kennedy met a McDermott cross with a downward header, and Alex Stepney appeared to keep it out at the foot of the post. Yates reported a conversation between the two afterwards. Stepney admitted he had never touched it. The ball had hit the post. Kennedy told him he would get the credit for it anyway.

All three goals came in a five-minute spell early in the second half. Hughes hesitated over a clearance, Stuart Pearson raced away, and his shot went in under Clemence's body. The Sunday Express timed it at 50 minutes 29 seconds.

Liverpool were level almost at once. Joey Jones made the chance, and Case finished it with what Yates called power and precision.

Three minutes later the Cup was gone. Smith, who had been playing beautifully, tussled with Jimmy Greenhoff inside the penalty area. By Yates's account Smith slipped, and Greenhoff got the ball to Lou Macari. Smith maintained afterwards that he had been pulled down. Macari's shot was going wide until it struck Greenhoff, who was turning away, and was deflected past Clemence.

Ian Callaghan came on for Johnson, and Liverpool pressed for the rest of the game. Case was their most dangerous player. Kennedy came closest, with a shot that hit the post. Yates noted that Kennedy had been within reach of becoming the first player to win two League and FA Cup doubles, having done it with Arsenal in 1971. For McDermott it was a second losers' medal at Wembley. The first had come with Newcastle in 1974, against Liverpool.

The players took it hard. Clemence told Yates: "If your name's on the Cup you win." Paisley said Liverpool had nothing to be ashamed of and that he was proud of them, and Yates wrote that the words hid a broken heart. Joey Jones came into the dressing room with blood on his face from a cut head and tears running down it.

For Tommy Docherty, after several Wembley disappointments, it was a Wembley win at last. For Liverpool there were four days to recover before Rome.

## 5. Borussia Mönchengladbach: Rome

**Liverpool 3 Borussia Mönchengladbach 1. European Cup final. Wednesday 25 May 1977, Stadio Olimpico, Rome. Attendance 57,000.**

The 62nd and last game of the season, and the trophy the club had chased since 1964. Terry McDermott scored first, Allan Simonsen equalised, and Tommy Smith headed Liverpool back in front. Phil Neal's penalty, after Berti Vogts brought down Keegan, made it 3–1. It was Keegan's last game for the club.

### Check notes

1. **Quotes, one per source:** "bloody marvellous" (agency, Hughes); "He nutmegged you, didn't he?" (LFChistory, Fagan); "If your name's on the Cup you win" (Daily Post, Clemence). The Daily Post carries several writers, so Yates and Burgess are treated as separate sources; still only one direct quote from the Post. The song titles are not quotes from a source.
2. **Minutes:** sources differ by a minute or two throughout (Case at Wembley 52' or 53'; Case v Zürich 32' or 33'). The pieces use "just after the half-hour", "three minutes later" and similar, and give a minute only where the sources agree or where it's a named paper's timing (Pearson, 50 min 29 sec, Sunday Express).
3. **The disallowed Hamilton goal** is contested, and the piece says so: Yates says offside, the Mirror doubted the TV evidence, the agency report says TV backed Thomas, and LFChistory today says it was wrong. Later accounts sometimes give a different reason for the decision. Those weren't checked here, so the piece sticks to what was reported at the time.
4. **Heighway's goal in Zürich:** the agency report says Kennedy's pass, LFChistory credits McDermott with the assist. The piece doesn't name the passer.
5. **Smith the only survivor of 1965 (Zürich second leg):** this comes from Yates, but the OCR is broken around it. Callaghan also played in 1965 but was injured on 20 April 1977, so the claim is consistent. **Worth a glance at the page image before publishing.**
6. **Everton and the League Cup final:** Yates's framing. The piece doesn't say how Everton's League Cup final went, because it wasn't checked here.
7. **Docherty:** the Daily Post says he won "after eight Wembley failures of one sort or another". The piece says "after several Wembley disappointments" because the count wasn't verified.
8. **Thompson for Smith in France:** from the LFChistory line-up (Thompson started, Smith was on the bench). The Saint-Étienne Match Revisited piece has Smith starting the second leg, which is consistent.
9. **Attendances:** Saint-Étienne away 28,000 (Daily Post and Mirror results panels; LFChistory 40,000). Zürich away 30,000 (Daily Post; LFChistory 28,000). Rome 57,000 (Daily Record). All match the season data file.
10. **Left out on purpose:** the ferry incident reported after the Saint-Étienne away trip (Liverpool Echo, 4 Mar 1977; not read in full), the gate receipts for the Everton games, and the "Liverpool must axe Tommy Smith" column in the Reading Evening Post.
11. **From general record, not re-checked today:** Kennedy's 1971 double with Arsenal; McDermott playing for Newcastle in the 1974 final. Both are well established, and Yates refers to both.

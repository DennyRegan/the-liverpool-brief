# Fact-check: Albert Stubbins (draft: /home/claude/bios/gpt/albert-stubbins.md)

Checked 10 November 2026. This is an independent check. The GPT draft and its source list were used only as pointers.

## Sensitive events flag

- **Death:** the draft mentions Stubbins's death ("When he died in December 2002"). It is a historical death and the draft handles it plainly. Wikipedia says he died "after a short illness" in Cullercoats. The draft does not mention the illness and does not need to.
- **Illness:** the draft says "Kay retired through illness in 1951". This is accurate (LFC: retired January 1951 because of illness). The wording is fine.
- **Injuries:** the draft mentions Stubbins's injuries and the lacerated knees from the Birmingham goal. These are sporting injuries and not sensitive.
- **No mention of Heysel or the Hillsborough disaster.**

## 1. Summary

The draft is reliable on the core record. The dates, the 178 appearances and 84 goals, the season-by-season figures, the debut and last match, the Birmingham hat-trick, the Molineux match, the confirmation of the title on 14 June 1947 and the 1950 Cup run all check out against LFChistory and liverpoolfc.com. The main problems are the transfer fee (sources disagree), the cause of the 1948–49 contract dispute (the draft gives only one of two reported causes and calls it amicable when another source describes bad feeling), the unsourced claim that Stubbins was asked for permission for the Sgt Pepper cover, and the description of his Ashington role. The draft contains no direct quotations.

**Errors and issues:**
1. **Transfer fee:** sources disagree. Liverpool paid £13,000 according to LFChistory, LFC "Liverpool's Greatest No.70", the Daily Mail of 13 Sept 1946 and efcstatto. It was £12,500 according to the LFC Boot Room timeline, the Telegraph obituary, Spartacus, Play Up Liverpool and Wikipedia, and £12,000 according to the Liverpool Daily Post obituary. Everyone agrees it was a **club** record. It was **not** a British record: the British record was still the £14,000 Arsenal paid for Bryn Jones in 1938, £1,000 more (Daily Mail 1946 and LFChistory). The draft does not claim a British record, which is correct. It should hedge the figure.
2. **Contract dispute, 1948–49:** the draft gives only Stubbins's later explanation (the Football Echo column promised by McConnell) and calls it amicable. The LFC Boot Room and Spartacus say the dispute was over his wish to live or train in the North-East while the club wanted him on Merseyside. Spartacus says this caused "a great deal of bad feeling". The "amicable" line is Stubbins's own word (Platt interview, LFChistory) and should be attributed to him, not stated as fact.
3. **Sgt Pepper "Stubbins recalled being asked for permission to use it":** no source found. LFC (as reported in the Irish Echo) says McCartney had Apple contact Stubbins to tell him he was on the cover. LFC also says McCartney later sent him a signed copy with the message "Long may you bob and weave". Replace the sentence.
4. **"brief player-coach spell at Ashington":** no source says player-coach. Play Up Liverpool says he signed as player-manager on 3 Sept 1953 and was released at his own request on 13 Oct 1953. The Telegraph says Jackie Milburn persuaded him to join and that he "later briefly managed" the club. Spartacus says he moved there in Sept 1953 to play in the North Eastern League. Use neutral wording.
5. **Source list:** Stubbins naming Molineux and the Everton semi-final as his strongest memories comes from LFChistory article 1181 (1950 Cup final profiles), not article 571 ("Unique Programmes"). Article 571 does not mention Stubbins. The claim is right; the attribution in the source list is not.
6. **Minor ambiguity:** "Burnley held them to a goalless draw at Ewood Park before winning the replay 1–0 at Maine Road". It is grammatically correct (Burnley won), but it is easy to misread as Liverpool winning. Clarify.
7. **Not in the draft, but for any later edit:** his date of birth is disputed. It is 13 July 1919 according to LFChistory, the LFC Boot Room, Spartacus and Play Up Liverpool, and 17 July 1919 according to the Telegraph obituary and Wikipedia. His wartime goal total also varies by source: 231 in 188 (Spartacus, Wikipedia list), 232 in 190 (Boot Room), 237 in 199/218 (Wikipedia text, Telegraph) and 244 (LFChistory, Daily Post). The draft's wording, "a formidable scoring reputation" with no figure, is the safe option. If a figure is added, use "more than 230 wartime goals".

## 2. Current status (as of 10 November 2026)

Deceased. Died 28 December 2002, aged 83, in Cullercoats, Tyne and Wear. LFChistory gives 28 Dec 2002; the Telegraph obituary gives "December 28, aged 83"; Wikipedia and Spartacus give 28 Dec 2002 in Cullercoats; Play Up Liverpool gives 28 Dec 2002, Cullercoats. The draft's "December 2002" is correct.

## 4. Corrections

2. **Contract dispute.**
   Draft: "In his later explanation, the disagreement concerned an arrangement McConnell had made for him to write a weekly Football Echo column. ...    Replace with: "Accounts of the disagreement differ. Club histories describe a dispute over his wish to live and train in the North-East while Liverpool wanted him on Merseyside. In his own later explanation, Stubbins pointed to an arrangement McConnell had made for him to write a weekly Football Echo column: after the chairman's death, he said, the board did not consider itself bound by the promise, and the uncertainty delayed his signing." and "Stubbins himself later described the matter as amicable, and injuries also restricted the season."

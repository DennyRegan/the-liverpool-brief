# Fact-check: Alex Raisbeck (GPT draft, /home/claude/bios/gpt/alex-raisbeck.md)

Checked 10 November 2026.

## Sensitive-content flags

- **No mention of Heysel or the Hillsborough disaster.** (The 1902 Ibrox disaster, in which Raisbeck played, is not mentioned in the draft either. If it is added later, it should be flagged.)
- **Deaths:** Raisbeck's own death (1949). LFChistory says he died in Walton hospital after a few weeks of illness, reportedly jaundice. The draft does not give a cause, which is the safe choice. Tom Watson's death (1915) and funeral, with Raisbeck as a pall-bearer.
- **Injury:** the long injury absence in 1908–09.
- **Child labour:** he started work in a coal mine at twelve. This is the subject's own account and is not sensitive, but it is personal background.

## 1. Summary

The draft is mostly reliable. The priority facts hold up: born 26 December 1878 (the draft does not give the date), died 12 March 1949; signed from Hibernian (after a loan at Stoke) for £350 in April 1898; 341 appearances and 19 goals; captain of the 1900–01 and 1905–06 title sides; 8 Scotland caps from 1900 to 1907; Partick Thistle from 1909. There are three clear factual errors: what Liverpool needed at Villa Park in 1899, the 1904–05 league figures, and the 1909 league table. Several memoir-based claims could not be found in the source text.

Errors:
1. **Villa Park, 29 April 1899.** "Liverpool needed a draw at Aston Villa to win the championship". Wrong. The clubs were level on 43 points and Villa had the better goal average (1.775 against 1.75), so only a win would do (LFChistory). This is the same error that appeared in the Tom Watson draft.
2. **1908–09 final table.** "Liverpool's victory left them seventeenth, two points above eighteenth-placed Manchester City." Wrong. Bradford City finished 18th on 34 points and stayed up. Manchester City finished 19th on 34 points and were relegated with Leicester Fosse. Liverpool's own position is disputed: 17th according to LFChistory, but 16th according to Wikipedia and StatCity, on goal average over Bury (0.877 against 0.818). What is safe to say is that Liverpool finished two points clear of the relegated Manchester City.
3. **1904–05 league figures.** "Raisbeck played 33 of Liverpool's 36 league matches ... conceded only 28 times." The Second Division had 18 clubs, so Liverpool played 34 league matches, and Raisbeck played 33 of them. Liverpool conceded 25 league goals. The 36 games and 28 goals come from LFChistory's all-competitions figure, which includes the two FA Cup ties.
4. **John Walker "whom he already knew"** cannot be sourced. In his memoir (part 5), Raisbeck calls Walker his "special pal" at Liverpool and lists several Scottish teammates who welcomed him. Nothing says he knew Walker before joining.
5. **Final whistle at Newcastle "an exceptionally satisfying moment"** could not be sourced. Part 11 of the memoir could not be retrieved, and no other source records this recollection.
6. **Memoir on star players versus a unit.** The draft says he argued that "well-known players assembled together could be less effective than less celebrated men accustomed to working as a unit". This was not found in that form. The closest passage (part 4) contrasts club football, where "you may get through a power of work, thanks to an understanding with your club-mates", with representative matches, where "there is more left to the individual".
7. **Captaincy start (minor, sources disagree).** The draft follows LFChistory: Storer was captain on his debut and Raisbeck took over in 1900–01. That is right. Note, though, that in the memoir (part 7) Raisbeck claims he was captain "from his first game in a red jersey". Leave the draft as written, but do not attribute a start date to Raisbeck's own account.
8. **Signing club (minor, sources disagree).** LFChistory gives "from Hibernian, 30 April 1898, £350". LFC.com's "Liverpool's Greatest" gives April 1898 and £350 after "a short loan at Stoke City". But LFC.com's player info page says Liverpool paid £350 "to tempt Stoke to part with their prized asset". The draft's wording, "from Hibernian" after a loan at Stoke, is the best-supported version. Keep it.

## 2. Current status (as of 10 November 2026)

Deceased. Alex Raisbeck died on 12 March 1949, aged 70, in Liverpool (Walton hospital, per LFChistory). He was working as a Liverpool scout at the time. He is buried in Anfield Cemetery (Scots Football Historians Group).

# Fact-check: Elisha Scott (draft: /home/claude/bios/gpt/elisha-scott.md)

Checked 10 November 2026. Independent check; the GPT draft and its source list were treated as pointers only.

## Sensitive events flag

- **Death:** the draft records Scott's death (Belfast, 16 May 1959). This is a historical death and is handled plainly. No illness is mentioned in the draft. Some sources say he had a series of strokes in the 1950s; the draft does not mention them and should not add them.
- **No Heysel or Hillsborough disaster references.**
- **Background only (not in the draft):** Belfast Celtic left the Irish League in 1949 after sectarian crowd violence. If any later edit mentions the end of Scott's Celtic career, handle this with care.

## 1. Summary

The draft is largely reliable on Scott's core Liverpool record (dates, 468/430 totals, debut, last match, title seasons, 31 caps, death date). Its weaknesses are in the colour material: several attributed recollections (McKinlay, Evening Express/Echo comments, the Villa "finest match" claim, Stephen Done's letters, Brodie, the Belfast Celtic history) could not be found. It also leaves out two things the brief asked about: the Dixie Dean rivalry, and the fact that Liverpool had agreed to sell Scott to Everton in 1934 before supporters' protests stopped the move.

**Errors and issues:**
1. Why Everton passed on him: the draft says "his stature". Sources disagree. LFC "Liverpool's Greatest" says size; the LFC Boot Room, Wikipedia and Read the League say he was too young. Use wording that covers both.
2. The draft says Tom Watson "brought him" to Liverpool. Watson was the manager and is credited by Read the League and Wikipedia. The LFC Boot Room and These Football Times credit chairman John McKenna. Soften the wording.
3. The draft understates the 1934 departure. Liverpool listed him at £250 and the club was set to sell him to Everton. Fan protests in the local press made the club change its mind (LFChistory, Read the League, These Football Times). "the possibility of a move to Everton provoking opposition" is accurate but incomplete.
4. Unverified attributed material: McKinlay's recollection of a reprimand, the Evening Express/Echo comments on concentration, Scott naming the 0–0 against Aston Villa on 10 September 1932 as his finest match, the April 1934 Echo letter via Stephen Done (that page would not load), the Belfast Celtic "contemporary club history" and Malcolm Brodie's recollections (belfastceltic.org needed approval, which was not given). See the Cut list.
5. The Manitoba Free Press piece (1929) describes Scott's first post-war matches against Scotland in 1919. These were Victory Internationals, not official caps; his first official cap was 13 March 1920. The draft's wording ("representative appearances") is acceptable. Do not change it to "caps".
6. The Daily Mail (30 September 1931) paraphrase goes slightly beyond the transcription found. "Catching when others might punch" and "narrowing a forward's opening" were not found word for word; the transcription found says "Scott never takes a chance", "never takes his eye of the ball" and "If Scott goes he gets the ball". Keep the paraphrase general.
7. The Dixie Dean rivalry is omitted, although it is well sourced. This is optional, but the brief flagged it.

## 2. Current status (as of 10 November 2026)

Deceased. Died 16 May 1959 in Belfast (LFChistory gives the date; Wikipedia gives the date and place; the Belfast Telegraph obituary of 16 May 1959 gives the date, says he died in hospital and says "aged 66"). The obituary age is wrong: he was born on 24 August 1893, so he was 65. Some sources say he was born in 1894 (LFC Boot Room, Play Up Liverpool). LFChistory and Wikipedia give 1893, and LFC's "40-and-a-half at his last appearance" in February 1934 only fits 1893. Keep 24 August 1893.

# Fact-check: Ron Yeats (draft: /home/claude/bios/gpt/ron-yeats.md)

Checked 10 November 2026. Independent check; the draft's own source list was treated as pointers only.

## SENSITIVE FLAGS (read first)

- **Death and illness:** the draft's final paragraph reports Yeats's death (6 September 2024, aged 86) and that he had lived with **Alzheimer's disease**. Both are confirmed: LFChistory says "a long battle with Alzheimer's"; the club says he "suffered with Alzheimer's in recent years"; ITV quotes the club as saying he "lived with Alzheimer's disease for some time". The draft's wording ("after living with Alzheimer's disease") is factual and respectful. **Keep it as it is.** Do not go further and name Alzheimer's as the *cause* of death. Wikipedia says "complications of Alzheimer's", but the club and the other obituaries do not.
- No mention of Heysel or the Hillsborough disaster.

## 1. Summary

The draft is largely reliable. Its statistics (454 appearances, 16 goals, 41 league games in 1961–62, ever-present in 1965–66, 48+ appearances in each of 1966–70, 16 in 1970–71, more than 400 as captain, two Scotland caps) all match LFChistory and the official club, and the main match facts check out (Anderlecht, Cologne, the 1965 final, the 1966 Dortmund final). The errors are small: one wrong month, one misspelt name, one unsourced injury claim, and one Tranmere detail that could not be verified.

Errors and issues:
1. **Joined Dundee United "in December 1957":** not supported. Yeats himself (LFChistory interview) says November 1957; the Courier says "late 1957"; Dundee United give his debut as January 1958. Use "late 1957".
2. **"Sigfried Held":** the Dortmund scorer is normally spelt **Siegfried Held**. (Wikipedia's final article also uses "Sigfried", which is probably where the error comes from.)
3. **"There had been an interruption through injury the previous year" (1958–59):** no source found. The only injury on record is a broken leg in 1956, before he joined United (Wikipedia). Cut it.
4. **"Missed only four matches" in 1959–60:** only partly sourced. United played 44 competitive matches (Wikipedia season page) and Wikipedia's career table gives Yeats 40 appearances, which implies four missed. The Courier says only that he played "almost every match". The draft's cited source (Arab Archive) could not be opened. Recommend softer wording.
5. **"Beat Arsenal at Highbury in the 1973 League Cup":** the match is right but the wording is ambiguous. It was the **1973–74** League Cup second round, Arsenal 0–1 Tranmere, on 2 October 1973. "1973 League Cup" could be read as the 1972–73 competition.
6. **Mark Palios "whom Yeats signed as a professional":** unverified. The only source given is Birkenhead News, which could not be opened, and no other source was found. Cut it, or verify it before publishing.
7. **Becoming Tranmere player-manager:** minor wording point. LFChistory says he joined Tranmere on New Year's Eve 1971 and managed them for about three years, and Wikipedia gives him as player-manager from 1971 to 1974. The draft is acceptable, but "became player-manager" is safer than implying he was appointed to that role on arrival.
8. **Aldridge credit:** this goes slightly beyond the source. Aldridge said only that Yeats "actually scouted me when I was at Oxford United". He does not say Yeats made the move possible.

## 2. Current status (as of 10 November 2026)

Deceased. Ron Yeats died on **6 September 2024**, aged 86 (LFChistory; Guardian obituary; the club said he died "on Friday night", and 6 September 2024 was a Friday). His death was announced on Saturday 7 September 2024.

## 4. Corrections

1. "Yeats came through Aberdeen Lads Club before joining Dundee United in December 1957."
   → "Yeats came through Aberdeen Lads Club before joining Dundee United late in 1957."
2. "That arrangement mattered during United's promotion season of 1959–60. Yeats missed only four matches across the campaign…"
   → "That arrangement mattered during United's promotion season of 1959–60, when Yeats played in almost every match…"
3. "There had been an interruption through injury the previous year, but by the time Liverpool approached…"
   → Cut the injury clause: "By the time Liverpool approached, he had experienced both…"
4. "Hunt cancelled out Sigfried Held's opening goal…"
   → "Hunt cancelled out Siegfried Held's opening goal…"
5. "…his side beat Arsenal at Highbury in the 1973 League Cup."
   → "…his side beat Arsenal 1–0 at Highbury in the League Cup in October 1973."
6. "Tranmere gave him the responsibilities of a player-manager…"
   → Acceptable. Optional: "At Tranmere he became player-manager…"
7. "John Aldridge later credited Yeats with watching him at Oxford United and helping make his move to Liverpool possible."
   → "John Aldridge later said Yeats had scouted him at Oxford United."
8. Optional precision: "The German records credit Libuda with the goal." The Liverpool club site calls it a Yeats own goal; LFChistory and Wikipedia credit Libuda. The current wording is acceptable, but "Most records credit Libuda with the goal" avoids implying that only German sources do.

Items re-verified today:

The full source sentence goes on: ", so I will be forever grateful to him for that". Cutting it is acceptable because the quoted part is a complete sentence.
- **Tommy Smith quote**: "He was my right foot and I was his left." This matches word for word and is Yeats speaking in the LFChistory interview (lfchistory.net/articles/558). - **"colossus"**: this is Shankly's word, quoted on liverpoolfc.com ("In the words of Bill Shankly, a 'colossus'") and LFChistory ("Shankly's 'Colossus'"). The bio uses the single word only, which is safe.
- **454 appearances / 16 goals**: LFChistory players/450 gives 454 and 16. Wikipedia gives 15 goals, so credit LFChistory for 16. liverpoolfc.com confirms 454.
- **417 games as captain**: LFChistory Stats/TotalGamesAsCaptain gives 417. The bio says "more than 400", which is consistent with this and with liverpoolfc.com ("more than 400 of those were as skipper").
- **First Liverpool captain to lift the FA Cup**: confirmed by liverpoolfc.com, which quotes Yeats: "the first captain of Liverpool to lift the FA Cup". LFChistory confirms that 1965 was the club's first FA Cup.
- **Two Scotland caps**: confirmed by LFChistory (v Wales, 3 October 1964; second cap 7 December 1965).
- **Tranmere win at Highbury**: Arsenal 0–1 Tranmere Rovers, Tuesday 2 October 1973, League Cup second round (footballsite.co.uk, 1973–74 League Cup).
- **Chief scout 1986–2006**: liverpoolfc.com: "In 1986… chief scout, serving for 20 years before retiring in 2006." LFChistory says Dalglish offered him the role, and Yeats said he retired "on cup final day in May 2006".
- **Death on 6 September 2024, aged 86**: LFChistory gives "passed away on 6th September 2024", and he was born on 15 November 1937. liverpoolfc.com says "died on Friday night at the age of 86". The bio says "after living with Alzheimer's disease" and does not state it as the cause of death, which is correct.

## 1. Summary

The draft is largely reliable. All five priority claims hold up: 742 matches, the first club to win the Second and First Division titles in successive seasons (1905, 1906), Sunderland titles in 1892, 1893 and 1895, born 1859 and died 6 May 1915 aged 56, and Burnley 1–0 at Crystal Palace on 25 April 1914. There is one clear statistical error (the 1904–05 goals and games figure) and one factual error about what Liverpool needed in the last game of 1898–99. A few small points need softer wording.

Errors:
1. **1904–05 defensive record.** "Conceded only 28 goals across their 36 fixtures". The league record was 25 goals conceded in 34 matches, because the Second Division had 18 clubs. The 28-in-36 figure from LFChistory includes the two FA Cup ties. The sentence sits beside "Doig played every league match", so it reads as a league figure and is wrong.
2. **Villa Park, 1899.** "Needing a draw to finish above their opponents". Wrong. The two clubs were level on 43 points and Villa had the better goal average, so only a win would do (LFChistory).
3. **Year Watson joined Sunderland (minor).** Sources disagree: 1889 (LFChistory; Wikipedia text and table) against 1888 (Wikipedia infobox). Keep 1889 but soften the wording slightly.
4. **1914 charity match (minor).** "Burnley paraded the trophy". The LFC source says only that the FA Cup was paraded and does not say who paraded it.
5. **Villa 5–1, November 1900 (minor).** Walker is not named in the contemporary account. Robertson and Raybould are.

## 2. Current status (as of 10 November 2026)

Deceased. Tom Watson died on 6 May 1915, aged 56, while still Liverpool secretary-manager. He is buried at Anfield Cemetery. Liverpool and Sunderland jointly funded a headstone for his previously unmarked grave, unveiled on the 100th anniversary of his death (May 2015).

## 4. Corrections

1. **Quoted:** "Doig played every league match in 1904–05. Liverpool conceded only 28 goals across their 36 fixtures, beat Manchester United 4–0 in the penultimate match and finished two points above Bolton Wanderers to win the Second Division."
   **Replace with:** "Doig played every league match in 1904–05. Liverpool conceded only 25 goals in 34 league fixtures, beat Manchester United 4–0 in the penultimate match and finished two points above Bolton Wanderers to win the Second Division."

2. **Quoted:** "Liverpool went to Aston Villa for their final league match needing a draw to finish above their opponents, but lost 5–0."
   **Replace with:** "Liverpool went to Aston Villa for their final league match level on points with their opponents, but Villa's better goal average meant only a win would do. Liverpool lost 5–0."

3. **Quoted:** "He had worked at Newcastle's East End and West End clubs before becoming Sunderland's secretary in 1889."
   **Safer wording:** keep as written (LFChistory and Wikipedia's text both say 1889), or use "before moving to Sunderland at the end of the 1880s" to avoid the 1888/1889 conflict.

4. **Quoted:** "...where Liverpool won and Burnley paraded the trophy."
   **Replace with:** "...where Liverpool won 1–0 and the Cup was paraded before the crowd."

5. **Quoted:** "Raybould supplied the finishing power, with Walker and Robertson among the players helping develop the attacks, while Raisbeck gave the team its central authority."
   **Replace with:** "Raybould supplied the finishing power, with Robertson among the players creating the attacks, while Raisbeck gave the team its central authority."

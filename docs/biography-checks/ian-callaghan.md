## 1. Summary

The draft is highly reliable. Every date, total, score, line-up and honour I checked is correct: birth, debut, the 857/68 and 640/49 totals, season appearance counts, the 1965 final, the four England caps, the 1977 FA Cup and European Cup finals, the last appearance, the 1978 final, the Swansea move and the 1979 interview. There are no substantive factual errors. The issues are a slightly overstated paraphrase, a wrong source link and some things the draft leaves out.

Issues:
1. **"later identified that afternoon as his most unforgettable footballing memory"**: the source (LFChistory) calls it his **favourite** memory. His words are "First has to be Wembley '65 and Liverpool lifting the FA Cup for the first time." Change to "favourite". This is minor, but "most unforgettable" is not in any source.
2. **Wrong source link**: the Englandstats URL points to Chris Lawler. Fix or remove it.
3. **"a knee operation"**: correct, but the club and LFChistory specify a **cartilage** operation. Using that word is optional and more precise.
4. **The one booking is not in the draft**: the brief asked about it. If you add it, it was on **22 March 1978, League Cup final replay v Nottingham Forest at Old Trafford (lost 0–1)**, his penultimate Liverpool game. LFChistory says "booked for the only time in his Liverpool career", and Wikipedia says it was his only career booking. Use "the only booking of his Liverpool career" as the safe form. Note that This Is Anfield (2003) wrongly says he was never cautioned.
5. **The MBE is not in the draft**: he was appointed MBE in the **1975 New Year Honours** (Wikipedia; the 1975 New Year Honours list names "Ian Robert Callaghan, Footballer, Liverpool Football Club"; liverpoolfc.com says "New Year Honours list"). You can add it if wanted.
6. **The 11-year gap**: the draft says "More than eleven years passed", which is correct. The exact gap is **11 years 49 days**, still the longest gap between England caps (Wikipedia; The Soccer History blog). England played 108 matches in between. You could add this, as it is safe.
7. **Debut age**: the draft's "six days after his eighteenth birthday" is correct (born 10 April 1942, debut 16 April 1960). liverpoolfc.com's past-player page wrongly says "aged 17". Keep the draft's wording.
8. **Later clubs**: the draft leaves out the 1979 loan to Canberra City (Australia), made while he was at Swansea. Cork United (1981) and Crewe (1981–82) are correct. His final professional game was Crewe away at Halifax Town on 20 March 1982 (National Football Museum), two months short of his 40th birthday (LFChistory). "Retiring in 1982" is correct.
9. **How 857 is counted**: this is LFChistory's and the club's figure for all competitive first-team games, including the Charity Shield and European Super Cup. Older counts differ: This Is Anfield (2003) gives 843 starts plus 5 as a substitute and 69 goals, and the Wikipedia club table adds up to 859/69. Keep 857/68, the official club figure.

## 2. Current status (as of 10 November 2026)

**Alive**, aged 84 (born 10 April 1942). There are no reports of his death. FourFourTwo published new interviews with him in March 2026 (21 March 2026, "I chose Liverpool over Everton..."). He is retired, Liverpool's all-time appearance record holder and ranked No. 10 in the club's "Liverpool's Greatest". The draft makes no claims about his current status, so nothing needs changing.

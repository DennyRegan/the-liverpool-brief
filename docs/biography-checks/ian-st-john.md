Checked 10 November 2026. This is an independent check. The draft's own source list was used only as a set of pointers. Some of the draft's cited URLs could not be opened here: the Motherwell FC pages, two liverpoolfc.com news pages and the UEFA page. Where they could not be opened, other sources were used.

## SENSITIVE FLAGS (read first)

- **Death:** the last paragraph says "St John died on 1 March 2021, aged 82." This is confirmed (LFChistory; Wikipedia; Scotsman obituary; theScore/AP, which says he died on a Monday, and 1 March 2021 was a Monday). The draft does not mention any illness, and it should stay that way. Sources say only "a long illness". Wikipedia mentions bladder-cancer surgery in 2014, but no source names a cause of death. **Do not add one.**
- **Disciplinary record / temper:** the draft says he was sent off at Fulham and Coventry. This is not a sensitive matter, but it is sourced to his own interview (confirmed below).
- **Being dropped by Shankly:** this was a personal hurt that "never fully healed" (Scotsman). The draft does not describe how it happened. If it is added, see the note under Corrections.
- No mention of Heysel or the Hillsborough disaster.

## 1. Summary

The draft is reliable on the big numbers. Birth and death dates, signing date and fee, the 425 appearances and 118 goals, the season-by-season goal figures, the honours, the 1965 final sequence, the Inter Milan goal and the Saint and Greavsie dates all check out. There are three real errors: the KR Reykjavik match is described wrongly, the Motherwell strike partnership is credited to St John when it formed after he left, and the minute of the Wembley winner is disputed. The rest are wording points.

Errors and issues:
1. **"Including twice in the opening 6–1 victory over KR Reykjavik":** wrong. Liverpool's opening European Cup match was the **5–0 win in Reykjavik (17 August 1964)**, and St John did not score in it. The 6–1 was the **second leg at Anfield (14 September 1964)**, Liverpool's first home European tie, and he scored twice in that game (23' and 75').
2. **"In the 111th minute" (1965 FA Cup final):** the sources disagree. The club's own timeline says 111, 11v11 says 113 and Wikipedia says 117. Drop the minute and say "late in extra time" or "with nine minutes of extra time left". Even that last version relies on the club's 111, so the safest wording is simply "late in extra time".
3. **"There he brought together Bobby Graham and Willie Pettigrew in attack":** misleading. St John brought Graham back from Liverpool and gave Pettigrew his debut and a full-time contract (Nutmeg). The Graham–Pettigrew partnership was formed **after St John left for Portsmouth**, under Willie McLean (Evening Times).
4. **"His Scotland career had produced nine goals in 21 appearances":** this matches LFChistory and Wikipedia. However, the Scottish FA records **20** caps, because it leaves out a substitute appearance against the Republic of Ireland on 9 June 1963. Optional safer wording is given below.
5. **"His last [appearance], in January, also brought his final Liverpool goal, closing the playing record before his departure later that year":** correct (23 January 1971; left 18 May 1971). Note that LFChistory dates his Hellenic spell from **February 1971**, before his formal departure. "During 1971" is fine.
6. **"Shankly had seen St John and Ron Yeats while managing Huddersfield Town and retained an interest in both":** the first half is confirmed, but only by St John's own account on liverpoolfc.com ("When the boss was at Huddersfield he had seen us play"). "Retained an interest" is a fair inference. Acceptable as it stands.
7. **"Followed up after Hunt's effort was saved" (Inter, 4 May 1965):** this has one source. This Is Anfield says Hunt's shot hit the keeper and St John poked in the rebound. The club article cited in the draft could not be opened. Keep it, but treat it as single-source.

## 2. Current status (as of 10 November 2026)

Deceased. Ian St John died on **1 March 2021**, aged 82, at Arrowe Park Hospital, Merseyside (Wikipedia gives the place; the date is from LFChistory, Scotsman and Wikipedia). Liverpool released the family's statement the next day.

## 4. Corrections

1. Draft: "He scored five times in Liverpool's first campaign in the competition, including twice in the opening 6–1 victory over KR Reykjavik."
   Replace with: "He scored five times in Liverpool's first campaign in the competition, including twice in the 6–1 win over KR Reykjavik at Anfield, the club's first home European tie."

2. Draft: "In the 111th minute Ian Callaghan crossed and St John headed past Gary Sprake to settle the match 2–1."
   Replace with: "Late in extra time Ian Callaghan crossed and St John headed past Gary Sprake to settle the match 2–1."

3. Draft: "There he brought together Bobby Graham and Willie Pettigrew in attack before leaving for Portsmouth after little more than a year."
   Replace with: "There he brought Bobby Graham back from Liverpool and gave the young Willie Pettigrew his debut, before leaving for Portsmouth after little more than a year."

4. (Optional, for precision) Draft: "His Scotland career had produced nine goals in 21 appearances."
   Safer: "He won 21 Scotland caps, scoring nine goals." Alternatively, add "(the Scottish FA counts 20, omitting a substitute appearance)". Either version is acceptable; the draft's figure matches LFChistory.

**On how he left (not in the draft, but the brief asked about it).** If the edit adds it, use this sourced version. After a poor run, Shankly dropped him for a game at Newcastle without telling him. St John found out while talking to Jackie Milburn (by then a journalist), who was handed the team sheet (Irish Times). The Scotsman dates it to autumn 1969 and says St John "learned of it from a journalist" and that the rift "never fully healed". St John's own words (LFChistory interview): "He let me down and he did it with other players as well." Safe wording: "Shankly eventually dropped him without telling him to his face; St John learned of it from a journalist, and the hurt never fully healed."

## Changes made (old → new, source)

1. "The hurt never fully healed." → "The rift never fully healed, according to the Scotsman."
   The claim is sourced. The Scotsman says Shankly's decision "caused a rift that never fully healed". The editor had stated it as unattributed fact and changed "rift" to "hurt". It now uses the source's word and names the source.
2. "By his own account, Bill Shankly had seen him and Ron Yeats play while he was managing Huddersfield Town." → "By St John's own account, Bill Shankly had seen him and Ron Yeats play while managing Huddersfield Town."
   "His" could be read as meaning Shankly. The account is St John's (legacy.liverpoolfc.com, "This could have all been Huddersfield").
3. "Timing and spring let him compete with bigger defenders." → "His timing let him outjump much taller defenders."
   LFChistory says "whose timing enabled him to outjump much taller defenders". No source mentions "spring", so I took it out.
4. "As the attack changed, St John moved back into midfield." → "In the later 1960s St John dropped back into midfield."
   No source supports "as the attack changed"; it was the editor's scene-setting. LFChistory says he "had dropped further back using his tactical nous in the middle of the park".

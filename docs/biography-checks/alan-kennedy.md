## 1. Summary

The draft is reliable. Every major figure checked out: birth date, the £330,000 fee, the debut, the season-by-season league appearances, the 359/20 and 251/15 totals, every match in 1983–84, the 1981 and 1984 European Cup finals, both League Cup finals and the two England caps. There are no hard factual errors. What needs fixing is one unverifiable detail (the "2015" date of the club interview), a birthplace on which the sources disagree, and one wording ambiguity over the number of league titles. The draft contains no direct quotations.

Numbered issues:

1. **"Recalling it in a 2015 interview with Liverpool"**: the club article is undated (it says only "On this day in 1981"). The year 2015 can't be verified.
2. **"Born in Sunderland"**: LFChistory says Sunderland; Wikipedia's infobox says Penshaw, a village in the City of Sunderland. This is minor, but "Born in Sunderland" is safe only in the broad sense.
3. **"five league championships"**: correct as medals won (1979, 1980, 1982, 1983, 1984). However, the official club page also lists 1985–86 for him (he played 8 league games before leaving), and the club's "Liverpool's Greatest" piece says "six First Division titles". Keep five, but word it so the count can't be challenged (see Corrections).
4. **"at the near post"** (Paris 1981): LFChistory ("inside Agustin's near post") and liverpoolfc.com ("near-post finish") support it. FourFourTwo (2022) describes the shot as going "across the face of goal into the far corner". Keeping it is acceptable because two strong sources support it, but cutting "at the near post" is safest.
5. **Not an error, a gap:** the draft says only "an injury" before the 1981 final. Three sources say it was a **broken wrist**. Kennedy played the whole first leg of the semi-final against Bayern at Anfield on 8 April 1981 and missed the following matches. The brief asked for the wrist to be checked, and it can safely be named.
6. **Not an error, a gap:** the draft does not mention the British full-back record fee or the "Barney Rubble" nickname. Both are confirmed below if Denny wants them added.

## 2. Current status (as of 10 November 2026)

- He lives on Merseyside. A Liverpool Echo feature (via inkl, undated) describes him combining youth coaching, after-dinner speaking, hospitality work for LFC and media appearances. Earlier, liverpoolfc.com (c. 2011) had him hosting fans in the Carlsberg Lounge on matchdays.
- He is ranked No. 39 in liverpoolfc.com's "Liverpool's Greatest" series.
- Safe wording if needed: "Kennedy still lives on Merseyside and remains a regular presence around Anfield through the club's hospitality and media work." Avoid naming a specific current job title, because none could be dated to 2026.

## 4. Corrections

1. Quoted: "Recalling it in a 2015 interview with Liverpool, Kennedy said the club doctor had expected an absence of about ten weeks."
   Replacement: "Recalling it later on the club's website, Kennedy said the club doctor had told him he would be out for about ten weeks."

2. Quoted: "An injury in the first leg of the semi-final against Bayern Munich threatened to keep him out."
   Optional, more precise replacement: "A broken wrist, suffered in the goalless first leg of the semi-final against Bayern Munich at Anfield, threatened to keep him out."

3. Quoted: "Kennedy helped Liverpool win five league championships before leaving in 1985."
   Safer replacement: "Kennedy won five league championship medals before leaving in 1985." (The club credits him with 1985–86 as well, so "helped Liverpool win" invites a count of six.)

4. Quoted: "Born in Sunderland on 31 August 1954, ..."
   Acceptable as written (LFChistory). If Denny wants to avoid the Penshaw conflict: "Born on Wearside on 31 August 1954, ..."

5. Quoted: "...struck a left-footed shot past Agustín at the near post."
   Acceptable (LFChistory and the club). Safest: "...struck a left-footed shot past Agustín." The sources disagree on which side of the goal it went in.

6. Quoted: "Four years later, Bob Paisley brought him to Anfield for £330,000."
   Accurate. Optional addition: "...for £330,000, then a British record for a full-back."

## 4. Corrections

1. "Paisley subsequently served on the board and returned to advise Dalglish when he became player-manager in 1985."
   - Problem: "returned" implies he had left, and the sources disagree on whether the directorship came before or after the adviser role.
   - Safe wording: **"In retirement Paisley joined the board and, from 1985, acted as an adviser to Dalglish when he became player-manager."**

2. "Neal later recalled a specific instruction that illustrated Paisley's preparation. Expecting Berti Vogts to mark Keegan closely, the manager wanted his forward to keep moving across the pitch. Keegan's running tested Vogts throughout the game and eventually won the penalty from which Neal completed the scoring."
   - Problem: the specific instruction could not be verified (cited UEFA article blocked; absent from other sources). The rest is confirmed.
   - Safe wording if the UEFA article cannot be checked: **"Keegan's running tormented Berti Vogts throughout the game and eventually won the penalty from which Neal completed the scoring."** Then cut or rework the closing line ("Paisley's tactical contribution could be expressed in a few words and still shape a final"), because it depends on the instruction.
   - If an editor can open the UEFA article and confirm Neal's account, the original can stay.

3. "Writing after Paisley's death, Ray Clemence recalled a manager who explained his tactical instructions simply, commanded respect and could be compassionate when a player had problems away from football."
   - Problem: the cited Independent piece could not be fetched. Byline, date and content are all unverified.
   - Recommend an editor opens the link to confirm it. If it cannot be confirmed, cut the sentence, or replace it with: **"He lacked Shankly's appetite for public performance, but the players already knew his football judgement."** (already in the draft, so the sentence can simply be cut).

4. "Recalling the 1978 final, Souness emphasised how effectively Liverpool could control European opponents through their passing."
   - Problem: unverified (cited UEFA article blocked).
   - Safe option: cut it. The paragraph stands without it on the confirmed Paisley material.

5. "Playing at left-half, he made 33 league appearances in Liverpool's title-winning season of 1946–47."
   - Problem: Wikipedia says 34. LFChistory (specialist source) says 33.
   - Keep 33 on LFChistory's authority, or use the safer wording: **"he was a regular in Liverpool's title-winning season of 1946–47, making 33 league appearances by LFChistory's count."** Either is acceptable.

Optional (accuracy, not error): "He stayed to work with the reserves and became the club's physiotherapist" could become **"He stayed on as reserve-team trainer, qualified as a physiotherapist and was made chief coach in 1957"** (LFChistory articles/2293).

If a match total is ever added: use **534 matches** (LFChistory, liverpoolfc.com) and do not credit him with the 1974 Charity Shield. LFChistory treats it as Shankly's last game in charge; Wikipedia's 535 includes it.

## Changes made (in place, /home/claude/bios/work/bob-paisley/bio.md)

1. **Trainer and coach**
   - Old: "When Shankly arrived in 1959, Paisley was already on the staff, and he became first-team trainer."
   - New: "He became first-team trainer in 1959, the year Bill Shankly arrived as manager."
   - Why: the old sentence suggests Paisley got the job when or after Shankly arrived. Wikipedia says he became first-team trainer in August 1959 when Albert Shelley retired, which was before Shankly arrived that December. liverpoolfc.com says Paisley was at Shankly's side "from the day he arrived at Anfield in 1959". The new wording is accurate whichever came first.
   - Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Paisley ; https://www.liverpoolfc.com/info/bob-paisley-0

2. **Paisley quotation and the paraphrase around it**
   - Old: "Paisley later described learning from continental teams how to intercept the ball and start attacks with a carefully chosen first pass. ... Small-sided games in training helped develop the quick decisions and movement his team needed."
   - New: "Paisley later explained that continental teams had learned to intercept the ball, and that Europe's best sides taught Liverpool how to build attacks from the back. "The pace of their movement was dictated by the first pass," he said. He also called the five-a-side games in training "key"."
   - Why: I opened the UEFA article this time. The quote "The pace of their movement was dictated by the first pass" is there word for word and is Paisley's, so it stays. The old paraphrase got the point the wrong way round: in UEFA's account it was the continental teams who learned to intercept, and that blunted the British strength in challenging for the ball. Liverpool learned from Europe's best sides how to play out from the back. The old line about small-sided games building "quick decisions and movement" went further than the source. UEFA gives Paisley's verbatim words as "Those five-a-side games were key."
   - Source: https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/022a-0e93411c2afb-540d557c72f0-1000--paisley-s-european-cup-legacy-at-liverpool/

3. **Board and adviser sentence**
   - Old: "In retirement Paisley joined the board and, from 1985, acted as an adviser to Dalglish when he became player-manager."
   - New: "In retirement Paisley advised Dalglish, who became player-manager in 1985, and also served as a director of the club."
   - Why: the sources disagree on the order of the two roles.
     - liverpoolfc.com says "On retirement, he was elected to the board of directors" and that he advised Dalglish.
     - LFChistory (managers/10) says he advised Dalglish for a couple of years before becoming a board member.
     - Wikipedia says he was an informal adviser from 1985 for two years and a director until early 1992.
   - The old wording took liverpoolfc.com's order as fact. The new wording keeps both roles and the confirmed 1985 date without claiming an order. I did not add the 1992 retirement as director.
   - Sources: https://www.liverpoolfc.com/info/bob-paisley-0 ; https://www.lfchistory.net/managers/10 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Paisley

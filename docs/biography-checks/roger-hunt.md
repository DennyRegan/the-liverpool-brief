# Fact-check: Roger Hunt (draft: /home/claude/bios/gpt/roger-hunt.md)

Checked 10 November 2026. This is an independent check. The draft's own source list was used only as a set of pointers. Some of the draft's cited URLs could not be opened here: the liverpoolfc.com obituary at the "445003" address and The FA obituary. ## SENSITIVE FLAGS (read first)

- **Death:** the last paragraph says "He died on 27 September 2021, aged eighty-three." This is confirmed (LFChistory; Wikipedia; This Is Anfield; Independent/Yahoo obituary). Liverpool FC's statement says he died at home "after a long illness". The draft does not mention illness and should not. No source names a cause, so **do not add one.**
- **Family members:** the draft names his daughter Julie and gives her recollections. They come from the club's own interview with her and are confirmed (see Quotes). The material is not sensitive, but it is personal family material.
- No mention of Heysel or the Hillsborough disaster.

## 1. Summary

The draft is very reliable. It has no outright factual errors on the points that matter. Birth and death dates, the signing, the Scunthorpe debut, the 285/492 and 244 league totals, the 41 goals in 1961–62, the title-season league tallies, the 1965 final sequence, the Hampden equaliser, the Match of the Day goal, the 1966 World Cup record, the 34 caps and 18 goals, the Hodgson record, the Bolton move and the testimonial all check out. The points below are about precision, not errors.

Issues:
1. **"Phil Taylor signed him from Stockton Heath in July 1958":** correct as a date of joining (LFChistory: 29 July 1958; club: "July 1958, aged 20"). However, he joined as an amateur and **turned professional in May 1959** (Independent/Yahoo obituary). The current wording is acceptable. An optional more precise version is given below.
2. **"Hunt broke the deadlock with a low header":** LFChistory calls it a "stooping header". The meaning is similar, but "stooping header" matches the source. It came in the 93rd minute from Gerry Byrne's cross. Optional change.
3. **"including 244 in the league":** this matches Liverpool FC, LFChistory, AFP and Wikipedia. The National Football Museum says **245**. Keep 244, the club's own figure.
4. **"In 2006 he entered the National Football Museum's Hall of Fame":** confirmed by the NFM itself. At the time it was usually called the English Football Hall of Fame. Acceptable as written.
5. **Substitution incident (from the checking brief, not in the draft):** the draft does **not** mention the famous substitution. If it is added, note that LFChistory and Play Up Liverpool both place it in the **FA Cup fifth-round replay v Leicester City at Anfield, 3 March 1969**, not against Stoke. Hunt was taken off with 17 minutes left for Bobby Graham and threw his shirt towards the dugout. I found no source for a Stoke version.
6. **Dortmund final:** the draft rightly does not name Dortmund's extra-time scorer. LFChistory records the winner as a **Ron Yeats own goal (107')**, while it is commonly credited to Libuda. Keep it unnamed.
7. **Birthplace:** "near Warrington" is the safe wording. LFChistory says Golborne, Wikipedia says Glazebury, and the Independent says he grew up in Culcheth. Do not name a village.
8. **MBE 2000:** confirmed (LFChistory, NFM, Wikipedia). One obituary (Independent via Yahoo) says "OBE", which is wrong. Keep MBE.

## 2. Current status (as of 10 November 2026)

Deceased. Roger Hunt died on **27 September 2021**, aged 83 (a Monday; Liverpool's statement says "Monday evening", at home). Wikipedia gives the place as Warrington.

## 4. Corrections

No sentence is factually wrong. These are optional precision changes:

1. "Phil Taylor signed him from Stockton Heath in July 1958, after former Liverpool player Bill Jones had spotted him in amateur football."
   - Optional: "Phil Taylor signed him from Stockton Heath in July 1958, initially as an amateur, after former Liverpool player Bill Jones had spotted him in Mid-Cheshire League football. He turned professional the following year." (The "turned professional in May 1959" point comes from a single source, the Independent obituary. "The following year" is safer.)
2. "Hunt broke the deadlock with a low header."
   - Suggested: "Three minutes into extra time, Hunt broke the deadlock with a stooping header from Gerry Byrne's cross."
3. "In 2006 he entered the National Football Museum's Hall of Fame."
   - Acceptable as written. If preferred: "In 2006 he was inducted into the English Football Hall of Fame at the National Football Museum."

Do **not** change 244 to 245, and do **not** name the Dortmund winner's scorer.
